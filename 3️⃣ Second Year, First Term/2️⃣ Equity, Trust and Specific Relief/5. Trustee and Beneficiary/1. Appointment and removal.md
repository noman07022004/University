
# 5.1 Appointment and Removal of Trustees ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** A trust cannot function without a human operator. Understanding _who_ can manage the property and _how_ to get rid of a bad manager is practical and essential.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4):** Questions often focus on the "Appointment of New Trustees" under Section 73 or scenarios where beneficiaries want to remove a trustee.
    

---

### I. Who Can Be a Trustee? (Capacity)

Before appointing a trustee, one must know who is eligible.

- **General Rule (Section 10, Trusts Act 1882):** Every person capable of holding property may be a trustee.
    
- **Competency:** However, where the trust involves the exercise of discretion, the person must be **competent to contract** (Major, Sound Mind).
    
- **Minors:** A minor can technically be a trustee (hold property), but they cannot be forced to perform duties or be sued for breach. Therefore, appointing a minor is practically avoided.
    
- **Aliens/Foreigners:** Can be trustees, but it is risky if they live outside the jurisdiction of the court.
    

---

### II. Appointment of Trustees

Trustees are appointed in three primary ways:

**1. By the Author (The Settlor)**

- The most common method. The author names the trustees in the Trust Deed or Will.
    
- **Acceptance:** No one is bound to accept a trust. The appointee can **disclaim** (refuse) the office. However, once they accept (even by conduct), they cannot resign easily.
    

**2. By the Court (Judicial Appointment)**

- **Maxim:** _"Equity will not let a trust fail for want of a trustee."_
    
- If all named trustees die, refuse to act, or are unfit, and the trust deed provides no mechanism to replace them, the **Civil Court** will appoint a new trustee (Section 74).
    

**3. By Existing Trustees (Statutory Power - Section 73)**

- This is the most practically important section. If a trustee dies, resigns, leaves Bangladesh for 6 months, or becomes unfit, the **continuing (surviving) trustees** (or the retiring trustee) have the statutory power to appoint a new trustee in writing. This avoids the expensive need to go to court every time a vacancy arises.
    

---

### III. Removal and Discharge of Trustees

A trustee can exit their office in two ways: **Discharge** (leaving voluntarily/legally) or **Removal** (being forced out).

**1. Discharge (Section 71)** A trustee is discharged from their office:

- **By Extinction:** The trust ceases to exist.
    
- **By Completion:** The duties are finished.
    
- **By Appointment of New Trustee:** When a replacement is appointed under Section 73.
    
- **By Consent:** If all beneficiaries are competent (adults) and consent to the trustee’s resignation.
    
- **By Court:** If the trustee petitions the court to be relieved of their duties (usually due to old age or illness).
    

**2. Removal (Forced)** A trustee can be removed if they are unfit or act against the trust. Grounds for removal include:

- **Misconduct:** Breach of trust or dishonesty.
    
- **Bankruptcy:** An insolvent person is presumed unfit to manage money.
    
- **Absence:** Continuous absence from Bangladesh (usually 6 months or more) preventing them from managing affairs.
    
- **Friction:** Severe hostility between the trustee and beneficiary that stops the trust from functioning (though mere disagreement is not enough).
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Section 73 (Trusts Act 1882):** This is the "Safety Valve" of trust law. In exams, if a problem asks "How can a new trustee be appointed without going to court?", the answer is almost always **Section 73**.
    
- **Disclaimer:** A person appointed as trustee can refuse _before_ they accept. But once they accept, they cannot just "quit." They must be formally _discharged_ (Sec 71).
    
- **Comparison:**
    
    - **Appointment:** Getting into the office.
        
    - **Discharge:** Getting out of the office validly.
        
    - **Removal:** Being kicked out for bad behavior.
