# 5. Trustee and Beneficiary ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** A Trust is nothing without the relationship between the Trustee and the Beneficiary. Understanding the strict "Fiduciary" nature of this relationship is essential to grasping why the law is so harsh on trustees who make mistakes.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a critical area for problem questions. You will often face scenarios where a trustee has acted negligently or profited from the trust, and you must determine their liability and the beneficiary's rights.

### Introduction

The relationship between a Trustee and a Beneficiary is one of the most significant "fiduciary" relationships in law. A fiduciary relationship is one of absolute trust and confidence. Because the Trustee holds the legal title and control over the property, the law imposes strict duties on them to prevent abuse. Conversely, the Beneficiary holds the "Equitable Estate" and possesses rights to ensure the trust is enforced. The **Trusts Act, 1882** governs these dynamics in Bangladesh.

---

### I. The Trustee: Appointment and Removal

_(Covering Subtopic: 5.1 Appointment and removal)_

**1. Appointment of Trustees**

- **Who can be a Trustee?** Generally, any person capable of holding property and competent to contract can be a trustee. A minor can be a trustee potentially, but they cannot undertake legal obligations, so it is practically avoided.
    
- **How are they appointed?**
    
    - **By the Author:** The settlor names them in the Trust Deed.
        
    - **By the Court:** If no trustee is named, or the named trustee dies/refuses, the Court (under its equitable jurisdiction) will appoint one. _Equity will not let a trust fail for want of a trustee._
        
    - **By Existing Trustees:** Under Section 73 of the Trusts Act, 1882, retiring or continuing trustees can sometimes appoint new ones to fill vacancies.
        

**2. Removal and Discharge** A trustee may be removed or discharged if:

- **Death:** The office is vacated.
    
- **Refusal:** They disclaim the trust.
    
- **Absence:** They leave Bangladesh for a continuous period (usually 6 months or more) without leaving a proxy.
    
- **Insolvency:** A bankrupt person is generally unfit to manage others' money.
    
- **Misconduct:** If the trustee acts dishonestly or dangerously with the property.
    

---

### II. Rights, Duties, and Powers of Trustees

_(Covering Subtopics: 5.2 Rights and duties, 5.3 Powers and functions)_

It is crucial to distinguish between **Duties** (things they _must_ do) and **Powers** (things they _can_ do if they choose).

**1. Duties of Trustees (Mandatory)** These are strict obligations. Failure to perform them is a "Breach of Trust."

- **Duty to Execute the Trust (Sec 11):** Must fulfill the purpose of the trust exactly as defined by the author.
    
- **Duty to Inform:** Must keep clear and accurate accounts and provide information to the beneficiary upon request.
    
- **Duty to Protect Title:** Must defend the trust property against legal attacks.
    
- **Duty not to Commingle:** Must keep trust property separate from their own private property.
    
- **Duty to Invest (Sec 20):** Must invest trust money only in "approved securities" (safe government bonds, etc.) and not in risky speculations.
    
- **Duty of Impartiality:** If there are multiple beneficiaries (e.g., one gets income for life, another gets the capital later), the trustee must not favor one over the other.
    

**2. Powers of Trustees (Discretionary)** The trustee has the authority to take certain actions to manage the trust efficiently.

- **Power of Sale:** To sell property if authorized or necessary.
    
- **Power to Maintain:** To use trust income for the maintenance or education of a minor beneficiary.
    
- **Power to Compromise:** To settle debts or disputes regarding the trust property to avoid expensive litigation.
    

---

### III. Liabilities and Disabilities of Trustees

_(Covering Subtopics: 5.4 Liabilities and disabilities, 5.5 Limitation)_

**1. Disabilities (The "No Profit" Rule)** A trustee suffers from certain legal disabilities to prevent conflicts of interest.

- **Cannot Buy Trust Property:** A trustee cannot buy the trust property for themselves, even at a fair market price. This is an absolute rule to prevent self-dealing.
    
- **Cannot Delegate:** _Delegatus non potest delegare_ (A delegate cannot delegate). A trustee cannot hand over their job to someone else, except for ministerial acts (like hiring a stockbroker or lawyer). The ultimate decision-making must remain with the trustee.
    
- **Cannot Charge for Services:** Unless the trust deed allows it, a trustee acts gratuitously (for free).
    

**2. Liabilities**

- **Liability for Breach of Trust:** If a trustee violates a duty (e.g., invests in a risky startup that fails), they must personally make good the loss.
    
- **Joint and Several Liability:** If there are co-trustees, and one commits a breach that the others could have prevented, _all_ may be liable.
    

**3. Limitation**

- **Section 10 of the Limitation Act, 1908:** This is a vital rule. It states that **no limitation period applies** to suits against a trustee for specific trust property.
    
- _Meaning:_ If a trustee steals trust property, the beneficiary can sue them 20 or 50 years later. The trustee cannot say, "It's been too long."
    
- _Exception:_ For general accounts or minor clerical errors, standard limitation periods (usually 3 or 6 years) may apply depending on the specific claim, but for recovery of property, time does not run.
    

---

### IV. Beneficiary: Rights and Liabilities

_(Covering Subtopic: 5.6 Rights and liabilities of the beneficiaries)_

**1. Rights of the Beneficiary**

- **Right to Rents and Profits:** To receive the income generated by the trust.
    
- **Right to Inspect Documents:** To see the trust deed, accounts, and vouchers.
    
- **Right to Compel Performance:** To sue the trustee to force them to carry out their duties.
    
- **Right to Transfer Interest:** A beneficiary can generally sell or mortgage their equitable interest in the trust.
    
- **The Rule in _Saunders v Vautier_:** If all beneficiaries are adults (sui juris) and of sound mind, and they are solely entitled to the property, they can collectively command the trustee to transfer the legal title to them and **terminate the trust**, overriding the creator's original wishes.
    

**2. Liabilities of the Beneficiary**

- **Liability to Indemnify:** If the trustee properly incurs expenses for the trust (e.g., paying property tax), the beneficiary (or the trust fund) must reimburse the trustee.
    
- **Liability for Breach:** If a beneficiary concurs or assists in a breach of trust (e.g., tells the trustee to invest illegally), they cannot later sue the trustee for that breach.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Fiduciary Relationship:** Always use this term. The trustee is a _fiduciary_. This explains why they cannot make a profit.
    
- **Section 10, Limitation Act:** Memorize this interaction. The general rule is "Delay defeats Equity," but for Trusts, the statute (Limitation Act) says time generally doesn't run against a fraudulent trustee.
    
- **Problem Question Tip:** If a trustee sells trust land to his wife:
    
    - This violates the "Disability" (cannot buy trust property).
        
    - The Beneficiary can either recover the land (Tracing) or sue the trustee for the profit (Personal Remedy).
        
- **Standard of Care:** The standard required of a trustee is that of a **"prudent man of business"** managing his own affairs. If they fall below this standard, they are negligent.
