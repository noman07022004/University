
# 5.2 Rights and Duties of Trustees ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** The "Duties" section is the core of Trust law. If a trustee fails in a duty, it is a "Breach of Trust." You must understand what is expected of a trustee to determine if they are liable.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is the most frequent area for problem questions. (e.g., "The trustee invested in a friend's business and lost money. Is he liable?"). You must cite the specific duties violated.
    

---

### I. Duties of Trustees (Mandatory Obligations)

Duties are acts the trustee **must** perform. Failure to do so leads to liability. These are codified in **Sections 11-22** of the **Trusts Act, 1882**.

**1. Duty to Execute the Trust (Section 11)**

- **The Rule:** The trustee must fulfill the purpose of the trust exactly as defined by the author.
    
- **Strictness:** They cannot deviate even if they think another way is better. If the deed says "Invest in Government Bonds," they cannot invest in Apple Inc., even if Apple makes more money.
    
- **Exception:** If the instruction is illegal or impossible.
    

**2. Duty to Inform and Account (Section 19)**

- **The Rule:** The trustee must keep clear and accurate accounts of the trust property.
    
- **Right to Inspect:** They must allow the beneficiary to inspect the accounts and documents at all reasonable times. A trustee cannot say "Trust me, it's fine" without showing the proof.
    

**3. Duty of Care (The "Prudent Man" Rule - Section 15)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee must deal with the trust property as carefully as a **"man of ordinary prudence"** would deal with **his own property**.
    
- **Implication:** They are not expected to be a genius or a psychic. If they acted reasonably but the market crashed, they are not liable. If they acted recklessly (e.g., leaving cash in an unlocked drawer), they are liable.
    

**4. Duty to Invest (Section 20)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee cannot leave money idle. They must invest it in "approved securities" (usually safe government bonds or debentures) to generate income.
    
- **Restriction:** They must not take risks. Speculating on the stock market with trust money is generally a breach unless the deed explicitly allows it.
    

**5. Duty of Impartiality (Section 17)**

- **The Rule:** Where there are multiple beneficiaries (e.g., Tenant for Life vs. Remainderman), the trustee must not favor one over the other.
    
- **Application:** They cannot invest in high-risk stocks that give high dividends (helping the Life Tenant) but risk destroying the capital (hurting the Remainderman).
    

**6. Duty Not to Profit (Fiduciary Duty)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee is a fiduciary. They cannot make a profit from their office.
    
- **No Remuneration:** Unless the deed says otherwise, a trustee acts for free (gratuitously).
    
- **No Self-Dealing:** They cannot buy trust property for themselves.
    

---

### II. Rights of Trustees (Privileges)

While the burden is heavy, the trustee does have certain rights to protect themselves. These are covered in **Sections 31-36** of the **Trusts Act, 1882**.

**1. Right to Title Deed (Section 31)**

- A trustee is entitled to have possession of the instrument of trust and all the documents of title relating to the trust property.
    

**2. Right to Reimbursement (Indemnity) (Section 32)**

- A trustee generally acts for free, but they should not lose their own money.
    
- **The Rule:** They have a right to be reimbursed out of the trust property for all expenses properly incurred in:
    
    - The execution of the trust.
        
    - The preservation of the trust property.
        
- **Lien:** They have a "lien" (right to hold) on the trust property until these expenses are paid.
    

**3. Right to Settlement of Accounts (Section 35)**

- When a trustee retires or the trust ends, they have a right to have their accounts examined and "settled" (approved) by the beneficiaries so they can be free from future liability.
    

**4. Right to Apply to Court for Opinion (Section 34)**

- This is a vital protection. If a trustee faces a difficult legal question (e.g., "Is this beneficiary entitled to the money?"), they can apply to the Court for an opinion.
    
- **Benefit:** If they follow the Court's advice, they are indemnified (protected) from liability, even if the advice turns out to be wrong later.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Standard of Care:** In any question about negligence, quote **Section 15**. The phrase **"man of ordinary prudence dealing with his own property"** is the golden standard.
    
- **Voluntary Office:** Remember, nobody is forced to be a trustee. But if you accept, you accept the duties. You cannot later say "I was too busy."
    
- **Conflict of Interest:** The duty not to profit is absolute.
    
    - _Scenario:_ Trustee uses trust money to buy a house, sells it for a profit, and puts the original money back.
        
    - _Result:_ He is liable for **Breach of Trust**. He must give the _profit_ to the trust as well.
