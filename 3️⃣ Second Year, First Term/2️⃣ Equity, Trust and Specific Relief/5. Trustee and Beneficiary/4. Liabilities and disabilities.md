
# 5.4 Liabilities and Disabilities of Trustees ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** The "Disabilities" of a trustee define the boundaries of the office. Understanding _why_ a trustee cannot buy trust property (conflict of interest) is central to the concept of Fiduciary Duty.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** Problem questions almost always involve a trustee who tries to do something forbidden—like delegating their job to a friend or buying the trust house for themselves. You must know these specific "Disabilities" to solve them.
    

### Introduction

In Trust law, the term **"Disability"** does not mean a physical handicap. It refers to a **legal incapacity**. Because a trustee is a fiduciary with immense power over someone else's property, the law places them under strict "disabilities" (prohibitions) to prevent abuse. Conversely, **"Liability"** refers to the consequences if they violate these rules.

---

### I. Disabilities of Trustees (What they CANNOT do)

The **Trusts Act, 1882** imposes specific negative obligations on trustees to ensure they act solely for the beneficiary.

**1. Disability regarding Renunciation (Section 46)**

- **The Rule:** Once a trustee has accepted the trust, they **cannot renounce** (quit) it at their own pleasure.
    
- **Exception:** They can only retire if:
    
    - The Court permits it.
        
    - The Beneficiary (if an adult) consents.
        
    - The Trust Deed explicitly allows it.
        

**2. Disability regarding Delegation (Section 47)**

- **The Maxim:** _Delegatus non potest delegare_ ("A delegate cannot delegate").
    
- **The Rule:** A trustee cannot transfer their duties to another person. The settlor chose _them_ for their personal wisdom and integrity; they cannot hand that responsibility to a stranger.
    
- **Exceptions:** They _can_ delegate if:
    
    - It is in the **regular course of business** (e.g., hiring a stockbroker to buy shares, or a lawyer to file a suit).
        
    - It is **necessary** (e.g., the trustee is ill).
        
    - The beneficiary consents.
        

**3. Disability regarding Remuneration (Section 50)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee has no right to remuneration (payment) for their trouble, skill, and loss of time. They must act gratuitously.
    
- **Rationale:** If a trustee were paid, they might be tempted to prolong the trust just to keep getting paid.
    
- **Exception:** If the Trust Deed specifies a salary.
    

**4. Disability regarding Self-Dealing (Purchase) (Section 52)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee **cannot buy** trust property for themselves or their agents.
    
- **Absolute Prohibition:** This rule is strict. It does not matter if the trustee pays a fair market price or even _more_ than the market price. The sale is voidable at the option of the beneficiary.
    
- **Rationale:** A person cannot be both the Seller (wanting a high price for the trust) and the Buyer (wanting a low price for themselves). This is a fundamental **Conflict of Interest**.
    

**5. Disability regarding Use of Property (Section 51)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee may not use or deal with the trust property for their own profit or for any other purpose unconnected with the trust.
    

---

### II. Liabilities of Trustees (Consequences of Breach)

When a trustee fails in their duty or violates a disability, they face legal liabilities.

**1. Liability for Breach of Trust (Section 23)**

- **The Rule:** A trustee is liable to make good the loss which the trust property or the beneficiary has sustained due to the breach.
    
- **Measure:** They must restore the property + interest.
    
- **No Set-Off:** A trustee cannot offset a profit in one transaction against a loss in another.
    
    - _Scenario:_ Trustee makes an illegal investment in Company A (Gains 50k) and an illegal investment in Company B (Loses 50k).
        
    - _Result:_ The Beneficiary can claim the 50k profit from A _and_ sue the Trustee to repay the 50k loss from B. The Trustee cannot say "I broke even."
        

**2. Liability for Co-Trustees (Section 26 & 27)**

- **General Rule:** A trustee is generally _not_ liable for the acts or defaults of a co-trustee. They are only liable for their own receipt of money.
    
- **Exception (Joint Liability):** They _become_ liable if they:
    
    - Handed over trust money to the co-trustee without ensuring its safety.
        
    - Allowed the co-trustee to hold money longer than necessary.
        
    - Failed to inquire what the co-trustee was doing (Willful Default).
        

**3. Liability of Trustee After Retirement**

- A trustee remains liable for any breaches committed _during_ their time in office, even after they retire. They cannot "quit and run" to escape liability.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Self-Dealing (Section 52):** This is the most common exam trap.
    
    - _Problem:_ "Trustee X sells the trust house to himself at the full market value of 1 Crore Taka."
        
    - _Answer:_ The sale is **voidable**. The beneficiary can set it aside. The fairness of the price is irrelevant because X was under a disability to buy.
        
- **Latin Maxim:** Memorize **_Delegatus non potest delegare_**. Using this phrase when discussing Section 47 (Delegation) guarantees higher marks.
    
- **Voluntary vs. Paid:** Remember that in Bangladesh (unlike some modern US jurisdictions), the default rule is that trustees are **volunteers** (unpaid).
    
- **Set-Off Rule:** The "No Set-Off" rule highlights the strictness of Equity. Trustees get no credit for lucky gambles, but full blame for unlucky ones.
