
# 5.6 Rights and Liabilities of the Beneficiaries ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** The beneficiary is the "owner" in Equity. Understanding their rights explains _why_ the trust exists. The "Rule in _Saunders v Vautier_" is a cornerstone concept that allows beneficiaries to override the trust deed.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a critical exam topic. Questions often involve beneficiaries who want to end the trust early or sue for information. You must know exactly what they can and cannot demand.
    

### Introduction

The **Beneficiary** (or _Cestui que trust_) is the person for whose benefit the trust is created. While they do not hold the legal title, they hold the **Equitable Estate**. In Bangladesh, their rights and liabilities are codified in **Sections 55-69** of the **Trusts Act, 1882**.

---

### I. Rights of the Beneficiary

The beneficiary has powerful rights to ensure they enjoy the benefits of the property and that the trustee remains honest.

**1. Right to Rents and Profits (Section 55)**

- **The Rule:** The beneficiary has the right to receive the rents, profits, and income of the trust property (after expenses are deducted).
    
- **Nature:** This is their primary enjoyment right.
    

**2. Right to Specific Execution (Section 56)**

- **The Rule:** The beneficiary is entitled to have the intention of the author of the trust specifically executed to the extent of the beneficiary's interest.
    
- **Transfer of Possession:** If there is only one beneficiary and they are competent (adult/sane), they can require the trustee to hand over possession of the trust property to them.
    

**3. The Rule in _Saunders v Vautier_ (Right to Terminate)**

- **The Principle:** This famous English case (1841) established that if **all** the beneficiaries are of **full age** (18+) and **sound mind**, and they are collectively entitled to the entire beneficial interest, they can unanimously command the trustee to transfer the legal title to them and **terminate the trust**.
    
- **Application:** This overrides the settlor’s instructions. Even if the will says "Hold the money until he is 30," if the beneficiary is 21 and wants it _now_, he can demand it (provided he is the sole owner).
    

**4. Right to Inspect and Take Copies (Section 57)**

- **The Rule:** The beneficiary has a right to inspect and take copies of the instrument of trust, the documents of title, and the accounts of the trust property.
    
- **Transparency:** A trustee cannot keep secrets regarding the trust management.
    

**5. Right to Transfer Beneficial Interest (Section 58)**

- **The Rule:** A beneficiary (if competent) can transfer (sell, mortgage, or gift) their beneficial interest to another person.
    
- **Effect:** The new person becomes the beneficiary and steps into their shoes.
    

**6. Right to Sue for Execution (Section 59)**

- **The Rule:** If no trustees are appointed, or all die/refuse to act, the beneficiary can file a suit for the execution of the trust. The court will then execute the trust itself or appoint new trustees.
    

**7. Right to Follow Trust Property (Tracing) (Section 63)**

- **The Rule:** As discussed in Topic 4.5, if the trustee illegally sells the property, the beneficiary can "follow" (trace) it into the hands of anyone _except_ a Bona Fide Purchaser for Value Without Notice.
    

---

### II. Liabilities of the Beneficiary

While beneficiaries enjoy rights, they also bear certain liabilities, primarily to protect the trustee or third parties.

**1. Liability to Indemnify Trustee (Section 32 implied)**

- **The Rule:** If a trustee incurs proper expenses in managing the trust (e.g., paying property tax, legal fees for defending the title), the beneficiary (or the trust fund) is liable to reimburse them. The trustee has a lien on the property until paid.
    

**2. Liability for Breach of Trust (Section 68)**

- **The Rule:** Generally, a beneficiary is not liable for a breach. **However**, if a beneficiary:
    
    - **Joins** in the breach of trust.
        
    - **Consents** to the breach (knowingly).
        
    - **Instigates** (encourages) the breach.
        
- **Consequence:** Their interest in the trust property can be **impounded** (seized) to compensate the trustee or other beneficiaries for the loss.
    
- **Example:** Beneficiary A tells Trustee to bet on a horse. The money is lost. Beneficiary A cannot sue Trustee. Furthermore, Beneficiary B (who didn't consent) can sue Trustee, and Trustee can take A's share to pay B.
    

**3. Liability to Third Parties**

- If the trustee runs a business for the trust (e.g., a shop), the trustee is personally liable to creditors. However, the trustee has a right of indemnity against the trust assets. Indirectly, the beneficiary's fund is liable for these business debts.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Saunders v Vautier:** You _must_ memorize this case name. It is the most important "Right" a beneficiary has—the right to say "I am an adult now, give me my money and go away."
    
- **Section 56 vs. Discretion:** Note that the right to "Specific Execution" (Section 56) applies to _fixed_ trusts. If the trustee has _discretion_ (e.g., "Pay X as much as you think fit"), the beneficiary cannot demand a specific amount; they can only demand that the trustee considers their case honestly.
    
- **Impounding:** This is a sophisticated concept. In problem questions where a beneficiary says "It's okay, go ahead and invest in crypto," and then sues when it crashes—cite **Section 68** (Liability/Impounding). They are estopped from suing.
