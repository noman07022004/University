

# 4.2 Nature and Definition of Trust ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** This is the foundation of Section B. You cannot analyze specific trust problems (like breach or appointment) without a precise understanding of the legal definition and the roles of the parties involved.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** The statutory definition (Section 3) is a mandatory memorization item. Exam questions often ask you to "Define Trust and distinguish it from Agency or Bailment."
    

---

### I. Statutory Definition (Section 3)

In Bangladesh, the definition of a Trust is codified in **Section 3 of The Trusts Act, 1882**. It serves as the authoritative academic and legal starting point.

> **"A 'trust' is an obligation annexed to the ownership of property, and arising out of a confidence reposed in and accepted by the owner, or declared and accepted by him, for the benefit of another, or of another and the owner."**

**Breakdown of the Definition:**

1. **"Obligation annexed to ownership":** A trust is not just a moral duty; it is a legal shackle attached to the property itself. If the property moves (except to a bona fide purchaser), the obligation moves with it.
    
2. **"Confidence reposed and accepted":** It requires mutual consent. The author places trust, and the trustee _must_ accept it. You cannot force someone to be a trustee against their will.1
    
3. **"For the benefit of another":** The essence of a trust is that the legal owner (Trustee) cannot enjoy the property; they hold it strictly for someone else (Beneficiary).2
    

---

### II. The Constituen3t Elements (The Parties)

Section 3 also defines the specific technical terms for the parties involved in a trust relationship.

1. **The Author of the Trust (Settlor):**
    
    - The person who reposes or declares the confidence.
        
    - They are the original owner of the property who decides to create the trust.
        
2. **The Trustee:**
    
    - The person who accepts the confidence.
        
    - They hold the **Legal Estate**. They have possession and management power but no right to enjoyment.
        
3. **The Beneficiary (Cestui que trust):**
    
    - The person for whose benefit the confidence is accepted.
        
    - They hold the **Equitable Estate**. They receive the rents, profits, and benefits.
        
4. **The Trust Property:**
    
    - The subject matter of the trust (money, land, shares). It must be transferable property.
        
5. **The Instrument of Trust:**
    
    - The legal document (deed or will) that declares the trust.
        

---

### III. Nature of Trust

To deeply understand the nature of a trust, one must distinguish it from similar legal relationships. This "Comparative Analysis" is a staple of university exams.

**1. Trust vs. Bailment**

- **Concept:** Bailment is when you give your goods to someone for a specific purpose (e.g., giving a watch to a repairman or a car to a valet).
    
- **Ownership:**
    
    - _Trust:_ The Trustee gets **Legal Ownership**.
        
    - _Bailment:_ The Bailee gets only **Possession**. The owner retains full ownership.
        
- **Tracing:** A beneficiary can trace trust property if sold; a bailor has fewer proprietary remedies if the bailee sells the goods to a third party.
    

**2. Trust vs. Agency**

- **Concept:** An Agent acts on behalf of a Principal (e.g., a lawyer acting for a client).
    
- **Ownership:**
    
    - _Trust:_ The Trustee owns the property.
        
    - _Agency:_ The Agent does not own the principal's property; they just manage it.
        
- **Control:** An Agent is subject to the direct control/instruction of the Principal. A Trustee is generally independent and follows the _Trust Deed_, not the daily orders of the Beneficiary (unless the trust is bare).
    

**3. Trust vs. Contract**

- **Concept:** A contract is a bargain for exchange (Quid pro quo).
    
- **Consideration:**
    
    - _Contract:_ Requires "Consideration" (something of value) to be valid.
        
    - _Trust:_ Does not require consideration. A trust can be a gift (voluntary).
        
- **Privity:** Only parties to a contract can sue. In a Trust, the Beneficiary (who is often not a party to the original deed) can sue to enforce it.
    

---

### IV. Classification of the Obligation

The nature of the trust obligation is **Fiduciary**.

- **Meaning:** A relationship of absolute faith and confidence.
    
- **Implication:** The Trustee is held to a higher standard than a normal business partner. They cannot make a secret profit, must avoid conflicts of interest, and must act solely in the best interest of the beneficiary.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Memorization:** You **must** memorize Section 3 of the Trusts Act, 1882 verbatim. It is the definition used in Bangladesh.
    
- **"Cestui que trust":** This is the old French legal term for "Beneficiary." Using it in your answer shows academic sophistication ("The _Cestui que trust_ has the equitable interest...").
    
- **Key Distinction:** The most important distinction for exams is **Trust vs. Bailment**. Remember: Trustee = Owner; Bailee = Possessor.
