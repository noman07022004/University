# 4.4 Creation and Extinction of Trust ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** Knowing _how_ to create a trust is fundamental. If you fail the "Three Certainties" test, you don't have a trust at all; you just have a failed gift.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a top-tier exam topic. Problem questions frequently describe a poorly written will (e.g., "I give the bulk of my money to my friends") and ask you to determine if a valid trust exists.
    

---

### I. Creation of Trust: The "Three Certainties" Rule

For a trust to be validly created (especially an Express Trust), the court must be satisfied that the author's intent was clear. The English case of **Knight v Knight (1840)** established the "Three Certainties," which are followed in Bangladesh.

**1. Certainty of Intention**

- **Rule:** The words used must show a clear, mandatory obligation to create a trust. Mere "precatory" words (words of hope, wish, or prayer) are insufficient.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - _Valid:_ "I **direct** X to hold this money for Y." (Mandatory).
        
    - _Invalid:_ "I give this money to X, **in the hope** that he will use it for Y." (Precatory).
        
- **Result of Failure:** If intention is uncertain, the person holding the property (X) takes it as a gift for themselves.
    

**2. Certainty of Subject Matter**

- **Rule:** The property subject to the trust must be clearly identified. You cannot hold "nothing" or "something vague" in trust.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - _Valid:_ "My house at 10 Main Street," or "The 50% share of my bank account."
        
    - _Invalid:_ "The **bulk** of my estate," or "Whatever is left over." (What is "bulk"? It is too vague).
        
- **Result of Failure:** If the subject matter is uncertain, the trust is void ab initio (from the start).
    

**3. Certainty of Objects**

- **Rule:** The beneficiaries (objects) must be identifiable. The trustee must know _who_ to pay.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - _Valid:_ "For my children," "For the employees of my company."
        
    - _Invalid:_ "For my **friends**" (Who is a friend?), "For good citizens."
        
- **Result of Failure:** If the beneficiaries are uncertain, the trust fails, and the property results back to the Settlor (Resulting Trust).
    

---

### II. Statutory Formalities (Section 5, Trusts Act 1882)

In Bangladesh, intention alone is not enough; specific formalities must be met under **Section 5**.

**1. Trust of Immovable Property (Land)**

- Must be declared by a non-testamentary instrument **in writing**, signed by the author or the trustee, and **registered**.
    
- _OR_ by the will of the author (which must be probated).
    
- **Key Takeaway:** You cannot create a valid oral trust for land.
    

**2. Trust of Movable Property (Money/Goods)**

- Can be created by a written instrument (as above).
    
- _OR_ simply by **transferring the ownership** of the property to the trustee (delivery of possession). No writing is strictly required if possession is handed over.
    

---

### III. Who Can Create a Trust? (Section 7)

Any person competent to contract can create a trust:

- Must be of **Majority Age** (18+).
    
- Must be of **Sound Mind**.
    
- Must not be disqualified by law.
    
- _Note:_ A minor can create a trust with the permission of a principal Civil Court.
    

---

### IV. Extinction of Trust (Section 77)

A trust is "extinguished" (comes to an end) in the following situations:

1. **Fulfillment:** When the purpose is completely fulfilled (e.g., "Hold for A until he turns 21." A turns 21, gets the money, trust ends).
    
2. **Impossibility:** When the purpose becomes impossible (e.g., "Trust to educate X," but X dies before the trust starts).
    
3. **Destruction:** When the trust property is destroyed (e.g., The house held in trust burns down and there is no insurance).
    
4. **Revocation:** When the trust is explicitly revoked.
    

---

### V. Revocation of Trust (Section 78)

Can a creator take back their trust? It depends on how it was created.

**1. Trust Created by Will**

- **Revocable:** A will can be revoked at any time during the testator's lifetime.
    

**2. Trust Created by Non-Testamentary Instrument (Deed)**

- **General Rule:** Once you transfer property to a trustee, you generally cannot take it back.
    
- **Exceptions (When you CAN revoke):**
    
    - If the author expressly **reserved the power of revocation** in the deed.
        
    - If the trust is for the **payment of debts**, and the author has not yet communicated it to the creditors (this is technically an agency, not a full trust yet).
        

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Problem Question Trap:** "A gives land to B orally to hold for C."
    
    - _Analysis:_ Check **Section 5**. Land requires writing and registration.
        
    - _Conclusion:_ The trust is invalid due to lack of formality.
        
- **Knight v Knight:** You must cite this case when discussing the Three Certainties. It is the academic standard.
    
- **"Bulk of my estate":** This specific phrase (from the case _Palmer v Simmonds_) is the classic example of "Uncertain Subject Matter." If you see it in an exam, immediately flag it as invalid.
    
- **Beneficiary Principle:** Remember, a private trust _must_ have a human beneficiary to enforce it. A trust "for the maintenance of my favorite horse" is generally invalid (or an imperfect obligation) because a horse cannot sue the trustee.
