# 4.1 History and Growth of Trust ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** You cannot understand the strange split between "Legal" and "Equitable" ownership without knowing _why_ it was invented. The history explains why the Trustee has the power but the Beneficiary gets the money.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4):** A common exam question is: _"The Trust is the 'Use' writ large." Discuss._ or _"Trace the origin and growth of the Trust."_
    

---

### I. The Ancestor: The "Use" (Medieval Period)

The modern Trust did not start as a "Trust." It began in medieval England under the name **"Use"** (derived from the Latin _opus_, meaning "on behalf of").

**1. The Mechanism: Feoffment to Uses** A landowner (called the **Feoffor**) would transfer the legal title of his land to a group of trusted friends (called the **Feoffees to Uses**) with the instruction that they hold the land "to the use of" the landowner himself or his family (the **Cestui que use**).

- **Legal Position:** The Feoffees (friends) became the legal owners at Common Law.
    
- **Beneficial Position:** The family had the right to enjoy the land.
    

**2. Why was this done? (The Motivation)** The "Use" was essentially a tax-avoidance scheme and a way to bypass rigid inheritance laws.

- **Avoiding Feudal Dues:** Under the feudal system, when a landowner died, his heir had to pay heavy taxes (reliefs, wardships) to the feudal lord or the King. But if the land was owned by a _group_ of friends (Feoffees), the land never technically "died" because if one friend died, the others survived. Thus, no tax was due.
    
- **Avoiding Primogeniture:** Common Law demanded that the eldest son inherit _everything_. By using a "Use," a father could direct his friends to let his younger sons or daughters enjoy the land, effectively writing a will (which was illegal for land at the time).
    
- **Protecting Crusaders:** Knights going on Crusades transferred their land to friends to manage while they were gone.
    

---

### II. The Role of Equity

**1. The Betrayal** Sometimes, the "trusted friends" (Feoffees) became dishonest. They would say, "We have the legal title deeds. The Common Law says we are the owners. Get off the land!"

- **Common Law’s Failure:** The Common Law courts agreed with the Feoffees. They only looked at the formal documents. They did not recognize the rights of the family (Cestui que use).
    

**2. The Chancellor’s Intervention** The betrayed families petitioned the King, and the **Chancellor** stepped in.

- **The Logic:** The Chancellor admitted that the Feoffees were the _legal_ owners. However, he ruled that it was **against their conscience** to keep the land for themselves when they promised to hold it for others.
    
- **The Remedy:** The Chancellor compelled the Feoffees to hold the land for the benefit of the family. This separated the **Legal Estate** (held by Feoffees) from the **Equitable Estate** (held by the Family).
    

---

### III. The Statute of Uses (1535)

**1. The King Strikes Back** King Henry VIII was furious because "Uses" were depriving him of his feudal taxes. He forced Parliament to pass the **Statute of Uses, 1535**.

**2. The Effect** The Statute declared that whoever had the "Use" (benefit) of the land automatically had the "Legal Title" as well.

- _Before:_ A holds for B. (A is owner, B is beneficiary).
    
- _After:_ The Statute "executed the use," turning B into the legal owner instantly. This eliminated the separation of estates and made B liable for taxes again.
    

---

### IV. The Resurrection: "Use upon a Use"

**1. The Loophole** Lawyers, determined to save the system, found a technical flaw in the Statute. They created a **"Use upon a Use."**

- _Formula:_ "To A, to the use of B, to the use of C."
    
- _Interpretation:_ The Statute of Uses "executed" the first use (moving the legal title from A to B), but it ran out of power and didn't touch the second use.
    
- _Result:_ B became the Legal Owner (Trustee), and C became the Beneficial Owner.
    

**2. The Birth of the "Trust"** By the 17th Century (specifically after the case _Sambach v Dalston_ in 1634), the Court of Chancery declared that it would enforce this second use. To avoid confusion with the old system, this second use was renamed a **"Trust."**

- Thus, the modern Trust was born: A legal shell holding an equitable core, protected by the Chancellor against the rigidity of the law.
