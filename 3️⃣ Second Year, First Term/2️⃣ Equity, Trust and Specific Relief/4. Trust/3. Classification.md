

# 4.3 Classification of Trusts ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** Trusts are not "one size fits all." Distinguishing between a trust you _intend_ to create (Express) and one the law _forces_ on you (Constructive) is key to understanding how Equity prevents fraud.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** A very common broad exam question is "Classify Trusts." Alternatively, you will get specific short notes on "Constructive Trust" or "Resulting Trust."
    

---

### I. Classification Based on Creation (Mode of Creation)

The most important academic classification divides trusts based on _how_ they come into existence.

#### 1. Express Trust

- **Definition:** A trust created by the intentional act of the author, either by words (oral) or in writing (deed/will).
    
- **Key Feature:** The intention is clear and explicit.
    
- **Example:** A writes a deed: "I transfer this house to B to hold in trust for my son C."
    

#### 2. Implied Trust

- **Definition:** A trust not created by express words but presumed by the court based on the conduct of the parties or the circumstances.
    
- **Sub-categories:** Implied trusts are generally divided into **Resulting** and **Constructive** trusts.
    

**A. Resulting Trust (From Latin _Resalire_ = To jump back)**

- **Concept:** This occurs when the beneficial interest "jumps back" (results) to the creator. It happens when the creator _intended_ to create a trust but failed to dispose of the beneficial interest completely.
    
- **Scenarios:**
    
    - **Failure of Object:** A transfers money to B "on trust," but forgets to name a beneficiary. B cannot keep the money; he holds it on a _Resulting Trust_ for A.
        
    - **Purchase in Another’s Name:** A pays for land but puts the title in B's name (and B is a stranger, not a wife/child). Equity presumes B is holding it on a _Resulting Trust_ for A.
        

**B. Constructive Trust (Imposed by Law)**

- **Concept:** This is a trust imposed by the court _regardless_ of the parties' intention. It is a remedy to prevent unjust enrichment or fraud.
    
- **Key Maxim:** "Equity will not allow a statute to be used as an instrument of fraud."
    
- **Example:**
    
    - **Secret Profit:** If a Trustee uses trust money to buy a lottery ticket and wins, the winnings belong to the Trust, not the Trustee. The court constructs a trust over the winnings.
        
    - **Vendor-Purchaser:** Once a contract for land is signed, the seller holds the land as a _Constructive Trustee_ for the buyer until the deed is registered.

### II. Classification Based on Object (Purpose)

#### 1. Private Trust

- **Definition:** A trust created for the benefit of specific, identifiable individuals.
    
- **Characteristics:**
    
    - **Enforceability:** The specific beneficiaries can sue to enforce it.
        
    - **Duration:** It must not violate the "Rule Against Perpetuities" (it cannot last forever).
        
    - **Example:** "For my three children."
        

#### 2. Public (Charitable) Trust

- **Definition:** A trust created for the benefit of the public at large or a significant section of it.
    
- **Characteristics:**
    
    - **Permanence:** It can last forever (Perpetuity rule does not apply).
        
    - **Cy-près Doctrine:** If the purpose fails, the court can redirect the funds to a similar purpose (Topic 4.6).
        
    - **Example:** "For the relief of poverty in Dhaka" or "For the education of the blind."
        
    - **The 4 Heads of Charity (Pemsel's Case):** Relief of poverty, Advancement of education, Advancement of religion, and Other purposes beneficial to the community.
        

---

### III. Classification Based on Duties (Nature of Duty)

#### 1. Simple (Bare) Trust

- **Definition:** The trustee has no active duties to perform. Their only job is to hold the legal title and transfer it to the beneficiary when asked.
    
- **Example:** A holds shares for B (an adult). A just signs the paperwork B tells him to sign.
    

#### 2. Special (Active) Trust

- **Definition:** The trustee has active duties to perform.
    
- **Example:** "To hold this apartment, collect the rent, pay for repairs, and invest the surplus for C."
    

---

### IV. Classification Based on Timing

#### 1. Executed Trust

- **Definition:** A trust where the settlor has done everything necessary to define the interests of the beneficiaries. The scheme is complete.
    
- **Interpretation:** The court follows the strict letter of the deed.
    

#### 2. Executory Trust

- **Definition:** A trust where the settlor has indicated a general intention but left the details to be filled in later by a formal settlement.
    
- **Interpretation:** The court interprets it liberally to fulfill the settlor's intent.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Constructive Trust is a Remedy:** In modern law, Constructive Trusts are often viewed more as a _remedy_ for bad behavior (like fraud) than a real property institution. If someone steals your asset, the court says, "You are now a Constructive Trustee of that asset for the victim."
    
- **Resulting vs. Constructive:**
    
    - _Resulting:_ Based on **presumed intention** (What did the owner _probably_ want?).
        
    - _Constructive:_ Based on **operation of law** (We don't care what you wanted; this is what is fair).
        
- **Section 80-94 of Trusts Act:** These sections of the Act deal with obligations "in the nature of a trust" (Implied Trusts). Citing these sections gets you extra marks.
