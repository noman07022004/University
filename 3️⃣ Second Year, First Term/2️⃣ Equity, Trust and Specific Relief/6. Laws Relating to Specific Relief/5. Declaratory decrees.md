# 6.5 Declaratory Decrees ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** This remedy is unique because it doesn't force anyone to _do_ anything immediately. It simply "declares" the truth to prevent future disputes. It requires understanding the difference between "asserting a right" and "enforcing a right."
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a top-tier exam topic. Problem questions constantly feature the **"Proviso to Section 42"** (the rule against mere declaration). If you miss this proviso, you fail the question.
    

### Introduction

A **Declaratory Decree** is a judicial declaration of the existing rights of the plaintiff. It does not award any new right, nor does it order specific relief (like possession or damages) immediately. Its purpose is to remove a cloud over the plaintiff's title or legal character. In Bangladesh, this is governed by **Sections 42 and 43** of the **Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877**.

---

### I. When Declaration is Granted (Section 42)

**1. The Rule** Any person entitled to any **legal character**, or to any **right as to any property**, may institute a suit against any person denying, or interested to deny, his title to such character or right.

**2. The Four Conditions** To succeed under Section 42, the plaintiff must prove:

1. **Entitlement:** The plaintiff must have a valid "Legal Character" (e.g., status as a son, wife, director) or "Right to Property" (e.g., ownership of land).
    
2. **Denial:** The defendant must be denying, or be interested in denying, that right. (You cannot sue someone who agrees with you just to get a certificate).
    
3. **Discretion:** The court has the discretion to grant or refuse the declaration.
    
4. **No Bar (The Proviso):** The suit must not be barred by the Proviso (see below).
    

**3. Examples of Declaratory Suits**

- A suit to declare that the plaintiff is the legitimate son of X.
    
- A suit to declare that a document is forged (though this overlaps with Cancellation).
    
- A suit to declare that the plaintiff is the owner of a piece of land (even if he is currently in possession).
    

---

### II. The Proviso: The Bar to Mere Declaration

This is the most critical part of the law.

**The Proviso states:**

> _"Provided that no Court shall make any such declaration where the plaintiff, being able to seek further relief than a mere declaration of title, omits to do so."_

**1. Objective** To prevent **Multiplicity of Suits**. The law wants to settle the whole dispute in one go. It does not want a plaintiff to sue for a Declaration in 2024 ("I am the owner") and then sue for Possession in 2025 ("Now kick him out").

**2. Application**

- **Scenario A (Plaintiff IN Possession):** If I am living in my house, and my neighbor spreads rumors that he owns it, I _cannot_ sue for possession (because I already have it). Therefore, I _can_ sue for a **Mere Declaration** to shut him up.
    
- **Scenario B (Plaintiff OUT of Possession):** If the neighbor has kicked me out and is living in my house, I _cannot_ sue for a Mere Declaration. I _must_ sue for **Declaration + Recovery of Possession**. If I ask only for Declaration, the court will dismiss my suit under the Proviso.
    

---

### III. Effect of Declaration (Section 43)

A declaratory decree is binding _in personam_, not _in rem_. It binds only:

1. The parties to the suit.
    
2. Persons claiming through them (e.g., their heirs or buyers).
    
3. Trustees (and legally, the beneficiaries) if a trustee is a party.
    

_It does not bind the whole world or strangers who were not part of the lawsuit._

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Proviso" Trap:** In any problem question, check **possession**.
    
    - If the Plaintiff says: "I want a declaration that I am the owner," look immediately to see if he is in possession.
        
    - If he is **NOT** in possession, you must advise him: _"The suit is barred by the Proviso to Section 42 unless he amends it to include a prayer for recovery of possession."_
        
- **Legal Character vs. Contract:** Note that Section 42 protects "Legal Character" (status), not just contractual rights. You can sue for a declaration that you were wrongfully dismissed from a government job (status), but usually not for a simple breach of commercial contract unless it affects property rights.
    
- **Discretion:** Even if all conditions are met, the court can refuse a declaration if it serves no useful purpose or if the motive is fraudulent.
