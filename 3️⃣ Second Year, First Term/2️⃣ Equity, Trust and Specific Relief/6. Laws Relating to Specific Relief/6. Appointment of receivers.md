# 6.6 Appointment of Receivers ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3):** As discussed in Topic 3.4, the _concept_ of a receiver is equitable (preserving property). This subtopic focuses strictly on the _statutory authority_ in Bangladesh under the Specific Relief Act. It is a procedural bridge.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3):** Questions are rarely standalone essays. You are usually asked to "Discuss the powers of a Receiver under the SRA and CPC" or it appears as a procedural step in a larger problem question.
    

### Introduction

The **Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877** does not create a new code for Receivers. Instead, **Section 44** acts as a "pointer," explicitly delegating the procedure and rules to the **Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), 1908**. This makes the Receiver a unique remedy that starts in Equity (SRA) but operates through Procedure (CPC).

---

### I. Statutory Authority (Section 44)

**Section 44** of the SRA states simply:

> _"The appointment of a Receiver pending a suit is a matter resting in the discretion of the Court. The mode and effect of his appointment, and his rights, powers, duties and liabilities, are regulated by the Code of Civil Procedure."_

**Implication:** To answer any legal question about Receivers in Bangladesh, you must look at **Order 40** of the CPC, which provides the substantive rules referenced by Section 44.

---

### II. When is a Receiver Appointed? (The Principles)

Although Section 44 refers to the CPC, the _discretion_ mentioned in Section 44 is guided by equitable principles established in case law (like _Krishna Swamy Chetty’s case_). The Court appoints a receiver when it is **"Just and Convenient."**

**Grounds for Appointment:**

1. **Preservation:** When the property is in danger of being wasted, damaged, or alienated by the party in possession.
    
2. **Prevention of Fraud:** When there is a strong presumption of fraud or misappropriation.
    
3. **Conflict of Interest:** When a trustee or partner is managing the property for their own benefit rather than the common good.
    

_Note:_ The appointment is an _interim_ measure (pendente lite). It does not decide who owns the property, but simply keeps it safe until the trial ends.

---

### III. Status and Function

- **Officer of the Court:** The Receiver is not an agent of the plaintiff or defendant. They are a representative of the Court. Interfering with them is Contempt of Court.
    
- **Custodia Legis:** When a Receiver takes possession, the property is in _custodia legis_ (in the custody of the law). No one can touch it without the Court's permission.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The Link is Key:** In an exam, if you only mention SRA Section 44, you will get poor marks. You **must** write: _"Section 44 of the SRA empowers the court to appoint a receiver, but the specific rules are governed by Order 40 of the CPC."_ This shows you understand the statutory structure.
    
- **Discretionary Nature:** Emphasize that, like all Specific Relief, this is **discretionary**. A plaintiff cannot demand a receiver as a matter of right; they must prove that the property is in "imminent danger."
