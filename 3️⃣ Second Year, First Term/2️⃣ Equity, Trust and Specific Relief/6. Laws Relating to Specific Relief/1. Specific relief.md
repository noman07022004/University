# 6.1 Specific Relief: Overview and Modes ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** This introductory topic frames the entire Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877. You must understand _how_ the court gives specific relief (the 5 modes) to navigate the rest of the Act.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4):** While less common as a standalone essay, short questions often ask: "Enumerate the modes of granting specific relief under Section 5."
    

---

### I. Nature and Definition

**Specific Relief** is a judicial remedy where the court enforces the _exact_ fulfillment of an obligation or the protection of a specific right, rather than awarding monetary compensation (damages).

- **Philosophy:** It is based on the equitable principle that sometimes money is not enough. If someone steals your family heirloom, you want the _heirloom_ back, not its cash value.
    
- **Source:** In Bangladesh, this is governed by the **Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877**.
    

---

### II. How Specific Relief is Granted (Section 5)

**Section 5** of the SRA explicitly lists the five modes through which a Civil Court grants specific relief. This is the structural backbone of the Act.

**1. By Taking Possession and Delivering It**

- **Mechanism:** The court orders the physical recovery of property from the defendant and hands it to the plaintiff.
    
- **Application:** Recovery of Immovable Property (Sections 8 & 9) and Movable Property (Sections 10 & 11).
    

**2. By Ordering a Party to Do an Act (Specific Performance)**

- **Mechanism:** The court compels a person to perform an obligation they are under a duty to perform.
    
- **Application:** Specific Performance of Contracts (Sections 12-30).
    

**3. By Preventing a Party from Doing an Act (Injunction)**

- **Mechanism:** The court forbids a party from doing an act they are under an obligation _not_ to do.
    
- **Application:** Preventive Relief / Injunctions (Sections 52-57).
    

**4. By Determining and Declaring Rights (Declaratory Decree)**

- **Mechanism:** The court formally declares the plaintiff's title or legal character, without necessarily awarding any further relief (like possession or money) at that moment.
    
- **Application:** Declaratory Decrees (Section 42).
    

**5. By Appointing a Receiver**

- **Mechanism:** The court appoints an impartial officer to take custody and management of property during litigation.
    
- **Application:** Appointment of Receiver (Section 44).
    

---

### III. Recovery of Possession of Property (Sections 8-11)

Although the syllabus lists "Specific Relief" generally, the recovery of possession is the first substantive part of the Act and represents the first mode (Delivery of Possession).

#### A. Recovery of Immovable Property (Land)

**1. Suit Based on Title (Section 8)**

- **Principle:** A person entitled to the possession of specific immovable property may recover it in the manner prescribed by the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC).
    
- **Requirement:** The plaintiff must prove they have the **Legal Title** (Ownership).
    
- **Limitation:** The suit must be filed within **12 Years** (Article 142, Limitation Act).
    

**2. Suit Based on Possession (Section 9) – _The Summary Suit_**

- **Principle:** If any person is dispossessed of immovable property **without his consent** and **otherwise than in due course of law**, he may recover possession, _notwithstanding any other title that may be set up in such suit_.
    
- **Objective:** To discourage vigilantism. Even if you are the true owner, you cannot just throw a squatter out by force; you must go through the court.
    
- **Key Differences:**
    
    - **Proof:** Plaintiff only proves they were in possession and were illegally kicked out. They do _not_ need to prove ownership.
        
    - **Limitation:** Must be filed within **6 Months** (Article 3, Limitation Act).
        
    - **Restriction:** No appeal or review lies against a Section 9 decree. It is meant to be fast. The loser can later file a regular suit on title (Section 8) to win it back.
        

#### B. Recovery of Movable Property (Goods)

**1. Suit Based on Entitlement (Section 10)**

- A person entitled to possession of specific movable property may recover it under CPC rules.
    

**2. Liability of Person in Possession (Section 11)**

- This section applies Equity. If a person has possession of movable property of which they are _not_ the owner, they can be compelled to deliver it specifically to the person entitled to immediate possession in four cases:
    
    - When the defendant is a **Trustee** for the plaintiff.
        
    - When compensation in money would **not afford adequate relief** (e.g., rare artifact).
        
    - When it is **extremely difficult to ascertain the actual damage** caused by its loss.
        
    - When the possession was **wrongfully transferred** from the plaintiff.
        

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Section 9 is Vital:** This is a favorite problem question.
    
    - _Scenario:_ "A owns a house. B has been squatting there for a year. A forcibly throws B out. B sues A."
        
    - _Answer:_ Under **Section 9**, B will win and get possession back, even though A is the owner. A used force instead of "due course of law." A must then file a separate suit under **Section 8** to evict B legally.
        
- **Specific vs. Compensatory:** Always contrast SRA with Contract Law. Contract Law = Compensation (Damages). SRA = Specific Remedy (Possession/Performance).
    
- **Section 7 (Specific Relief Only):** Remember Section 7 states that specific relief cannot be granted for the mere purpose of enforcing a **Penal Law**. The SRA is for civil rights only.
