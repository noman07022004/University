>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** While the concepts are rooted in Equity (which you learned in Topics 1-3), this topic is strictly statutory. You must understand how the **Specific Relief Act, 1877** codifies those equitable principles into binding Bangladeshi law.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is arguably the most important topic for procedural law exams. Problem questions almost always involve a plaintiff seeking a specific remedy under specific sections (e.g., "Advise X if he can get a decree under Section 42").
    

### Introduction

The **Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877** is the statutory embodiment of equitable remedies in Bangladesh. While the Contract Act deals with compensation (damages), the SRA deals with compelling a party to perform a specific duty. It provides remedies that are individual and specific, rather than general damages.

---

### I. Specific Relief: Overview and Possession

_(Covering Subtopic: 6.1 Specific relief)_

**1. Nature of Specific Relief** Specific relief is given by:

- Taking possession of certain property and delivering it to the claimant.
    
- Ordering a party to do the very act they are under an obligation to do (Specific Performance).
    
- Preventing a party from doing an act they are under an obligation not to do (Injunction).
    
- Determining and declaring the rights of parties (Declaration).
    
- Appointing a Receiver.
    

**2. Recovery of Possession (Knowledge Gap Filler)** Although not explicitly listed as a subtopic, the SRA begins with **Sections 8-11**, which are fundamental.

- **Section 8 (Based on Title):** A person with legal title can recover possession of immovable property (regular civil suit, 12-year limitation).
    
- **Section 9 (Based on Possession):** If a person is dispossessed _without consent_ and _otherwise than in due course of law_, they can recover possession quickly (summary suit, 6-month limitation) even without proving title. This prevents vigilantism.
    

---

### II. Specific Performance of Contract

_(Covering Subtopic: 6.2 Specific performance of contract)_

This is the core of the Act (**Sections 12-30**).

**1. When Granted (Section 12)** The court may grant specific performance in four cases:

- **Trust:** When the act agreed to be done is in the performance of a trust.
    
- **No Standard for Damages:** When there is no standard for ascertaining the actual damage caused by the non-performance (e.g., an agreement to sell a rare painting).
    
- **Damages Inadequate:** When monetary compensation would not afford adequate relief.
    
    - _Presumption:_ The law presumes that for **Immovable Property (Land/Buildings)**, money is _never_ adequate. For **Movable Property (Goods)**, money is usually adequate, unless the goods are unique.
        

**2. When Refused (Section 21)** Contracts that cannot be specifically enforced include:

- Contracts for personal services (e.g., painting a portrait, singing).
    
- Contracts requiring constant court supervision (e.g., building a complex dam).
    
- Revocable contracts.
    
- Contracts made by trustees in breach of their trust.
    

**3. Discretion of Court (Section 22)** Specific performance is discretionary. The court is not bound to grant it just because it is lawful. The court considers:

- **Hardship:** If enforcing it would cause unforeseen hardship to the defendant.
    
- **Unfairness:** If the plaintiff has taken unfair advantage.

### III. Correcting and Ending Instruments

_(Covering Subtopics: 6.3 Rectification and cancellation of instruments, 6.4 Rescission of contracts)_

When written documents do not reflect the true intention or are void, the SRA provides three distinct remedies.

**1. Rectification of Instruments (Sections 31-34)**

- **Scenario:** The parties agreed on X, but due to **fraud or mutual mistake**, the written document says Y.
    
- **Remedy:** The court corrects ("rectifies") the document to reflect the true intention.
    
- _Key Condition:_ It must be a _mutual_ mistake (both parties made it), not a unilateral one.
    

**2. Rescission of Contracts (Sections 35-38)**

- **Scenario:** A valid contract exists, but one party wants to "rescind" (cancel/unmake) it.
    
- **When Granted:**
    
    - The contract is voidable (e.g., induced by coercion or undue influence).
        
    - The contract is unlawful for causes not apparent on its face.
        
- **Equity's Requirement:** The party rescinding must restore any benefit received ("He who seeks equity must do equity").
    

**3. Cancellation of Instruments (Sections 39-41)**

- **Scenario:** A written document (like a forged deed) exists that is void or voidable, and if left alone, it might cause injury to the plaintiff.
    
- **Remedy:** The court orders the document to be "delivered up and cancelled."
    
- _Example:_ If someone forged a deed selling your house, you seek Cancellation to destroy that forged paper so it doesn't cloud your title in the future.

### IV. Declaratory Decrees

_(Covering Subtopic: 6.5 Declaratory decrees)_

**1. Nature (Section 42)** Sometimes a plaintiff doesn't need coercive relief (like seizing property) yet, but needs their legal status clarified to prevent future disputes.

- **Scope:** Any person entitled to any **legal character** (e.g., "I am the legitimate son") or **right to property** may institute a suit against any person denying that right.
    

**2. The Proviso (Crucial Restriction)**

- The court will _not_ grant a mere declaration if the plaintiff is able to seek **further consequential relief** but omits to do so.
    
- _Example:_ If A is illegally occupying B's house, B cannot just sue for a _Declaration_ that "I am the owner." B must sue for _Declaration AND Recovery of Possession_. This prevents multiple lawsuits for the same issue.
    

---

### V. Preventive Relief: Injunctions

_(Covering Subtopic: 6.7 Injunction)_

The SRA divides injunctions into Temporary (regulated by Civil Procedure Code) and Perpetual (regulated by SRA).

**1. Perpetual Injunction (Section 54)** Granted at the end of the trial to permanently prevent the defendant from:

- Breaching an obligation (contractual or tortious).
    
- Invading the plaintiff's right to enjoyment of property.
    

**2. Mandatory Injunction (Section 55)** When necessary to prevent a breach, the court may compel the performance of certain acts. (e.g., "Pull down the wall you illegally built").

**3. Injunction to Perform Negative Agreement (Section 57)** Even if a contract cannot be specifically enforced (like a personal service contract), the court may grant an injunction to perform the _negative_ part of it.

- _The Lumley v Wagner Principle:_ A singer cannot be forced to sing for A (affirmative), but she can be stopped by injunction from singing for B (negative) if she agreed strictly not to sing elsewhere.
    

---

### VI. Appointment of Receivers

_(Covering Subtopic: 6.6 Appointment of receivers)_

**Section 44** of the SRA simply states that the appointment of a Receiver is regulated by the **Code of Civil Procedure (Order 40)**.

- **Function:** As discussed in Topic 3, a Receiver acts as the "hand of the court" to protect property (collect rents, manage estates) during the pendency of a lawsuit.
    
- **Discretion:** The court appoints a receiver only when it appears "just and convenient" to preserve the property from waste or damage.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Section Numbers:** In SRA exams, citing the section number is mandatory for good grades.
    
    - **Sec 12:** Specific Performance.
        
    - **Sec 42:** Declaratory Decree.
        
    - **Sec 54:** Perpetual Injunction.
        
- **Difference between Rescission and Cancellation:**
    
    - **Rescission (Sec 35)** ends a contractual _obligation_ (stops the deal).
        
    - **Cancellation (Sec 39)** destroys the physical _document_ (voids the paper).
        
- **The "Clean Hands" Maxim in SRA:** Section 22 (Discretion) explicitly codifies this. If the plaintiff has done something unfair, the court can refuse Specific Performance even if the contract is valid.
    
- **Declaratory Decree Trick:** Watch out for exam scenarios where a person asks _only_ for a declaration but is out of possession. You must cite the **Proviso to Section 42** and state that the suit will fail unless they amend it to include recovery of possession.
