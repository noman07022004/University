# 6.7 Injunction ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** Injunctions are the most commonly sought specific relief in the real world. Understanding the statutory distinction between a "Temporary" stop-gap measure and a "Perpetual" final decree is essential for any legal practitioner.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a massive exam topic. You will frequently be asked to "Distinguish between Temporary and Perpetual Injunctions" or solve problems involving Mandatory Injunctions (e.g., "Neighbor built a wall blocking light; advise the plaintiff").
    

### Introduction

**Preventive Relief** is granted at the discretion of the Court by **Injunction**. An injunction is a judicial process by which one who is threatening to invade the legal or equitable rights of another is restrained from commencing or continuing such act, or is commanded to restore matters to their former condition.

In Bangladesh, the law of injunctions is split between two statutes:

- **Temporary Injunctions:** Governed by the **Code of Civil Procedure (CPC)**.
    
- **Perpetual (Permanent) Injunctions:** Governed by the **Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877** (Sections 52–57).
    

---

### I. Classification (Section 53)

Section 53 defines the two primary categories:

**1. Temporary Injunctions**

- **Duration:** They continue until a specified time, or until the further order of the Court.
    
- **Stage:** They may be granted at _any stage_ of a suit.
    
- **Regulation:** They are regulated by the **Code of Civil Procedure (Order 39)**.
    
- **Purpose:** To preserve the _status quo_ pending the trial.
    

**2. Perpetual Injunctions**

- **Duration:** Permanent.
    
- **Stage:** Can only be granted by the **decree** made at the hearing and upon the merits of the suit (i.e., at the very end of the case).
    
- **Regulation:** Regulated by the **Specific Relief Act (Sections 54-57)**.
    
- **Effect:** The defendant is perpetually enjoined (forbidden forever) from the assertion of a right or from the commission of an act which would be contrary to the rights of the plaintiff.
    

---

### II. Perpetual Injunction: When Granted (Section 54)

The Court may grant a perpetual injunction to prevent the breach of an obligation existing in favor of the plaintiff, whether expressly or by implication.

**1. Contracts:** When the defendant invades the plaintiff's right to, or enjoyment of, property, the court may grant an injunction where:

- The defendant is a **trustee** of the property for the plaintiff.
    
- There is **no standard for ascertaining the actual damage** caused.
    
- Money would **not afford adequate relief**.
    
- It is probable that pecuniary compensation **cannot be got** (insolvency).
    

_Note: These criteria mirror the conditions for Specific Performance in Section 12._

---

### III. Mandatory Injunction (Section 55)

Most injunctions are "Prohibitory" (Do not do X). However, Section 55 allows for "Mandatory" (Positive) injunctions.

- **The Rule:** When, to prevent the breach of an obligation, it is necessary to compel the performance of certain acts which the Court is capable of enforcing, the Court may in its discretion grant an injunction to prevent the breach complained of, and also to compel performance of the requisite acts.
    
- **Example:** If A illegally builds a wall blocking B's window (Right to Light), the court can issue a Mandatory Injunction ordering A to **pull down** the wall.
    

---

### IV. When Injunction is Refused (Section 56)

Section 56 lists 11 specific grounds where an injunction **cannot** be granted. The most important ones for exams are:

1. **Stay of Proceedings:** To stay a judicial proceeding pending at the institution of the suit (unless to prevent multiplicity of proceedings).
    
2. **Stay of Superior Court:** To stay proceedings in a Court not subordinate to that from which the injunction is sought.
    
3. **Government Duties:** To interfere with the public duties of any department of the Government.
    
4. **Criminal Matters:** To stay proceedings in any criminal matter.
    
5. **Specific Performance Bar:** To prevent the breach of a contract the performance of which would not be specifically enforced (e.g., you cannot enjoin a painter to paint).
    
6. **Nuisance:** Where the act is not reasonably clear that it will be a nuisance.
    
7. **Equitable Conduct:** When the plaintiff's conduct or agents make them unfit for assistance (Unclean Hands).
    
8. **Personal Interest:** When the plaintiff has no personal interest in the matter.
    

---

### V. Injunction to Perform Negative Agreement (Section 57)

This is a special exception to the rule that "Service Contracts cannot be enforced."

- **The Problem:** You cannot force a singer to sing (Personal Service).
    
- **The Solution (Section 57):** If a contract has an **affirmative agreement** to do a certain act (Sing for A) and a **negative agreement** not to do a certain act (Not sing for anyone else), the Court may grant an injunction to perform the **negative agreement**, even if it cannot enforce the affirmative one.
    
- **Case Law:** This codifies the principle of **_Lumley v Wagner (1852)_**.
    
    - _Scenario:_ A singer agreed to sing at Lumley's theater and _not_ to sing anywhere else. She breached both.
        
    - _Remedy:_ Court could not force her to sing for Lumley (Slavery concern), but it _did_ enjoin her from singing for the rival, Wagner.
        

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Section 52-57 Framework:** Memorize this range.
    
    - Sec 53: Types.
        
    - Sec 54: Perpetual.
        
    - Sec 55: Mandatory.
        
    - Sec 56: Refusals.
        
    - Sec 57: Negative Agreements.
        
- **Mandatory Injunction Caution:** Courts are very hesitant to grant Mandatory Injunctions (Section 55) because they are harsh (destroying property). In problems, argue that it is a "last resort" remedy.
    
- **Section 56 Check:** In any problem question asking "Can X get an injunction?", briefly check Section 56. If X is asking to stop a Criminal Case, the answer is immediately **No** (Sec 56e).
