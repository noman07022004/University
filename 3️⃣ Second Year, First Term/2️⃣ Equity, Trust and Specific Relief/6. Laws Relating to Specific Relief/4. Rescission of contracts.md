
# 6.4 Rescission of Contracts ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** Rescission is the "undo button" for contracts. It is conceptually distinct from "breach" (where the contract stays alive but you sue for money) or "termination" (ending it for the future). Rescission wipes the contract out _ab initio_ (from the beginning).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4):** Often tested alongside Rectification. Problem questions frequently involve a party wanting to "get out" of a deal because they were tricked.
    

### Introduction

**Rescission** means the cancellation or "unmaking" of a contract. The court annuls the contract, freeing the parties from their obligations and, as far as possible, restoring them to the positions they occupied before the contract was made (_Restitutio in integrum_). In Bangladesh, this is governed by **Sections 35-38** of the **Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877**.

---

### I. When Rescission is Adjudged (Section 35)

Section 35 states that any person interested in a contract may sue to have it rescinded, and such rescission may be adjudged by the court in three cases:

**1. Voidable Contract**

- **The Rule:** Where the contract is voidable or terminable by the plaintiff.
    
- **Grounds:** This typically happens when consent was obtained by **Coercion, Undue Influence, Fraud, or Misrepresentation** (as defined in the Contract Act, 1872).
    
- _Example:_ A holds a gun to B's head and forces him to sign a contract to sell his house. B can sue for rescission because the contract is voidable at his option.
    

**2. Unlawful Contract (Not apparent on face)**

- **The Rule:** Where the contract is unlawful for causes _not apparent on its face_ and the defendant is more to blame than the plaintiff.
    
- _Example:_ A pays B to smuggle goods, but the contract just says "Transport fees." If A repents before the smuggling happens, he might seek rescission, provided he is less guilty (e.g., he was manipulated).
    

**3. Default in Specific Performance Decree**

- **The Rule:** If a court grants a decree for Specific Performance (e.g., ordering a buyer to pay the money and take the land), and the buyer _fails_ to pay within the time fixed by the court, the seller can apply to have the contract rescinded.
    

---

### II. When Rescission is Refused (Bars to Rescission)

The right to rescind is not absolute. Under the principles of Equity, the court may refuse rescission if:

1. **Affirmation:** The plaintiff, after discovering the fraud, expressly or impliedly **ratified** (accepted) the contract (e.g., they kept using the goods).
    
2. **Restitution Impossible:** The parties cannot be substantially restored to their original positions (e.g., the subject matter has been consumed or destroyed).
    
3. **Third Party Rights:** A third party has acquired rights in good faith and for value during the subsistence of the contract.
    
    - _Example:_ A tricks B into selling a ring. A then sells the ring to C (innocent buyer). B cannot rescind the contract with A to get the ring back from C.
        
4. **Severability:** If only part of the contract is sought to be rescinded and that part cannot be severed from the rest.
    

---

### III. The Requirement of Equity (Section 38)

Section 38 embodies the maxim: **"He who seeks equity must do equity."**

- **The Rule:** On adjudging the rescission of a contract, the Court may require the party to whom such relief is granted to make any **compensation** to the other which justice may require.
    
- **Objective:** To prevent unjust enrichment. You cannot cancel a contract to sell your land but keep the deposit money the buyer paid you. You must return the benefit.
    

---

### IV. Distinction: Rescission vs. Cancellation vs. Rectification

|**Feature**|**Rescission (Sec 35)**|**Cancellation (Sec 39)**|**Rectification (Sec 31)**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Subject**|**Contracts** (Active obligations).|**Instruments** (Written documents).|**Instruments** (Written errors).|
|**Focus**|Ending the _contractual relation_.|Destroying the _physical paper_ evidence.|Correcting the _terms_ to match intent.|
|**Core Ground**|Voidable consent (Fraud/Coercion).|Fear of serious injury (Quia Timet).|Mutual Mistake or Fraud.|

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Rescission vs. Termination:** This is a key conceptual difference.
    
    - **Termination:** Ends the contract from _now_ on (e.g., firing an employee). Past acts are valid.
        
    - **Rescission:** Wipes the contract out from the _start_ (ab initio). It's as if the contract never existed.
        
- **Restitutio in Integrum:** Use this Latin phrase. It means "restoration to original condition." It is the goal of rescission. If you can't restore (e.g., you ate the cake), you can't rescind.
    
- **Section 35(b) Trap:** Be careful with "Unlawful Contracts." Rescission is only allowed if the parties are _not_ _in pari delicto_ (not equally at fault). If both are equally guilty criminals, the court will refuse to help either (Maxim: "Clean Hands").
