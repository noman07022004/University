
## 3.3 Injunction ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** Injunctions are the "shield" of Equity. They are the primary tool used to stop legal wrongs before they cause irreversible damage. Understanding the difference between "stopping" an act and "undoing" one is crucial.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a high-frequency exam topic. You will often see problem questions asking for advice on how to stop a neighbor from building a wall or how to prevent a business partner from selling assets.

---

### I. Definition and Nature

An **Injunction** is a judicial process or order requiring a person to do, or refrain from doing, a particular act.

- **Nature:** It acts _in personam_. It is a command to the defendant's conscience. Disobedience is punishable by imprisonment or attachment of property for Contempt of Court.
    
- **Purpose:** To prevent the infringement of a legal right or to restore the status quo.
    

---

### II. Classification of Injunctions

Injunctions are classified based on **Nature** (what they do) and **Time** (how long they last).

#### 1. Based on Nature

- **Prohibitory Injunction (Negative):**
    
    - **Definition:** Restrains a person from doing a specific act.
        
    - **Example:** "Do not build a wall on this land." "Do not publish this defamatory article."
        
    - **Status:** This is the most common form. It maintains the status quo.
        
- **Mandatory Injunction (Positive):**
    
    - **Definition:** Compels a person to perform a positive act, usually to _undo_ a wrong already done.
        
    - **Example:** "Demolish the wall you have illegally built."
        
    - **Status:** This is a "severe" remedy. Courts are very cautious and will only grant it if the injury is serious and the defendant acted recklessly or quickly to steal a march on the plaintiff.
        

#### 2. Based on Time (Jurisdictional Split)

In Bangladesh, this distinction is critical because they are governed by different laws.

- **Temporary (Interlocutory) Injunction:**
    
    - **Governing Law:** **Order 39, Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), 1908.**
        
    - **Duration:** Lasts only until a specified time or until the final hearing of the suit.
        
    - **Purpose:** To preserve the property in dispute _during_ the lawsuit so that the final judgment is not made useless (e.g., stopping the sale of a house while the court decides who owns it).
        
- **Perpetual (Permanent) Injunction:**
    
    - **Governing Law:** **Sections 52-57, Specific Relief Act (SRA), 1877.**
        
    - **Duration:** Granted at the _end_ of the trial as part of the final judgment. It lasts forever.
        
    - **Purpose:** To permanently settle the rights of the parties.
        

---

### III. Principles for Granting Injunctions

Since Injunctions are discretionary, courts follow specific principles (often called the "Triple Test" for Temporary Injunctions):

1. **Prima Facie Case:** The plaintiff must show an arguable case that they have a legal right. They don't need to prove they will definitely win, just that there is a serious question to be tried.
    
2. **Irreparable Injury:** The plaintiff must show that if the injunction is _not_ granted, they will suffer a loss that cannot be compensated by money (damages).
    
3. **Balance of Convenience:** The court weighs the mischief. Who will suffer more?
    
    - If the injunction is granted but the plaintiff is wrong? (Defendant suffers).
        
    - If the injunction is refused but the plaintiff is right? (Plaintiff suffers).
        
    - The court rules in favor of the party who risks the greater hardship.
        

---

### IV. When is an Injunction Refused?

Under Section 56 of the Specific Relief Act, an injunction cannot be granted:

- To stay a judicial proceeding (unless to prevent multiplicity of suits).
    
- To interfere with the public duties of any government department.
    
- To prevent a breach of contract which cannot be specifically enforced (e.g., you can't enjoin a singer to sing, because you can't force her to sing).
    
- When the plaintiff has no personal interest in the matter.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Statutory Reference:** Always distinguish the source. Temporary = **CPC**. Perpetual = **SRA**. This shows you know your procedural law.
    
- **Status Quo:** This is a key keyword. A temporary injunction is meant to maintain the _status quo_ (the existing state of affairs).
    
- **Lumley v Wagner:** This case is relevant here too. It established that while you cannot force a positive act (singing), you _can_ enforce a negative stipulation (not singing elsewhere) via injunction.
    
- **Quia Timet Injunction:** A Latin term ("because he fears"). This is an injunction granted _before_ any harm has occurred, because the threat is imminent. (e.g., The neighbor is mixing cement but hasn't started building the illegal wall yet).
