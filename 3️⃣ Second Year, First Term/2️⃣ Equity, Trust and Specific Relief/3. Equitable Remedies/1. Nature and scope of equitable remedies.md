## 3.1 Nature and Scope of Equitable Remedies ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4):** This topic sets the ground rules for _all_ equitable relief. You must understand that these remedies are not "rights" but "privileges" granted at the court's discretion.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4):** While you might not get a standalone essay on "Nature and Scope," almost every problem question requires you to apply the "Nature" (Discretion/Adequacy of Damages) to determine if a remedy is available.

---

### I. Nature of Equitable Remedies

The nature of equitable remedies is fundamentally different from Common Law remedies (like damages). It is defined by three key characteristics:

**1. Discretionary (Not a Right)**

- **Common Law:** Remedies are a matter of **Right** (_ex debito justitiae_). If a plaintiff proves a breach of contract, the judge _must_ award damages, even if it is just 1 Taka.
    
- **Equity:** Remedies are a matter of **Grace** (Discretion). Even if a plaintiff proves a wrong, the court is not legally bound to grant specific relief.
    
    - The judge weighs factors like the plaintiff's conduct ("Clean Hands"), delay ("Laches"), and the hardship to the defendant.
        
    - _Example:_ If A builds a wall 1 inch onto B's land, B has a right to damages. But if B asks for a Mandatory Injunction to demolish the whole wall, the court may refuse it as "oppressive" and award money instead.
        

**2. Acts _In Personam_ (Coercive)**

- **Coercion:** Equitable remedies are commands directed at the specific individual.
    
- **Consequence:** If a defendant disobeys a Common Law judgment (e.g., fails to pay), the sheriff seizes their goods (_in rem_). If a defendant disobeys an Equitable order (e.g., "Do not sing for anyone else"), they are personally guilty of **Contempt of Court** and can be imprisoned or fined until they apologize and comply.
    

**3. Specific and Preventive**

- **Common Law:** Remedial and compensatory. It looks backward: "The harm is done; here is money to fix it."
    
- **Equity:** Specific and preventive. It looks forward: "We will stop the harm from happening (Injunction)" or "We will make sure the promise is actually kept (Specific Performance)."
    

---

### II. Scope of Equitable Remedies

The scope answers the question: _When will Equity step in?_

**1. The "Adequacy of Damages" Test** The jurisdiction of Equity to grant remedies is **supplementary**. It only acts when the Common Law remedy (money) is inadequate.

- **Land:** Damages are _presumed_ inadequate for land contracts because every piece of land is unique. (Specific Performance is almost always granted).
    
- **Goods:** Damages are _presumed_ adequate for ordinary goods (e.g., rice, coal) because you can buy them elsewhere with the money. Equity only intervenes if the goods are unique (e.g., a rare painting, an antique vase).
    

**2. Practical Classification** The scope of equitable remedies covers various specific situations where legal rights need protection:

- **Enforcement of Contracts:** Specific Performance (Topic 3.2).
    
- **Protection of Rights:** Injunctions (Topic 3.3).
    
- **Administration of Assets:** Appointment of Receivers (Topic 3.4).
    
- **Correction of Documents:** Rectification and Cancellation (Topic 6).
    
- **Declaration of Status:** Declaratory Decrees (Topic 6).
    

**3. Limitations on Scope** Equity will _not_ extend its scope to:

- Enforce contracts for **Personal Services** (slavery concerns).
    
- Enforce contracts requiring **Constant Supervision** (practical difficulty).
    
- Grant remedies that are **Futile** (Equity does not act in vain).
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Adequacy" Mantra:** In any exam question asking "Can X get Specific Performance?", your first sentence must be: _"Specific performance is a discretionary remedy granted only when damages are not an adequate relief."_
    
- **Section 12 of Specific Relief Act:** In Bangladesh, the "Scope" is statutorily defined in Section 12. It lists exactly when Specific Performance can be granted (e.g., when no standard exists for ascertaining damage).
    
- **Discretion is Judicial:** "Discretion" does not mean the judge does whatever they want. It means "Judicial Discretion"—governed by settled principles and precedents, not caprice.
