# 1.3 Nature and Definition ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** You cannot apply Equity if you don't know what it _is_. This topic clarifies that "Equity" in law is not just "fairness" in the dictionary sense—it is a technical system.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3):** Direct questions on "Define Equity" are rare in higher-level exams, but you will often need to write a paragraph defining it in the introduction of your essays.

---

### I. Definition of Equity

Defining Equity is notoriously difficult because it is not a single concept but a historical accident. As the jurist **Sir Henry Maine** noted, it is a "fresh body of rules" that grew alongside the law.

**1. The Aristotelian Definition (Philosophical)** The oldest definition comes from Aristotle in his _Nicomachean Ethics_.

- **Definition:** Equity (_Epikeia_) is "a correction of law where it is defective owing to its universality."
    
- **Explanation:** Laws are written for general situations ("Do not kill," "Pay your debts"). But real life is messy. Sometimes, following the strict letter of the general law creates an unjust result in a specific, unique case. Equity steps in to "correct" that specific injustice without changing the general law.
    

**2. Maitland’s Definition (Historical/Technical)** The most famous legal definition comes from the historian **F.W. Maitland**.

- **Definition:** "Equity is that body of rules which is administered by our courts of justice which, were it not for the operation of the Judicature Acts, would be administered only by those courts which would be known as Courts of Equity."
    
- **Explanation:** This definition sounds circular, but it highlights a key truth: Equity is defined by _history_, not by a single principle. It is simply "the stuff the Chancellor used to do." It includes Trusts, Injunctions, and Specific Performance, but not criminal law or torts.

### II. Nature of Equity

To understand the nature of Equity, one must contrast it with the Common Law.

**1. Equity Acts _In Personam_**

- **Common Law acts _In Rem_:** It focuses on the property or the thing. If you win a land dispute in Common Law, the Sheriff forcibly removes the intruder.
    
- **Equity acts _In Personam_:** It focuses on the _conscience_ of the individual.
    
    - _Mechanism:_ The Court of Equity orders the defendant personally: "You must do X." If the defendant refuses, they are thrown in jail for **Contempt of Court** until they agree. The court operates by coercing the individual's will.
        
    - _Example:_ If a defendant illegally builds a wall, Equity orders _him_ to pull it down.
        

**2. Equity is Discretionary**

- **Common Law:** Remedies are a **Right** (ex debito justitiae). If you prove a breach of contract, the judge _must_ award damages, even if you are a bad person.
    
- **Equity:** Remedies are a **Privilege**. The court has the _discretion_ to grant or refuse relief.
    
    - _The "Clean Hands" check:_ Before granting a remedy, the judge looks at the plaintiff's conduct. If the plaintiff acted unfairly, waited too long (Laches), or tricked the defendant, the court will say, "We refuse to help you," leaving them only with their Common Law damages.
        

**3. Equity is Supplemental**

- Equity does not seek to destroy the Common Law but to fill its gaps.
    
- **Maitland's Metaphor:** "Equity was a gloss on the common law." Just as a student writes notes in the margins of a textbook to explain difficult passages, Equity wrote "notes" on the Common Law to fix its harshness.
    

**4. Equity is Undefined (Historically) but Fixed (Modern)**

- **Early Equity:** Was fluid and based on "Natural Justice" (morality).
    
- **Modern Equity:** Is "Technical Equity." It is bound by precedent. A judge today cannot invent a new equitable remedy just because they feel like it; they must find a precedent in the reported cases.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Double Meaning" Trap:** In exams, distinguish between:
    
    - **Equity (Lower Case 'e'):** General fairness, equality, natural justice.
        
    - **Equity (Upper Case 'E'):** The specific system of legal rules (Trusts, Injunctions) developed by the Court of Chancery.
        
- **The "Gloss" Concept:** When asked about the nature of Equity, always explain that it is a **supplementary system**. It cannot exist without the Common Law. (e.g., You can't have an "Equitable Remedy" for breach of contract if the Common Law didn't first invent the concept of "Contract").
    
- **Penn v Lord Baltimore (1750):** This is a famous case illustrating _Equity acts in personam_. The English court decided a dispute about land in America (outside its jurisdiction). How? By ordering the _people_ (who were in England) to settle the boundary, effectively acting on their consciences rather than the land itself.
