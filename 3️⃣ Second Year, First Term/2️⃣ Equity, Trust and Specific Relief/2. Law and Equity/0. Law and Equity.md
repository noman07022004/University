
## 2. Law and Equity ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ 

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** This topic explains _how_ Equity actually operates alongside the law. The concept of "Equitable Estate" is the theoretical basis for Trusts, and the Maxims are the practical tools judges use to decide cases.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is a high-priority exam topic. You will almost certainly face a problem question or essay requiring you to apply specific "Maxims of Equity" to a factual scenario.

---

Having established the historical origin of Equity, we now turn to its functional relationship with the Common Law. The interaction between "Law" (Common Law) and "Equity" is not merely historical; it defines how modern courts handle property rights and remedies. This topic explores the dual nature of ownership (Legal vs. Equitable estates) and the twelve guiding principles—the **Maxims of Equity**—that dictate how equitable discretion is exercised.

---

### I. Relationship Between Law and Equity

_(Covering Subtopic: 2.1 Relationship between law and equity)_

The relationship between Law and Equity has evolved from one of intense rivalry to cooperative "fusion."

**1. Historical Conflict and Supremacy** As noted in Topic 1, the two systems initially clashed. Common Law courts felt their jurisdiction was being usurped(দখল করা) by the Chancellor. This conflict was resolved by the _Earl of Oxford’s Case_ (1615), establishing that **Equity prevails** over the Common Law when they conflict.

**2. The Judicature Acts (1873-1875)** In England, the _Judicature Acts_ fused the _administration_ of Law and Equity.

- **Before the Acts:** A plaintiff had to go to a Common Law court for damages and a separate Court of Chancery for an injunction.
    
- **After the Acts:** A single court could administer both remedies.
    
- **The "Fusion Fallacy(ভ্রান্ত ধারণা)":** A critical academic debate exists here. Did the Acts fuse the _substantive rules (মূল নিয়ম)_ of Law and Equity, or just their _administration_?
    
    - _Orthodox View (Ashburner’s Metaphor):_ "The two streams of jurisdiction, though they run in the same channel, run side by side and do not mingle their waters." This means the rules remain distinct—an equitable claim is still equitable, even if a Common Law court hears it.
        

**3. Relationship in Bangladesh** In Bangladesh, there was never a need for Judicature Acts. The British introduced a system where the same courts (Civil Courts) have always been empowered to administer both Common Law and Equity simultaneously. Section 6 of the _Civil Courts Act, 1887_ empowers courts to apply "Justice, Equity, and Good Conscience".

### II. Equitable Estate and Legal Estate

_(Covering Subtopic: 2.2 Equitable estate and legal estate)_

This distinction is the conceptual engine behind the "Trust." Equity recognized that one person could hold the legal title to property while another person was the true owner in the eyes of conscience.

**1. Legal Estate (The "Paper" Owner)**

- **Definition:** This refers to the ownership recognized by the Common Law. The person with the Legal Estate has their name on the title deed.
    
- **Characteristics:** It deals with "rights in rem" (rights against the world). The legal owner has the power of management and administration of the property.
    

**2. Equitable Estate (The "Beneficial" Owner)**

- **Definition:** This is the ownership recognized by the Court of Equity. It focuses on who _enjoys the benefit_ of the property.
    
- **Characteristics:** It deals with "rights in personam" (originally against the trustee, now more broadly protected).
    
- **Example:** If Father transfers land to Uncle to hold for the benefit of Son:
    
    - **Uncle** has the _Legal Estate_ (he signs the papers, pays the taxes).
        
    - **Son** has the _Equitable Estate_ (he collects the rent, lives in the house).
        
    - If Uncle sells the land to a buyer who knows about the trust, the buyer takes the Legal Estate but must still honor the Son's Equitable Estate.

### III. Maxims(নীতিবাক্য) of Equity

_(Covering Subtopic: 2.3 Maxims of equity)_

Maxims are not rigid statutes but general guiding principles that judges use to determine if a plaintiff deserves equitable relief. They are the "moral code" of Equity. Here are the most critical maxims grouped by function:

#### Group A: The Moral Standing of the Plaintiff

**1. He who seeks Equity must do Equity**

- **Meaning:** A plaintiff asking for an equitable remedy (like specific performance) must be willing to fulfill their own legal obligations in return.
    
- **Example:** If a tenant asks the court to stop an eviction(উচ্ছেদ) (injunction), the court will only grant it if the tenant agrees to pay all unpaid rent.
    

**2. He who comes into Equity must come with Clean Hands**

- **Meaning:** The plaintiff’s past conduct _regarding the specific dispute_ must be honest.
    
- **Distinction:** "Do Equity" looks to the future (what the plaintiff _will_ do); "Clean Hands" looks to the past (what the plaintiff _did_).
    
- **Example:** A tenant illegally sublets an apartment and then asks the court to prevent the landlord from entering. The court will refuse because the tenant’s hands are "unclean."
    

#### Group B: Technical Operation of Equity

**3. Equity will not suffer a wrong to be without a remedy**

**suffer** = **"to permit"** or **"to allow."**
**wrong** = **legal wrong**
- **Meaning:** This is the root of Equity. If a legal right has been violated but the Common Law creates a procedural wall, Equity will invent a remedy.
    
- **Application:** This maxim created the "Trust" (to protect beneficiaries when the law ignored them) and the "Mortgagor's Equity of Redemption."
    

**4. Equity follows the Law (_Aequitas sequitur legem_)**

- **Meaning:** Equity does not recklessly override the law. It respects legal estates and rules unless they are unconscionable.
    
- **Application:** Equity respects the law of primogeniture (historically) or statutory limitation periods, unless there is fraud.
    

#### Group C: Fairness and Timing

**5. Delay defeats Equity (Doctrine of Laches)**

- **Meaning:** Equity aids the vigilant, not the indolent. If a plaintiff waits too long to claim a right, the court may deny relief even if the limitation period hasn't expired, because evidence may be lost or the other party prejudiced.
    
- **Example:** If you wait 10 years to ask for Specific Performance of a land contract, Equity will say you slept on your rights.
    

**6. Equality is Equity**

- **Meaning:** When there is no clear basis for division, Equity prefers equal division.
    
- **Application:** Used in the distribution of assets among creditors or beneficiaries when shares are not specified.
    

#### Group D: Intent vs. Form

**7. Equity looks to the intent rather than the form**

- **Meaning:** Equity ignores technical defects if the intention is clear.
    
- **Application:** If a mortgage deed is technically defective but the money was lent and the land pledged, Equity treats it as a valid mortgage.
    

**8. Equity looks on that as done which ought to be done**

- **Meaning:** If you are _contractually obligated_ to do something (e.g., sell land), Equity treats you as if you have _already done it_ from the moment the contract becomes binding.
    
- **Application:** This is the basis of **Specific Performance**. In a contract for the sale of land, the buyer becomes the "equitable owner" the moment the contract is signed, even before the deed is transferred.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Ashburner's Metaphor:** Memorize the "Two streams running side by side" quote for the Relationship section. It impresses examiners.
    
- **Exam Strategy for Maxims:** Do not just list them. In problem questions, identify _which_ maxim applies.
    
    - _Scenario:_ A plaintiff lied to the defendant during negotiations and now wants an injunction.
        
    - _Answer:_ Apply the "Clean Hands" maxim to argue the court should deny the injunction.
        
- **Legal vs. Equitable:** This distinction is crucial for **Topic 4 (Trusts)**. You cannot understand a Trust without understanding that the Trustee holds the Legal Estate and the Beneficiary holds the Equitable Estate.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Remember that Maxims are not "Law" in Bangladesh in the statutory sense (like an Act of Parliament), but they are applied as principles of "Justice, Equity, and Good Conscience" under the Civil Courts Act.
