
## 2.3 Maxims of Equity ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** The "Maxims" are the axioms or general principles upon which the entire system of Equity is built. They are not rigid statutes but "guiding stars" that judges use to navigate complex cases.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This is arguably the most practical topic for exams. You will frequently face problem scenarios where you must identify _which_ maxim applies to grant or deny relief. Essays on specific maxims (e.g., "Discuss the maxim 'He who comes into equity must come with clean hands'") are also common.
    

---

The "Maxims of Equity" are a collection of concise ethical statements that evolved over centuries in the Court of Chancery. They condense the broad philosophy of "fairness" into workable legal rules. While scholars list varying numbers, twelve maxims are generally recognized as the core foundation.

---

### I. Maxims Related to the Moral Standing of the Plaintiff

These maxims ensure that the person asking for help is morally deserving.

**1. He who seeks Equity must do Equity**

- **Meaning:** If a plaintiff wants an equitable remedy, they must be willing to fulfill their own legal and moral obligations related to the dispute. It focuses on the **future** conduct of the plaintiff.
    
- **Application:**
    
    - **Doctrine of Election:** You cannot accept the benefit of a will while rejecting the burdens it places on you.
        
    - **Mortgage:** A mortgagor cannot ask the court to return their property (Redemption) unless they are willing to pay the debt with interest.
        
- **Case Example:** _Lodge v National Union Investment Co Ltd (1907)_. A borrower sued an illegal moneylender to get his securities back. The court said, "We will only order him to return your securities if you repay the loan capital, even though the loan contract was technically void."
    

**2. He who comes into Equity must come with Clean Hands**

- **Meaning:** The plaintiff must not have been guilty of any misconduct _regarding the transaction in question_. It focuses on the **past** conduct of the plaintiff.
    
- **Distinction:** "Must do equity" is about fulfilling obligations; "Clean hands" is about not having committed fraud or deceit.
    
- **Application:** If a tenant illegally sublets a flat and then asks for an injunction to stop the landlord from entering, the court will refuse because his hands are "unclean" regarding that tenancy.
    
- **Limit:** The misconduct must be related to the _specific lawsuit_. A general "bad character" (e.g., the plaintiff is a known liar in other matters) does not bar relief.
    

---

### II. Maxims Related to Technical Operation

These maxims explain how Equity interacts with the law and procedural gaps.

**3. Equity will not suffer a wrong to be without a remedy (_Ubi jus ibi remedium_)**

- **Meaning:** If a legal right exists but the Common Law fails to provide a mechanism to enforce it, Equity will invent a remedy.
    
- **Application:**
    
    - **Trusts:** Common Law ignored the beneficiary's rights; Equity created the Trust to protect them.
        
    - **Auxiliary Jurisdiction:** Equity invented "Discovery of Documents" because Common Law had no way to force evidence collection.
        

**4. Equity follows the Law (_Aequitas sequitur legem_)**

- **Meaning:** Equity does not recklessly override the Common Law. It respects legal estates, statutes, and rights unless they are used unconscionably.
    
- **Application:**
    
    - **Primogeniture:** Historically, Equity respected the harsh feudal rule that the eldest son inherits everything, even if it seemed unfair to younger siblings, because that was the Law.
        
    - **Statutes:** Equity cannot override a clear Act of Parliament (e.g., Limitation Act).
        

---

### III. Maxims Related to Time and Priority

**5. Delay defeats Equity (Doctrine of Laches)**

- **Meaning:** "Equity aids the vigilant, not the indolent." If a plaintiff sleeps on their rights for too long, Equity may refuse to help.
    
- **Rationale:** Evidence may be lost, witnesses may die, or the defendant may have changed their position believing the dispute was over.
    
- **Application:** Even if the statutory limitation (e.g., 3 years) hasn't fully expired, a court might deny _Specific Performance_ if the plaintiff waited 2 years without cause, causing hardship to the defendant.
    

**6. Where the Equities are equal, the Law prevails**

- **Meaning:** If two innocent parties are fighting, and one has the Legal Estate while the other has only an Equitable Estate, the Legal owner wins.
    
- **Application:** This protects the **Bona Fide Purchaser**. If Trustee sells to Buyer (who knows nothing), both Beneficiary and Buyer are "innocent." But Buyer gets the Legal Deed. So, Law prevails, and Buyer keeps the land.
    

**7. Where the Equities are equal, the first in time prevails**

- **Meaning:** If _both_ parties have only Equitable interests (no legal title involved), the one created first wins.
    
- **Application:** Trustee mortgages the trust land twice (illegally) to Lender A and Lender B. Neither gets the legal title. Lender A (first in time) gets priority over Lender B.
    

---

### IV. Maxims Related to Intent and Form

**8. Equity looks to the intent rather than the form**

- **Meaning:** Equity focuses on the substance of the transaction, not the technical appearance.
    
- **Application:**
    
    - **Mortgages:** Even if a mortgage deed says "If you don't pay by Tuesday, the land is mine forever," Equity looks at the _intent_ (it was a loan security, not a sale) and allows the borrower to repay late (_Equity of Redemption_).
        

**9. Equity looks on that as done which ought to be done**

- **Meaning:** If a person is under a contractual obligation to perform an act, Equity treats them as if they have already performed it from the moment the contract became binding.
    
- **Application:**
    
    - **Walsh v Lonsdale:** An agreement to grant a lease is treated as an actual lease.
        
    - **Specific Performance:** The buyer of land is treated as the owner from the moment the contract is signed, not just when the deed is registered.
        

---

### V. Other Key Maxims

**10. Equality is Equity**

- **Meaning:** Where there is no clear basis for division, Equity prefers equal shares.
    
- **Application:** Used in distributing assets among creditors or beneficiaries when specific proportions aren't defined.
    

**11. Equity acts _In Personam_**

- **Meaning:** As discussed in 1.3, equitable remedies are directed at the conscience of the individual.
    
- **Application:** A Bangladeshi court can order a defendant to transfer land in London, provided the defendant is personally present in Bangladesh.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Memorization Strategy:** Do not try to memorize all 12 word-for-word if you are struggling. Focus on the **"Big Four"** for problem questions:
    
    1. Clean Hands (Defense against a plaintiff).
        
    2. He who seeks equity must do equity (Conditions on a plaintiff).
        
    3. Laches (Time limits).
        
    4. Done which ought to be done (Walsh v Lonsdale / Contracts).
        
- **Bangladesh Context:** In Bangladesh, these maxims are applied via the **"Justice, Equity, and Good Conscience"** clause. However, remember that **Statute prevails over Maxims**. If the _Contract Act_ says a contract is void, you cannot use a Maxim to save it.
    
- **Problem Question Tip:** If a scenario involves a plaintiff who lied or cheated _before_ coming to court, explicitly write: _"Applying the maxim 'He who comes into equity must come with clean hands', the court will likely refuse the remedy."_
