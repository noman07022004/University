

## 2.1 Relationship Between Law and Equity ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐ ⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5):** This is the central conceptual pillar of the course. You cannot understand how modern courts work without grasping how these two systems settled their differences to live under one roof.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5):** This topic is a favorite for examiners. You will almost certainly encounter questions discussing the "Fusion" of Law and Equity or the maxim "Equity follows the Law."

---

### I. The Evolution of the Relationship

The relationship between Common Law and Equity is not static; it has evolved through three distinct phases: **Conflict**, **Compromise**, and **Fusion**.

#### 1. Phase I: Conflict (The Rivalry)

Initially, the Common Law courts and the Court of Chancery were rivals.

- **The Tension:** Common Law judges (like Chief Justice Coke) viewed the Chancellor's interference as an illegal usurpation of their authority. They argued that if a Common Law court gave a judgment, it should be final.
    
- **The Weapon:** The Chancellor used the "Common Injunction" to forbid a winning plaintiff at Common Law from executing their judgment.
    
- **The Climax:** The _Earl of Oxford’s Case_ (1615).
    
    - King James I ruled that the Chancellor was right.
        
    - **The Ruling:** In any conflict between the strict rules of Law and the dictates of conscience (Equity), **Equity prevails**.
        

#### 2. Phase II: Compromise (The Supplement)

After 1615, the two systems settled into a working relationship.

- **Equity Follows the Law:** Equity stopped trying to overrule the law arbitrarily. It accepted legal rights (like land ownership) but added a layer of "conscience" on top.
    
- **The "Gloss(টীকা)" Role:** As Maitland explained, Equity became a "gloss" or appendix to the Common Law. It did not try to cover every area of life (e.g., there is no "equitable theft"). It simply stepped in where the Common Law had a specific gap.
    

#### 3. Phase III: Fusion (The Judicature Acts)

The **Judicature Acts of 1873-1875** physically brought the two systems together.

- **Before:** Two separate courts, two separate expensive lawsuits.
    
- **After:** One High Court. A single judge could now say: "I recognize you have a contract (Law), but I will not let you enforce it because you cheated (Equity)."
    

---

### II. The Nature of the Modern Relationship: The "Fusion" Debate

The most critical academic aspect of this topic is the debate: **Did the Judicature Acts fuse the _rules_ or just the _courts_?**

#### 1. The Orthodox View: Administrative Fusion Only

This is the dominant academic view.

- **Theory:** The Acts were procedural. They allowed one court to administer both systems, but the systems themselves remained distinct.
    
- **Ashburner’s Metaphor:** _"The two streams of jurisdiction, though they run in the same channel, run side by side and do not mingle their waters."_
    
- **Implication:** You cannot mix and match remedies recklessly. You still need to prove an "equitable" right to get an "equitable" remedy. A judge cannot award "equitable damages" for a purely Common Law tort unless a specific statute allows it.
    

#### 2. The Radical View: Substantive Fusion

Some modern judges (notably **Lord Denning**) argued that the two streams _have_ mingled.

- **Theory:** The distinction is obsolete(অপ্রচলিত). Courts should just apply "The Law" to achieve the best result.
    
- **Example:** In cases like _United Scientific Holdings Ltd v Burnley Borough Council (1878)_, Lord Diplock stated: _"The waters of the confluent streams have surely mingled now."_
    
- **Status:** While tempting, this view is treated with caution. Academic consensus(ঐক্যমত্য) still leans towards the Orthodox view to maintain legal certainty.
    

---

### III. Maxims Governing the Relationship

The relationship is practically governed by specific maxims (which we will cover in detail in 2.3, but are relevant here):

1. **Equity follows the Law (_Aequitas sequitur legem_):** Equity respects legal estates and rules. It only interferes when the _use_ of that legal right is unconscionable.
    
2. **Where the Equities are equal, the Law prevails:** If both parties are innocent and morally equal, the one who holds the legal title wins. Equity will not strip a legal owner of their property unless the other party has a _better_ moral claim.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The Metaphor Battle:** Your exam answer _must_ mention the "Two Streams" metaphor. Contrast **Ashburner** (Orthodox/No Fusion) with **Lord Diplock** (Radical/Fusion).
    
- **Section 25 of Judicature Act 1873:** This is the statutory anchor. It codified the 1615 ruling: _"in all matters... in which there is any conflict... the Rules of Equity shall prevail."_
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Remember to conclude that in Bangladesh, this "conflict" was theoretical. Our courts have been "fused" from birth. We apply both, but the _principles_ remain distinct—we still distinguish between a "legal owner" and an "equitable owner" (Trustee vs. Beneficiary).
