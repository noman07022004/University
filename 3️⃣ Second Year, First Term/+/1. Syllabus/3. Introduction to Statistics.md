Course Code: 0542 28 Stat 2151
Year: Second
Term: First
Course Title: Introduction to Statistics
Course Status: Core
Credit: 4.0
Prerequisite(s): None
Rationale: The course of statistics is introduced to develop a foundation on basic statistical tools useful for empirical research.

## Course Contents

### Section A

1. Descriptive Statistics:
    1. Introduction to descriptive statistics,
    2. Measures of central location,
    3. Measures of dispersion,
    4. Measure of correlation;

2. Measuring Disparity between Two Counting:
    1. Proportions,
    2. Permutations and combinations,
    3. Fluctuation theory;

3. Elements of Probability:
    1. Fundamentals,
    2. Selection effect,
    3. Bayes' theorem,
    4. Screening devices and diagnostic tests,
    5. Monte Carlo methods,
    6. Foundation of probability;

4. Distributions:
    1. Introduction to probability distributions,
    2. Binomial distribution,
    3. Normal distribution and central limit theorem,
    4. Testing statistical hypothesis,
    5. Hypergeometric distribution,
    6. Tests of normality,
    7. Poisson distribution,
    8. Geometric and exponential distributions,
    9. Gamma distributions;

5. Statistical Inference for Two Proportions:
    1. Fisher's exact test of equality for two proportions,
    2. Chi-squared and z-score tests for equality of two proportions,
    3. Confidence intervals for proportions,
    4. Statistical power in hypothesis testing,
    5. Legal and statistical significance,
    6. Maximum likelihood estimation;

6. Multiple Proportions:
    1. Using chi-squared to test goodness of fit,
    2. Bonferroni's inequality and multiple comparisons,
    3. Phi-squared and Tau B;

7. Hypothesis Testing and P-Values:
    1. The Wald test,
    2. P-values,
    3. x2 distribution;


### Section B

8. Comparing Means:
    1. Student's T-Test,
    2. Analysis of variance for comparing several means;
    
9. Combining Evidence across Independent Strata:
    1. Mantel-Haenszel and Fisher methods for combining evidence,
    2. Meta-Analysis;

10. Sampling Issues:
    1. Random sampling theory,
    2. Capture/recapture;

11. Epidemiology:
    1. Introduction,
    2. Attributable risk,
    3. Epidemiologic principles of causation;

12. Survival Analysis:
    1. Death-density, survival, and hazard functions,
    2. Proportional hazards model,
    3. Quantitative risk assessment;

13. Nonparametric Methods:
    1. Sign test,
    2. Wilcoxon signed-rank test,
    3. Wilcoxon rank-sum test,
    4. Spearman's rank correlation coefficient;

14. Simple Regression Models:
    1. Introduction,
    2. Estimating and interpreting coefficients,
    3. Measures of indeterminacy for the regression equation,
    4. Statistical significance of the regression coefficients,
    5. Explanatory factors for a regression equation,
    6. Reading multiple-regression computer printout,
    7. Confidence and prediction intervals,
    8. Assumptions of the regression model,
    9. Transformations of variables;

15. Complex Regression Models:
    1. Time series,
    2. Interactive models,
    3. Locally weighted regression,
    4. Under-adjustment bias in employment regressions,
    5. System of equations,
    6. Logit and probit regression,
    7. Poisson regression,
    8. Negative binomial regression,
    9. Jack Knife,
    10. Cross-validation and bootstrap.
