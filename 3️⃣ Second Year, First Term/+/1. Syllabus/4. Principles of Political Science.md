Course Code: 0312 28 DS 2153
Year: Second
Term: First
Course Title: Principles of Political Science
Course Status: Core
Credit: 3.0
Prerequisite: None
Rationale: This course is introduced to build a stronger foundation on several theories of political science needed for legal studies.

## Course Contents

### Section A

1. State:
    1. Introduction,
    2. Ideas of Hobbes, 
    3. Ideas of Locke, 
    4. Ideas of Rousseau, 
    5. Ideas of Socialism;

2. Theories Justifying the state:
    1. Social contract,
    2. Utilitarianism,
    3. Principle of fairness;

3. Ruler:
    1. Introduction,
    2. Plato against democracy,
    3. Rousseau and the general will,
    4. Representative democracy;

4. Idea of Liberty:
    1. John S. Mill's Idea on liberty,
    2. Justification,
    3. Problems with liberalism;

5. Individualism, Justice and Feminism:
    1. Individualism and anti-individualism,
    2. Rights of women,
    3. Transcending liberal individualism;

6. Distribution of Property:
    1. Problem of distributive justice,
    2. Property and markets,
    3. Rawls' theory of justice,
    4. Criticism of Rawls,
    5. Marx's idea of alienation and property;
    
7. Political Ideologies:
    1. Liberalism,
    2. Conservatism,
    3. Socialism,
    4. Nationalism;

8. States:
    1. Institutionalized power,
    2. Effective, weak, and failed states,
    3. Unitary and federal systems,
    4. Electoral systems,
    5. States and the economy;
  
9. Constitutions:
    1. Theories,
    2. Constitutional supremacy;

10. Regimes:
    1. Representative democracy,
    2. Democracy in practice,
    3. Totalitarianism,
    4. Authoritarianism;
 
11. Political Culture:
    1. Definition,
    2. Decay of political culture,
    3. Elite and mass subcultures,
    4. Minority subcultures,
    5. Political socialization;
    
12. Public Opinion:
    1. Definition,
    2. Shaping public opinion,
    3. Polls;

13. Political Communication:
    1. Mass media and politics,
    2. Social media,
    3. Television,
    4. Mass media and Government;


### Section B

14. Parties:
    1. Functions of parties,
    2. Parties in democracies,
    3. Classification of political parties,
    4. Party systems,
    5. Future of party system;

15. Interest Groups:
    1. Ubiquity of interest groups,
    2. Interest groups, and government,
    3. Effective interest groups,
    4. Interest group strategies;

16. Elections:
    1. Voters, 
    2. Voting systems;

17. Legislatures:
    1. Origins of parliaments,
    2. Presidential and parliamentary systems,
    3. Bicameral and unicameral parliament,
    4. Functions of the legislatures;

18. Executives and Bureaucracies:
    1. Presidents and Prime Ministers,
    2. Executive leadership,
    3. Cabinets,
    4. Bureaucracy;

19. Judiciaries:
    1. Courts, bench, and bar,
    2. Role of courts,
    3. Supreme Court's political role;

20. Political Economy:
    1. Definition,
    2. Government and the economy,
    3. Poverty,
    4. Costs of welfare,
    5. Size of the government;

21. Violence and Revolution:
    1. System breakdown,
    2. Types of violence,
    3. Terrorism,
    4. Revolutions and aftermath of revolution;

22. International Relations:
    1. Definition,
    2. Power and national interest,
    3. Importance of economics,
    4. War and peace,
    5. Sovereignty and beyond sovereignty.
