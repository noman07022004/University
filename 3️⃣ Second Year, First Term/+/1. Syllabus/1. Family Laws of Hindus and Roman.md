Course Code: 0421 28 Law 2101
Year: Second
Term: First
Course Title: Family Laws of Hindus and Roman Law
Course Status: Core
Credit: 3.0
Prerequisite(s): None
Rationale: This course is designed to introduce learners to the provisions of Hindu personal law applicable in Bangladesh.
## Course Contents

### Section A
1. Historical Development of Hindu Law:
    1. Ancient period,
    2. Colonial period,
    3. Post-colonial period,
    4. Bangladesh period,
    5. Definition of a Hindu,
    6. Caste System,
    7. Nature and scope;

2. Sources of Hindu Law:
    1. Ancient sources,
    2. Modern sources;

3. Schools of Hindu Law:
    1. Origins and rationality,
    2. Mitakshara and Dayabhaga schools,
    3. Migration and application of personal laws,
    4. Changing of school,
    5. Applicability in Bangladesh;

4. Marriage:
    1. Definition, Nature, Object,
    2. Types,
    3. Essentials,
    4. Applicability of Child Marriage Restraint Act,
    5. Completion of marriage,
    6. Polygamy,
    7. Presumption of marriage,
    8. Registration,
    9. Remarriage,
    10. Legal effects of a valid marriage;

5. Divorce:
    1. Divorce in Bangladesh,
    2. Comparison with India;

6. Maintenance:
    1. Liability,
    2. Persons associated with maintenance,
    3. Maintenance in statutory provisions of Bangladesh,
    4. Wife's right to maintenance,
    5. Amount of maintenance,
    6. Widow's right to maintenance;

7. Adoption:
    1. Rationale and Objectives,
    2. Categories,
    3. Ceremony,
    4. Requirements,
    5. Consequences;

8. Guardianship and Custody:
    1. Concept,
    2. Distinction between guardianship and custody,
    3. Classification of guardianship,
    4. Powers of different types of guardians;

9. Gift:
    1. Elements,
    2. Transfer of ownership,
    3. Consideration,
    4. Registration,
    5. Possession,
    6. Transfer of interest,
    7. Perpetuity,
    8. Termination,
    9. _Donatio mortis causa_;

10. Will:
    1. Characteristics,
    2. Classification,
    3. Forms,
    4. Essential requirements,
    5. Effectiveness,
    6. Revocability,
    7. Capacity of the testator and receiver,
    8. Scope of property,
    9. Will to unborn person,
    10. Revocation;

11. Endowments:
    1. Categories,
    2. Essential requisites,
    3. Intention and purpose,
    4. Capacity of the settlor,
    5. Object,
    6. Subject of endowment,
    7. Ascertainability,
    8. Powers, functions, and duties of a _Shebait_ and _Mahant_,
    9. Alienation of debutter property;

12. Stridhana:
    1. Property received from Relatives,
    2. Gifts from strangers,
    3. Property by partition,
    4. Maintenance and inheritance,
    5. Rights to Stridhana,
    6. Inheritance of Stridhana property;

13. Widow's Estate:
    1. Nature and features,
    2. Alienation of property,
    3. Burden of proof;


### Section B
14. General Principles of Inheritance:
    1. Basics,
    2. Joint and undivided family system,
    3. Guiding principles under two schools,
    4. Difference of rules for male and female,
    5. Modes and devolution of property;

15. Female Heirs:
    1. Capacity,
    2. Limited or Life interest;

16. Doctrines (Inheritance):
    1. Doctrine of representation,
    2. Doctrine of _spes successionis_,
    3. Co-heirs,
    4. Succession _per stirpes_ and succession _per capita_;

17. Exclusion:
    1. Exclusion on moral and religious grounds,
    2. Exclusion on physical or mental defects,
    3. Statutory provisions for exclusion in Bangladesh;
18. Sources of Roman Law:
    1. The Twelve Tables;

19. Roman Law of Persons:
    1. Citizenship,
    2. Status,
    3. Slavery;

20. Roman Civil Law Concepts:
    1. Property and Ownership,
    2. Contracts and Torts,
    3. Marriage and Inheritance,
    4. Wills and Intestacy.
