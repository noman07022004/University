Course Title: Principles of Political Science
Course Status: Core
Credit: 3.0

Rationale: This course is introduced to build a stronger foundation on several theories of political science needed for legal studies.
### Course Contents
#### Section A
1. State: Introduction, Ideas of Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Socialism; Theories Justifying the state: Social contract, Utilitarianism, Principle of fairness; Ruler: Introduction, Plato against democracy, Rousseau and the general will, Representative democracy; Idea of Liberty: John S. Mill's Idea on liberty, Justification, Problems with liberalism; Individualism, Justice and Feminism: Individualism and anti-individualism, Rights of women, Transcending liberal individualism; Distribution of Property: Problem of distributive justice, Property and markets, Rawls' theory of justice, Criticism of Rawls, Marx's idea of alienation and property;
2. Political Ideologies: Liberalism, Conservatism, Socialism, Nationalism; States: Institutionalized power, Effective, weak, and failed states, Unitary and federal systems, Electoral systems, States and the economy; Constitutions: Theories, Constitutional supremacy; Regimes: Representative democracy, Democracy in practice, Totalitarianism, Authoritarianism;
3. Political Culture: Definition, Decay of political culture, Elite and mass subcultures, Minority subcultures, Political socialization; Public Opinion: Definition, Shaping public opinion, Polls; Political Communication: Mass media and politics, Social media, Television, Mass media and Government;
4. Parties: Functions of parties, Parties in democracies, Classification of political parties, Party systems, Future of party system; Interest Groups: Ubiquity of interest groups, Interest groups, and government, Effective interest groups, Interest group strategies;
5. Elections: Voters and voting systems; Legislatures: Origins of parliaments, Presidential and parliamentary systems, Bicameral and unicameral parliament, Functions of the legislatures; Executives and Bureaucracies: Presidents and Prime Ministers, Executive leadership, Cabinets, Bureaucracy; Judiciaries: Courts, bench, and bar, Role of courts, Supreme Court's political role;
6. Political Economy: Definition, Government and the economy, Poverty, Costs of welfare, Size of the government; Violence and Revolution: System breakdown, Types of violence, Terrorism, Revolutions and aftermath of revolution; International Relations: Definition, Power and national interest, Importance of economics, War and peace, Sovereignty and beyond sovereignty.

### Course Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of the course, the students will be able to:
1. understand the process of working of the political institutions;
2. analyze the existing structure and inter-relations of the legal and political institutions;
3. evaluate the significance, efficiency and adaptability of the political institutions;
4. apply the acquired knowledge about the political institutions in legal domain;
5. synthesize and develop novel critical discourse and perspective.
