Course Code: 0542 28 Stat 2151
Year: Second
Term: First
Course Title: Introduction to Statistics
Course Status: Core
Credit: 4.0
Prerequisite(s): None
Rationale: The course of statistics is introduced to develop a foundation on basic statistical tools useful for empirical research.

### Course Contents
#### Section A
1. Descriptive Statistics: Introduction to descriptive statistics, Measures of central location, Measures of dispersion, Measure of correlation; Measuring Disparity between Two Counting: Proportions, permutations and combinations, Fluctuation theory;
2. Elements of Probability: Fundamentals, Selection effect, Bayes' theorem, Screening devices and diagnostic tests, Monte Carlo methods, Foundation of probability; Distributions: Introduction to probability distributions, Binomial distribution, Normal distribution and central limit theorem, Testing statistical hypothesis, Hypergeometric distribution, Tests of normality, Poisson distribution, Geometric and exponential distributions, Gamma distributions;
3. Statistical Inference for Two Proportions: Fisher's exact test of equality for two proportions, Chi-squared and z-score tests for equality of two proportions, Confidence intervals for proportions, Statistical power in hypothesis testing, Legal and statistical significance, Maximum likelihood estimation; Multiple Proportions: Using chi-squared to test goodness of fit, Bonferroni's inequality and multiple comparisons, Phi-squared and Tau B; Hypothesis Testing and P-Values: The Wald test, P-values, x2 distribution;

#### Section B
4. Comparing Means: Student's T-Test, Analysis of variance for comparing several means; Combining Evidence across Independent Strata: Mantel-Haenszel and Fisher methods for combining evidence, Meta-Analysis; Sampling Issues: Random sampling theory, Capture/recapture;
5. Epidemiology: Introduction, Attributable risk, Epidemiologic principles of causation; Survival Analysis: Death-density, survival, and hazard functions, Proportional hazards model, Quantitative risk assessment; Nonparametric Methods: Sign test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, Spearman's rank correlation coefficient;
6. Simple Regression Models: Introduction, Estimating and interpreting coefficients, Measures of indeterminacy for the regression equation, Statistical significance of the regression coefficients, Explanatory factors for a regression equation, Reading multiple-regression computer printout, Confidence and prediction intervals, Assumptions of the regression model, Transformations of variables; Complex Regression Models: Time series, Interactive models, Locally weighted regression, Under-adjustment bias in employment regressions, System of equations, Logit and probit regression, Poisson regression, Negative binomial regression, Jack Knife, Cross-validation and bootstrap.

### Course Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of the course, the students will be able to:
1. understand the foundational knowledge of forming the project and using different research methods;
2. apply the concepts in their own research projects;
3. analyze their research idea within the purview of the scientific methods;
4. evaluate the purpose and functionality of methods of the researches of others as well as their own;
5. create an exhaustive, efficient methodical approach for their own research ideas.
