Year: Second

Course Title: Family Laws of Hindus and Roman Law
Course Status: Core
Credit: 3.0
Prerequisite(s): None
Rationale: This course is designed to introduce learners to the provisions of Hindu personal law applicable in Bangladesh. Besides this course focuses also Buddhist and Christian.

### Course Content
#### Section A
1. Historical Development of Hindu Law: Ancient period, Colonial period, Post-colonial period, Bangladesh period, Definition of a Hindu, Caste System; Nature and scope; Sources of Hindu Law: Ancient sources, Modern sources; Schools of Hindu Law: Origins and rationality, Mitakshara and Dayabhaga schools, Migration and application of personal laws, Changing of school, Applicability in Bangladesh;
2. Marriage: Definition, Nature, Object, Types, Essentials, Applicability of Child Marriage Restraint Act, Completion of marriage, Polygamy, Presumption of marriage, Registration, Remarriage, Legal effects of a valid marriage; Divorce: Divorce in Bangladesh, Comparison with India; Maintenance: Liability, Persons associated with maintenance, Maintenance in statutory provisions of Bangladesh, Wife's right to maintenance, Amount of maintenance, Widow's right to maintenance; Adoption: Rationale, Objectives, Categories, Ceremony, Requirements, Consequences; Guardianship and Custody: Concept, Distinction between guardianship and custody, classification of guardianship, Powers of different types of guardians;
3. Gift: Elements, Transfer of ownership, Consideration, Registration, Possession, Transfer of interest, Perpetuity, Termination, Donation mortis cause; Will: Characteristics, Classification, Forms, Essential requirements, Effectivity, Revocability, Capacity of the testator and receiver, Scope of property, Will to unborn person, Revocation; Endowments: Categories, Essential requisites, Intention and purpose, Capacity of the settlor, Object, Subject of endowment, Ascertainability, Powers, functions, and duties of a shebait and mahant, Alienation of debutter property; Stridhana: Property received from Relatives, Gifts from strangers, Property by partition, Maintenance and inheritance, Rights to stridhana, Inheritance of stridhana property, Widow's Estate: Nature and features, Alienation of property, Burden of proof;
#### Section B
4. General Principles of Inheritance: Basics, joint and undivided family system, Guiding principles under two schools, Difference of rules for male and female, Modes and devolution of property; Female Heirs: Capacity, Limited of life interest; Doctrines: Doctrine of representation, Doctrine of spes successionis, Co-heirs, Succession per stirpes and succession per capita; Exclusion: Exclusion on moral and religious grounds, Exclusion on physical or mental defects, Statutory provisions for exclusion in Bangladesh;
5. Sources of Roman Law: The twelve Tables, Law of Persons: Citizenship, Status, and Slavery, Property and Ownership, Contracts and Torts, Marriage and Inheritance, Wills and Intestacy.


### Course Learning Outcomes
Upon successful completion of the course, the students will be able to:
1. understand and demonstrate knowledge on different provisions of Hindu, Christan and Buddist family law like marriage, divorce, dower, maintenance etc.;
2. illustrate, summarize and explain those provisions in the light of real-life issues or problems;
3. apply, demonstrate that knowledge in solving various family law disputes like divorce, dower, maintenance, restitution of conjugal rights ets.;
4. analyze, compare and contrast among those personal laws;
5. evaluate those provisions, their necessities, importance in the real life of the people of those religions and
6. device a plan to adapt those laws in their real life following those religions or advice to adapt them.
