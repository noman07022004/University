### 19.2 Role of Courts ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The courts are the "referees" of the political game. Without them, laws are just words on paper. Understanding their role is essential to understanding the concept of "Constitutionalism."
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often ask for the "Functions of the Judiciary" or to discuss how courts protect individual liberty.
    

---

In a modern political system, the **Role of Courts** extends far beyond simply punishing criminals. The courts act as the essential stabilizing mechanism of the state, ensuring that conflicts are resolved peacefully and that the government itself obeys the law.

Political scientists identify several core roles that courts perform in any developed society:

#### 1. Adjudication (Dispute Resolution)

This is the most visible and historic role of the courts. In any society, conflicts are inevitable—between individuals, between corporations, or between the citizen and the state.

- **The Alternative to Violence:** If there were no courts, disputes would be settled by force (violence). Courts provide a civilized, neutral forum where disputes are settled by rules.
    
- **Types of Disputes:**
    
    - **Civil Cases:** Private disputes (e.g., contracts, property, divorce). The court acts as an arbitrator to determine who owes what.
        
    - **Criminal Cases:** Offenses against the public order (e.g., theft, murder). Here, the court acts as the arm of the state to enforce social norms and punish deviance.
        

#### 2. Interpretation of Laws (Statutory Construction)

Legislatures write laws, but they cannot foresee every possible situation. Laws are often vague, ambiguous, or general.

- **Making Law Meaningful:** When a law says "drive with reasonable care," what does "reasonable" mean? Is driving 50mph in rain "reasonable"? The courts must interpret the text and apply it to specific facts.
    
- **Judge-Made Law:** By interpreting the law, judges effectively _make_ law. Once a high court decides that "reasonable" means X, that definition becomes the rule for everyone else (Precedent).
    

#### 3. Guardianship of the Constitution

In countries with a written constitution (like the USA, India, or Bangladesh), the courts play a supreme role.

- **Protecting the Pact:** The Constitution is the contract between the people and the government. The courts ensure that the government (Executive and Legislature) does not violate this contract.
    
- **Protecting Rights:** Courts are the primary defenders of civil liberties. If the police arrest you illegally or the parliament passes a racist law, the courts are the only institution with the power to say "Stop."
    

#### 4. Federal Umpire

In federal systems (like the USA, India, or Canada), there is a constant power struggle between the **Central Government** and the **State/Provincial Governments**.

- **The Role:** The Supreme Court acts as the neutral umpire to decide who has the power to do what. (e.g., Can the state government tax a federal bank? The Court decides).
    

#### 5. Administration of Estates and Minor Roles

Courts also handle administrative tasks that require oversight, such as managing the wills of deceased people, appointing guardians for orphans, or overseeing bankruptcy proceedings.

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **Rule of Law vs. Rule by Law:**
    
    - **Rule of Law:** Everyone, including the government and the King, is subject to the law. The courts are independent. (Democracy).
        
    - **Rule by Law:** The government uses the law as a weapon to control the people, but the government itself is above the law. The courts obey the dictator. (Authoritarianism).
        
- **Stare Decisis (Precedent):** This is a key legal term. It means "to stand by things decided." In the common law system (UK, US, Bangladesh), once a higher court makes a decision, lower courts _must_ follow it. This ensures the law is consistent and predictable.
