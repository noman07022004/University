### 19.1 Courts, Bench, and Bar ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** The legal system has its own language. Understanding the distinction between the "Bench" (judges) and the "Bar" (lawyers) is fundamental to understanding how justice is administered.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While essential for context, exams usually focus more on the _power_ of courts (like Judicial Review) rather than the definition of the Bar. However, short notes on "Bench-Bar Relations" are common.
    

To understand the operation of the legal system, we must look at its three structural components: the physical institutions (**Courts**) and the two groups of people who operate them (**The Bench** and **The Bar**).

#### 1. The Courts (The Institution)

The "Court" refers to the tribunal with the authority to adjudicate legal disputes and dispense justice.

- **Hierarchy:** Courts are almost always organized in a pyramid structure.
    
    - **Lower Courts (Trial Courts):** This is where cases start. They hear evidence, listen to witnesses, and establish the facts (e.g., "Did Person A steal the car?").
        
    - **Appellate Courts:** These courts do not hear new witnesses. They only review the decisions of lower courts to ensure the law was applied correctly.
        
    - **Supreme Court (Apex Court):** The final court of appeal. Its decisions set precedents that all lower courts must follow.
        

#### 2. The Bench (The Judges)

The term **"The Bench"** is a metonym for the **Judiciary** (the judges collectively). It comes from the historical practice where judges sat on a raised bench above the rest of the courtroom.

- **Role:** The Judge is the impartial umpire.
    
    - In **Adversarial Systems** (UK, USA, Bangladesh), the judge listens to both sides and decides the winner based on the arguments presented.
        
    - In **Inquisitorial Systems** (France, Germany), the judge actively investigates the case, questions witnesses, and searches for the truth themselves.
        
- **Independence:** To function correctly, the Bench must be independent of the Executive. Judges should not fear being fired if they rule against the government.
    

#### 3. The Bar (The Lawyers)

The term **"The Bar"** refers to the legal profession (lawyers/advocates). Historically, the "bar" was a physical railing in the courtroom that separated the judges and official business from the public. Only qualified lawyers were allowed to cross the "bar" to speak to the judges.

- **Role:** Lawyers are "Officers of the Court." Their primary duty is to their client (to defend them zealously), but they also have a duty to the court not to lie or fabricate evidence.
    
- **The Structure:**
    
    - In some countries (like the UK), the profession is split between **Solicitors** (who do the paperwork and deal with clients) and **Barristers** (specialists who wear wigs and argue in court).
        
    - In others (like the USA or Bangladesh), the profession is fused; a lawyer can do both.
        

#### 4. The Bench-Bar Relationship

The quality of justice depends on the relationship between these two.

- **Symbiosis:** A judge cannot decide a case correctly if the lawyers do not research and present the law accurately. Conversely, lawyers cannot work if judges are biased or incompetent.
    
- **Recruitment:** In common law countries (like the UK or US), judges are often recruited _from_ the Bar. A senior lawyer who has practiced for decades is appointed to the Bench. This ensures judges have practical experience.
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **Adversarial vs. Inquisitorial:** This is the most important academic distinction in this topic.
    
    - _Adversarial (Common Law):_ A battle between two lawyers; the judge is a referee.
        
    - _Inquisitorial (Civil Law):_ An investigation by the state/judge to find the truth.
        
- **Sub Judice:** A Latin rule that means a matter is currently "under judgment." In many countries, the media and politicians are forbidden from discussing the details of a case while it is _sub judice_ to avoid influencing the outcome.
    
- **Contempt of Court:** The power of the Bench to punish anyone (including lawyers or journalists) who disrespects the court or disobeys its orders.
