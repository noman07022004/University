### 11.1 Definition of Political Culture ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This concept explains why the _same_ system of government (e.g., democracy) works perfectly in one country but collapses in another. It distinguishes the "Hardware" of politics (Constitutions/Parliaments) from the "Software" (People's beliefs).
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A standard question. Exams often ask for the definition by **Almond and Verba** or to distinguish between **Political Culture** and **Public Opinion**.
    

---

**Introduction: The Software of Democracy** Political institutions (parliaments, courts, constitutions) are like the hardware of a computer. However, hardware is useless without software. **Political Culture** is that software. It is the set of attitudes, beliefs, and sentiments that give order and meaning to a political process. It is the "psychological dimension" of politics. If a country has a democratic constitution but a culture that values dictatorship and violence, the democracy will fail.

**1. The Classic Definition (Almond & Verba)** The term was popularized by **Gabriel Almond** and **Sidney Verba** in their groundbreaking book _The Civic Culture_ (1963). They defined it as:

> _"The pattern of individual attitudes and orientations toward politics among the members of a political system."_

**2. Three Components of Political Culture** Almond and Verba argued that political culture is made up of three types of "orientations" (ways of looking at the world):

- **A. Cognitive Orientation (Knowledge):**
    
    - _Question:_ What do citizens **know** about their government?
        
    - _Example:_ Do they know the name of the Prime Minister? Do they know how a bill becomes a law? Do they know their rights?
        
- **B. Affective Orientation (Feelings):**
    
    - _Question:_ How do citizens **feel** about their government?
        
    - _Example:_ Do they feel proud when they see the flag? Do they feel angry when they see the police? Is there a sense of loyalty or alienation?
        
- **C. Evaluative Orientation (Judgment):**
    
    - _Question:_ How do citizens **judge** the performance of their government?
        
    - _Example:_ Do they believe the government is fair? Do they think the laws are just? This involves combining knowledge and feelings to make a moral judgment.
        

**3. Why Political Culture Matters** Political culture acts as a filter.

- **Legitimacy:** A government is only stable if the political culture supports it. If the culture believes "Leaders are chosen by God" (Traditional), a secular democracy will feel illegitimate.
    
- **Boundaries:** It defines what is "acceptable" behavior. In the UK, losing an election means you shake hands and leave. In some other cultures, losing an election means you start a civil war. The _law_ is the same, but the _culture_ is different.
    

**4. Political Culture vs. Public Opinion** It is important to distinguish these two:

- **Public Opinion:** Short-term views on specific issues (e.g., "Do you support the new tax law?"). It changes rapidly.
    
- **Political Culture:** Long-term, deep-seated values (e.g., "Do you believe in liberty?"). It takes generations to change.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Analogy for Exam:** Use the **"Hardware/Software"** analogy. The State is the machine; Culture is the operating system.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Bangladesh has a complex political culture. It is a mix of **Subject Culture** (respect for authority/charismatic leaders) and **Participant Culture** (massive voter turnout and street activism).
    
- **Key Name:** Always mention **Gabriel Almond** and **Sidney Verba** when defining this term. They are the "fathers" of this field.
