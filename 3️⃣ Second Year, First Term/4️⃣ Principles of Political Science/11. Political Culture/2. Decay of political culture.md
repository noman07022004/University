### 11.2 Decay of Political Culture ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Understanding _why_ a democracy falls apart is often more important than understanding how it was built. Political culture doesn't just grow; it can rot. This topic explains the transition from a stable society to a chaotic one.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** While less frequent than standard definitions, exams often ask for an analysis of **"Political Decay"** (referencing Samuel Huntington) or the causes of **"Democratic Backsliding."**
    

---

**Introduction: When the Software Crashes** If Political Culture is the "software" that runs the state, **Political Decay** is the virus that crashes it. It refers to the erosion of the shared values, trust, and civic habits that sustain a political system. When political culture decays, the formal institutions (parliament, courts) might still exist, but they stop functioning effectively because the people no longer believe in them or play by the rules.

**1. Symptoms of Decay** How do we know a political culture is rotting? Political scientists identify several key symptoms:

- **Cynicism and Distrust:**
    
    - A healthy culture has "Skeptical Trust" (trust but verify).
        
    - A decaying culture has **Cynicism**. Citizens believe _all_ politicians are corrupt, _all_ elections are rigged, and the system is fundamentally broken. When trust vanishes, people stop paying taxes or obeying laws.
        
- **Polarization (The "Us vs. Them" Mentality):**
    
    - In a healthy culture, the opponent is a "rival" to be defeated.
        
    - In a decaying culture, the opponent is an **"Enemy"** to be destroyed. Politics becomes war. There is no middle ground or compromise.
        
- **Violence as a Tool:**
    
    - When culture decays, arguments move from the Parliament to the streets. Riots, assassinations, and clashes become normalized methods of political expression.
        
- **The Rise of Anti-System Parties:**
    
    - Voters start electing demagogues or extremists who promise to "burn the system down" rather than fix it.
        

**2. Theoretical Framework: Samuel Huntington**

The concept of "Political Decay" is most famously associated with **Samuel Huntington** in his book _Political Order in Changing Societies_ (1968).

- **The Gap Hypothesis:** Huntington argued that decay happens when **Social Mobilization** (people becoming educated/aware and wanting to participate) happens faster than **Institutionalization** (the government's ability to organize and satisfy them).
    
- **The Result:** If people want to participate but the institutions are weak or corrupt, the energy turns into chaos and violence. The culture shifts from "civic" to "praetorian" (mob rule).
    

**3. The Loss of Social Capital (Robert Putnam)** Another view comes from **Robert Putnam** (_Bowling Alone_).

- **Social Capital:** The networks of trust and cooperation in a society (e.g., joining clubs, unions, or community groups).
    
- **Decay:** Putnam argued that modern people are becoming isolated ("Bowling alone"). When we stop interacting with our neighbors, we lose the ability to trust strangers. A society of isolated individuals cannot sustain a democracy.
    

**4. Consequences of Decay** When political culture decays, the vacuum is often filled by **Authoritarianism**.

- **The Strongman Appeal:** When society feels chaotic and corrupt, people often welcome a dictator who promises "Law and Order." They willingly trade their freedom for stability.
    
- **State Failure:** If the decay is severe enough, the state itself may collapse into civil war (e.g., Lebanon, Yugoslavia).
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Term - "Democratic Backsliding":** This is the modern academic term for the decay of democratic culture (seen currently in countries like Hungary, Venezuela, or even the USA). It is a slow erosion, not a sudden coup.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "What causes political instability?", argue that it is often a lag between **Culture** and **Institutions**. If the culture becomes violent/impatient but the institutions are slow/rigid, the system breaks.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Student politics in Bangladesh is often cited as an example of decay—originally a noble force for liberation (1952, 1971), it later decayed into "arms and muscle" politics due to the erosion of ideological values.
