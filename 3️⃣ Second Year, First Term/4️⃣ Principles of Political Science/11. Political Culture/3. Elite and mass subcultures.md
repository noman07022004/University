### 11.3 Elite and Mass Subcultures ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Political culture is rarely uniform across a whole country. There is almost always a sharp divide between the "rulers" and the "ruled." Understanding this split is crucial for analyzing modern phenomena like **Populism** (where the Masses revolt against the Elites).
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A frequent topic. Exams often ask to compare the **characteristics of Elite vs. Mass political behavior** or to discuss **Vilfredo Pareto’s** theory of elites.
    

---

**Introduction: The Two Worlds of Politics** While we often speak of a "British Political Culture" or "Bangladeshi Political Culture," in reality, culture is stratified. Political Scientists (like Gaetano Mosca and Vilfredo Pareto) observed that in every society, two distinct groups emerge:

1. **The Elite:** A small minority that holds power, is highly educated, and deeply involved in political affairs.
    
2. **The Mass:** The vast majority of the population that is largely passive and reacts to politics rather than shaping it. The "Subculture" concept suggests that these two groups live in different mental worlds with different values and rules.
    

**1. The Elite Subculture** This group consists of politicians, bureaucrats, journalists, business leaders, and intellectuals.

- **High Complexity:** They understand the complex details of policy (e.g., how the Central Bank works). Their views are usually ideologically consistent (e.g., if they are Liberal, they are Liberal on _all_ issues).
    
- **High Efficacy:** They believe they can change the system. They know which phone numbers to call and how to lobby.
    
- **Tolerance:** Studies (like Stouffer’s) often show that elites are _more_ committed to democratic norms (free speech for unpopular groups) than the masses, because they understand the "rules of the game."
    
- **Role:** They act as the "Gatekeepers" of democracy, filtering demands and maintaining stability.
    

**2. The Mass Subculture** This group consists of the ordinary citizenry.

- **Low Complexity:** They tend to view politics in simple, emotional terms (Good vs. Evil, Us vs. Them). They are often less interested in policy details and more interested in immediate results (prices, jobs).
    
- **Inconsistency:** Their views often contradict. A voter might support "Lower Taxes" (Right-wing) but also "Higher Welfare Spending" (Left-wing) simultaneously.
    
- **Lower Tolerance:** The mass subculture is often more authoritarian. They are more likely to support banning unpopular speech or cracking down on minorities if they feel threatened.
    
- **Role:** They provide the "Legitimacy" and the vote. They act as the ultimate check on the elites.
    

**3. The Interaction (The Stability Gap)**

For a democracy to work, there must be a balance between these two subcultures.

- **Healthy Democracy:** The Elites respect the Masses' power to vote them out, and the Masses trust the Elites' expertise to run the government.
    
- **The Populist Revolt:** When the gap becomes too wide—when Elites become too arrogant or culturally different (e.g., "Globalist" elites vs. "Nationalist" masses)—the Masses lose trust. They may elect a populist leader to "smash the elite." This is the friction point in many modern democracies today.
    

**4. Pareto’s "Circulation of Elites"** The Italian sociologist **Vilfredo Pareto** argued that history is just a graveyard of aristocracies.

- **The Theory:** The Masses never truly rule. History is just a cycle of one Elite replacing another.
    
- **Lions vs. Foxes:**
    
    - **Foxes:** Elites who rule by cunning and manipulation (common in democracies).
        
    - **Lions:** Elites who rule by force and strength (common in dictatorships).
        
- **Circulation:** When the "Foxes" become too corrupt and weak, "Lions" rise from the masses to overthrow them, becoming the new Elite.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Term - "The Iron Law of Oligarchy":** (Recap from Topic 10). Remember that even in organizations dedicated to the masses (like Labor Unions), an _Elite Subculture_ inevitably forms at the top.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Is the Mass Subculture dangerous?", be nuanced. You can argue that while the masses can be intolerant/emotional, they are the only safeguard against Elite tyranny. A democracy without active masses is just an oligarchy.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** In Bangladesh, the "Elite Subculture" is often concentrated in Dhaka (the "Civil Society"), engaging in seminars and talk shows, while the "Mass Subculture" in rural areas operates on patronage and loyalty to local leaders.
