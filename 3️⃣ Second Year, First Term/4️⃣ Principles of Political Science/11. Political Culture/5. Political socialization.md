### 11.5 Political Socialization ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** If Political Culture is the "software," Political Socialization is the "installation process." It explains _how_ individuals acquire their political beliefs. You are not born a Liberal or a Conservative; you are taught to be one.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A very high-frequency exam topic. Questions often ask to **"Discuss the agents of political socialization"** or to explain the difference between **Primary** and **Secondary** socialization.
    

---

**Introduction: The Learning Process** Political Socialization is the lifelong process by which individuals learn and internalize the political values, beliefs, and behaviors of their society. It is the transmission belt of culture. Just as parents teach children how to eat or speak, society teaches them how to relate to the state. Without this process, a country's political culture would die out in one generation.

**1. The Agents of Socialization**

We learn politics from specific institutions and groups. These are called "Agents."

- **A. The Family (The Primary Agent):**
    
    - **Role:** The most powerful influence. It starts early (age 3–4).
        
    - **Mechanism:** Children tend to inherit the party loyalty of their parents. If your parents hate the government, you likely will too.
        
    - **Monopoly:** Because small children trust their parents explicitly, these early lessons are very hard to unlearn later in life.
        
- **B. Education (Schools/Universities):**
    
    - **Role:** The state's official tool for indoctrination.
        
    - **Mechanism:** Schools teach the "Civic Creed"—saluting the flag, singing the anthem, and learning "sanitized" history (e.g., "Our country has always been the good guy").
        
    - **Goal:** To create loyal, obedient citizens.
        
- **C. Peer Groups:**
    
    - **Role:** Friends, neighbors, and colleagues.
        
    - **Mechanism:** As teenagers, people want to fit in. If your friend group is radical, you might adopt radical views to be accepted. This often challenges the views learned from the family.
        
- **D. Mass Media:**
    
    - **Role:** The "New Parent." In the digital age, social media, TV, and news shape our perception of reality more than anything else.
        
    - **Mechanism:** It sets the agenda—telling us _what_ to think about (e.g., "Immigration is a crisis" vs. "Climate Change is a crisis").
        

**2. Stages of Socialization**

- **Primary Socialization (Childhood):**
    
    - Learns basic loyalties (Nationalism, Flag, President).
        
    - **"The Benevolent Leader" Hypothesis:** Children tend to view the President/King as a "father figure" who is good and helpful (unless they grow up in a marginalized minority).
        
- **Secondary Socialization (Adulthood):**
    
    - Learns specific issues (Taxes, Foreign Policy).
        
    - Happens through the workplace (unions), marriage, or major events (wars, depressions).
        

**3. The Primacy vs. Recency Principle**

- **The Primacy Principle:** "What is learned first is learned best." (Early family lessons stick forever).
    
- **The Recency Principle:** "What is learned most recently has the most impact." (A major event like the 2024 Revolution in Bangladesh can overwrite early learning).
    
- _Consensus:_ Primacy usually wins for deep values (Identity), but Recency wins for specific opinions (Voting).
    

**4. Importance for the State**

- **Stability:** Successful socialization creates a stable state because citizens voluntarily obey the law.
    
- **Continuity:** It ensures the system survives the death of leaders.
    
- **Change:** If the state wants to change society (e.g., Communist China), it must take control of socialization (schools/media) to "re-program" the youth.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Concept - "Resocialization":** This happens when an individual dramatically changes their political personality (e.g., a lifelong Communist becomes a Capitalist). It usually happens after a traumatic event (war, migration to a new country, or prison).
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** **Universities** (like Dhaka University) act as massive agents of political socialization in Bangladesh. Unlike in the West, where students might be passive, here the "campus culture" is deeply political, often serving as the training ground for future national leaders.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** When discussing **Family**, mention that its influence is _declining_ in the modern world, while the influence of **Social Media** is _rising_.
