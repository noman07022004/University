### 21.3 Terrorism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Terrorism is a defining feature of modern global politics. It challenges the state's monopoly on violence and forces a rethinking of security, civil liberties, and international relations.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often focus on the difficulty of **defining** terrorism (the "freedom fighter" debate) or its strategic logic as a weapon of the weak.
    

---

**Terrorism** is a specific and highly controversial form of political violence. While the term is often used as an insult for any enemy, in political science, it has a precise technical meaning: **The deliberate use or threat of violence against civilians to create fear (terror) in order to achieve a political, religious, or ideological goal**.

It is fundamentally a form of **Psychological Warfare**. The target of the violence (the victims) is not the _target_ of the message. The victims are merely props used to send a terrifying message to the wider audience (the government or society).

#### 1. Key Characteristics

To distinguish terrorism from other violence (like war or crime), it must have these features:

- **Political Objective:** It is not done for personal gain (like a robbery) or madness. It seeks to change a law, a border, or a government system.
    
- **Targeting Civilians:** This is the crucial distinction. Soldiers fight soldiers. Terrorists attack "soft targets" (markets, buses, schools) who cannot defend themselves.
    
- **Creation of Fear:** The physical damage is often small compared to the psychological impact. A single bomb kills few, but it makes millions fear taking the bus.
    
- **Asymmetric Warfare:** It is the "Weapon of the Weak." Small groups cannot fight a powerful army (like the US or Indian military) on a battlefield. So, they bypass the military to attack the society directly.
    

#### 2. Types of Terrorism

- **Domestic Terrorism:** Violence committed by groups within a country against their own state (e.g., The Oklahoma City bombing in the US, or Naxalite violence in India).
    
- **International/Transnational Terrorism:** Groups that operate across borders and attack foreign targets (e.g., Al-Qaeda or ISIS attacking targets in Europe or the US).
    
- **State-Sponsored Terrorism:** When a sovereign state secretly funds, arms, or trains terrorist groups to attack another country (a "proxy war") to avoid direct blame.
    
- **State Terrorism:** When a government uses terror tactics (kidnapping, torture, public executions) against its _own_ population to force obedience (e.g., The Reign of Terror in France or Stalin’s purges).
    

#### 3. Strategic Logic (Why do they do it?)

Terrorism is not irrational; it is a calculated strategy.

- **Provocation:** Attacks aim to provoke the government into a brutal overreaction (e.g., bombing a village). This overreaction angers the population, driving more recruits to the terrorists.
    
- **Agenda Setting:** It forces the world to pay attention to a forgotten cause. (e.g., The Munich Olympics attack in 1972 put the Palestinian cause on the global map).
    
- **Attrition:** To inflict painful costs (economic and psychological) on the enemy until they give up and withdraw (e.g., The IRA bombing London to force the British out of Northern Ireland).
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **The Definition Problem:** There is no single internationally agreed definition of terrorism. Why? Because **"One man's terrorist is another man's freedom fighter."** States often label their enemies "terrorists" to delegitimize them, while supporters see them as "revolutionaries."
    
- **Academic Solution:** Focus on the _method_, not the _cause_. If a "freedom fighter" blows up a school bus full of children, they are using a _terrorist tactic_, regardless of how noble they think their goal is.
    
- **Cyber-Terrorism:** A modern evolution. Attacks on critical infrastructure (power grids, banks) using computer code rather than bombs to cause panic and chaos.
