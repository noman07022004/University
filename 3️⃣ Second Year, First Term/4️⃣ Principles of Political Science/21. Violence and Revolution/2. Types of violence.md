### 21.2 Types of Violence ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Political violence is not random; it has structure and specific goals. Distinguishing between a "Coup" and a "Revolution," or "Separatism" and "Primordial violence," is vital for accurate political analysis.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Short questions frequently ask for definitions of specific types (e.g., "Define Coup d'état") or to distinguish between them.
    

---

Political violence is the use of physical force to achieve political goals. While it often looks chaotic on the news, political scientists categorize violence based on **who** is fighting and **what** they want.

Fred R. von der Mehden and other scholars generally classify political violence into five distinct types:

#### 1. Primordial (Communal) Violence

This is violence between different communities within a state, not necessarily directed _at_ the government, but often arising from ancient or deep-seated hatreds.

- **Nature:** It is based on **ascriptive** identities—traits you are born with (race, religion, language, ethnicity).
    
- **Goal:** To defend the group's honor or dominance against a rival group.
    
- **Examples:** Religious riots between Hindus and Muslims in South Asia; tribal warfare in parts of Africa; or ethnic clashes in the Balkans.
    

#### 2. Separatist (Secessionist) Violence

This occurs when a specific region or group wants to leave the existing state to form a new, independent nation.

- **Nature:** It is geographically concentrated. The group feels they are a distinct "nation" trapped inside the wrong state.
    
- **Goal:** Independence or high autonomy.
    
- **Examples:** The Tamil Tigers (LTTE) in Sri Lanka, the struggle for Bangladesh in 1971 (before it became a formal war), or the Kurds in the Middle East.
    

#### 3. Revolutionary Violence

This is the most ambitious form of violence. The goal is not just to separate or kill a rival group, but to **overthrow the entire existing regime** and replace it with a new social and political order.

- **Nature:** It is ideological. It seeks to change the _rules_ of the society (e.g., from Monarchy to Republic, or Capitalism to Communism).
    
- **Examples:** The French Revolution, the Russian Revolution (1917), or the Maoist insurgencies.
    

#### 4. Coups d'état (Military Takeovers)

A _Coup d'état_ (blow against the state) is a sudden, violent, or illegal seizure of power from a government.

- **Nature:** It is usually carried out by a small group within the existing state apparatus—typically the **Military**. It is "top-down" violence.
    
- **Goal:** To replace the leadership (the President/PM) without necessarily changing the social structure or ideology of the country.
    
- **Examples:** The military takeovers in Myanmar, Thailand, or Pakistan.
    

#### 5. State Violence (Repression)

We often forget that the biggest perpetrator of violence is often the government itself.

- **Nature:** The use of police, military, or secret services against its own citizens.
    
- **Goal:** To crush dissent, maintain order, or eliminate political enemies.
    
- **Examples:** The "Great Purge" in the Soviet Union, police brutality against protestors, or in extreme cases, genocide (the state trying to wipe out a specific group).
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **Coup vs. Revolution:** This is a classic exam distinction.
    
    - **Coup:** Fast, involves few people (elite), changes the _leader_.
        
    - **Revolution:** Slow/Long, involves masses (people), changes the _system_.
        
- **Relative Deprivation:** Often cited as a cause for these types of violence (especially Revolutionary and Separatist). It is the feeling that "we are getting less than we deserve" compared to others.
    
- **Asymmetric Warfare:** A term used when a weak group (revolutionaries/separatists) fights a strong state. They cannot fight head-on, so they use **Guerrilla Warfare** or **Terrorism** (which leads to the next topic).
