### 21.1 System Breakdown ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** To understand why revolutions happen, you must first understand why the previous system failed. Revolutions rarely happen to strong governments; they happen to broken ones.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often ask about the "Causes of Revolution" or "Political Decay." Understanding "System Breakdown" provides the theoretical framework for these answers.
    

**System Breakdown** (or Political Decay) occurs when a political system loses the capacity to maintain order and govern effectively. It is the precursor to major violence. Stable democracies or dictatorships do not simply explode into revolution overnight; they first go through a process of breakdown.

To understand this academically, we often use **David Easton’s Systems Theory**.

- **The Normal State:** A political system receives "Inputs" (demands/support from people) and produces "Outputs" (laws/decisions).
    
- **Breakdown:** This occurs when the "Inputs" (demands like riots, protests, economic needs) become too overwhelming for the government to process. The system overloads and crashes, much like a computer.
    

#### 1. Legitimacy Crisis

The primary cause of breakdown is a loss of **Legitimacy**.

- **Definition:** Legitimacy is the psychological feeling among the people that the government has the _right_ to rule.
    
- **The Erosion:** When a government is corrupt, ineffective, or unjust for a long time, people stop obeying the law voluntarily. They only obey out of fear. Once the fear vanishes (perhaps the police refuse to shoot protestors), the system collapses immediately.
    
- **Example:** The collapse of the Soviet Union. It had thousands of tanks, but the system had lost all legitimacy in the eyes of its people and elite, so it crumbled without a major war.
    

#### 2. Effectiveness Failure

A government must actually _work_.

- **Economic Failure:** As discussed in Political Economy, if a state cannot feed its people or stop hyperinflation (e.g., Venezuela or Weimar Germany), breakdown is inevitable.
    
- **Security Failure:** If the state loses the "monopoly on violence"—meaning criminals, warlords, or terrorists control parts of the country—the system has effectively broken down (e.g., Failed States).
    

#### 3. Huntington’s Thesis (Political Decay)

Samuel P. Huntington famously argued that violence happens when **social mobilization** outpaces **political institutionalization**.

- **The Gap:** Modernization (education, urbanization) makes people wake up and demand more (rights, money). If the political institutions (parties, courts) are too weak or slow to satisfy these new demands, the result is "Political Decay" and violence.
    

#### 4. The Role of Elites

Systems rarely break down solely due to peasant anger. They break down when the **Ruling Elite** divides.

- **Elite Fracture:** When parts of the army, business leaders, or politicians turn against the ruler, the system loses its pillars of support.

### Important Notes 📝

- **The "J-Curve" Theory:** James C. Davies argued that revolutions do not happen when things are bad, but when things are getting better and then suddenly crash. This creates an **"Intolerable Gap"** between what people _expect_ and what they _get_. This disappointment leads to breakdown.
    
- **Vacuum of Power:** Remember, revolutions act like a vacuum. They suck power into an empty space left by a collapsing state. As Theda Skocpol famously said, _"Revolutions are not made; they come."_ (Meaning they happen when the old state collapses, not just because revolutionaries are strong).
