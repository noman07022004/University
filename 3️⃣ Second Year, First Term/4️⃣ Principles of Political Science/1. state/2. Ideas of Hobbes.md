## 1.2 Ideas of Hobbes ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Thomas Hobbes is arguably the first "modern" political philosopher. His arguments establish the very logic of sovereignty. You cannot understand the concept of the modern state without grappling with his "Social Contract."
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Hobbes is an extremely frequent exam topic. Questions often ask you to explain his view of "Human Nature" and how it leads inevitably to an "Absolute Sovereign," or to compare his views with Locke's.

---

Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679) wrote his masterpiece, _Leviathan_ (1651), during the English Civil War. He witnessed chaos, beheadings, and anarchy. Consequently, his political theory is driven by a single obsession: **Order**. He asked: _How can we save humanity from its own destructive nature?_ His answer was the creation of a supreme, terrifying power—the State—which he called the **Leviathan**.

---

### 1. View of Human Nature (Psychological Egoism) 
Hobbes was a materialist. He believed humans are simply "matter in motion," driven by physical sensations.

- **Driven by Passion:** Humans are not naturally good or guided by a divine purpose. They are driven by "Appetites" (desires) and "Aversions" (fears).
    
- **The Restless Desire:** The most fundamental drive is self-preservation. However, to ensure survival, humans constantly seek more power. Hobbes famously wrote: _"I put for a general inclination of all mankind, a perpetual and restless desire of power after power, that ceaseth only in death."_
    
- **Rational but Selfish:** Humans have reason, but it is merely a tool to satisfy their desires.
    

### 2. The State of Nature 
Hobbes asks us to imagine a pre-political condition where there is no government, no laws, and no police. He calls this the **State of Nature**.

- **Equality of Ability:** Interestingly, Hobbes argues everyone is equal. Even the weakest man has the strength to kill the strongest (through secret machination or alliance). This equality breeds insecurity.
    
- **War of All Against All:** Because resources are scarce and everyone wants them (competition), and everyone fears everyone else (diffidence(দ্বিধা)), and everyone wants reputation (glory), the State of Nature is a state of constant war.
    
- **The Condition:** In this state, there is no industry, no culture, and no society. The most famous quote in political philosophy describes life here as:
    
    > _"Solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and short."_
    
- **No Morality:** In the State of Nature, nothing is "unjust." Force and fraud are the two cardinal virtues. Justice requires laws, and laws require a state.
    

### 3. The Laws of Nature and The Covenant 
Rational human beings realize that the State of Nature is a nightmare. To escape it, their reason suggests certain "Laws of Nature" (rules for survival).

- **First Law:** Seek peace and follow it.
    
- **Second Law:** Be willing to lay down your right to "everything" if others are willing to do the same.
    
- **The Social Contract (Covenant):** The only way to enforce these laws is to create a common power. Individuals agree to **surrender all their rights** and power to one man (or assembly) on one condition: that everyone else does the same.
    
    - _Formula:_ "I authorize and give up my right of governing myself, to this man... on this condition, that thou give up thy right to him, and authorize all his actions in like manner."
        

### 4. The Sovereign (The Leviathan) 
The result of this contract is the creation of the **State** (The Leviathan).

- **Nature of the Sovereign:** The ruler (Monarch or Assembly) is the "Mortal God."
    
- **Absolute Authority:** The Sovereign's power is:
    
    - **Unlimited:** Citizens cannot limit the ruler (e.g., via a constitution) because they surrendered _all_ rights to create him.
        
    - **Indivisible:** Power cannot be shared (e.g., between King and Parliament) because divided power leads to conflict and a return to the State of Nature.
        
    - **Irrevocable:** Once the contract is made, the people cannot back out. You cannot change the government.
        
- **Rights of the Subject:** The _only_ right a subject keeps is the **Right of Self-Preservation**. If the King orders you to kill yourself, you can refuse. Otherwise, obedience must be absolute.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **The "Trap" in the Contract:** Crucially, for Hobbes, the contract is **between the people themselves**, not between the people and the King. The King is a beneficiary of the contract, not a party to it. Therefore, the King _cannot_ break the contract, and the people can never accuse him of injustice.
    
- **Visualizing the Leviathan:** Look closely at the cover of his book (the image above). The Giant King's body is literally made up of hundreds of tiny people. This symbolizes that the State is an artificial person made of the citizens' will.
    
- **Why "Leviathan"?** It is a reference to a biblical sea monster (Book of Job). Hobbes uses it to symbolize a power so vast that no individual can challenge it.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Why is Hobbes' Sovereign absolute?", the answer is logic: If you limit the Sovereign's power, who enforces that limit? A judge? Then _that judge_ becomes the real Sovereign. To stop the infinite regress of conflict, there must be one final, unanswerable voice.
