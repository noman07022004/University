## 1.5 Ideas of Socialism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** To fully understand the modern state, one must understand the most powerful critique leveled against it. The socialist view challenges the "neutrality" of the state, arguing it is biased towards the rich. This perspective is essential for understanding political economy and revolutions.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is a high-yield topic. Exams often ask for "The Marxist Theory of the State" or require a comparison between the Liberal view (State as neutral referee) and the Socialist view (State as class instrument).

---
While Hobbes, Locke, and Rousseau viewed the State as a necessary creation of the "General Will" or "Consent" to benefit everyone, **Socialism** (specifically Marxism) offers a radically different perspective. Socialists argue that the Liberal view is a myth. They claim that the State does not represent the whole community; rather, it is a tool used by the dominant economic class to oppress the working class.

---

### 1. The Base and Superstructure

To understand the socialist view of the state, one must understand the Marxist concept of **Historical Materialism**.

- **The Economic Base:** This is the foundation of society—the "Mode of Production" (e.g., slavery, feudalism(সামন্ততন্ত্র), capitalism(পুঁজিবাদ)) and the relations of production (who owns the factories/land).
    
- **The Superstructure:** Everything else in society—law, religion, culture, and **The State**—is built on top of this base.
    
- **The Implication(অন্তর্নিহিত অর্থ):** The State is not an independent entity. Its form and laws are determined by the economic system. In a Capitalist economy, you inevitably get a Capitalist State.
    

### 2. The State as an Instrument of Class Rule 
In the _Communist Manifesto_ (1848), Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels famously declared:

> _"The executive of the modern state is but a committee for managing the common affairs of the whole bourgeoisie."_

- **Origin of the State:** Unlike the Social Contract theorists who said the state arose to stop chaos, Engels (in _The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State_) argued the state arose to protect **Private Property**. Once society split into "Haves" and "Have-nots," the "Haves" created the state (police, courts, prisons) to protect their wealth from the "Have-nots."
    
- **Lack of Neutrality:** The police do not exist to protect "justice"; they exist to protect the property of the rich. The law is not blind; it is written by the ruling class to serve their interests.
    

### 3. The Transition: Dictatorship of the Proletariat(সর্বহারা শ্রেণী) 
Marx did not believe the state could be simply abolished overnight (like Anarchists). Because the Capitalist class is powerful, the Working Class (Proletariat) needs a state to defeat them.

- **The Revolution:** The workers must overthrow the Bourgeoisie.
    
- **The Dictatorship of the Proletariat:** This is a temporary transitional state. The workers seize control of the state machinery.
    
    - _Purpose:_ To seize the means of production (factories/banks) from the capitalists and prevent a counter-revolution.
        
    - _Nature:_ It is still a class state (the ruling class is now the workers), using force to suppress the old exploiters.
        

### 4. The Withering Away of the State

This is the ultimate goal of orthodox Marxism/Communism.

- **End of Classes:** Once the Dictatorship of the Proletariat abolishes private property, society becomes classless.
    
- **End of Conflict:** Since the state only exists to manage class conflict, if there are no classes, there is no need for a state.
    
- **Withering Away:** The state is not "abolished"; it **"withers away"** (a phrase coined by Engels).
    
- **Administration of Things:** It is replaced by a non-political "administration of things" (managing production and resources) rather than the "governing of persons" (police/coercion).
    

### 5. Evolutionary Socialism (Social Democracy) 
It is important to note a softer branch of socialism (Bernstein, Fabians).

- **The View:** They disagree with the need for violent revolution. They believe the State is not _purely_ a tool of oppression.
    
- **The Strategy:** The working class can use **Universal Suffrage** (voting) to capture the state peacefully. Once in power, they can use the state machinery to pass reforms (welfare, minimum wage) that transform capitalism into socialism gradually. This is the model of modern Welfare States (e.g., Sweden).
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Distinction (Liberal vs. Marxist):**
    
    - _Liberal:_ State = Neutral Referee (Good).
        
    - _Marxist:_ State = Tool of Oppression (Bad).
        
- **Anarchism vs. Marxism:** Both hate the state.
    
    - _Anarchists:_ Abolish the state _immediately_ (Day 1).
        
    - _Marxists:_ Keep the state temporarily (Dictatorship of Proletariat) until it withers away.
        
- **Exam Strategy:** When writing about this, referencing **Engels' book** _The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State_ adds significant academic weight to your answer.
    
- **Reality Check:** Critics point out that in actual Communist regimes (USSR, China), the state did _not_ wither away; it became massive, totalitarian, and oppressive. This is a common critique used in essays.
