## 14. Parties ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Political parties are the "gears" of modern democracy. You cannot understand how public will is translated into government policy without understanding the vehicle that carries it.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is a critical exam topic. Questions frequently ask for the **Functions of Political Parties**, the classification of **Party Systems** (Two-Party vs. Multi-Party), or the distinction between "Cadre" and "Mass" parties.
    

---

A **Political Party** is a group of people organized to acquire and exercise political power. Unlike Interest Groups (which seek to _influence_ the government), Political Parties seek to _become_ the government. Edmund Burke famously defined a party as "a body of men united for promoting by their joint endeavors the national interest, upon some particular principle in which they are all agreed." Parties serve as the vital link between the state and the citizens, organizing the chaotic public will into coherent policy choices.

---

### 14.1 Classification of Political Parties

Before understanding what they do, we must classify what they _are_. Parties are typically classified by their structure and ideology.

- **Cadre Parties (Elite Parties):**
    
    - **Structure:** Small, loosely organized groups dominated by politically active elites (notable individuals). They have no mass membership.
        
    - **Focus:** Rely on the personal quality/reputation of leaders rather than ideology.
        
    - **Example:** Early 19th-century parties (Whigs/Tories) or some modern personalized parties.
        
- **Mass Parties:**
    
    - **Structure:** Have a huge, dues-paying membership base. They are highly organized with local branches.
        
    - **Focus:** Ideally ideological (Socialist, Religious). They aim to spread their message to the masses and mobilize the working class.
        
    - **Example:** The Labour Party (UK) or The Communist Party.
        
- **Catch-All Parties:**
    
    - **Structure:** Modern evolution of mass parties. They reduce their ideological baggage to appeal to the widest possible spectrum of voters (the "median voter").
        
    - **Focus:** Winning elections by being vague and inclusive.
        

### 14.2 Functions of Parties (And Parties in Democracies)

In a democracy, parties perform several indispensable functions that keep the system running.

1. **Interest Aggregation:** Society has thousands of conflicting demands (farmers want high food prices, consumers want low food prices). Parties "aggregate" (combine) these diverse interests into a single, manageable platform.
    
2. **Political Recruitment:** Parties find, train, and select candidates for office. They act as the "HR Department" of democracy.
    
3. **Political Socialization:** As discussed in Topic 11, parties educate voters on issues and simplify complex choices (e.g., "If you care about the environment, vote Green").
    
4. **Organizing Government:** Once elected, the party provides cohesion. It ensures that 300 individual MPs vote together to pass laws.
    
5. **The Role of Opposition:** In a democracy, the party that _loses_ is just as important as the winner. They form the "Official Opposition," scrutinizing the government's actions and offering an alternative "Government-in-waiting." This ensures accountability.
    

### 14.3 Party Systems

The "Party System" refers not to the parties themselves, but to the _interaction_ between them. It is determined by the number of parties that have a realistic chance of winning power.

1. **One-Party System:** Only one party is legally allowed to hold power. It is effectively the state itself (e.g., China, North Korea, USSR).
    
2. **Dominant-Party System:** Multiple parties are allowed to compete, but one party wins almost every election for decades due to its organizational power or popularity (e.g., LDP in Japan, ANC in South Africa).
    
3. **Two-Party System:** Two major parties dominate. One forms the government, the other the opposition. Power alternates between them. It tends to be stable but limits voter choice (e.g., USA, UK).
    
4. **Multi-Party System:** Three or more parties have the capacity to gain control of government, separately or in coalition. It offers more choice but can lead to unstable coalition governments (e.g., Germany, Italy, Israel).
    

### 14.4 Future of Party System

Is the era of the political party ending? Political scientists observe several trends suggesting a decline or transformation.

- **Partisan Dealignment:** Voters are becoming less loyal to parties. Instead of voting "Democrat because my father was a Democrat," voters now decide based on issues or candidate personality (Issue Voting).
    
- **Decline in Membership:** Mass party membership has collapsed. People prefer joining single-issue interest groups (like Greenpeace) rather than broad political parties.
    
- **Rise of Populism/Anti-Party Parties:** We see the rise of movements that claim to represent "the people" against the "corrupt parties." Paradoxically, these movements often become parties themselves (e.g., Five Star Movement in Italy).
    
- **Personalization of Politics:** With TV and Social Media, voters focus on the _Leader_ rather than the _Party Brand_.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Law - Duverger's Law:** I mentioned this in Topic 8, but it is _essential_ here.
    
    - _The Law:_ "Single-Member District electoral systems tend to produce Two-Party Systems."
        
    - _The Corollary:_ "Proportional Representation systems tend to produce Multi-Party Systems."
        
- **Exam Terminology:**
    
    - **Coalition Government:** When no single party wins 50% of the seats, two or more parties must join together to rule. This is common in Multi-Party systems.
        
    - **Whip:** A party official whose job is to ensure party discipline (making sure MPs vote the way the party leadership wants).
        
- **Iron Law of Oligarchy (Michels):** Again, relevant here. Even in "Mass Parties" that claim to be democratic, a small elite leadership group inevitably takes control.
