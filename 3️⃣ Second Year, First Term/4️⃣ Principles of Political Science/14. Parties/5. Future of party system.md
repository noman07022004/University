### 14.5 Future of Party System ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic connects historical theory to current events. It helps explain why we see the rise of populism, the decline of traditional parties, and political instability today.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Essay questions often ask: "Are political parties in decline?" or "Discuss the changing nature of party systems in the 21st century."
    

---

The final subtopic of this section addresses the **Future of the Party System**. For decades, political scientists have debated whether parties are dying (the "Crisis of Parties" thesis) or merely evolving. The traditional "Mass Party" model—with millions of loyal, dues-paying members and local branches—is undeniably fading in most advanced democracies. However, parties themselves remain essential for government.

The future of party systems is characterized by three major trends: **Dealignment**, **Cartelization**, and the rise of **Digital/Movement Parties**.

#### 1. Partisan Dealignment

The most visible trend is the weakening bond between voters and parties.

- **Decline in Identification:** In the past, people were born into "Labour" or "Conservative" families and voted that way for life. Today, voters are more independent and volatile. They vote based on specific issues or leaders rather than party loyalty.
    
- **Membership Collapse:** Parties have lost massive numbers of active members. Instead of relying on volunteers, modern parties hire professional consultants, pollsters, and media experts.
    
- **Result:** This leads to **high volatility** in elections. New parties can rise from nowhere (e.g., Macron's _En Marche_ in France), and established parties can collapse overnight.
    

#### 2. The "Cartel Party" Thesis (Katz & Mair)

If parties are losing members and money, how do they survive? Two scholars, Richard Katz and Peter Mair, proposed that parties have evolved into **"Cartel Parties."**

- **State Dependence:** Instead of relying on members for funds, parties now rely on **State Funding** (subsidies provided by the government).
    
- **Collusion:** Major parties act like a "cartel" (a monopoly group). They work together to pass laws that give them state money and media time, while making it very hard for new, smaller parties to compete.
    
- **Shift:** Parties have moved from being agents of _society_ (representing people to the state) to being agents of the _state_ (managing the people for the government).
    

#### 3. Rise of Populist and Anti-System Parties

As trust in traditional "cartel" parties falls, voters look for alternatives.

- **Anti-Establishment:** We see the rise of parties that claim to represent "the pure people" against "the corrupt elite."
    
- **Fragmentation:** Party systems are becoming more fragmented. Instead of two stable blocks, parliaments are often split among many angry, smaller parties, making coalition-building very difficult (e.g., recent elections in Israel, Spain, or Italy).
    

#### 4. Digital and "Flash" Parties

Technology is changing the structure of parties.

- **The Cyber Party:** New parties (like the _Five Star Movement_ in Italy or _Pirate Parties_ in Europe) use online platforms to let members vote directly on policies, bypassing traditional leadership structures.
    
- **Personalistic Parties:** Parties are increasingly vehicles for a single charismatic individual rather than an ideology (e.g., Trump’s influence on the GOP or Imran Khan’s PTI).
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **Dealignment vs. Realignment:**
    
    - **Dealignment:** Voters leave parties and become independent (current trend).
        
    - **Realignment:** Voters switch loyalty from one party to another permanently (e.g., the US South switching from Democrat to Republican in the 1960s).
        
- **Katz & Mair:** Mentioning the **"Cartel Party"** theory by Katz and Mair is the "gold standard" for high marks in university exams on this topic. It shows you understand the deep structural changes, not just the surface-level news.
    
- **Crisis of Legitimacy:** The key problem for the future is not that parties will disappear (we need them to run parliaments), but that they are losing their **legitimacy** and connection to the ordinary citizen.
