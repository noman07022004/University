### 10.1 Representative Democracy ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the dominant political system in the modern world. Almost every "free" country is a representative democracy. Understanding its mechanics (how the public controls the government without actually _being_ the government) is essential.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Exams often ask to distinguish between **Direct** and **Indirect** democracy, or to explain the different **Models of Representation** (Trustee vs. Delegate).
    

---

**Introduction: The Solution to Scale** A **Regime** is the set of fundamental rules and institutions that determine how a state is governed (e.g., Democracy, Dictatorship). **Representative Democracy** (also called Indirect Democracy) is a regime where citizens do not govern the state directly. Instead, they elect officials to make laws and policies on their behalf. It was developed because **Direct Democracy** (where everyone votes on every law, like in Ancient Athens) became impossible as states grew into millions of people. You cannot fit 170 million Bangladeshis into a town square to debate a tax law.

**1. The Mechanism of Indirect Rule**

In this system, there is a "Chain of Delegation":

1. **The People (Principal):** The ultimate source of authority.
    
2. **The Election:** The mechanism to transfer authority.
    
3. **The Representatives (Agent):** The Parliament/President who wields the power.
    
4. **Accountability:** The power is not permanent. If the representatives fail, the people can fire them at the next election.
    

**2. Models of Representation** One of the biggest debates in political science is: _How should a representative behave?_ There are three main theories:

- **A. The Trustee Model (The Burkean View):**
    
    - **Theorist:** Edmund Burke (MP for Bristol).
        
    - **Argument:** Voters elect a representative for their **judgment** and **wisdom**, not just to be a messenger.
        
    - **Quote:** "Your representative owes you, not his industry only, but his judgment; and he betrays, instead of serving you, if he sacrifices it to your opinion."
        
    - _Scenario:_ If voters want a law that the MP thinks is dangerous or wrong, the MP should vote _against_ the voters to protect them.
        
- **B. The Delegate Model:**
    
    - **Argument:** The representative is merely a "mouthpiece" for the people.
        
    - _Scenario:_ If 60% of the district wants Law X, the MP _must_ vote for Law X, even if he personally hates it. If he uses his own judgment, he is being undemocratic.
        
- **C. The Mandate Model (Party Model):**
    
    - **Argument:** In modern politics, people don't vote for individuals (Mr. Smith); they vote for a **Party** (AL/BNP/Democrat).
        
    - _Rule:_ The representative acts as a loyal soldier of the party. They must follow the "Party Manifesto" because that is what the people voted for.
        

**3. Liberal Democracy** Representative democracy is almost always paired with **Liberalism**, creating **Liberal Democracy**.

- **Limits on Power:** Just because representatives are elected, it doesn't mean they can do anything. They are limited by the Constitution and Human Rights.
    
- **Majority Rule, Minority Rights:** The 51% cannot use their representatives to enslave the 49%.
    
- **Pluralism:** There must be free competition between multiple political parties. A "one-party election" is not a representative democracy; it is a sham.
    

**4. Critique of Representative Democracy**

- **The Elite Problem:** Critics (like Marxists) argue that it is a "democracy for the rich." Running for office costs millions, so only the wealthy or those funded by corporations get elected.
    
- **Apathy:** Because citizens only vote once every 4-5 years, they become passive and disengaged from politics ("Political Apathy").
    
- **The Gap:** Representatives often become a professional political class (The Establishment) who live in the capital and lose touch with the struggles of ordinary people.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Distinction:**
    
    - **Direct Democracy:** Citizens vote on _Laws_ (Referendums).
        
    - **Representative Democracy:** Citizens vote on _People_ (Elections).
        
- **Exam Strategy:** If writing about the **Trustee Model**, always mention **Edmund Burke**. His speech to the Electors of Bristol (1774) is the classic text for this concept.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Bangladesh uses the **Mandate/Party Model** heavily. Under **Article 70** of the Constitution, an MP loses their seat if they vote against their party in Parliament. This creates very strong party discipline but weakens the "Trustee" independence of the MP.
