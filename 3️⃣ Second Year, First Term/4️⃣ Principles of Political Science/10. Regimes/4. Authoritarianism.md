### 10.4 Authoritarianism ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Most non-democratic countries in the world are not Totalitarian; they are Authoritarian. This is the "default" mode of human government historically. Understanding how these regimes survive without full terror (like Stalin) and without elections (like Democracy) is key to political analysis.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Exams often ask to **distinguish Authoritarianism from Totalitarianism** or to describe the **Juan Linz classification** of authoritarian regimes.
    

---

Introduction: The Politics of Obedience

Authoritarianism is a regime type that sits somewhere between Democracy and Totalitarianism.

Unlike Democracy, the people do not choose their leaders. Unlike Totalitarianism, the state does not try to control every single aspect of your life.

The social contract here is simple: "You stay out of politics, and we will let you make money and live your life."

It relies on submission to authority rather than individual freedom.

1. Juan Linz’s Definition

The Spanish sociologist Juan Linz provided the classic definition of Authoritarianism. He argued that these regimes have four distinct features:

1. **Limited Pluralism:** Unlike Totalitarianism (which allows _zero_ opposition), Authoritarian regimes might allow some independent groups (e.g., the Church, business associations, or even weak opposition parties) to exist, as long as they don't threaten the leader's power.
    
2. **Mentality, not Ideology:**
    
    - _Totalitarianism_ has an elaborate **Ideology** (e.g., Marxism: "We will build a utopia").
        
    - _Authoritarianism_ relies on a vague **Mentality** (e.g., "Patriotism," "Order," "Stability," or "Modernization"). They don't promise heaven on earth; they just promise to keep the chaos away.
        
3. **Political Apathy:** The regime wants the population to be **passive** and demobilized. They prefer citizens to watch TV and ignore politics, rather than marching in the streets (even in support).
    
4. **Ill-Defined Executive Power:** The leader's power is vast and often ignores the law, but it is not totally unlimited (restrained often by the Army or the Church).
    

5. Types of Authoritarian Regimes

Authoritarianism comes in many flavors:

- **A. Military Regimes (Juntas):**
    
    - _Rule by:_ A committee of military officers.
        
    - _Logic:_ They claim to save the nation from corrupt politicians.
        
    - _Example:_ Myanmar, Bangladesh (1982-1990 under Ershad), Egypt (currently).
        
- **B. Personalist Dictatorships:**
    
    - _Rule by:_ A single charismatic individual.
        
    - _Logic:_ Loyalty is to the _person_, not the state.
        
    - _Example:_ Saddam Hussein (Iraq), Gaddafi (Libya).
        
- **C. One-Party States:**
    
    - _Rule by:_ A single party that controls the government.
        
    - _Logic:_ The party ensures stability.
        
    - _Example:_ Vietnam, China (post-Mao).
        
- **D. Theocracy:**
    
    - _Rule by:_ Religious leaders.
        
    - _Logic:_ Divine authority.
        
    - _Example:_ Iran.
        

**3. Totalitarianism vs. Authoritarianism (The Key Distinction)**

|**Feature**|**Authoritarianism**|**Totalitarianism**|
|---|---|---|
|**Goal**|Maintain Power & Order.|Transform Society / Utopia.|
|**Citizen Role**|Passive Obedience.|Active Participation (Zeal).|
|**Control**|Political sphere only.|Public _and_ Private life.|
|**Terror**|Targeted (at enemies).|Random / Widespread.|
|**Example**|Franco's Spain, Ershad's BD.|Hitler's Germany, Kim's N. Korea.|

4. The "Hybrid Regime" (Modern Authoritarianism)

In the 21st century, pure authoritarianism is rare. Most dictators now fake democracy. This is called Competitive Authoritarianism or a Hybrid Regime.

- **The Trick:** They hold elections, but the playing field is tilted. The opposition is allowed to run, but they are harassed, denied media coverage, or jailed on fake charges.
    
- **Why?** To satisfy the international community and avoid sanctions while keeping total power.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Why is Authoritarianism unstable?", argue that it faces a **Succession Crisis**. In a democracy, when a leader dies, you have an election. In a dictatorship, when the leader dies, there is often a power struggle or civil war because there are no clear rules.
    
- **The "Dictator's Dilemma":** An authoritarian leader never knows if his people truly love him or are just faking it out of fear. This paranoia forces him to spend more and more on spies and security.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Political scientists often classify the period of **HM Ershad (1982–1990)** as a classic **Military-Authoritarian** regime. It had limited pluralism (parties existed but were weak) and relied on a "mentality" of development rather than a grand ideology.
