### 10.2 Democracy in Practice ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** It is easy to understand democracy as an _ideal_ (government by the people). It is much harder to understand democracy as a _reality_. This topic bridges the gap between the theory of the classroom and the messy reality of actual politics (lobbying, corruption, low voter turnout).
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Exams frequently ask about **Robert Dahl’s concept of "Polyarchy"** or to discuss the debate between **Pluralism** and **Elitism**.
    

---

**Introduction: Ideal vs. Real** In theory, democracy means "Rule by the People." In practice, however, 170 million people cannot rule. They are busy, often uninformed, and divided. Therefore, political scientists (specifically Robert Dahl) argue that we should not look for "perfect" democracy, which doesn't exist. Instead, we study **"Polyarchy"** (Rule by Many)—the actual, imperfect form of government found in countries like the USA, UK, or Bangladesh. "Democracy in practice" is about the institutions that make this rule possible and the power struggles that happen within them.

**1. Polyarchy: The Gold Standard**

Since "Democracy" is a vague word, Robert Dahl (the most important democratic theorist of the 20th century) created a checklist. For a country to be a "democracy in practice" (Polyarchy), it must have:

1. **Elected Officials:** Control over government decisions is constitutionally vested in elected officials.
    
2. **Free and Fair Elections:** Elections are frequent and free from coercion.
    
3. **Inclusive Suffrage:** Practically all adults have the right to vote.
    
4. **Right to Run for Office:** Practically all adults can run for election.
    
5. **Freedom of Expression:** Citizens can criticize the government without fear of punishment.
    
6. **Alternative Sources of Information:** Citizens can get news from sources other than the government (Free Press).
    
7. **Associational Autonomy:** Citizens can form parties and interest groups (Civil Society).
    

**2. Who Actually Rules? (Elitism vs. Pluralism)** Even if a country has elections, who _really_ holds the power? There are two competing theories:

- **A. Pluralism (The Optimistic View):**
    
    - **Theory:** Power is dispersed among many different groups (unions, business, farmers, students).
        
    - **Mechanism:** No single group dominates. If Big Business tries to pollute, the Environmentalists fight back. The government acts as a "neutral referee" or broker between these competing groups.
        
    - **Conclusion:** Democracy works because different voices balance each other out.
        
- **B. Elite Theory (The Cynical View):**
    
    - **Theory:** C. Wright Mills argued that democracy is a sham. Real power is held by a tiny **Power Elite** (corporate CEOs, top generals, key politicians).
        
    - **Mechanism:** Voters choose between "Candidate A" (funded by the rich) and "Candidate B" (funded by the rich). The outcome is the same.
        
    - **Conclusion:** Democracy in practice is just a mask for oligarchy (rule by the few).
        

**3. The Iron Law of Oligarchy** Sociologist Robert Michels formulated the **"Iron Law of Oligarchy."**

- **The Law:** * "Who says organization, says oligarchy."*
    
- **Explanation:** As soon as you organize a democratic party (e.g., the Labour Party), you need leaders to run it. Over time, these leaders become experts/bureaucrats who want to stay in power. They detach from the ordinary members.
    
- **Result:** Even democratic parties eventually become ruled by a small elite at the top.
    

**4. Challenges in Practice** Real democracies face issues that theory doesn't always predict:

- **Short-Termism:** Politicians only care about the next election (4-5 years away). They rarely tackle long-term problems like climate change or national debt because the solutions are painful.
    
- **Tyranny of the Majority:** The majority can democratically vote to oppress a minority (e.g., banning a minority religion).
    
- **Voter Apathy:** In many stable democracies, citizens are so comfortable or cynical that they stop voting (low turnout), leaving power to the motivated extremes.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Term - "Illiberal Democracy":** A modern phenomenon (coined by Fareed Zakaria). This is a state that has _elections_ (Democracy) but lacks _rights_ (Liberalism). The leader wins the vote but then crushes the press and courts (e.g., Hungary, Turkey).
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Is Democracy possible?", use **Dahl’s Polyarchy**. Argue that while _ideal_ democracy is impossible, _Polyarchy_ is a measurable, achievable standard.
    
- **Relevance:** Think about Bangladesh. It has elections (Point 1-4 of Polyarchy), but political scientists often debate the health of its "Alternative Information" or "Associational Autonomy" (Points 6-7).
