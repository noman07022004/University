### 5.2 Rights of Women ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** For centuries, "Rights of Man" literally meant _males_. Political Science historically ignored half the human race. Understanding how this exclusion happened—and how it was challenged—is essential for a complete view of democracy.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A significant topic. Exams often ask about **Mary Wollstonecraft**, the concept of the **"Public vs. Private Sphere,"** or **J.S. Mill’s arguments** against the subjection of women.
    

---

**Introduction: The Great Omission** Classic political theory (from Aristotle to Rousseau) claimed to speak for "Humanity," but it systemically excluded women.

- **The Assumption:** Women were viewed as "naturally" different: emotional, irrational, and suited only for the domestic sphere.
    
- **The Contradiction:** When Liberals (like Locke) argued that "All men are born free and equal," they had to explain why women were _not_ free. They justified this by claiming women were not fully rational "citizens" but rather dependents of their husbands.
    
- **The Goal:** The movement for Women's Rights (Feminism) sought to prove that women are rational human beings entitled to the same **Natural Rights** as men.
    

**1. The Public vs. Private Sphere**

The biggest barrier to women's rights was the ideological separation of life into two spheres.

- **The Public Sphere (Polis):** The world of politics, work, war, and reason. This was the domain of **Men**.
    
- **The Private Sphere (Oikos):** The world of the home, family, emotion, and child-rearing. This was the domain of **Women**.
    
- **The Trap:** Liberal theorists argued the State should only interfere in the _Public_ sphere. The _Private_ sphere was "sacred" and off-limits. This meant that marital rape, domestic violence, and financial control were considered "private family matters," not political crimes.
    

**2. Key Thinkers and Arguments**

**A. Mary Wollstonecraft (1759–1797)** The "Mother of Feminism." In her groundbreaking book, _A Vindication of the Rights of Woman_ (1792), she argued:

- **Reason, not Gender:** Women appear "silly" or "irrational" not because of nature, but because of a lack of **Education**. If you treat a person like a doll, they will act like a doll.
    
- **The Argument:** If women are educated like men, they will be as rational and virtuous as men. Therefore, they deserve the same rights.
    

**B. John Stuart Mill & Harriet Taylor** J.S. Mill, influenced by his wife Harriet Taylor, wrote _The Subjection of Women_ (1869).

- **The Critique of Custom:** He argued that the subordination of women was a relic of the "Law of the Strongest" (barbarism) and had no place in a modern civilized society.
    
- **The "Hothouse" Analogy:** Critics said women were naturally weaker. Mill replied: You don't know what women are "naturally" like because you have never let them grow freely. You have kept them in a "hothouse" of oppressive social rules.
    
- **Utility:** He used a Utilitarian argument: By suppressing women, society wastes the talent of half the human race.
    

**3. The Fight for Legal Rights (First Wave Feminism)** The early rights movement focused on three main legal pillars:

1. **Suffrage (The Vote):** The symbol of citizenship. Without the vote, women were "political minors."
    
2. **Property Rights:** Under the legal doctrine of _Coverture_, a woman's legal rights were subsumed by her husband upon marriage. She could not own property, sign contracts, or keep her own wages. The struggle was to be recognized as a distinct "legal person."
    
3. **Education:** The right to attend universities and enter professions (medicine, law).
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Slogan - "The Personal is Political":** This later feminist slogan (from the 1960s) attacks the Public/Private divide. It argues that what happens _inside_ the home (who does the dishes, who raises the kids, domestic abuse) is a political power dynamic that the state _must_ address.
    
- **Rousseau's Sexism:** Be aware that Rousseau (the champion of freedom) was a deep sexist. He argued women should be educated only to please men. Wollstonecraft wrote her book specifically to attack Rousseau's view.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Why is the exclusion of women a problem for Liberalism?", the answer is **Inconsistency**. Liberalism is based on "Individualism" (everyone is an individual), but it treated women as a "Group" (defined by their sex, not their character).
