## 5. Individualism, Justice and Feminism ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This topic critiques the previous ones. It challenges the assumption that the "rational individual" (central to Hobbes, Locke, and Mill) is the best way to understand politics. It introduces critical perspectives that are essential for modern political analysis.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Questions often focus on the feminist critique of the state (specifically the public/private divide) or the debate between "Individualists" (Liberals) and "Communitarians" regarding the nature of justice.

---
Classic political theory (like that of Locke or Mill) is built on Individualism—the idea that the primary unit of society is the independent human being, and the state exists to protect their rights. However, this view has been challenged by "Anti-individualist" theories which argue that humans are inherently social and cannot be understood in isolation. This section explores this tension and applies it to Feminism. Feminism begins by demanding equal rights for women within the individualist framework (Liberal Feminism) but eventually moves to transcend that framework, arguing that the "abstract individual" of political theory is actually a man, ignoring the unique realities of women's lives.

---

### 5.1 Individualism and Anti-Individualism

This is the central debate in contemporary political philosophy about the nature of the "Self."

- **Individualism (The Liberal View):**
    
    - **Concept:** Society is just a collection of distinct individuals. The individual is prior to society.
        
    - **The Unencumbered Self:** Thinkers like Rawls or Locke view the self as capable of stepping back from their social roles to make rational choices.
        
    - **Goal:** The state should be neutral and protect individual rights/liberties.
        
- **Anti-Individualism (The Communitarian View):**
    
    - **Concept:** You cannot understand a person without their social context. We are "constituted" by our community, language, and culture.
        
    - **Critique:** Communitarians (like Michael Sandel) argue that the "unencumbered self" is a myth. We are born into "encumbered" roles (family, nation, religion) that we did not choose but which define our obligations.
        
    - **Goal:** The state should not be neutral; it should promote the "Common Good" and civic virtue, not just protect private rights.
        

### 5.2 Rights of Women (Liberal Feminism)

The first phase of feminism sought to apply the principles of Individualism to women.

- **Mary Wollstonecraft:** In _A Vindication of the Rights of Woman_ (1792), she argued against the idea that women were naturally irrational or inferior. She claimed women appeared that way only because they were denied education.
    
- **The Argument:** If all "individuals" have natural rights (as Locke said), and women are human individuals, then women must have the same rights as men.
    
- **Key Demands:**
    
    - **Suffrage:** The right to vote.
        
    - **Legal Personhood:** The right to own property and sign contracts (which married women previously lost under the doctrine of _coverture_).
        
    - **Education:** Access to universities and professions.
        
- **Limit:** This approach accepts the existing political system but demands entry into it.
    

### 5.3 Transcending Liberal Individualism (Radical and Socialist Feminism)

Later feminist thinkers argued that simply giving women "equal rights" is not enough because the entire system of "Liberal Individualism" is biased against them.

- **The "Public/Private" Divide:** Liberalism divides life into the **Public Sphere** (government, work, politics) and the **Private Sphere** (family, home). It protects rights in the public sphere but ignores the private sphere.
    
- **The Critique:** Feminists argue that women are oppressed in the _private_ sphere (domestic violence, unpaid labor, child-rearing), which liberalism ignores.
    
- **"The Personal is Political":** This famous slogan means that power relationships exist inside the home, not just in parliament.
    
- **Transcending the "Abstract Individual":**
    
    - Liberal theory assumes the citizen is an independent, rational adult.
        
    - Feminists point out that this ignores the reality of **dependency**. Everyone starts as a helpless child and may become a dependent elder. The work of caring for these dependents (Care Work) traditionally falls to women.
        
    - **Justice:** True justice requires valuing "Care" as much as "Rights," and restructuring society so that the burden of domestic work doesn't prevent women from participating in the public sphere.
        

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Text:** **Mary Wollstonecraft's** _A Vindication of the Rights of Woman_ (1792) is the foundational text for Liberal Feminism.
    
- **Exam Terminology:**
    
    - **Glass Ceiling:** The invisible barrier preventing women from reaching the highest levels of leadership despite having legal rights.
        
    - **Second Shift:** The phenomenon where women work a full-time job and then come home to perform a "second shift" of housework and childcare.
        
- **Distinction:**
    
    - _Liberal Feminism:_ "Give us the same rights as men." (Equality of Opportunity).
        
    - _Radical Feminism:_ "Change the system because it is patriarchal." (Challenge the structure).
