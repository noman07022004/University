### 9.2 Constitutional Supremacy ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the bedrock principle of modern democracy in countries like the USA and Bangladesh. It establishes that the government is not God; the Law is God. Understanding this explains why courts can strike down laws passed by powerful parliaments.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A critical exam topic. Questions frequently ask to distinguish between **Constitutional Supremacy** and **Parliamentary Supremacy**, or to discuss **Article 7** of the Bangladesh Constitution.
    

---

**Introduction: The Hierarchy of Laws** In a legal system, not all laws are equal. Imagine a pyramid. At the very top sits the **Constitution**. Below it are laws passed by Parliament (Statutes), and below those are local bylaws. **Constitutional Supremacy** is the doctrine that the Constitution is the highest law of the land ("The Supreme Law").

- **The Rule:** If _any_ ordinary law, executive order, or government action contradicts the Constitution, that law is **void** (illegal and invalid) to the extent of the inconsistency. The Constitution always wins.
    

**1. The "Grundnorm" Theory** The Austrian jurist **Hans Kelsen** described the legal system as a hierarchy of norms.

- **Grundnorm (Basic Norm):** The Constitution is the "Grundnorm"—the source of all other legal authority.
    
- **Logic:** Parliament has the power to make laws only because the Constitution _gives_ it that power. Therefore, the creator (Constitution) is superior to the creation (Parliament).
    

**2. Constitutional Supremacy vs. Parliamentary Supremacy**

This is the major divide in democratic theory.

- **A. Constitutional Supremacy (The American/Bangladeshi Model):**
    
    - **The Boss:** The Constitution.
        
    - **Limit:** Parliament is **limited**. It cannot pass a law that violates Fundamental Rights (e.g., banning free speech). If it does, the Courts will cancel it.
        
    - **Judicial Review:** The Judiciary is very powerful because it acts as the "Guardian of the Constitution."
        
    - **Examples:** USA, Bangladesh, India.
        
- **B. Parliamentary Supremacy (The British Model):**
    
    - **The Boss:** The Parliament.
        
    - **No Limit:** Parliament is **sovereign**. It can legally pass _any_ law it wants (e.g., "All blue-eyed people must pay double tax"). No court can strike down an Act of Parliament.
        
    - **Role of Courts:** Courts can only _interpret_ the law, not invalidate it.
        
    - **Example:** United Kingdom. (Though this is changing slightly due to human rights laws).
        

**3. Judicial Review: The Enforcement Mechanism** Constitutional supremacy would be a meaningless slogan without a mechanism to enforce it. That mechanism is **Judicial Review**.

- **Definition:** The power of the High Court/Supreme Court to review the actions of the Legislature and Executive and declare them unconstitutional.
    
- **Origin:** This principle was firmly established in the famous US Supreme Court case **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**. Chief Justice John Marshall argued: "A law repugnant to the Constitution is void."
    

**4. The Bangladesh Context (Article 7)** For students of Bangladeshi political science, **Article 7** of the Constitution is the most important article to memorize. It explicitly enshrines Constitutional Supremacy:

> _"All powers in the Republic belong to the people... This Constitution is the solemn expression of the will of the people... if any other law is inconsistent with this Constitution that other law shall, to the extent of the inconsistency, be void."_

- **Meaning:** In Bangladesh, Parliament is _not_ sovereign; the Constitution is.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Exam Quote:** Memorize this phrase: **"The Constitution is the yardstick by which all other laws are measured."**
    
- **The "Basic Structure" Doctrine:** In many countries (India, Bangladesh), the Courts have ruled that Parliament cannot even _amend_ the Constitution if that amendment destroys its "Basic Structure" (e.g., democracy, independence of judiciary). This is the ultimate form of Constitutional Supremacy—even the amendment power has limits.
    
- **Connection:** This topic connects directly to **Topic 19 (Judiciaries)** because the Judiciary is the protector of supremacy.
