## 3.1 Introduction to the Ruler ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Before analyzing specific theories (like Plato or Rousseau), one must understand the fundamental question of political science: _Who should rule?_ This section sets the framework for classifying political systems based on the number and quality of rulers.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** While rarely a standalone essay question, understanding the distinction between "Rule by Law" and "Rule by Man," or **Aristotle's Classification of Constitutions**, is essential for answering questions in later sections.
    

---

### 1. The Concept of Rulership

In Topics 1 and 2, we discussed the abstract "State." Topic 3 focuses on the concrete machinery that operates it: the Ruler (or Government).

- **The Definition:** The Ruler is the individual or group that exercises the sovereign power of the state. They act as the "pilot" of the ship of state.
    
- **The Central Question:** Political philosophy has always struggled with two distinct questions regarding the ruler:
    
    1. **Descriptive:** Who _actually_ rules? (e.g., The rich? The military? The people?)
        
    2. **Normative:** Who _ought_ to rule? (e.g., The wisest? The strongest? The majority?)
        

### 2. Aristotle's Classification of Rulers

The most enduring framework for understanding rulers comes from **Aristotle** (384–322 BC). He classified governments based on two criteria:

1. **Quantity:** How many people hold power? (One, Few, or Many).
    
2. **Quality:** In whose interest do they rule? (Public Interest = "Normal/Good"; Self Interest = "Perverted/Bad").
    

|**Number of Rulers**|**Normal Form (Rules for the Common Good)**|**Perverted Form (Rules for Self-Interest)**|
|---|---|---|
|**One**|**Monarchy** (Rule by a virtuous King)|**Tyranny** (Rule by a selfish Dictator)|
|**Few**|**Aristocracy** (Rule by the Best/Wise)|**Oligarchy** (Rule by the Rich)|
|**Many**|**Polity** (Rule by the Middle Class/Citizens)|**Democracy** (Rule by the Poor/Mob Rule)|

- _Note on Democracy:_ Aristotle actually considered "Democracy" a _bad_ thing (Mob Rule). He preferred "Polity." In modern times, what we call "Democracy" is actually what Aristotle called "Polity."
    

### 3. The Evolution of Rulership

The concept of the ruler has evolved through history:

- **The God-King (Theocratic):** In ancient times (e.g., Egypt), the ruler was seen as divine or appointed by God. Disobedience was a sin.
    
- **The Absolute Monarch (Hobbesian):** In the medieval/early modern era, the ruler was the supreme owner of the state. "L'État, c'est moi" ("I am the State") – Louis XIV.
    
- **The Representative (Modern):** In the modern era, the "ruler" is no longer a person but an **Office** (e.g., The Presidency). The person holding the office is merely a temporary servant of the law, not the master of the people.
    

### 4. The Iron Law of Oligarchy

A critical concept to introduce here is Robert Michels' theory. He argued that no matter what the official title is (Democracy, Socialism, etc.), every organization is eventually ruled by a small elite group.

- **The Law:** _"Who says organization, says oligarchy."_
    
- **Implication:** Even in a democracy, the "people" don't really rule; a small political class (party leaders, donors) actually pulls the levers.
