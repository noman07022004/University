### 12.3 Polls ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** In modern politics, nobody guesses what the public thinks; they measure it. Understanding how a survey of just 1,000 people can accurately predict the views of 170 million is essential for understanding political strategy and media reports.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A frequent topic. Exams often ask to distinguish between **Straw Polls** and **Scientific Polls**, or to explain concepts like **Random Sampling** and **Margin of Error**.
    

---

**Introduction: The Thermometer of Democracy** If Public Opinion is the "temperature" of the body politic, the **Poll** is the thermometer. An opinion poll is a survey of public opinion from a particular sample. It is the scientific method of quantifying the "Will of the People." Before polls, leaders relied on riots or letters to guess what people wanted. Today, they get daily data reports.

**1. The Science of Sampling**

The magic of polling lies in **Sampling Theory**. You do not need to ask everyone (the "Universe") to know the result. You only need a small, representative slice.

- **The Universe:** The total group you want to study (e.g., all voters in Bangladesh).
    
- **The Sample:** The small group you actually interview (e.g., 1,000 people).
    
- **Randomness:** The golden rule. Every person in the universe must have an equal chance of being selected. If you only interview people at a shopping mall, the poll is garbage because it excludes poor people who don't shop there.
    
- **The Magic Number:** Surprisingly, a random sample of just **1,000 to 1,500 people** can accurately predict the opinion of millions, usually within a +/- 3% margin of error.
    

**2. Types of Polls** Political scientists distinguish between scientific and non-scientific polls.

- **A. Straw Polls (Unscientific):**
    
    - **Method:** Asking whoever is around or whoever volunteers (e.g., a TV channel asking viewers to SMS their vote, or an internet click poll).
        
    - **Reliability:** **Zero.** It suffers from "Self-Selection Bias" (only angry or motivated people bother to vote). It does not represent the public.
        
- **B. Scientific Polls (Representative):**
    
    - **Method:** Using mathematical random sampling (e.g., random digit dialing).
        
    - **Reliability:** High. This is what professional agencies (like Gallup or Nielsen) use.
        
- **C. Exit Polls:**
    
    - **Method:** Interviewing voters _immediately_ after they leave the polling station.
        
    - **Purpose:** To predict the election result before the official count is finished. They are usually very accurate because they ask people who actually voted.
        
- **D. Tracking Polls:**
    
    - **Method:** Asking the same questions every day/week to track changes over time.
        
    - **Purpose:** To see if a campaign speech or scandal helped or hurt a candidate.
        

**3. Assessing Accuracy (The Math)** When reading a poll, you must look for two numbers:

- **Margin of Error:** No poll is perfect. A margin of +/- 3% means if the poll says "50% support Party A," the real number is somewhere between 47% and 53%.
    
- **Confidence Level:** Usually 95%. It means "We are 95% sure the real answer falls within this margin."
    

**4. Problems and Limitations** Even scientific polls can fail (as seen in the 2016 US Election).

- **Wording Bias:** The way you ask the question changes the answer.
    
    - _Question A:_ "Do you support killing murderers?" (Yes: 60%)
        
    - _Question B:_ "Do you support the death penalty?" (Yes: 50%)
        
- **The Bradley Effect (Social Desirability Bias):** People lie to pollsters to look "good." For example, they might say they will vote for a minority candidate to not seem racist, but then vote against him in private.
    
- **Non-Response Bias:** Modern people stop answering unknown phone calls. If only old people answer the phone, the poll will be biased toward old people's views.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Figure:** **George Gallup** is the "father of modern polling." He famously predicted the 1936 US election correctly using only 5,000 people, while a magazine using 2 million people (Straw Poll) got it wrong.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Polling is still a developing field in Bangladesh. Due to political polarization and fear (people afraid to speak the truth to strangers), poll results in Bangladesh are often viewed with skepticism compared to Western democracies.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Are polls good for democracy?", present a balanced view.
    
    - _Pro:_ They tell leaders what the people really want.
        
    - _Con:_ They turn elections into a "Horse Race" (who is winning) rather than a debate on issues.
