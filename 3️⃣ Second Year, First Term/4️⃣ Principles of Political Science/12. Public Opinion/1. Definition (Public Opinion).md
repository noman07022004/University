### 12.1 Definition of Public Opinion ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** In a democracy, the government is supposed to follow the "Will of the People." But what _is_ that will? Is it what the majority wants? Is it what the loud protesters want? Defining "Public Opinion" is the first step to understanding democratic legitimacy.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A standard short question. Exams frequently ask for the definition by **V.O. Key** or **Lord Bryce** and to list the **characteristics** of public opinion.
    

---

**Introduction: The Voice of the People** Public Opinion is one of the most slippery concepts in political science. It is not a single, unified voice (like a chorus singing in perfect harmony). It is a messy, conflicting collection of individual views. However, in a democracy, it is the ultimate source of power. If the public turns against a government, that government falls. Therefore, political scientists define it as the aggregate of individual attitudes or beliefs shared by some portion of the adult population.

**1. Classic Definitions** To write a good answer, you must quote the giants of the field.

- **V.O. Key (The Realist Definition):**
    
    - _"Public opinion is those opinions held by private persons which governments find it prudent to heed."_
        
    - _Meaning:_ Key argues that opinion only matters if the government is afraid of it. If you have an opinion but keep it secret, it is not "Public Opinion." It must be powerful enough to force the government to listen.
        
- **Lord Bryce (The Idealist Definition):**
    
    - _"The aggregate of the views men hold regarding matters that affect or interest the community."_
        
    - _Meaning:_ This focuses on the _collective_ nature of the opinion regarding public issues.
        

**2. The Three Components** Public opinion is not just "what people think." It has three specific dimensions that analysts measure:

1. **Direction:**
    
    - Is the public **For** or **Against** a specific policy? (e.g., "Do you support the death penalty?" Yes/No).
        
2. **Intensity:**
    
    - How **strongly** do they feel about it?
        
    - _Example:_ 60% of people might support a law, but if they don't care much, the government might ignore them. But if the 40% who oppose it are willing to riot in the streets (High Intensity), the government will listen to the minority.
        
3. **Salience (Relevance):**
    
    - How **important** is this issue right now?
        
    - _Example:_ People might have an opinion on "Space Exploration," but it has low salience compared to "Inflation," which affects their daily lives.
        

**3. "The Public" vs. "The Mob"** Political scientists distinguish between different types of "Publics":

- **The General Public:** Everyone in the country. (Usually uninformed and uninterested).
    
- **The Attentive Public:** The minority (maybe 15-20%) who actually follow the news and know what is going on.
    
- **The Policy Elite:** The tiny group who actively shape the opinion (journalists, politicians).
    
- _Theory:_ "Public Opinion" is usually just the opinion of the **Attentive Public**, not the whole population.
    

**4. Consensus vs. Divisive Opinion**

- **Consensus Opinion:** When almost everyone agrees on a fundamental value (e.g., "Democracy is good," "Murder is bad"). This holds the country together.
    
- **Divisive Opinion:** When the public is sharply split (e.g., "Abortion," "Tax rates"). This is where politics happens.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Concept - "The Phantom Public":** Walter Lippmann, a famous journalist, argued that "Public Opinion" is a myth. He said most people are too busy to understand complex issues, so what we call "Public Opinion" is actually just people reacting to simple slogans created by elites.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** When defining it, emphasize that it is **Dynamic** (it changes constantly). What was unpopular yesterday (e.g., gay marriage) might be popular today.
    
- **The Requirement of Expression:** Remember, an unexpressed opinion is just a "private thought." To be _Public Opinion_, it must be expressed (through voting, polls, protests, or letters).
