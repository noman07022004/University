### 7.4 Nationalism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Nationalism is arguably the most successful political ideology in history. It created the modern world map (the "Nation-State"). Understanding it is essential to understand why countries exist, why wars are fought, and the difference between "Patriotism" and "Nationalism."
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A critical exam topic. Questions frequently ask to define **"Nation" vs. "State,"** to explain **Benedict Anderson’s "Imagined Communities,"** or to distinguish between **Civic** and **Ethnic** Nationalism.
    

---

**Introduction: The Ideology of the Nation-State** While Liberalism focuses on the _Individual_ and Socialism on the _Class_, **Nationalism** focuses on the **Nation**. It is the belief that the world is divided into distinct nations, each with its own character and destiny, and that the only legitimate form of government is **Self-Determination** (each nation should rule itself in its own state). It turns cultural identity (language, religion, history) into political power.

**1. What is a Nation?** Nationalism relies on the definition of a "Nation." Political scientists distinguish it from a "State."

- **The State:** A political/legal entity with a government, territory, and sovereignty (e.g., The UK Government).
    
- **The Nation:** A psycho-cultural entity. A group of people bound together by shared language, religion, history, or culture (e.g., The Scottish people, the Kurdish people).
    
- **The Nation-State:** The ideal goal of nationalism—where the cultural boundaries match the political boundaries (e.g., Japan, where the Japanese people govern the Japanese territory).
    

**2. Core Themes of Nationalism**

- **A. Organic Community (Primordialism):**
    
    - Nationalists view the nation not as a random group of people, but as an ancient, organic family.
        
    - _Metaphor:_ The nation is like a "Super-Family." You don't choose it; you are born into it. You owe it your loyalty and sacrifice (even your life in war) because it is your extended self.
        
- **B. Self-Determination:**
    
    - The core political demand. No nation should be ruled by foreigners.
        
    - _Rousseau’s Influence:_ Nationalism applies the idea of "General Will" to the nation. The people (the nation) must be the sovereign.
        
- **C. Identity Politics:**
    
    - Nationalism answers the question: _Who am I?_ It provides a sense of belonging and rootedness in a confusing modern world.
        

**3. Types of Nationalism**

This is the most important distinction for exams.

- **Civic Nationalism (Liberal/Inclusive):**
    
    - **Basis:** Citizenship and Loyalty to the Constitution.
        
    - **Membership:** Anyone can join if they accept the values. It is "blind" to race/religion.
        
    - **Example:** USA, France ("To be French is to accept the Republic").
        
    - **Philosophy:** Rational and political.
        
- **Ethnic Nationalism (Conservative/Exclusive):**
    
    - **Basis:** Blood, Heritage, Language, and Religion.
        
    - **Membership:** You are born into it. You cannot "become" a member just by signing a paper.
        
    - **Example:** Germany (historically), Japan, or religious nationalisms.
        
    - **Philosophy:** Emotional and mystical.
        

**4. Theories of Origin (When did it start?)**

- **Primordialists:** Believe nations are ancient (people have always been "French" or "Chinese").
    
- **Modernists (Benedict Anderson):**
    
    - In his famous book _Imagined Communities_ (1983), Anderson argued that nations are **modern inventions**.
        
    - **The Argument:** Before the printing press and mass literacy, people didn't know they belonged to a "Nation." They only knew their local village.
        
    - **Imagined Community:** The nation is "imagined" because you will never meet most of your fellow members, yet you feel a deep camaraderie with them. It was constructed by newspapers, maps, and museums to create political unity for the modern state.
        

**5. The Two Faces of Nationalism**

- **The Liberator:** In the 19th/20th century, nationalism was a force for freedom (Anti-Colonialism). It helped India, Bangladesh, and Vietnam break free from Empires.
    
- **The Destroyer:** It can also lead to Xenophobia (fear of foreigners), ethnic cleansing, and war (WWI/WWII). When one nation claims superiority over others ("My country, right or wrong"), it becomes **Chauvinism**.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Quote:** **"Nationalism is the belief that the political and the national unit should be congruent."** (Ernest Gellner). Memorize this definition.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Is Nationalism backward?", use the **Modernist** argument to show it is actually a product of _modernity_ (industrialization/democracy), not ancient tribalism.
    
- **Relevance:** The 1971 Liberation War of Bangladesh is a classic example of **Linguistic/Ethnic Nationalism** transforming into political **Self-Determination**.
