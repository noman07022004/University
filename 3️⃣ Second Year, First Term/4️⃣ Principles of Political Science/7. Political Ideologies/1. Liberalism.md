### 7.1 Liberalism ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Liberalism is the default operating system of the modern Western world and most democracies. It is impossible to understand contemporary politics, human rights, or economics without grasping Liberalism.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A frequent exam topic. Questions often ask to distinguish between **Classical** and **Modern** Liberalism or to discuss its core values (Liberty, Individualism, Tolerance).
    

---

**Introduction: The Ideology of the Individual** Liberalism (from the Latin _liber_, meaning "free") is an ideology based on the paramount value of individual liberty. It emerged in the 17th and 18th centuries (The Enlightenment) as a reaction against absolute monarchy and religious dogma. Its central goal is to give individuals as much freedom as possible to pursue their own lives, limited only by the need to protect the freedom of others.

**1. Core Values of Liberalism**

- **Individualism:** The individual is more important than the collective (State, Church, Class). Society exists to serve the individual, not the other way around.
    
- **Freedom/Liberty:** The primary political value. Every person should be free to think, speak, worship, and trade as they please.
    
- **Reason:** Liberals believe the world has a rational structure that humans can understand through logic and science (Rejection of blind faith/tradition).
    
- **Equality:** All humans are born equal in moral worth ("Foundational Equality") and should have equal legal rights ("Formal Equality").
    
- **Toleration:** A willingness to accept views and lifestyles with which one disagrees (Voltaire: "I disapprove of what you say, but I will defend to the death your right to say it").
    
- **Consent:** Government authority comes from the people (Social Contract), not from God or history.
    

**2. Classical Liberalism (17th–19th Century)**

This is the original form of liberalism (Locke, Adam Smith, Early Mill).

- **View of the State:** The state is a "Necessary Evil." It should be minimal ("Nightwatchman State"). Its _only_ job is to protect life, liberty, and property.
    
- **Economy:** Total Laissez-faire capitalism. No welfare, no regulations, no minimum wage. The market regulates itself.
    
- **Philosophy:** **Negative Liberty** (Freedom _from_ interference). If the government leaves you alone, you are free.
    
- **View of Human Nature:** Humans are rational, self-reliant, and capable. If someone is poor, it is likely due to their own lack of effort (Social Darwinism).
    

**3. Modern (Social) Liberalism (Late 19th Century–Present)** This form emerged when liberals realized that the "free market" was creating massive poverty and inequality (Industrial Revolution). Thinkers like **T.H. Green** and **John Rawls** revised the ideology.

- **View of the State:** The state is a "Necessary Good." It should play an enabling role.
    
- **Economy:** Regulated capitalism (Keynesianism). The state should manage the economy, provide welfare (health, education), and tax the rich to help the poor.
    
- **Philosophy:** **Positive Liberty** (Freedom _to_ act). You are not "free" if you are starving or illiterate. The state must provide the _capacity_ to be free.
    
- **View of Human Nature:** Humans are social and can be held back by structural injustice. Poverty is not always the individual's fault.
    

**4. Neoliberalism (The Counter-Revolution)** In the 1980s (Reagan/Thatcher), there was a return to Classical Liberal economic ideas, known as Neoliberalism.

- **Core Belief:** The "Nanny State" (Modern Liberalism) has become too big and inefficient.
    
- **Policy:** Privatization, deregulation, tax cuts, and free trade. Return to the "invisible hand."
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Distinction:**
    
    - **Classical Liberal:** "The government that governs _least_ governs best."
        
    - **Modern Liberal:** "The government must help people help themselves."
        
- **Confusion Alert:** In the USA, "Liberal" usually means Left-wing (Modern Liberal/Democrat). In Europe/Australia, "Liberal" often means Right-wing (Classical Liberal/Free Market). In Political Science, we use the broad definition covering both.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Is Liberalism coherent?", argue that despite the split (Classical vs. Modern), both sides agree on the **primacy of the individual** and **Reason**; they just disagree on the _means_ (State vs. Market).
