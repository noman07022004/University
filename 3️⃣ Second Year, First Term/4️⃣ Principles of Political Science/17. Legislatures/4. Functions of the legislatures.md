### 17.4 Functions of the Legislatures ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The legislature is the "engine room" of democracy. Understanding what it _actually_ does (versus what it is _supposed_ to do) is crucial for analyzing the gap between democratic theory and reality.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a staple exam question. You are very likely to see questions like "Discuss the main functions of a modern legislature" or "Is the power of the legislature declining in the modern state?"
    

---

While the primary image of a legislature is a room full of people debating laws, its role extends far beyond merely voting on bills. In modern political science, the **Functions of the Legislature** are categorized into law-making, financial control, oversight, and representation.

#### 1. Law-Making (Legislation)

This is the most obvious function. In every democracy, the legislature is the only body with the legal authority to enact **statutes** (laws).

- **The Process:** It involves drafting, debating, amending, and voting on bills.
    
- **Modern Reality (Executive Dominance):** It is important to note for your exams that in modern times, legislatures rarely _write_ the laws. Most bills are drafted by the **Executive** (bureaucrats and ministers) and presented to the legislature for approval. The legislature acts more as a "refining" body than an "originating" body.
    

#### 2. Control of the Executive (Oversight)

In a system of checks and balances, the legislature must ensure the government does not abuse its power.

- **Question Hour:** In parliamentary systems, ministers must face the legislature daily to answer questions about their performance.
    
- **Committees:** Specialized groups of legislators (e.g., the Defense Committee) investigate specific government departments, summon witnesses, and audit spending.
    
- **Vote of No Confidence:** In parliamentary systems, this is the "nuclear option." The legislature can fire the entire executive branch if it loses trust in them.
    

#### 3. Financial Function (Power of the Purse)

History tells us this is the oldest function. The executive cannot spend a single penny or collect a single tax without legislative approval.

- **The Budget:** Every year, the government must present a budget. The legislature debates the allocation of resources (e.g., how much for health vs. military). If the budget is rejected, the government effectively stops functioning.
    

#### 4. Representation

Legislators act as the voice of the people.

- **Trustee vs. Delegate:**
    
    - **Trustee Model (Edmund Burke):** The legislator uses their own judgment to do what is best for the nation, even if their constituents disagree.
        
    - **Delegate Model:** The legislator acts strictly as a mouthpiece, voting exactly how their constituents want.
        
- **Ventilation of Grievances:** The legislature is a forum where the problems of the common people are voiced and brought to the attention of the rulers.
    

#### 5. Constituent Service (Casework)

This is the function most visible to the average citizen. Legislators act as "social workers" for their voters.

- **Ombudsman Role:** If a citizen is having trouble with the bureaucracy (e.g., a lost pension check or a visa issue), they contact their MP/Congressman to cut through the red tape.
    

#### 6. Legitimization

This is a subtle but vital function. By debating and voting on government policies, the legislature gives them "moral authority."

- **Consent:** When a law passes through parliament, it signals that the "people have consented." This makes citizens more willing to obey the law than if a dictator simply issued a decree.
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **The "Decline of Legislatures" Thesis:** A famous concept by **Lord Bryce**. He argued that in the 20th century, legislatures became weaker while the Executive (Prime Minister/President) became stronger. Why? Because modern problems (economics, nuclear war) require quick, technical decisions that a debating society cannot handle efficiently.
    
- **Rubber Stamp:** A derogatory term for a legislature that automatically approves everything the leader asks for without real debate (common in authoritarian regimes).
    
- **Exam Tip:** When listing functions, do not just list "Law-making." You will get higher marks if you explain _Executive Oversight_ and _Financial Control_, as these are the functions that actually keep democracy alive.
