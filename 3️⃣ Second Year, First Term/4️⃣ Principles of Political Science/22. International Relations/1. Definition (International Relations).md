### 22.1 Definition of International Relations ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Before analyzing wars or treaties, one must understand what "International Relations" (IR) actually encompasses. Is it just politics, or does it include trade and culture?
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often ask for a definition or to distinguish between "International Politics" and "International Relations."
    

**International Relations (IR)** is the study of interactions among the various actors that participate in international politics, including states, international organizations, non-governmental organizations, and multinational corporations.

#### 1. The Core Distinction: Domestic vs. International

The defining feature of IR is the absence of a central authority.

- **Domestic Politics:** Hierarchical. If two citizens fight, the Police (State) intervenes. There is a monopoly on violence.
    
- **International Politics:** **Anarchic**. If two countries fight, there is no "World Police" with the power to stop them. The UN exists, but it has no independent army. Therefore, states must rely on **Self-Help** (their own power) to survive.
    

#### 2. Narrow vs. Broad Definitions

- **International Politics (Narrow):** This focuses strictly on the official diplomatic and military relations between governments (e.g., Treaties, Wars, Alliances). Hans Morgenthau famously defined it as the "struggle for power" among nations.
    
- **International Relations (Broad):** This includes _all_ interactions—not just political, but also economic (trade), social (tourism, migration), and cultural. It recognizes that Bill Gates (Microsoft) or Greta Thunberg (Activist) can influence the world just as a President can.
    

#### 3. Key Actors in IR

Unlike the 19th century, where only Kings and Presidents mattered, modern IR has many players:

- **State Actors:** Sovereign countries (USA, China, Bangladesh). These are still the most powerful actors.
    
- **Non-State Actors:**
    
    - **IGOs (Inter-Governmental Organizations):** Groups made of states (UN, WTO, NATO).
        
    - **NGOs (Non-Governmental Organizations):** Private groups with global missions (Red Cross, Amnesty International).
        
    - **MNCs (Multinational Corporations):** Companies with budgets bigger than some countries (Apple, Tesla, BP).
        

#### 4. Sovereignty

The foundational concept of IR (since the Peace of Westphalia in 1648).

- **Definition:** A state has absolute authority within its own territory. No other state can tell it what to do.
    
- **Modern Challenge:** Globalization and Human Rights laws are weakening sovereignty. If a dictator kills his own people, the world now claims the right to intervene (R2P - Responsibility to Protect).
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **Anarchy:** In IR, "Anarchy" does not mean chaos or violence. It simply means **"No Ruler"** (Absence of World Government). Order is possible in anarchy through cooperation, but it is harder to maintain.
    
- **Zero-Sum vs. Non-Zero-Sum:**
    
    - _Zero-Sum:_ If I win, you lose (War, Territory).
        
    - _Non-Zero-Sum:_ We can both win (Trade, Climate Change agreements).
        
- **Westphalian System:** Always reference the **Treaty of Westphalia (1648)**. This is the birth certificate of the modern state system and International Relations.
