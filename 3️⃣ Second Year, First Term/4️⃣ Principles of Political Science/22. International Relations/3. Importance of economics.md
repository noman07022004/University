## 22.3 Importance of economics ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 
**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** In the modern world, military power often takes a back seat to economic power. Understanding International Political Economy (IPE) is essential to explain phenomena like the US-China trade war, the EU, or the rise of the Global South.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often link this to other topics (e.g., "How does economic interdependence affect war?"). It is frequently tested as "International Political Economy" or through concepts like "Neo-colonialism" and "Globalization."

### Introduction: From "Low Politics" to "High Politics"

Traditionally, International Relations focused on "High Politics" (war, diplomacy, and security). Economics was considered "Low Politics"—secondary to survival.

However, in the contemporary world, this distinction has blurred. Today, economics is not just about wealth; it is a source of power and a tool of foreign policy. The study of this intersection is known as **International Political Economy (IPE)**. As the scholar **Susan Strange** argued, you cannot understand the international system without understanding the financial and economic structures that underpin it.

### 1. Economics as a Source of Power

Just as military strength defines a state's hard power, economic strength defines its ability to sustain that power.

- **Wealth is Power:** A strong economy allows a state to build a strong military, fund technological research, and exert influence diplomatically. (e.g., The collapse of the Soviet Union was primarily due to economic failure, not military defeat).
    
- **GDP as a Metric:** In modern IR, a nation's ranking is often determined by its GDP rather than just its army size. The rise of China is analyzed primarily through its economic growth.
    

### 2. Economic Statecraft: The Tools of Influence

States use economic instruments to achieve political goals. This is often called "Economic Statecraft."

- **The Stick (Sanctions):** States use trade embargoes, freezing of assets, or tariffs to punish other states. (e.g., Western sanctions on Russia or Iran to force policy changes).
    
- **The Carrot (Foreign Aid):** Wealthy states provide Official Development Assistance (ODA) to developing nations to buy loyalty, secure alliances, or gain access to resources. (e.g., China’s "Belt and Road Initiative" creates infrastructure in return for political influence).
    

### 3. Theoretical Perspectives on Economics in IR

To provide an academic answer, you must briefly categorize how different ideologies view the importance of economics:

- **Mercantilism / Economic Nationalism:**
    
    - _View:_ Economics is a tool of the state. Politics rules over economics.
        
    - _Goal:_ Maximize national wealth (surplus balance of trade) to maximize national power.
        
    - _Thinkers:_ Alexander Hamilton, Friedrich List.
        
- **Economic Liberalism:**
    
    - _View:_ Economics and politics should be separate. Free trade benefits everyone ("Comparative Advantage").
        
    - _Goal:_ Global efficiency and peace. Liberals argue that **Economic Interdependence** (trading with each other) reduces the likelihood of war because war becomes too profitable.
        
    - _Thinkers:_ Adam Smith, David Ricardo.
        
- **Marxism / Structuralism:**
    
    - _View:_ Economics _is_ politics. The global economic system is exploitative.
        
    - _Goal:_ To understand how the "Core" (rich nations) exploits the "Periphery" (poor nations).
        
    - _Thinkers:_ Immanuel Wallerstein (World Systems Theory).
        

### 4. Globalization and Interdependence

The importance of economics has peaked in the era of globalization.

- **Complex Interdependence:** Scholars **Keohane and Nye** argue that military force is becoming less useful because states are tied together by trade and finance. Destroying an enemy's economy might destroy your own stock market.
    
- **Institutions:** Global governance is largely economic today, managed by institutions like the **WTO** (World Trade Organization), **IMF** (International Monetary Fund), and **World Bank**. These organizations dictate the rules of global behavior.
    

### 5. The North-South Divide

Economics defines the primary fault line in international relations today: the gap between the developed "Global North" and the developing "Global South." International politics is often a struggle by the South to change the economic rules set by the North (e.g., demands for a New International Economic Order).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Key Term:** Use the term **"International Political Economy" (IPE)**. This shows you know the specific sub-field name.
    
- **Interdependence:** When discussing peace, mention the **"Capitalist Peace Theory"** or the liberal idea that "countries with McDonald's rarely fight each other" (though this is a simplification, the academic term is _commercial liberalism_).
    
- **Current Affairs:** In an exam, link this to current events. Mentioning "Trade Wars" (US-China) or "Energy Diplomacy" (Russia-Europe gas pipelines) makes your answer relevant and high-scoring.
    
- **The "Bretton Woods" System:** A quick mention of the Bretton Woods conference (1944) adds depth, as it established the modern economic architecture (IMF, World Bank) that manages IR today.
