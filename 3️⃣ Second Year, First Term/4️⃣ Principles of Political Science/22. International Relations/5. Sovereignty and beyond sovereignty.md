## 22.5 Sovereignty and beyond sovereignty ⭐⭐⭐⭐ 

**Rating Justification:**
- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Sovereignty is the "Big Bang" of International Relations. It is the defining feature of a "State." You cannot understand international law or the UN Charter without grasping this concept.
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A favorite topic for examiners asking questions like "Is the nation-state obsolete?" or "Discuss the challenges to state sovereignty in the age of globalization."
    

---

### Introduction: The Cornerstone of the State System

The concept of sovereignty is the organizing principle of the modern world. For over 350 years—since the **Peace of Westphalia in 1648**—international relations has been defined as relations between "Sovereign States."

However, in the 21st century, we are moving into a "Post-Westphalian" era. The absolute power of the state is being eroded by forces "beyond sovereignty," such as globalization, human rights laws, and environmental crises.

### A. The Concept of Sovereignty

Sovereignty, in political science, means **supreme authority**. It is the absolute power to make laws and enforce them within a specific territory, without subordination to any external authority.

#### 1. The Classical Definition

The French jurist **Jean Bodin** (1576) is considered the father of the concept. He defined it as:

> **"The supreme power over citizens and subjects, unrestrained by law."**

#### 2. The Two Aspects of Sovereignty

To provide a complete legal and political definition, you must distinguish between its two faces:

- **Internal Sovereignty:** The state has the ultimate authority _inside_ its borders. It has a monopoly on the legitimate use of force (Max Weber) and is the final lawmaker. No internal group (like a church or corporation) is above the state.
    
- **External Sovereignty:** The state is independent _outside_ its borders. It recognizes no superior. Legally, all sovereign states are equal (e.g., in the UN General Assembly, Vanuatu has one vote, just like the USA). This implies the principle of **Non-Interference**—states should not meddle in each other's domestic affairs.
    

---

### B. Beyond Sovereignty: The Erosion of State Power

The phrase "Beyond Sovereignty" refers to the decline of the state's absolute control. In the modern world, states can no longer do whatever they want behind their borders.

#### 1. Globalization and Economic Interdependence

- **The Argument:** Global markets have made borders porous. MNCs (Multinational Corporations) and electronic capital flows bypass government control.
    
- **Impact:** A state cannot effectively control its own currency or economy in isolation. Decisions made by the World Bank or Wall Street affect national sovereignty.
    

#### 2. International Human Rights and "R2P"

- **The Shift:** Historically, how a king treated his subjects was his own business. Today, it is the world's business.
    
- **R2P (Responsibility to Protect):** Adopted by the UN in 2005, this doctrine states that if a state fails to protect its people from genocide or war crimes, it _forfeits_ its sovereignty, and the international community has the right to intervene (even militarily).
    
- **Significance:** Sovereignty is no longer a _right_; it is a _responsibility_.
    

#### 3. Supranationalism (The EU Model)

- **Definition:** States voluntarily give up parts of their sovereignty to a higher authority for mutual benefit.
    
- **Example:** The **European Union (EU)**. Member states cannot pass laws that contradict EU laws. They have "pooled" their sovereignty, moving "beyond" the traditional nation-state.
    

#### 4. Global Ecological Crisis

- **The Reality:** Climate change, pandemics, and nuclear fallout respect no borders.
    
- **Impact:** These problems define a "planetary interest" that supersedes "national interest." Solving them requires global governance mechanisms that limit what individual states can do (e.g., carbon emission limits).
    

---

### C. Synthesis: The "Disaggregated" State

Is sovereignty dead? No. But it has changed. We are not seeing the _end_ of the state, but the _transformation_ of the state. States are now just one actor among many (NGOs, Terrorist groups, UN, MNCs). We are moving from a world of **"Government"** (state-controlled) to a world of **"Governance"** (managed by many actors).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Westphalian System":** You _must_ use this term. It refers to the system established in 1648 that enshrined sovereignty and non-interference. "Beyond Sovereignty" is effectively "Post-Westphalian."
    
- **Legal Context:** Since this is a pre-law course, mention **Article 2(7) of the UN Charter**. It forbids the UN from intervening in domestic jurisdiction. This is the legal anchor of sovereignty. However, contrast this with **Chapter VII**, which allows intervention for peace and security. This tension is the core of the "Beyond Sovereignty" debate.
    
- **Stephen Krasner:** If you want extra marks, cite scholar **Stephen Krasner**, who called sovereignty "Organized Hypocrisy"—meaning states pretend to respect it, but powerful states violate it whenever it suits them (e.g., US invasion of Iraq, Russian invasion of Ukraine).
