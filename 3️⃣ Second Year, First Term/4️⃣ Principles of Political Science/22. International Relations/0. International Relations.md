## 22. International Relations ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** No state exists in a vacuum. Domestic politics is constantly shaped by external forces (trade, war, climate change). Understanding IR is essential to grasp why states act the way they do on the global stage.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** A very high-frequency topic. Exams almost always require you to explain **Realism** (Power/National Interest) vs. **Liberalism** (Economics/Cooperation) or to discuss the concept of **Sovereignty** in a globalized world.

---

International Relations (IR) is the study of interactions among states, international organizations, and non-state actors. Unlike domestic politics, where a government exists to enforce laws, the international system is characterized by **Anarchy**. This does not mean chaos; it means there is **no world government** to police the states. In this "self-help" system, every state must look out for itself. This section explores how states survive in this environment, how they define their interests, and whether the future lies in sovereign isolation or global cooperation.

---

### 22.1 Power and National Interest (The Realist View)

For "Realists" (the dominant school of thought in IR), the world is a dangerous place.

- **National Interest (_Raison d'état_):** States do not act based on morality or friendship; they act to secure their **National Interest**. The primary interest is always **Survival**.
    
- **Power:** To ensure survival, states must acquire power.
    
    - **Hard Power:** Military and economic strength (guns and money). The ability to coerce others.
        
    - **Soft Power:** Cultural influence and diplomatic reputation (movies, values, ideology). The ability to persuade others to want what you want.
        
- **The Security Dilemma:** This is the tragedy of IR. When State A builds up its military just for _defense_, State B feels threatened and builds up _its_ military. This leads to an arms race where everyone is less safe, even though no one wanted war.
    

### 22.2 War and Peace

Why do states fight, and how can peace be maintained?

- **Causes of War:**
    
    - **Individual Level:** Human nature (greed/aggression) or leaders' misperceptions.
        
    - **State Level:** Certain types of states (e.g., dictatorships) are more aggressive.
        
    - **System Level:** The structure of anarchy forces states to fight for dominance.
        
- **Keeping the Peace:**
    
    - **Balance of Power:** Peace is preserved when no single state becomes too powerful. If one state rises (like Napoleon's France), others naturally form a coalition to balance against it.
        
    - **Collective Security:** An alternative idea (used by the UN). "An attack on one is an attack on all." If a state acts aggressively, all other states agree to gang up and punish it (e.g., the Gulf War 1991).
        

### 22.3 Importance of Economics (The Liberal View)

"Liberals" in IR argue that Realists focus too much on war and ignore the vast amount of peaceful cooperation that happens daily.

- **Economic Interdependence:** When states trade with each other, they become dependent on one another for prosperity. War becomes too expensive. (e.g., China and the USA are rivals, but their economies are so linked that war would destroy both).
    
- **The Commercial Peace:** The belief that free trade promotes peace. "If goods don't cross borders, soldiers will."
    

### 22.4 Sovereignty and Beyond Sovereignty

The modern international system is built on the **Treaty of Westphalia (1648)**, which established **Sovereignty**: the idea that a state has absolute authority within its borders and no outside power can interfere.

- **Challenges to Sovereignty:** Today, this concept is eroding.
    
    - **Supranational Organizations:** The **European Union (EU)** creates laws that supersede the laws of member states.
        
    - **Humanitarian Intervention:** The "Responsibility to Protect" (R2P) doctrine suggests that if a state massacres its own people, the international community has a right to violate its sovereignty to stop it (e.g., NATO in Libya).
        
    - **Globalization:** Global financial markets, the internet, and climate change do not respect borders. A state cannot control its own currency or environment in isolation anymore.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **The "Democratic Peace Theory":** A crucial concept for exams. It states that **democracies almost never go to war with other democracies**. (They still fight dictatorships, but not each other).
    
- **Key Term - "Anarchy":** In IR, this does _not_ mean chaos/riots. It strictly means **"Absence of a central government."**
    
- **Zero-Sum vs. Non-Zero-Sum:**
    
    - _Realists_ see the world as **Zero-Sum** (If China wins, the US loses).
        
    - _Liberals_ see the world as **Non-Zero-Sum** (We can both win through trade).
        
- **The "Thucydides Trap":** A historical pattern where a rising power (like Athens/China) threatens a ruling power (like Sparta/USA), often leading to war.
