### 13.4 Mass Media and Government ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the practical application of political communication. It explores the constant power struggle: the Government tries to _control_ the message, and the Media tries to _expose_ the truth. Understanding censorship, leaks, and press freedom is vital for this.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Exams often ask about the **methods of government control over media** or the concept of **"Manufacturing Consent"** (Noam Chomsky).
    

---

**Introduction: The Battle for Control** The relationship between the Government and the Media is fundamentally a struggle over information. Information is power. If the government controls what people know, it controls how they vote and behave. Therefore, every government (whether democratic or authoritarian) tries to manage the media. The spectrum ranges from **Total Control** (North Korea) to **Regulation** (UK/USA) to **Laissez-Faire** (Internet).

**1. Government Strategies to Manage Media** Governments use a "Carrot and Stick" approach to handle the press.

- **A. The Stick (Censorship and Punishment):**
    
    - **Direct Censorship:** Banning specific news stories, books, or websites (e.g., The Great Firewall of China).
        
    - **Legal Threats:** Using laws like "Official Secrets Acts," "Digital Security Acts," or "Defamation" to arrest journalists who criticize the state.
        
    - **License Revocation:** Threatening to cancel the broadcasting license of a TV channel if they are too critical.
        
- **B. The Carrot (Access and Leaks):**
    
    - **Press Conferences:** The government sets the stage. The Press Secretary briefs the media, giving the government's official version of events.
        
    - **Access Journalism:** "If you write nice things about me, I will give you an exclusive interview. If you criticize me, I will never talk to you again." Journalists often self-censor to keep their access to power.
        
    - **Trial Balloons:** The government intentionally "leaks" a secret idea (e.g., "The PM is thinking of raising taxes") to see how the media and public react. If the reaction is bad, they deny it. If good, they do it.
        

**2. Manufacturing Consent (Chomsky and Herman)**

Edward Herman and Noam Chomsky developed the **Propaganda Model**. They argue that even in "free" democracies like the USA, the media serves the government/elite interests, not because of censorship, but because of market forces.

- **The 5 Filters:** News must pass through these filters before reaching you:
    
    1. **Ownership:** Media is owned by huge corporations (who don't want radical change).
        
    2. **Advertising:** Media needs ad money, so they won't offend big business advertisers.
        
    3. **Sourcing:** Media relies on government officials for cheap, easy news (Press Releases).
        
    4. **Flak:** If media criticizes power, they get attacked (lawsuits/complaints).
        
    5. **Ideology:** Anti-Communism (or Anti-Terrorism) is used to silence dissenters as "enemies."
        

**3. The Adversarial Role (The Watchdog)** In theory, the media's job is to hold the government accountable.

- **Investigative Journalism:** Deep research to uncover secrets (e.g., The Pentagon Papers, Watergate, Panama Papers).
    
- **The Effect:** When media successfully exposes corruption, it forces the government to resign or change laws. This is the "Fourth Estate" functioning correctly.
    

**4. Social Media and Government Control** The internet made control harder, so governments adapted.

- **Internet Shutdowns:** In times of crisis (protests), governments simply turn off the internet to stop information flow (common in parts of Asia and Africa).
    
- **Cyber Surveillance:** Using software (like Pegasus) to spy on journalists' phones.
    
- **Troll Armies:** Instead of deleting criticism, the government floods the comment section with thousands of fake pro-government comments to drown it out.

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Law - "Freedom of Information Act (FOIA/RTI)":** Laws that allow citizens to demand documents from the government. In Bangladesh, the **Right to Information Act (2009)** is a crucial tool for journalists to force the government to be transparent.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** The **Digital Security Act (DSA)** (now Cyber Security Act) is the most debated topic here. Critics argue it creates a "culture of fear" where journalists self-censor to avoid arrest, effectively tilting the balance heavily in favor of the Government.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Does the media control the government or vice versa?", argue that it is a **constant negotiation**. In wartime, the Government usually wins (security); in peacetime scandals, the Media often wins.
