### 13.2 Social Media ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Social media has fundamentally rewired how politics works. It has shifted power from gatekeepers (editors) to the masses (users), but also introduced new dangers like algorithms and echo chambers. You cannot understand 21st-century elections or revolutions without this.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Extremely high frequency. Exams often ask about the role of social media in **political mobilization** (e.g., The Arab Spring) or the concepts of **"Echo Chambers"** and **"Filter Bubbles."**
    

---

Introduction: The Fifth Estate

If traditional media (TV, Newspapers) is the "Fourth Estate," political scientists often call Social Media the "Fifth Estate."

Unlike traditional media, which is one-to-many (one broadcaster speaks to millions of passive listeners), social media is many-to-many. It is decentralized, interactive, and user-generated. It allows politicians to bypass the press and speak directly to voters, and it allows voters to talk back.

1. The Democratization of Information

Social media has lowered the barrier to entry for political speech.

- **Gatekeeping:** In the past, if you wanted to share a political opinion, you needed a newspaper editor to publish your letter. Today, you just hit "Tweet" or "Post."
    
- **Citizen Journalism:** Ordinary people with smartphones can record protests, police brutality, or corruption and broadcast it to the world instantly (e.g., George Floyd video), often forcing the mainstream media to cover stories they ignored.
    

**2. Mobilization and "Hashtag Activism"**

Social media is a potent tool for organizing.

- **The Arab Spring (2011):** Often called the "Facebook Revolution." Activists in Egypt and Tunisia used Facebook to organize protests and Twitter to coordinate movements in real-time, bypassing state-controlled TV.
    
- **Slacktivism:** Critics argue that social media creates "lazy" activism. Posting a hashtag (#SaveTheWorld) makes people feel good but often leads to no real-world political change. This is called "Clicktivism."
    

3. The Dark Side: Algorithms and Polarization

While it connects people, social media also divides them.

- **The Filter Bubble / Echo Chamber:**
    
    - **Mechanism:** Algorithms (Facebook/YouTube) want to keep you glued to the screen to show you ads. They notice you like "Party A." So, they show you _only_ good news about Party A and bad news about Party B.
        
    - **Result:** You live in a "bubble" where everyone agrees with you. You stop understanding the other side, leading to extreme **Polarization**.
        
- **Disinformation (Fake News):**
    
    - False information spreads 6 times faster than the truth because it is often more emotional or shocking.
        
    - **Bot Armies:** Regimes use automated accounts (Bots) to flood the internet with pro-government propaganda, creating a fake illusion of popularity (Astroturfing).
        

4. Direct Communication (The Trump Model)

Social media allows leaders to bypass the "filter" of professional journalism.

- **Old Model:** The President gives a speech $\rightarrow$ Journalists analyze and fact-check it $\rightarrow$ Public reads it.
    
- **New Model:** The President tweets $\rightarrow$ Public reads it directly.
    
- **Impact:** This gives leaders more power to frame the narrative, but it removes the "check and balance" of fact-checking.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Key Term - "Micro-targeting":** Political campaigns (like Cambridge Analytica) harvest your social media data (likes, friends) to build a psychological profile of you. They then send you specific political ads designed to trigger your specific fears or hopes.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Facebook is the dominant political platform in Bangladesh. It is used for everything from organizing student protests (Quota Reform Movement) to spreading rumors that incite communal violence. The government's attempts to regulate it (Digital Security Act) reflect the tension between control and freedom.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Is social media good for democracy?", provide a balanced answer. It increases **Participation** (Good) but decreases **Truth/Consensus** (Bad).
