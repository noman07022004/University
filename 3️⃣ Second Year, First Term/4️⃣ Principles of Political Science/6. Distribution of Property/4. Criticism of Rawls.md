### 6.4 Criticism of Rawls ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** No theory is perfect. To fully grasp Rawls, you must understand why his opponents on both the Right (Libertarians) and the Left (Communitarians/Marxists) reject his conclusions. This dialectic is the engine of modern political philosophy.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Exams often pair Rawls with his arch-rival **Robert Nozick**. A question like "Compare and contrast Rawls' and Nozick's views on property" is a classic.
    

---

**Introduction: The Targets on the Back** Because Rawls' _A Theory of Justice_ became the dominant theory of the welfare state, it drew fire from every direction.

- **From the Right (Nozick):** He allows too much state interference.
    
- **From the Community (Sandel):** He ignores social bonds.
    
- **From the Development View (Sen):** He focuses on the wrong metric.
    

**1. The Libertarian Critique: Robert Nozick**

Rawls’ Harvard colleague, **Robert Nozick**, wrote _Anarchy, State, and Utopia_ (1974) specifically to refute Rawls.

- **Entitlement Theory (Process over Pattern):** Nozick argues that Rawls makes a fundamental mistake by looking for a "Pattern" (e.g., "inequality is only okay if it helps the poor").
    
    - Nozick argues justice is historical. If you acquired your wealth fairly (without stealing) and transferred it fairly, **you are entitled to it**. It doesn't matter if you have 100x more than the poor.
        
- **The "Wilt Chamberlain" Argument:** This is Nozick’s famous thought experiment.
    
    - Imagine a society that is perfectly equal (Rawls' ideal).
        
    - Now, imagine the famous basketball player Wilt Chamberlain signs a contract: "Everyone pays 25 cents to watch me play."
        
    - 1 million people happily pay. Wilt is now $250,000 richer than everyone else.
        
    - **The Point:** "Liberty upsets patterns." To maintain Rawls' "fair" distribution, the state would have to ban people from spending their money freely or constantly tax Wilt to bring him back down.
        
    - _Conclusion:_ You cannot have both full Liberty and enforced Equality. Rawls sacrifices Liberty.
        

**2. The Communitarian Critique: Michael Sandel** As discussed in Topic 5, Communitarians attack the **"Veil of Ignorance."**

- **The Unencumbered Self:** Sandel argues that the "Original Position" is a fantasy. It asks us to imagine humans stripped of their family, religion, and history.
    
- **The Problem:** A person stripped of all these things is not a human being; they are a ghost. Real justice isn't about what "ghosts" would agree to; it's about what _real communities_ with shared values believe is right.
    
- **Detached:** By prioritizing abstract "Fairness" over the "Common Good," Rawls creates a cold, legalistic society where people don't care about each other.
    

**3. The Capabilities Critique: Amartya Sen** Nobel Prize winner **Amartya Sen** argues that Rawls focuses on the wrong "currency" of justice.

- **Primary Goods vs. Capabilities:** Rawls focuses on distributing "Primary Goods" (income, rights, opportunities).
    
- **The Flaw:** People are physically different.
    
    - _Example:_ If you give a bicycle (Good) to an able-bodied person and a disabled person, you have treated them "equally" in terms of goods. But the disabled person cannot _use_ it.
        
- **Conclusion:** Justice shouldn't just look at what you _have_ (resources), but what you can actually _do_ and _be_ (capabilities).
    

**4. The Marxist Critique**

- **Apology for Capitalism:** Marxists argue that Rawls’ "Difference Principle" is just a clever excuse for inequality.
    
- **The Bribe:** It tells the poor: "Let the rich get richer, because a few crumbs will fall to you." Marxists argue the poor should not accept crumbs; they should own the bakery (abolish class distinction entirely).
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **Self-Ownership:** Nozick’s core premise is **Self-Ownership**. If I own myself, I own my labor. If I own my labor, I own the fruits of my labor (money). Taxing my money to help the poor (as Rawls wants) is forced labor (slavery).
    
- **Exam Strategy:** When writing about Nozick, use the phrase **"Liberty upsets patterns."** It summarizes his entire critique of Rawls in three words.
    
- **Comparison Table:**
    
    - **Rawls:** Justice = Fairness (Benefit the least advantaged).
        
    - **Nozick:** Justice = Entitlement (Respect property rights).
        
    - **Sandel:** Justice = Common Good (Respect community values).
