### 6.3 Rawls' Theory of Justice ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** John Rawls is widely considered the most important political philosopher of the 20th century. His 1971 book, _A Theory of Justice_, revived the entire discipline. His theory ("Justice as Fairness") is the modern standard argument for the Welfare State.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is a "super-topic." Exams almost inevitably ask about the **"Veil of Ignorance,"** the **"Difference Principle,"** or to contrast Rawls with Utilitarianism.
    

---

**Introduction: Justice as Fairness** John Rawls aimed to solve the problem of distributive justice by combining **Liberty** (Liberalism) with **Equality** (Socialism). He rejected Utilitarianism because it could justify sacrificing a minority for the happiness of the majority. His goal was to derive principles of justice that everyone—rich or poor, religious or atheist—would voluntarily agree to if they were being perfectly fair.

**1. The Thought Experiment: The Original Position**

To discover fair rules, Rawls asks us to imagine a hypothetical situation called the **Original Position** (his version of the State of Nature).

- **The Veil of Ignorance:** In this position, you are stripped of all knowledge about who you will be in the real world.
    
    - You do not know your **Class** (rich or poor).
        
    - You do not know your **Talent** (genius or slow).
        
    - You do not know your **Health** (athlete or disabled).
        
    - You do not know your **Gender, Race, or Religion**.
        
- **The Task:** From behind this veil, you must choose the rules that will govern society.
    
- **The Logic:** Since you don't know if you will be a billionaire or a homeless person, you will naturally want to construct a society that is fair for _everyone_, just in case you end up at the bottom. Self-interest forces you to be fair.
    

**2. The Maximin Strategy** Rawls argues that rational people behind the veil would be risk-averse. They wouldn't gamble on being a King. They would follow the **Maximin** strategy: **Maximize the Minimum**.

- _Meaning:_ Make the "worst-case scenario" as good as possible. You want to ensure that even if you are born poor and sick, you still have a decent life.
    

**3. The Two Principles of Justice** Rawls argues that people behind the Veil would agree to two specific principles, in a strict order of priority (Lexical Priority).

- **1. The Liberty Principle (First Priority):**
    
    - Everyone has an equal right to the most extensive basic liberties (free speech, vote, religion) compatible with a similar liberty for others.
        
    - _Rule:_ You cannot trade freedom for money. You cannot enslave 1% of the people to make the economy grow 10%.
        
- **2. The Social and Economic Principle (Second Priority):**
    
    - Social and economic inequalities are allowed _only if_ they meet two conditions:
        
    - **a) Fair Equality of Opportunity:** Inequalities must be attached to offices and positions open to all under conditions of fair competition (e.g., no discrimination, equal access to education).
        
    - **b) The Difference Principle (Crucial Concept):** Inequalities must be to the greatest benefit of the **least advantaged** members of society.
        

**4. Explaining the Difference Principle**

This is the most radical part of Rawls' theory. He does **not** argue for perfect equality (Communism). He admits that incentives (higher pay for doctors/inventors) are needed to make the economy grow.

- **The Test:** You can pay a brain surgeon 10x more than a janitor _only if_ that high pay encourages the surgeon to do work that eventually benefits the janitor (e.g., by inventing a cure or paying taxes that fund the janitor's healthcare).
    
- **Trickle Down?** Not exactly. It's not about wealth "trickling down" by accident; it's a moral requirement. If the rich getting richer _doesn't_ help the poor, the inequality is unjust and must be removed.
    

---

### Important Notes, Tips, and Facts

- **"Justice as Fairness":** This is the name of Rawls' theory. It means justice is whatever principles would be agreed to in a _fair_ initial situation (the Original Position).
    
- **The Natural Lottery:** Rawls argues that your talent, IQ, and rich parents are morally arbitrary—you didn't "earn" them; you won the "Natural Lottery." Therefore, you have no absolute moral right to hoard all the rewards they bring.
    
- **Exam Strategy:** If asked "Why does Rawls reject Utilitarianism?", the answer is the **Separateness of Persons**. Utilitarianism treats society as one big blob; Rawls treats every individual as a separate entity that cannot be sacrificed.
