### 18.3 Cabinets ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The Cabinet is the "core" of the executive in parliamentary systems. It is where the actual decisions of the country are made.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a critical exam topic. You will frequently be asked to explain "Collective Responsibility" or the distinction between the "Cabinet" and the "Council of Ministers."
    

---

The **Cabinet** is the supreme directing authority of the government. It acts as the bridge that connects the executive branch to the legislative branch in a parliamentary system.

While the legislature debates and the bureaucracy implements, the Cabinet **decides**. Walter Bagehot, the famous constitutional scholar, described the Cabinet as the _"hyphen which joins, a buckle which fastens, the legislative part of the state to the executive part."_

#### 1. Cabinet vs. Ministry (The Council of Ministers)

In academic political science, it is vital to distinguish between the "Cabinet" and the broader "Ministry" (or Council of Ministers), although these terms are often used interchangeably in casual conversation.

- **The Ministry (Council of Ministers):** This is the large body of _all_ government ministers (often 50–70 people). It includes junior ministers, ministers of state, and deputy ministers. They rarely meet as a single group.
    
- **The Cabinet:** This is the inner circle—a smaller body (usually 15–25 people) consisting only of the most senior ministers (e.g., Finance, Defense, Home Affairs). They meet weekly to make the big policy decisions.
    
- **The Relationship:** Every Cabinet member is a Minister, but not every Minister is a Cabinet member.
    

#### 2. Core Principles of the Cabinet System

The Cabinet system operates on two fundamental constitutional conventions:

- **A. Collective Responsibility (The "Sink or Swim" Rule):**
    
    - **Definition:** All Cabinet members are collectively responsible to Parliament for the decisions of the government.
        
    - **Implication:** Decisions are taken jointly. Once a decision is reached in the Cabinet room, _every_ minister must publicly support it, even if they argued against it in private.
        
    - **Consequence:** If a minister cannot publicly defend a Cabinet decision, they must resign. If the Parliament passes a Vote of No Confidence against the Prime Minister, the _entire_ Cabinet resigns together. They stand or fall as a unit.
        
- **B. Individual Ministerial Responsibility:**
    
    - **Definition:** Each minister is personally responsible for the conduct of their specific department.
        
    - **Implication:** If a major scandal or error occurs within a department (e.g., a train crash due to negligence in the Railway Ministry), the Minister in charge is expected to take the blame and potentially resign, shielding the rest of the Cabinet.
        

#### 3. Functions of the Cabinet

1. **Policy Formulation:** They determine the government's agenda—what bills will be introduced to Parliament and what the foreign policy will be.
    
2. **Supreme Control of the Executive:** They coordinate the vast machinery of government. If the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Finance disagree on funding, the Cabinet resolves the dispute.
    
3. **Crisis Management:** In times of war, pandemic, or economic collapse, the Cabinet functions as the crisis command center.
    

#### 4. The "Kitchen Cabinet"

In reality, a full Cabinet of 20 people can sometimes be too large for sensitive or quick decisions.

- **Definition:** An informal, small group of the Prime Minister's closest advisors and most trusted senior colleagues who make the _real_ decisions before the official Cabinet meeting even starts.
    
- **Criticism:** It bypasses the formal constitutional structures and reduces the accountability of other ministers.
    

---

### Important Notes 📝

- **Shadow Cabinet:** In countries like the UK, the Opposition Party forms a "Shadow Cabinet." These are MPs who "shadow" or mark specific ministers (e.g., a Shadow Defense Minister watches the real Defense Minister). This prepares them to take over immediately if they win the next election.
    
- **Presidential Cabinets:** Note the difference! In the USA, the Cabinet is **advisory**. The President can ask for their advice but is not bound to follow it. In the UK/India/Bangladesh, the Prime Minister is theoretically bound by the Cabinet's collective decision.
    
- **Bagehot's Quote:** Memorize the "hyphen and buckle" quote by Walter Bagehot. It is the standard academic definition of the Cabinet in a parliamentary system.
