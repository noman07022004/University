## 3.5 Applicability in Bangladesh ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the practical application of the entire module. As a law student in Bangladesh, you must know exactly which rules apply to the clients or cases you will encounter locally.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is highly probable for exams. Whether in a problem question or a theoretical essay, you must be able to state definitively that the **Dayabhaga School** is the primary authority in Bangladesh, while identifying the exceptions.

---

The application of Hindu Law in Bangladesh is unique. While the subcontinent has two major schools, the geographical and historical evolution of Bengal has firmly established the **Dayabhaga School** as the paramount(সর্বাপেক্ষা গুরুত্বপূর্ণ) authority here. However, due to the nature of Hindu Law being "Personal" rather than "Territorial," the Mitakshara school also co-exists within the territory of Bangladesh for specific communities.

---

### 1. The General Rule: Dominance of Dayabhaga

For the vast majority of Hindus in Bangladesh (Bengali Hindus), the law applicable is the **Dayabhaga School**.

- **Authority:** This is based on the digest written by **Jimutavahana**.
    
- **Legal Consequence:** When a Bangladeshi court decides a matter regarding inheritance or joint family property for a Bengali Hindu, it applies the Dayabhaga principles:
    
    - **Absolute Ownership:** The father is the absolute owner of the property during his lifetime.
        
    - **No Right by Birth:** Sons have no claim until the father dies.
        
    - **Inheritance vs. Survivorship:** Property passes by succession to heirs, not by survivorship to co-owners.
        
    - **Widow’s Rights:** A widow inherits her husband's share along with his sons (though as a limited life interest) even in an undivided family.
        

### 2. The Exception: Application of Mitakshara

The **Mitakshara School** applies in Bangladesh only as an exception. It applies to Hindu families who are not of Bengali origin but have settled in Bangladesh, provided they have not abandoned their original customs.

- **Target Communities:**
    
    - **Migrant Communities:** Families who migrated from Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, or other Mitakshara regions (often referred to as "Up-country Hindus").
        
    - **Business Communities:** Specific trading communities (like Marwaris) who traditionally follow the Mitakshara (often the Benares or Mithila sub-school).
        
    - **Tea Garden Workers:** Many tea laborers (e.g., Santhals, Oraons, Mundas) migrated from Central India (Mitakshara regions) during the British era. Courts have often held that in matters of succession, they are governed by Mitakshara principles unless a special custom is proved.
        
- **Burden of Proof:** If a Hindu in Bangladesh claims to be governed by Mitakshara law, the burden of proof is on them to show their family migrated from a Mitakshara region and retained their old laws.
    

### 3. The Role of the Courts

The Supreme Court of Bangladesh acts as the final interpreter.

- **Precedents:** The courts follow the decisions of the Privy Council and the Dhaka High Court which have interpreted the _Dayabhaga_ text over the last two centuries.
    
- **Codification Status:** Since Hindu Law is **uncodified** in Bangladesh (unlike India), the courts rely heavily on the text of the _Dayabhaga_ and established judicial precedents. They do _not_ apply the Indian _Hindu Succession Act, 1956_.
    

### 4. Summary of Legal Position in Bangladesh

|**Feature**|**Legal Rule in Bangladesh**|
|---|---|
|**Primary School**|**Dayabhaga** (Jimutavahana).|
|**Inheritance**|Based on "Religious Efficacy" (Pinda-Dana).|
|**Joint Family**|Father is absolute owner; Sons are not coparceners until father's death.|
|**Women's Rights**|Governed by traditional law + _Hindu Women’s Rights to Property Act, 1937_.|
|**Marriage**|Governed by ancient custom + _Hindu Marriage Registration Act, 2012_.|
|**Divorce**|Not generally recognized (unless by custom).|

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Assam" Connection:** Historically, the Dayabhaga school prevailed in "Bengal and Assam." Even today, if you have a case involving a Hindu family with roots in Sylhet or Assam, Dayabhaga is the default presumption.

> **Crucial Exam Distinction:** In an exam problem set in Bangladesh:
> 
> - If the father sells the family house and the son objects $\rightarrow$ The **Father wins** (Dayabhaga Rule: Absolute Ownership).
>     
> - In India (Mitakshara), the son would likely win or be able to claim his share.
>     
> - _Tip:_ Always check the jurisdiction. If it says "A Hindu in Dhaka," apply Dayabhaga.
>     

> **Statutory Layer:** Remember that while Dayabhaga is the base, specific British-era statutes like the _Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856_ and _Hindu Women’s Rights to Property Act, 1937_ apply to **all** Hindus in Bangladesh, regardless of whether they are Dayabhaga or Mitakshara.
