## 3.2 Mitakshara and Dayabhaga Schools ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This distinction is the heartbeat of Hindu Law. It dictates how property is owned, shared, and inherited. Without grasping this, you cannot solve any problem regarding Joint Family Property or Partition.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** The question "Distinguish between Mitakshara and Dayabhaga Schools" is statistically one of the most frequent questions in university law examinations. It is a core competency requirement.

---

The two major schools of Hindu Law, **Mitakshara** and **Dayabhaga**, represent two distinct legal philosophies regarding property and inheritance. While both are based on the same ancient Smritis, they differ radically in their interpretation. The **Mitakshara** is the orthodox school based on "Communal Ownership," while the **Dayabhaga** (followed in Bangladesh) is the reformist school based on "Individual Ownership."

---

### 1. The Mitakshara School

- **Author & Source:** Written by **Vijnaneshwara** (11th Century). It is a running commentary on the _Yajnavalkya Smriti_.
    
- **Extent:** It applies to the whole of India **except** Bengal and Assam.
    
- **Core Doctrine: _Janmasvatvavada_ (Right by Birth)**
    
    - Under this school, a son acquires a right/interest in the ancestral property **the moment he is born**.
        
    - He becomes a joint owner (Coparcener) with his father immediately.
        
- **Nature of Ownership:**
    
    - **Joint Tenancy:** The family members hold the property as a single unit. No individual member can say, "This specific 1/3rd represents my share" until a partition happens.
        
    - **Survivorship:** If a coparcener dies, his interest does _not_ pass to his heirs (wife/daughter). It "survives" (merges) into the remaining male coparceners.
        

### 2. The Dayabhaga School

- **Author & Source:** Written by **Jimutavahana** (12th Century). It is a digest of all codes.
    
- **Extent:** It applies specifically to **Bengal (Bangladesh)** and Assam.
    
- **Core Doctrine: _Uparamasvatvavada_ (Right by Death)**
    
    - Under this school, there is **no right by birth**.
        
    - The son's right to the property arises **only upon the death** of the father. As long as the father is alive, the son has no legal claim.
        
- **Nature of Ownership:**
    
    - **Absolute Ownership:** The father is the absolute owner of the property during his lifetime. He can sell, mortgage, or gift it without the son's consent.
        
    - **Tenancy in Common:** When heirs eventually inherit (after the father's death), they hold the property with defined shares (e.g., "I own exactly 1/3rd"), even if they haven't physically divided the land yet.
        
    - **Succession:** Property passes by inheritance to heirs, not by survivorship.
        

---

### 3. Key Differences (Detailed Analysis)

The divergence between the two schools can be analyzed through five specific legal points:

#### A. Basis of Right (Birth vs. Death)

- **Mitakshara:** Follows the principle of _Janmasvatvavada_. The right to property is congenital(জন্মগত) (by birth).
    
- **Dayabhaga:** Follows the principle of _Uparamasvatvavada_. The right to property is contingent on the owner's death.
    

#### B. The Position of the Father

- **Mitakshara:** The father is merely a manager (_Karta_). His power is restricted. He cannot alienate (sell) the ancestral property without the consent of his sons (unless for "Legal Necessity").
    
- **Dayabhaga:** The father is the **Absolute Owner**. He has full power of alienation. He can sell the entire family property without the consent of his sons, and the sons cannot legally object.
    

#### C. The Concept of Coparcenary (Joint Ownership)

- **Mitakshara:** A Coparcenary is formed between a Father and his Sons (and grandsons/great-grandsons). They are co-owners.
    
- **Dayabhaga:** There is **NO Coparcenary** between a Father and his Sons. A Coparcenary is only formed between **Brothers** (after the father dies) when they inherit the property together.
    

#### D. Partition

- **Mitakshara:** A son can demand partition of the ancestral property at any time, even against the father's will.
    
- **Dayabhaga:** A son **cannot demand partition** as long as the father is alive.
    

#### E. The Share of the Members

- **Mitakshara:** The share is **Fluctuating**. It is not defined until partition.
    
    - _Example:_ If a father has 2 sons, each technically has 1/3rd interest. If a 3rd son is born, everyone's interest instantly drops to 1/4th.
        
- **Dayabhaga:** The share is **Fixed** (Quasi-severalty).
    
    - Since the right arises only after death, the number of heirs is fixed at that moment. Births or deaths in the family do not automatically alter the shares of the living members.

### Comparative Table

|**Feature**|**Mitakshara School (India)**|**Dayabhaga School (Bangladesh)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Right to Property**|Arises by Birth (_Janmasvatvavada_).|Arises by Death (_Uparamasvatvavada_).|
|**Father's Power**|Restricted (Manager/Karta).|Absolute Owner.|
|**Alienation**|Father needs son's consent to sell.|Father can sell at will.|
|**Coparcenary**|Exists between Father and Son.|Exists only between Brothers (after Father's death).|
|**Survivorship**|Yes (Property merges to survivors).|No (Property goes to heirs by succession).|
|**Partition**|Son can demand it anytime.|Son cannot demand it while father is alive.|
|**Widow's Right**|Historically, could not inherit if husband was joint.|Could inherit husband's share even in joint family.|

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Exam Terminology:** Using the Sanskrit terms **"Janmasvatvavada"** (Theory of Right by Birth) and **"Uparamasvatvavada"** (Theory of Right by Death) generally guarantees higher marks. It shows deep academic engagement.

> **Applicability in Bangladesh:** Always remember that for problem questions set in Bangladesh:
> 
> - The **Father is Supreme**.
>     
> - If a son tries to sue his father to stop a land sale, the son will **lose** under Dayabhaga law.
>     
> - There is no "Survivorship."
>     

> **The "Unity of Possession":** While Dayabhaga heirs have defined shares (Unity of Title), they still live together until partition (Unity of Possession). Do not confuse "Joint Family" (living together) with "Mitakshara Coparcenary" (legal joint ownership). Dayabhaga families are Joint Families, but _not_ Mitakshara Coparcenaries.
