## 4.5 Completion of Marriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** It is legally vital to pinpoint the exact moment a man and woman become husband and wife. Before this moment, they are strangers in law; after this moment, they are bound for life.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Problem questions frequently feature scenarios where a ceremony is interrupted (e.g., the groom dies after the 5th step). You must know whether the marriage was completed or not to determine if the "widow" inherits property.

---

In many legal systems, a marriage is complete when a contract is signed. In Hindu Law, however, marriage is a process of religious rituals. The question of "Completion" refers to the precise point in the ceremony where the union becomes irrevocable. Until this specific moment is reached, the parties are merely betrothed, and either party can back out without legal penalty. Once the moment passes, the marriage is binding, even if they never consummate the marriage.

---

### 1. The Stages of the Ceremony

The Hindu marriage ceremony involves several steps, but legally, they are divided into **preliminary** (revocable) and **essential** (irrevocable) stages.

#### A. Betrothal (_Vagdana_)

- **Definition:** This is the engagement—the "giving of one's word." The father of the bride promises to give his daughter to the groom.
    
- **Legal Status:** It is merely a promise. It creates no legal rights.
    
- **Revocability:** It is **revocable**. If the promise is broken, the aggrieved party can sue for damages (money spent on preparations) but **cannot** sue for "Specific Performance" (the court cannot force them to marry).
    

#### B. Gift of the Bride (_Kanyadan_)

- **Definition:** The formal giving away of the daughter by her father (or guardian) to the groom.
    
- **Significance:** While spiritually significant, this alone does not complete the marriage.
    

### 2. The Essential Rites for Completion

According to the _Manusmriti_ and confirmed by modern courts, there are two Shastric ceremonies essential for the validity of a marriage:

1. **Vivaha Homa:** Invocation before the sacred fire.
    
2. **Saptapadi (Seven Steps):** The taking of seven steps by the bride and groom jointly around the sacred fire.
    

### 3. The Decisive Moment: The Seventh Step

The legal doctrine regarding the completion of marriage is strict and specific.

- **The Rule:** The marriage becomes complete and binding **on the completion of the Seventh Step**.
    
- **The "Incomplete" Scenario:** If the ceremony is interrupted after the 6th step (e.g., the groom collapses, or a dispute breaks out), legally, **there is no marriage**. The woman is still a spinster, not a widow or wife.
    
- **Irrevocability:** Once the 7th step is taken, the marriage is indissoluble. Even if the couple divorces 10 minutes later (conceptually), they are legally married.
    

### 4. Consummation is Not Required

A common misconception is that a marriage is not complete until sexual intercourse (consummation) takes place.

- **Hindu Law Position:** Consummation is **irrelevant** to the validity or completion of the marriage.
    
- **Legal Effect:** If the rites (_Saptapadi_) are performed, they are husband and wife, even if they never live together or have sex. The wife is entitled to inherit his property immediately after the 7th step.
    

### 5. Customary Variations

The requirement of _Saptapadi_ applies to orthodox marriages. However, Hindu Law allows **Custom** to override the text.

- **Customary Ceremonies:** If a specific community (e.g., the Santhal tribe or certain lower castes) has a valid ancient custom where marriage is completed by a different act—such as exchanging garlands (_Kanthibadal_) or tying a _Thali_ (Mangalsutra)—then the marriage is complete when _that_ specific act is performed, even without the sacred fire.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Interrupted Wedding" Problem:** This is a classic exam trope.
> 
> - _Scenario:_ "During the wedding, the groom faints after the 4th round of the fire and dies."
>     
> - _Verdict:_ The girl is **NOT** a widow. She cannot inherit his property. The marriage was never completed.
>     

> **Consummation Distinction:** Contrast this with English/Canon law where non-consummation can make a marriage voidable. In Hindu Law, a non-consummated marriage is perfectly valid.

> **Proof:** If the validity is challenged in court, the burden of proof lies on the person claiming the marriage happened to prove that the essential ceremonies (specifically _Saptapadi_) were performed.
