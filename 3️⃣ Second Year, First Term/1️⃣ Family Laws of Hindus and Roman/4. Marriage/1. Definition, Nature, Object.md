## 4.1 Definition, Nature, and Object of Hindu Marriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the philosophical core of Hindu family law. You cannot understand the legal rules regarding divorce (or lack thereof), widowhood, or adoption unless you understand that marriage is a "Sacrament" and not a contract.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A very common exam question is "Discuss the nature of Hindu Marriage. Is it a sacrament or a contract?" This section provides the direct answer to that essay.
    

---

In the Hindu legal system, marriage is viewed with a reverence that distinguishes it sharply from Western or Islamic concepts of marriage. It is not merely a social institution or a civil contract for cohabitation; it is a religious mandate. In the Vedic period, it was established as a holy union that is essential for a complete life. Without a wife (_Sahadharmini_), a man is considered incomplete and cannot perform the necessary religious sacrifices.

---

### 1. Definition of Hindu Marriage

Unlike modern statutes that provide precise definitions, ancient Hindu texts describe marriage through its function and permanence.

- **Vedic Concept:** Marriage is the union of "bones with bones, flesh with flesh, and skin with skin." It is a permanent fusion of two personalities into one.
    
- **Legal Definition:** It is defined as "the holy union for the performance of religious duties." It is the relationship between husband and wife which is sanctified by fire and holy texts, creating an indissoluble bond.
    
- **Distinction:** It is distinct from a "Civil Contract" (like in Muslim Law).
    
    - In a contract, parties negotiate terms, can dissolve it by mutual consent, and it is primarily for worldly purposes.
        
    - In Hindu marriage, the duties are fixed by _Dharma_, it cannot be dissolved by mutual consent (traditionally), and it is primarily for spiritual purposes.
        

### 2. Nature of Marriage: A Sacrament (_Samskara_)

The most critical legal feature of Hindu marriage is its nature as a **Sacrament** (_Samskara_).

- **Meaning of Samskara:** A _Samskara_ is a purificatory ceremony that sanctifies the body and mind, making an individual fit for the next stage of life. Marriage (_Vivaha_) is the most important of the ten sacraments in Hindu life.
    
- **Characteristics of the Sacramental Nature:**
    
    1. **Religious Necessity:** Marriage is not optional for a strict Hindu; it is a duty. A man needs a wife to perform _Yajnas_ (sacrifices) to please the gods.
        
    2. **Indissolubility:** Once the sacred fire witnesses the union (_Saptapadi_), the bond cannot be untied. Traditionally, "divorce" is unknown to Hindu law because a sacrament cannot be reversed.
        
    3. **Eternal Bond:** The union is believed to last not just for this lifetime, but for seven lives. Even death does not technically dissolve the spiritual tie (which is why widow remarriage was historically banned).
        
    4. **Holy Union:** It is a union of souls, not just a physical arrangement.
        
- **Modern View in Bangladesh:** While India has diluted this nature by introducing divorce (making it arguably a contract), **Bangladesh** retains the strict sacramental nature. Since there is no statutory divorce in Bangladesh for Hindus, the marriage remains a permanent, indissoluble sacrament in the eyes of the law.
    

### 3. Object of Marriage

Why does Hindu Law insist on marriage? The ancient Smritis (especially _Rig Veda_ and _Manusmriti_) define three specific objects (purposes) of marriage, ranked in strict order of importance.

#### A. Dharma (Religious Duty) – The Primary Object

- The highest aim of marriage is the performance of religious duties.
    
- A man alone is considered only "half a man." He becomes complete only when he secures a wife.
    
- Many religious rites (_Agnihotra_) cannot be performed by a bachelor or a widower; they require the presence of the wife.
    

#### B. Praja (Progeny/Procreation) – The Secondary Object

- The second aim is to beget children, specifically a **Son** (_Putra_).
    
- **Religious Reason:** The word _Putra_ means "one who delivers from Hell." It is believed that a son saves the father's soul from the hell called _Put_ by offering funeral cakes (_Pinda_) after death.
    
- **Legal Consequence:** This emphasis on having a son led to the legal practices of adoption (if one has no son) and historically, polygamy (marrying a second wife if the first has no son).
    

#### C. Rati (Pleasure) – The Tertiary Object

- The lowest aim of marriage is sexual gratification and physical pleasure.
    
- While recognized as a legitimate human need (_Kama_), it is subordinate to duty and procreation. If there is a conflict between pleasure and duty, duty prevails.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Exam Question:** _"Is Hindu marriage a sacrament or a contract?"_
> 
> - **Answer for Bangladesh:** In Bangladesh, it is **purely a sacrament**.
>     
> - **Reasoning:** Because the essential features of a contract (dissolution by divorce, mutual consent) are absent in the uncodified law of Bangladesh.
>     

> **Key Term:** Memorize the term **"Sahadharmini"**. It means "Partner in Dharma." Use this word to describe the wife's role in your answer to impress the examiner.

> **Hierarchy of Objects:** Remember the order: **Dharma > Praja > Rati**. Do not mix them up. The fact that _Dharma_ is first proves the religious nature of the institution.
