## 4.3 Essentials of a Valid Marriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the checklist for a valid marriage. You must understand exactly what makes a marriage void (e.g., incest) versus what merely makes it irregular or punishable (e.g., underage).
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Problem questions almost always hinge on one of these essentials being missing. A common scenario involves two cousins marrying or a marriage without the sacred fire—you need to know if these are valid.

---

For a Hindu marriage to be legally valid and binding in Bangladesh, it must satisfy specific conditions laid down by the ancient Smritis and modified by modern judicial interpretation. Unlike a contract which requires offer and acceptance, a Hindu marriage requires the fulfillment of religious mandates.

The essentials can be broadly categorized into two groups: **Substantive Requirements** (Capacity and Relationship) and **Formal Requirements** (Ceremonies).

---

### 1. Capacity of the Parties

Who is capable of entering into a Hindu marriage?

- **Identity of Religion:** Both parties must be Hindus. A marriage between a Hindu and a non-Hindu (Muslim/Christian) is not a valid _Hindu_ marriage (though it may be valid under the Special Marriage Act).
    
- **Mental Capacity (Soundness of Mind):**
    
    - **Ancient Law:** A marriage of a lunatic or idiot was valid because marriage is a sacrament, not a contract requiring consent.
        
    - **Modern Law:** Courts in Bangladesh generally hold that since marriage requires the performance of complex duties, the parties must be capable of understanding the ceremony. However, the marriage of a lunatic is often still treated as **voidable** rather than void ab initio, depending on the degree of incapacity.
        
- **Age (The Distinct Position in Bangladesh):**
    
    - **Statute:** The _Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017_ sets the minimum age (21 for males, 18 for females).
        
    - **Validity:** Crucially, violating this age limit **does NOT invalidate** the marriage in Hindu Law. If two minors marry with proper rites, the marriage is **valid** but the guardians/adults are punishable with imprisonment.
        

### 2. Absence of Prohibited Relationships

A marriage is null and void if the parties are related to each other within the prohibited degrees. This is based on the rule of exogamy (marrying outside the bloodline).

#### A. Sapinda Relationship (Prohibited Degrees)

- **Definition:** "Sapinda" means one who shares the same "Pinda" (particles of the body or funeral cake). It refers to close blood relatives.
    
- **The Rule:** Marriage between Sapindas is strictly prohibited and **Void**.
    
- **Extent:**
    
    - **Father's Side:** Prohibited up to **7 degrees** (generations) upwards.
        
    - **Mother's Side:** Prohibited up to **5 degrees** upwards.
        
    - _Calculation:_ You count the person themselves as the first degree.
        
- **Example:** A man cannot marry his first cousin, second cousin, or even distant cousin on the father's side if they fall within the 7th generation.
    

#### B. Sagotra (Same Lineage)

- **Ancient Rule:** Marriage between persons of the same _Gotra_ (lineage from a common ancient Sage) was void.
    
- **Modern Position:** This rule was abolished in India by statute. In Bangladesh (Dayabhaga), while orthodox families avoid it, the _Hindu Marriage Disabilities Removal Act, 1946_ (passed before partition) validated marriages between persons of the same _Gotra_. Thus, **Sagotra marriages are VALID** in Bangladesh today.
    

### 3. Performance of Ceremonies (Formalities)

This is the most critical essential. A Hindu marriage is not constituted by mere consent or exchange of garlands.

- **Requirement:** There must be the performance of the customary rites and ceremonies.
    
- **Key Rites:** The _Saptapadi_ (taking seven steps around the sacred fire) is the definitive test. Without it, the marriage is generally incomplete. (Covered in detail in 4.5).
    

### 4. The Doctrine of _Factum Valet_

What happens if a minor rule is broken? The ancient doctrine of _Factum Valet_ ("A fact cannot be altered by a hundred texts") comes into play.

- **Principle:** _"Quod fieri non debuit, factum valet"_ (What ought not to be done, is valid when done).
    
- **Application:** It cures defects in **directory** (optional/moral) rules, but not in **mandatory** (legal) rules.
    
    - _Example (Cured):_ If a text says "Do not marry a girl with red hair," and a man does so, the marriage is valid. The text was only a moral recommendation.
        
    - _Example (Not Cured):_ If a man marries his sister. This violates a mandatory legal rule (Sapinda). _Factum Valet_ cannot save this marriage; it is void.
        
- **Relevance:** This doctrine is frequently used to validate child marriages or marriages performed without the consent of the guardian, prioritizing the "completed sacrament" over technical flaws.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Exam Trap (Caste):** Historically, parties had to be of the same caste. In modern Bangladesh law, **Inter-caste marriages are VALID**. Do not write that they are void.

> **Sapinda Limit:** Memorize the numbers: **7** on the father's side, **5** on the mother's side.

> **Factum Valet:** This is a "Grade A" exam concept. Use it to solve problems where a marriage has taken place despite a minor irregularity (e.g., the bride was younger than the groom by 2 days, violating a minor text).
> 
> - _Formula:_ **Directory Rule Violated** = Valid (Factum Valet applies). **Mandatory Rule Violated** = Void (Factum Valet fails).
