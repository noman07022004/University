## 4.7 Presumption of Marriage ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** This is an evidentiary rule rather than a substantive law of marriage. It helps solve cases where direct proof of the wedding ceremony (which might have happened 40 years ago) is missing.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Often appears in problem questions where the validity of a marriage is challenged due to lack of witnesses or documents. You need to know when the court will "assume" a marriage exists.

---

In many legal disputes, especially regarding inheritance, it may be difficult to prove that a marriage ceremony took place decades ago. Witnesses may have died, and priests may be untraceable. In such cases, the law adopts a charitable view. Based on the maxim _Semper praesumitur pro matrimonio_ ("The presumption is always in favor of the validity of a marriage"), the court prefers to treat a couple as married rather than treating the woman as a concubine and the children as illegitimate.

---

### 1. The Rule of Presumption

The "Presumption of Marriage" is a rule of evidence. It states that where it is proved that a man and a woman have lived together continuously for a long period, and have been treated by their friends and society as husband and wife, the law will **presume** that they were legally married.

- **Requirements:**
    
    1. **Long Cohabitation:** They must have lived under the same roof for a significant number of years.
        
    2. **Repute:** The society, neighbors, and family must have regarded and treated them as a married couple.
        
- **Legal Effect:** Once these facts are established, the burden of proving that _no_ marriage took place shifts to the person denying the marriage.
    

### 2. Presumption of Rites (Factum Valet)

This presumption extends to the ceremonies as well. If it is proved that a marriage ceremony took place in fact, the court will presume that **all necessary religious rites** (like _Saptapadi_ and _Homa_) were performed with due regularity.

- **Logic:** The law assumes that people generally follow the correct procedures for serious matters like marriage. It will not demand video evidence of the "7th step" for a wedding that happened 30 years ago.
    

### 3. Rebuttal of Presumption

This presumption is **rebuttable**, not conclusive. It can be overturned if strong evidence is provided to the contrary.

- **Example of Rebuttal:** If it can be shown that the relationship began as "concubinage" (mistress) and no subsequent marriage ceremony ever occurred, the presumption of marriage cannot be drawn, no matter how long they lived together.
    
- **Capacity:** The presumption cannot arise if the parties legally _could not_ marry (e.g., if they were within the prohibited degrees of relationship/Sapinda).
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Concubine" Distinction:** A concubine (_Avaruddha Stree_) is a woman kept by a man who is not his wife.
> 
> - **Marriage:** Rights to inheritance and full maintenance.
>     
> - **Concubinage:** No right to inheritance; limited rights to maintenance only in ancient law (obsolete now).
>     
> - _Tip:_ In exams, if facts mention "They lived together for 20 years and had children," start with the **presumption of valid marriage**, not concubinage.
>     

> **Why is this vital in Bangladesh?** Since marriage registration was non-existent for Hindus until 2012 (and is still optional), this "Presumption" is often the _only_ way a widow can prove her status in court to inherit her husband's property.
