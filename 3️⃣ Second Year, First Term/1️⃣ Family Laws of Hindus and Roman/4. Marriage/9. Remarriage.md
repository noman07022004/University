## 4.9 Remarriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic marks the most significant departure from ancient orthodox law (where a woman could only marry once) to statutory law. It connects directly to the topic of "Widow's Estate" in inheritance.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A critical exam area. Problem questions frequently test the rule regarding the **forfeiture of property** upon remarriage. You must know whether a widow keeps her first husband's property after marrying a second husband.
    

---

### Introduction

In the ancient Shastric Hindu Law, marriage was an eternal sacrament that continued even after death. Consequently, the remarriage of a widow was strictly prohibited (_"Once is a maiden given in marriage"_ - Manu). While a widower (man) could remarry immediately, a widow was expected to live a life of austerity or commit _Sati_ (historically).

This rigid rule was changed during the British period through the efforts of social reformers like **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**. Today, in Bangladesh, widow remarriage is fully legal under the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**.

### 1. The Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856

This historic Act legalized the marriage of Hindu widows and declared that such marriages would not be considered illegitimate.

- **Legal Validity:** Section 1 of the Act explicitly declares that no marriage contracted between Hindus shall be invalid, and the issue (children) of no such marriage shall be illegitimate, by reason of the woman having been previously married or betrothed.
    
- **Ceremonies:** The same religious rites and ceremonies (like _Saptapadi_) that are necessary for a first marriage are required to constitute a valid remarriage.
    

### 2. Legal Effects of Remarriage (The Forfeiture Rule)

While the Act allows a widow to remarry, it imposes a severe penalty regarding her property rights derived from her deceased husband. This is the most crucial point for legal practice and exams.

#### **A. Forfeiture of Property (Section 2)**

- **The Rule:** Upon remarriage, a Hindu widow **loses (forfeits)** all rights and interests she had inherited in her deceased husband’s property.
    
- **Nature of Interest:** Under the _Hindu Women’s Rights to Property Act, 1937_ and Dayabhaga law, a widow holds a "Limited Estate" (Widow's Estate) in her husband's property.
    
- **Consequence:** The moment she remarries, the law treats her as if she has "civilly died" with respect to that property. The property immediately passes to the next heirs of her first husband (e.g., his brothers or nephews).
    
- **Exception:** She does **not** forfeit property she owned absolutely (_Stridhana_) or property she inherited from her own parents. She only loses what she inherited _as a widow_ from her first husband.
    

#### **B. Guardianship of Children (Section 3)**

- **The Rule:** If the deceased husband had left no instructions, the court _may_ (it is discretionary, not mandatory) remove the remarried widow from the guardianship of her children from the first marriage if it is in the children's best interest.
    
- **Presumption:** Usually, courts prefer the mother to retain custody unless the second husband is deemed unsuitable.
    

### 3. Remarriage of Divorced Women

The 1856 Act applies only to **widows**. What about women who are divorced?

- **Traditional Law:** Since traditional Hindu Law (Dayabhaga) does not recognize divorce, the concept of a "divorcee" generally does not exist.
    
- **Customary Divorce:** In lower castes where divorce is allowed by custom, a woman can remarry.
    
- **Legal Gap:** If a woman obtains a divorce (which is rare and difficult in Bangladesh), her right to remarry is governed by the custom of her community. There is no specific statute in Bangladesh regulating the remarriage of divorced Hindu women, unlike in India.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Forfeiture" Trap:** This is the #1 trick in inheritance problems.
> 
> - _Scenario:_ "W, a widow, inherits her husband H's land. Two years later, she marries X. Does she keep the land?"
>     
> - _Answer:_ **No.** She forfeits the land. It goes to H's reversioners (next heirs).
>     
> - _Logic:_ She inherited the land _as the surviving half of H's body_. By marrying X, she ceases to be H's surviving half.
>     

> **Crucial Comparison:**
> 
> - **Muslim Law:** A widow keeps her share of inheritance even if she remarries.
>     
> - **Hindu Law (Bangladesh):** A widow **loses** her share of inheritance if she remarries.
>
