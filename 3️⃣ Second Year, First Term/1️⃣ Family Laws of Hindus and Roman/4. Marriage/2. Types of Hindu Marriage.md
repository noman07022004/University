## 4.2 Types of Marriage ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Knowing the ancient forms is essential to understand the evolution of marriage from "capture" and "purchase" to "gift" and "mutual consent." Crucially, the form of marriage (Approved vs. Unapproved) dictates how a woman's property (_Stridhana_) is inherited after her death.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While questions rarely ask for a list of all eight, short notes on "Brahma Marriage," "Gandharva Marriage," or "Asura Marriage" are common.

---
The ancient Hindu sages recognized that human behavior varies, and thus they classified marriage into different forms based on the method of securing the bride. The _Manusmriti_ lists **eight forms** of marriage. These are broadly categorized into two groups: **Approved (_Prashasta_)** and **Unapproved (_Aprashasta_)**.

This classification is not merely historical; it has legal consequences. The specific form of marriage determines the line of succession for a woman's _Stridhana_ (exclusive property) if she dies without children.

---

### 1. Approved Forms (_Prashasta_)

These forms were considered righteous and were historically encouraged for the higher castes. In these forms, the bride is given as a "gift" (_Kanyadan_).

1. **Brahma:**
    
    - **Definition:** The father gifts his daughter to a man of good character and learning, whom he has invited, without receiving any consideration (money/goods) in return.
        
    - **Status:** This is the most honorable form. It is the presumption for all Hindu marriages in modern Bangladesh unless proved otherwise.
        
2. **Daiva:**
    
    - **Definition:** The father gives his daughter to a priest (_Ritvij_) as a sacrificial fee while he is performing a religious sacrifice (_Yajna_).
        
    - **Status:** Obsolete (as grand Vedic sacrifices are rare).
        
3. **Arsha:**
    
    - **Definition:** The father gives his daughter after receiving a pair of cattle (a cow and a bull) from the groom.
        
    - **Distinction:** The cattle are not a "price" for the bride but are for religious purposes (milk/sacrifice).
        
    - **Status:** Obsolete.
        
4. **Prajapatya:**
    
    - **Definition:** Similar to Brahma, but the father specifically addresses the couple with the condition: _"May you both perform your civil and religious duties together."_
        
    - **Status:** Obsolete (merged with Brahma).
        

### 2. Unapproved Forms (_Aprashasta_)

These forms were historically disapproved because they involved payment (selling the daughter) or force.

5. **Asura:**
    
    - **Definition:** Marriage by **Purchase**. The groom pays a price (_Sulka_ or Bride Price) to the bride's father or kinsmen to secure the marriage.
        
    - **Key Feature:** The transaction looks like a sale. This is distinct from "Dowry" (where the bride's side pays).
        
    - **Status:** Still exists in some communities but is socially discouraged.
        
6. **Gandharva:**
    
    - **Definition:** Marriage by **Mutual Love and Consent**. It is a voluntary union of a maiden and her lover, born of desire.
        
    - **Modern Parallel:** This is the ancient equivalent of the modern "Love Marriage."
        
    - **Status:** Highly relevant today. If a couple falls in love and marries, it is often termed a Gandharva marriage (provided religious rites are performed).
        
7. **Rakshasa:**
    
    - **Definition:** Marriage by **Capture**. The groom forcibly abducts the maiden from her home, often slaying her kinsmen (a relic of warrior times).
        
    - **Status:** Obsolete and criminal.
        
8. **Paishacha:**
    
    - **Definition:** The most base form. The man seduces a girl who is sleeping, intoxicated, or mentally disordered.
        
    - **Status:** Universally condemned. Under modern penal codes, this constitutes rape.
        

### 3. Modern Applicability in Bangladesh

In the current legal landscape of Bangladesh:

- **Standard Presumption:** Every marriage is presumed to be in the **Brahma** form (gift of the daughter) unless proved otherwise.
    
- **Gandharva:** Recognized as valid, provided the essential ceremonies (like _Saptapadi_) are performed. Mutual consent alone without rites does not constitute a valid Hindu marriage.
    
- **Validity:** Even if a marriage falls under an "Unapproved" form (like Asura), it is still **legally valid** if the marriage rites were performed. The "sin" of the father taking money does not invalidate the status of the wife.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Stridhana" Connection:** Why do we study these dead forms? Because of **Inheritance**.
> 
> - If a woman dies without children:
>     
>     - If married in an **Approved Form** (Brahma) $\rightarrow$ Her property goes to her **Husband** and his heirs.
>         
>     - If married in an **Unapproved Form** (Asura) $\rightarrow$ Her property goes to her **Father** and his heirs.
>         
> - _Tip:_ This is a crucial point for inheritance problem questions.
>     

> **Asura vs. Brahma:** The test to distinguish them is: **"Did the father take money for his own benefit?"**
> 
> - If yes = Asura (Sale).
>     
> - If he took money to buy jewelry for the bride = Brahma (Not a sale, just a dowry arrangement).
>     

> **Common Misconception:** Students often think "Gandharva marriage" means living together without marriage. **Incorrect.** Even in Gandharva marriage, the nuptial rites (fire, steps) are necessary for legal validity in modern courtrooms.
