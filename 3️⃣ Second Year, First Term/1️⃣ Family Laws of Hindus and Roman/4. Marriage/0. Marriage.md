## 4. Marriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Marriage is the central pivot of Hindu society. It is the foundation for other legal concepts like legitimacy, maintenance, widowhood, and inheritance. Understanding its sacramental nature is crucial to understanding why divorce is difficult in traditional Hindu law.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a high-priority exam topic. Questions on "Essentials of a Valid Marriage," "Legal Effects," and "Registration" are standard. Problem questions often involve the validity of a marriage performed without certain rites or between minors.
    

---

In Hindu Law, marriage is viewed fundamentally differently than in Western or Muslim law. It is not merely a civil contract but a **religious sacrament** (_Samskara_). It is a holy union for the performance of religious duties (_Dharma_). Traditionally, it is believed to be an indissoluble tie that binds the couple not just in this life, but for seven lives.

However, in the modern context of Bangladesh, while the sacramental nature remains, legislative interventions (like the Child Marriage Restraint Act and Registration Act) have introduced legal requirements that sit alongside religious customs.

---

### 1. Definition, Nature, and Object

#### Nature: A Sacrament

Hindu marriage is a **Sacrament** (_Samskara_), not a contract.

- **Holy Union:** It is the last of the ten sacraments prescribed for purification.
    
- **Indissoluble:** Historically, it could not be dissolved by divorce. The bond was eternal.
    
- **Religious Duty:** A man cannot perform many religious sacrifices (_Yajnas_) without a wife (_Sahadharmini_).
    

#### Object

According to the ancient texts, the object of marriage is threefold:

1. **Dharma (Religious Duty):** To perform religious rites together.
    
2. **Praja (Progeny):** To beget a son ("Putra") who can save the ancestors from hell ("Put") by offering funeral cakes ("Pinda").
    
3. **Rati (Pleasure):** Sexual gratification.
    

---

### 2. Forms (Types) of Marriage

Ancient Hindu texts recognized eight forms of marriage, divided into approved and unapproved forms.

- **Approved Forms (Valid & Respectable):**
    
    1. **Brahma:** The father gifts his daughter to a learned man of good character without receiving any consideration. (This is the most common form today).
        
    2. **Daiva:** Giving the daughter to a priest during a sacrifice.
        
    3. **Arsha:** Giving the daughter after receiving a cow and bull (not as price, but for religious use).
        
    4. **Prajapatya:** Giving the daughter with the condition: "May you both practice Dharma together."
        
- **Unapproved Forms (Disapproved but sometimes Valid):**
    
    5. **Asura:** Marriage by purchase (Paying "Bride Price" or Sulka to the father).
    
    6. **Gandharva:** Marriage by mutual love and consent (Modern "Love Marriage").
    
    7. **Rakshasa:** Marriage by capture or abduction.
    
    8. **Paishacha:** Marriage by seduction/rape of a sleeping or intoxicated girl (Strictly prohibited/void).
    
- **Current Status:** In modern Bangladesh, only the **Brahma** (arranged) and **Gandharva** (love) forms are generally recognized. The others are obsolete.
    

---

### 3. Essentials and Completion of Marriage

For a Hindu marriage to be legally valid in Bangladesh, certain conditions must be met.

#### Essentials of a Valid Marriage

1. **Identity of Caste:** Historically, parties had to be of the same caste. In modern law, inter-caste marriages are generally recognized as valid.
    
2. **Prohibited Degrees:** The parties must not be within the prohibited degrees of relationship (_Sapinda_ relationship). Marriage between close blood relatives is void unless a custom allows it.
    
3. **Ceremonies:** The performance of religious rites is mandatory.
    

#### Completion of Marriage (Ceremonies)

A Hindu marriage is not valid without the performance of customary rites.

- **Saptapadi (Seven Steps):** The bride and groom take seven steps together around the sacred fire. The marriage becomes **complete and binding** on the taking of the **seventh step**.
    
- **Vivaha Homa:** Offering oblations to the sacred fire.
    
- **Customary Rites:** If a community has a specific custom (e.g., exchanging garlands for Sudras), that custom can replace the Vedic rites.
    

---

### 4. Legal Formalities and Registration

#### Applicability of Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017

While Hindu texts allow early marriage, the State law overrides this.

- **Minimum Age:** The groom must be **21** and the bride **18**.
    
- **Effect of Violation:** In Bangladesh, a child marriage is **penal** (punishable with jail/fine) but **not void**. The marriage remains valid if performed with correct religious rites, though the adults involved face punishment. This is a crucial distinction from Indian law.
    

#### Registration (Hindu Marriage Registration Act, 2012)

- **Status:** Registration of Hindu marriage in Bangladesh is currently **optional** (unlike Muslim marriage which is mandatory).
    
- **Purpose:** It serves as conclusive proof of marriage for legal matters (passports, visa, suing for maintenance).
    
- **Non-Registration:** Non-registration does _not_ invalidate the marriage if the religious ceremonies were performed.
    

#### Presumption of Marriage

Where it is proved that a man and woman have lived together continuously as husband and wife for a long period, the law presumes that they are legally married, unless the contrary is clearly proved.

---

### 5. Polygamy and Remarriage

#### Polygamy

- **Traditional Law:** Under strict Hindu Law (Dayabhaga), a man is permitted to have more than one wife at the same time (**Polygamy**).
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** While India abolished polygamy in 1955, in **Bangladesh, Hindu polygamy is still legally valid**. A Hindu man can marry a second wife without the consent of the first, though this is socially rare today. Polyandry (one woman, multiple husbands) is forbidden.
    

#### Remarriage

- **Widow Remarriage:** Historically forbidden. Validated by the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**. A widow can legally remarry.
    
    - _Note:_ Upon remarriage, she generally forfeits her right to maintenance and inheritance from her _first_ husband's estate (limited estate).
        
- **Divorcee Remarriage:** Since strict Hindu law in Bangladesh does not recognize divorce, the concept of "remarriage after divorce" technically does not exist, unless the divorce was valid under a specific custom (e.g., in lower castes).
    

---

### 6. Legal Effects of a Valid Marriage

Once a valid marriage is completed (Saptapadi), the following legal effects arise:

1. **Conjugal Rights:** Both spouses are entitled to the society and comfort of each other.
    
2. **Legitimacy:** Children born of the marriage are legitimate and entitled to inherit.
    
3. **Maintenance:** The wife is entitled to be maintained by her husband for life (as long as she is chaste).
    
4. **Change of Gotra:** The wife enters the _Gotra_ (lineage) of her husband and leaves her father's _Gotra_.
    
5. **Inheritance:** The wife becomes a prospective heir to her husband (under Dayabhaga law).
    
6. **Guardianship:** The husband becomes the lawful guardian of his minor wife.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Saptapadi" Rule:** In a problem question, if the facts say "The couple walked around the fire 5 times and then stopped," there is **NO marriage**. It must be 7 steps.

> **Child Marriage Validity:** A very common trick question: _"A 25-year-old Hindu man marries a 15-year-old Hindu girl. Is the marriage void?"_
> 
> - **Answer:** No. In Bangladesh, it is **Valid but Punishable**. The marriage holds, but the husband can be jailed under the Child Marriage Restraint Act.
>     

> **Registration vs. Validity:** Always emphasize that **Religious Rites (Saptapadi) = Validity**. Registration is merely **Evidence**. You can be married without a certificate, but you cannot be married with _only_ a certificate and no rites.
