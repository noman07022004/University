## 4.6 Polygamy ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** While the concept is simple, understanding _why_ it still exists in Bangladesh (due to the lack of reforms that happened in India) is crucial for comparative legal analysis.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Frequently asked in objective questions or as part of problem questions involving inheritance (e.g., "A man dies leaving two widows. How is the property divided?").


---

Polygamy refers to the practice of having more than one spouse at the same time. In the context of Hindu Law, this is specifically **Polygyny** (one man having multiple wives). Historically, Vedic society favored monogamy, but polygamy was recognized and practiced, especially among kings and the wealthy, often for the purpose of securing a son (_Putra_) if the first wife proved barren.

In the modern legal landscape, there is a sharp divide: while India abolished Hindu polygamy in 1955, **Bangladesh** continues to follow the traditional uncodified law, making polygamy legally valid for Hindu men.

---

### 1. The Traditional Rule: Unlimited Polygyny

Under the ancient Shastric law (Dayabhaga and Mitakshara), a Hindu man has the absolute right to marry any number of wives.

- **No Consent Needed:** He does not require the consent of his first wife to marry the second.
    
- **No Justification Needed:** While ancient moral texts (_Dharma Shastras_) recommended taking a second wife only under specific conditions (e.g., if the first wife is barren, suffers from an incurable disease, or is "sharp-tongued"), these were considered **moral precepts**, not **legal mandates**.
    
- **Legal Validity:** Therefore, even if a man marries a second wife without any "valid" reason and against the wishes of his first wife, the second marriage is **legally valid** and binding.
    

### 2. Status in Bangladesh

In Bangladesh, the law regarding Hindu marriage has remained static since 1947.

- **Legality:** Polygamy is **legal** for Hindu men. There is no statute in Bangladesh (unlike the _Muslim Family Laws Ordinance_ for Muslims) that restricts a Hindu man's right to marry multiple times.
    
- **Social Reality:** While legally permitted, the practice is socially declining due to economic constraints and changing social values. However, purely from a legal standpoint, a Hindu man can theoretically have 100 wives.
    
- **Contrast with India:** In India, the _Hindu Marriage Act, 1955_ made monogamy mandatory. A second marriage there is void and punishable (Bigamy). In Bangladesh, it is neither void nor punishable.
    

### 3. Prohibition of Polyandry

- **Definition:** Polyandry is the practice of a woman having multiple husbands at the same time.
    
- **Legal Status:** Polyandry is strictly **prohibited** in Hindu Law.
    
- **Mythological Exception:** While the _Mahabharata_ depicts Draupadi with five husbands, this is treated as a unique divine exception, not a legal precedent.
    
- **Consequence:** If a Hindu woman marries a second husband while her first husband is alive (and without a valid divorce, which is also rare), the second marriage is **Void ab initio** and she can be prosecuted for Bigamy under the Penal Code.
    

### 4. Rights of Co-Wives

If a man exercises his right to polygamy, what are the rights of the wives?

- **Equal Status:** All wives are legally legitimate. There is no distinction between the "first wife" and "second wife" in terms of status.
    
- **Maintenance:** The husband is personally liable to maintain all his wives.
    
- **Inheritance:** Upon the husband's death, all surviving widows inherit his property.
    
    - **Dayabhaga Rule:** They take the share of the widow as a single unit and divide it equally among themselves.
        
    - _Example:_ If a man leaves 2 widows and 2 sons. The property is divided into 3 shares (1 for Son A, 1 for Son B, and 1 for the Widows collectively). The two widows then split their 1 share equally.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Inheritance Calculation:** This is the most common exam application. Remember: **Multiple widows do NOT get separate shares like sons.**
> 
> - If W1, W2, S1, S2 survive.
>     
> - Total Shares = 3 (Son 1, Son 2, and "The Widows").
>     
> - W1 and W2 share the single widow's share.
>     

> **Crucial Distinction:** **Muslim Law vs. Hindu Law in Bangladesh.**
> 
> - **Muslim Man:** Needs permission from Arbitration Council to marry again.
>     
> - **Hindu Man:** Needs **NO permission** from anyone to marry again.
>     

> **"Reason" for Remarriage:** If a problem states _"Mr. X married a second wife because his first wife was rude,"_ the marriage is valid. The reason is irrelevant legally.
