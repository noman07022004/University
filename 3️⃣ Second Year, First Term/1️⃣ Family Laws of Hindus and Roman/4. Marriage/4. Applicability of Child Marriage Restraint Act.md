## 4.4 Applicability of Child Marriage Restraint Act ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic highlights the clash between "Religious Law" (which permits child marriage) and "Statutory Law" (which prohibits it). Understanding how the court balances these two—by punishing the offender but saving the marriage—is a key legal concept.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a favorite area for problem questions. You will likely face a scenario where a 22-year-old man marries a 16-year-old girl, and you must determine: a) Is the marriage valid? b) Did they commit a crime?
    

---


The ancient Hindu texts (_Smritis_) did not set a minimum age for marriage; in fact, historically, pre-puberty marriage was encouraged. However, the State intervened to curb this practice for social welfare. The primary law governing this in Bangladesh is the **Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017** (which repealed the 1929 Act).

The central legal principle you must grasp is the distinction between **"Validity"** and **"Penality."** While the Act makes child marriage a crime, it does _not_ necessarily make the marriage void under Hindu Law in Bangladesh.

---

### 1. Definitions and Age Limits

The Act applies to all citizens of Bangladesh, regardless of religion. It establishes specific age thresholds to define a "child."

- **Definition of Child:**
    
    - **Male:** A person who has not completed **21 years** of age.
        
    - **Female:** A person who has not completed **18 years** of age.
        
- **Child Marriage:** Any marriage where at least one of the contracting parties is a child.
    

### 2. Legal Status: "Valid but Punishable"

This is the most critical concept for your exams.

- **Validity:** Under Hindu Law in Bangladesh, a marriage is a sacrament (_Samskara_). Once the religious rites (like _Saptapadi_) are completed, the marriage is valid. The _Child Marriage Restraint Act_ does **not** contain any provision that declares a child marriage void.
    
- **Consequence:** Therefore, if a Hindu minor marries, the marriage remains **Valid**, binding, and enforceable. The wife has the right to maintenance and inheritance.
    
- **Punishability:** However, the persons involved (the adult groom, the parents, and the priest) commit a criminal offense and can be jailed.
    
- **Comparison:** This differs from the _Special Marriage Act_, where underage marriage is void. In Hindu Law, the "Sacrament" overrides the "Statute" regarding validity, but the "Statute" overrides "Religion" regarding punishment.
    

### 3. Penalties under the 2017 Act

The 2017 Act introduced stricter penalties compared to the old 1929 law.

- **For Adult Contracting Party:** An adult who marries a child faces imprisonment up to **2 years** or a fine up to **1 Lakh Taka**, or both.
    
- **For Parents/Guardians:** Any person who promotes, permits, or negligently fails to prevent a child marriage faces imprisonment (6 months to 2 years) or a fine up to **50,000 Taka**, or both.
    
- **For the Solemnizer (Priest/Iyer):** The person who conducts the marriage (e.g., the Brahmin priest) faces imprisonment (6 months to 2 years) or a fine up to **50,000 Taka**.
    
- **For the Minor:** Interestingly, the 2017 Act includes a provision to detain the minor for up to 1 month or fine them, but a proviso states that if the adults are punished, the minor generally is not.
    

### 4. The "Special Provision" (Section 19)

The 2017 Act introduced a controversial but legally important exception that did not exist in the 1929 Act.

- **The Exception:** A marriage involving a minor is **not an offense** if it is performed in **"special circumstances"** for the "best interest of the minor".
    
- **Conditions:**
    
    1. Instructions from the Court.
        
    2. Consent of the parents/guardians.
        
    3. Follows the procedure defined in the Rules (2018).
        
- **Implication:** This effectively allows child marriage in limited cases (e.g., accidental pregnancy), providing a legal loophole that protects the parties from criminal prosecution.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The Golden Rule for Problems:** If an exam question asks: _"H (22) marries W (15) with proper Hindu rites. Is the marriage valid?"_
> 
> - **Correct Answer:** Yes, the marriage is **Valid**.
>     
> - **Reason:** The Child Marriage Restraint Act makes it _penal_ (punishable) but does not declare it _void_. The doctrine of _Factum Valet_ cures the defect of age because age is considered a directory (moral) rule in Dayabhaga religious law, not a mandatory legal one for validity.
>     

> **Distinction from India:** Be careful not to use Indian case law. In India, child marriage can be "voidable" (the minor can cancel it). In Bangladesh, it is strictly valid.

> **Section 19:** Mentioning **Section 19 (Special Provision)** in your answer demonstrates that you are up-to-date with the latest 2017 statute, distinguishing you from students quoting the old 1929 Act.
