### 14.5 Modes and Devolution of Property ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This subtopic synthesizes the entire mechanism of inheritance. You must know _how_ the property moves (Does it vanish into the group? Or does it transfer to a specific person?) to solve any legal problem.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Questions frequently ask to "Discuss the modes of devolution under Mitakshara Law" or "How does the doctrine of survivorship operate in Bangladesh?"
    

---

#### I. Introduction: The Two Paths

In Hindu Law, property does not always "inherit" in the English sense of the word. Depending on the nature of the property and the school of law, it devolves (passes down) via two distinct legal mechanisms:

1. **Survivorship:** The extinction of the deceased’s interest and its absorption by the surviving members.
    
2. **Succession:** The transmission of the deceased’s specific title to his defined heirs.
    

---

#### II. Mode 1: Survivorship (Applicable to Mitakshara Joint Property)

This mode is unique to the **Mitakshara School** and applies _only_ to **Joint Family/Ancestral Property**.

1. The Principle

In a Mitakshara coparcenary, no individual member has a specific share (e.g., "I own 1/4th") while the family is undivided. The ownership is vested in the whole body of the coparcenary.

- **Mechanism:** When a coparcener dies, his interest does _not_ descend to his own heirs (like his daughter). Instead, his interest simply extinguishes. The shares of the surviving coparceners automatically increase.
    
- **Maxim:** "The interest of a deceased member lapses on his death and merges in the coparcenary property."
    

2. The Exception in Bangladesh (Crucial)

The strict rule of survivorship was modified by the Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937.

- **Before 1937:** If a coparcener died leaving a widow but no son, the widow got nothing (only maintenance). The brothers took everything by survivorship.
    
- **After 1937:** The Act states that the widow of a deceased coparcener shall have the **same interest** in the property as her husband had.
    
    - _Effect:_ This "arrests" (stops) the rule of survivorship. The widow steps into her husband's shoes. She can even demand partition. However, if she dies, her share goes back to the husband's heirs or the coparcenary, not her own heirs.
        

---

#### III. Mode 2: Succession (Applicable to Dayabhaga & Separate Property)

This mode applies to:

1. **All property** under the **Dayabhaga School** (Joint or Separate).
    
2. **Separate/Self-acquired property** under the **Mitakshara School**.
    

3. The Principle

Succession is the transmission of property from the dead owner to the living heir.

- **Mechanism:** The heir derives their title _from_ the deceased. The estate does not fluctuate; it passes definitively.
    
- **Order of Heirs:** The law provides a strict list of heirs (e.g., Son → Grandson → Widow → Daughter). The nearest heir on the list takes the property, excluding the more remote ones.
    

**2. Per Stirpes vs. Per Capita**

- **Per Stirpes (By Roots):** Used for male descendants. (e.g., If a son is dead, his share goes to his sons).
    
- **Per Capita (By Heads):** Used for other heirs (e.g., If inheritance goes to two daughters, they share equally per head).
    

---

#### IV. Summary Comparison

|**Feature**|**Survivorship**|**Succession**|
|---|---|---|
|**School**|Mitakshara (Joint Property only).|Dayabhaga (All property) & Mitakshara (Separate property).|
|**Event**|Operates on the death of a Coparcener.|Operates on the death of the Owner (_Prepositus_).|
|**Effect**|Deceased's interest merges with survivors.|Deceased's interest transfers to specific heirs.|
|**Shares**|Shares are fluctuating/undefined until partition.|Shares are defined and specific.|
|**Females**|Generally excluded (except Widow under 1937 Act).|Can inherit (Widow, Daughter, Mother, etc.).|

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Floating" Interest:** Describe Mitakshara coparcenary interest as "floating" or "fluctuating." It solidifies only upon partition.
    
- **Problem Question Tip:**
    
    - _Scenario:_ A (Mitakshara father) and B (Son) own ancestral land. A dies.
        
    - _Result:_ B takes the whole property by **Survivorship**, not succession. (Unless A left a widow, who claims her share under the 1937 Act).
        
- **Dayabhaga Simplicity:** Remember that for Bengali Hindus (Dayabhaga), you _never_ apply Survivorship. Even if brothers live together, when one dies, his share goes to his widow/sons by **Succession**.
