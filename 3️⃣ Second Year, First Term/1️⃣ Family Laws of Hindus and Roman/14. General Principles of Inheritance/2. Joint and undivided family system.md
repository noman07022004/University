### 14.2 Joint and Undivided Family System ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The "Joint Family" is the fundamental unit of Hindu society. You cannot understand property law without distinguishing between the "Joint Family" (social unit) and the "Coparcenary" (legal property-holding unit).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Examiners often ask for the distinction between a "Joint Family" and a "Coparcenary," or the rights of a coparcener versus a simple member.
    

---

#### I. Concept and Composition

The Hindu Joint Family is a patriarchal organization that is the normal condition of Hindu society.

**1. Definition of Joint Family** A Hindu Joint Family consists of all persons lineally descended from a common male ancestor, and includes their:

- Wives
    
- Unmarried Daughters (upon marriage, a daughter leaves her father's joint family and joins her husband's).
    

It is a **large body** that includes men, women, and children. Every member has a right to **maintenance** (food, shelter, clothing) from the family funds, but not everyone has a right to the _property_ itself.

**2. Presumption of Jointness**

- **The Legal Rule:** There is a presumption in Hindu Law that every Hindu family is joint in food, worship, and estate.
    
- **Burden of Proof:** If a person claims that the family is _separated_ (partitioned), the burden lies on them to prove it.
    

---

#### II. The Coparcenary (The Inner Circle)

Inside the large "Joint Family," there exists a smaller, exclusive body called the **Coparcenary**.

**1. Definition** A Coparcenary is a narrower body of persons who have a **legal right to the property**. Not all members of the Joint Family are coparceners.

**2. Composition (The 4-Generation Rule)** Under **Mitakshara Law**, the coparcenary is limited to the **Father and his three male lineal descendants**.

- Father (Generation 1)
    
- Son (Generation 2)
    
- Grandson (Generation 3)
    
- Great-Grandson (Generation 4)
    

**The Rule of Limit:** The great-great-grandson is _not_ a coparcener as long as the original Father is alive. He is removed by more than 3 degrees.

**3. Rights of a Coparcener**

- **Right by Birth:** He acquires an interest in the property the moment he is born.
    
- **Right to Demand Partition:** He can demand that the property be divided and take his share.
    
- **Right of Survivorship:** If he dies without male issue, his share goes to the other coparceners (subject to the 1937 Act).
    

---

#### III. Difference Between Schools (Crucial)

The concept of the Joint Family operates very differently in the two schools of law.

**1. Mitakshara Joint Family**

- **Foundation:** Right by birth (_Janmasvatvavada_).
    
- **Coparcenary:** Begins immediately. A father and son are coparceners. The father cannot sell ancestral property without the son's consent (except for legal necessity).
    
- **Interest:** The interest of a coparcener is **fluctuating**. It decreases if a new member is born and increases if a member dies. No one can say "I own 1/3rd" until a partition actually happens.
    

**2. Dayabhaga Joint Family (Bangladesh)**

- **Foundation:** Right arising on death (_Uparamasvatvavada_).
    
- **No Father-Son Coparcenary:** While the father is alive, he is the **Absolute Owner**. The son has no right to the property, no right to demand partition, and no right to stop the father from selling it.
    
- **When it Form:** A Dayabhaga Coparcenary only forms **after the father dies**. The sons inherit the property together and live as a joint family.
    
- **Interest:** The coparceners (brothers) hold **Defined Shares** (Quasi-Severalty). Each brother knows his share (e.g., 1/3rd). It does not fluctuate.
    

---

#### IV. The Karta (Manager)

The head of the Joint Family is called the **Karta**.

- **Who:** Usually the senior-most male member (Father or eldest brother).
    
- **Power:** He has vast powers to manage the property, represent the family in legal suits, and receive income. He is not accountable for how he spends the money unless there is proof of fraud or misappropriation.
    

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Females in Coparcenary:**
    
    - _Historically:_ Females were never coparceners; they were only members of the Joint Family entitled to maintenance.
        
    - _Statutory Change:_ The **Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937** gave the widow the _same interest_ as her deceased husband. While it didn't technically make her a "coparcener" (she couldn't become Karta), it gave her the right to demand partition, effectively putting her inside the property-holding circle.
        
- **Exam Trap:** If a question asks _"Does a son have a right to partition in Bangladesh?"_
    
    - **Answer:** Generally **NO**, because most Bengali Hindus follow Dayabhaga law, where the son has no rights while the father is alive. If the family is Mitakshara (e.g., from Up-country/Bihar settled in Bangladesh), then **YES**.
        
- **Diagram Tip:** In an exam, draw two circles. A big circle labeled "Joint Family" (includes wives, daughters) and a smaller circle inside labeled "Coparcenary" (only males + widow under 1937 Act). This visual explains the concept instantly.
