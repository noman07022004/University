### 14.1 Basics (Inheritance) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This subtopic defines the vocabulary of Hindu Law. You cannot understand "Survivorship" or "Coparcenary" if you do not understand the distinction between _Apratibandha Daya_ and _Sapratibandha Daya_.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While less likely to be a standalone long essay, these definitions are often required for 2-mark definitions or as the introductory paragraph for problem questions.
    

---

#### I. Definition of Inheritance (_Daya_)

In Hindu Law, the term for inheritance is **_Daya_** (Heritage). It refers to property which a person holds as a result of their relationship to a deceased owner. The person who inherits is the _Dayada_ (Heir), and the deceased owner whose property is being distributed is legally termed the **Prepositus** (Propositus).

The central question in Hindu inheritance is: _In what capacity does the person take the property?_

1. **By Survivorship:** Taking a share because you were already a co-owner (Coparcener) and the other co-owner died.
    
2. **By Succession:** Taking a share because the owner died and the law designates you as the new owner.
    

---

#### II. Classification of Heritage (_Daya_)

The Mitakshara school divides heritage into two fundamental categories. This distinction is vital for understanding when "Inheritance" actually begins.

**1. Unobstructed Heritage (_Apratibandha Daya_)**

- **Definition:** Property in which a person acquires an interest **by birth**.
    
- **Context:** This applies _only_ to the Mitakshara Joint Family. When a son is born, he instantly becomes a co-owner of his father's/grandfather's ancestral property.
    
- **"Unobstructed":** The right is not "obstructed" by the death of the owner. The son doesn't have to wait for the father to die to have an interest; he has it while the father is alive.
    

**2. Obstructed Heritage (_Sapratibandha Daya_)**

- **Definition:** Property in which a person acquires an interest only **on the death** of the last owner.
    
- **Context:** This applies to:
    
    - All property under the **Dayabhaga School** (where there is no right by birth).
        
    - Separate/Self-acquired property under the **Mitakshara School**.
        
- **"Obstructed":** The right is "obstructed" by the life of the owner. As long as the owner is alive, the heir has _no_ right, only a hope (_Spes Successionis_).
    

---

#### III. The Two Systems of Inheritance in Bangladesh

Since Bangladesh follows both schools (Dayabhaga for Bengali Hindus, Mitakshara for others), the "Basics" differ based on the community.

**1. Under Dayabhaga Law (Bengal School)**

- **Uniformity:** There is only **one** mode of devolution: **Succession**.
    
- **No Survivorship:** Even if a father and sons live together, the father is the absolute owner. When he dies, the sons inherit by succession (_Per Stirpes_).
    
- **Spiritual Basis:** The right to inherit is strictly based on the capacity to offer spiritual benefit (_Pinda_) to the deceased.
    

**2. Under Mitakshara Law**

- **Duality:** There are **two** modes of devolution:
    
    - **Survivorship:** Applies to **Joint Family/Ancestral Property**. When a coparcener dies, his share merges into the surviving coparceners. (Note: The _Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937_ creates an exception here, allowing the widow to step in).
        
    - **Succession:** Applies to **Separate Property** (property the man earned himself). This passes to his heirs (Widow, Son, Daughter, etc.) by succession rules.
        
- **Consanguinity:** The right to inherit is based on blood relationship (_Propinquity_), not just spiritual offerings.
    

---

#### IV. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Prepositus":** In exam problems, always identify the _Prepositus_ first. (e.g., "In this problem, X is the Prepositus..."). It shows the examiner you know legal terminology.
    
- **The 1937 Act:** You cannot discuss "Basics" in Bangladesh without mentioning that the _Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937_ modified the basic concept of Survivorship. It gave the widow the same share as a son, even in undivided property, disrupting the pure Mitakshara "male-only" survivorship.
    
- **Terminological Precision:**
    
    - _Ancestral Property:_ Inherited from Father, Paternal Grandfather, or Great-Grandfather.
        
    - _Separate Property:_ Earned by oneself or inherited from others (like uncle, brother, mother).
