
### 14.4 Difference of rules for male and female ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The distinction between genders is the most defining feature of classical Hindu law. If you treat a daughter's inheritance the same way as a son's (regarding what they can _do_ with the property), your legal analysis will be fundamentally flawed in the context of Bangladesh.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often ask to "Distinguish between the nature of estate taken by a male and a female heir."
    

---

#### I. Introduction: The Patriarchal Foundation

Classical Hindu Law is inherently patriarchal. The rules governing inheritance differ radically depending on whether the heir is male or female. These differences manifest in **three key areas**: the nature of the ownership they acquire, the order in which they inherit, and how that property passes on after their death.

#### II. Nature of the Estate (Absolute vs. Limited)

This is the most critical legal distinction.

**1. Male Heirs: Absolute Estate**

- **The Rule:** Generally, when a male inherits property (whether from his father, uncle, or brother), he takes it as an **Absolute Owner**.
    
- **Powers:** He has full power to sell, mortgage, gift, or destroy the property (subject only to the rights of coparceners in Mitakshara joint property).
    
- **Fresh Stock of Descent:** He becomes a "fresh stock of descent." When he dies, the property passes to _his own_ heirs.
    

**2. Female Heirs: Limited Estate**

- **The Rule:** When a female inherits property from a _male_ (e.g., a widow from her husband, a daughter from her father), she takes only a **Limited Estate** (Widow's Estate).
    
- **Powers:** She is the owner, but she **cannot alienate** (sell/transfer) the corpus of the property except for "Legal Necessity."
    
- **No Fresh Stock:** She does _not_ become a fresh stock of descent. When she dies, the property does not go to her heirs; it reverts to the heirs of the last full male owner (Reversioners).
    

> **Exception (Stridhana):** If the property is _Stridhana_ (e.g., gifts received at marriage), the female holds it as an **Absolute Owner**, just like a male. However, inherited property is rarely Stridhana.

#### III. Mode of Devolution

The rules for how property descends differ based on the sex of the holder.

**1. Succession to a Male**

- **Priority:** The law prioritizes **Agnates** (relations through males).
    
- **Structure:** The property stays within the agnatic family line as much as possible to ensure the performance of spiritual rites (_Pinda_).
    
- **Female Inclusion:** Females are generally excluded unless specifically named by texts or statute (e.g., The _Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937_ admitted the widow; ancient texts admitted the daughter, mother, paternal grandmother).
    

**2. Succession to a Female (Stridhana)**

- **Priority:** Succession to a woman's absolute property (_Stridhana_) follows a completely different chain.
    
- **Female Preference:** It often prioritizes **female heirs**. For example, in many forms of Stridhana, daughters inherit before sons.
    
- **Line:** It traces relationships through the female line (cognates) more readily than the male succession rules do.
    

#### IV. Co-heirs and Survivorship

When multiple heirs of the same sex inherit together, the rules differ:

**1. Male Co-heirs (e.g., Two Sons)**

- **Dayabhaga:** They take as Tenants-in-Common (defined shares).
    
- **Mitakshara:** They take as Joint Tenants (Coparceners) with the right of survivorship.
    

**2. Female Co-heirs (e.g., Two Daughters)**

- **The Rule:** Under _both_ schools, two daughters inheriting from their father take the property **jointly with the right of survivorship**.
    
- **Implication:** If Daughter A dies, her share goes to the surviving Daughter B, _not_ to Daughter A's own children. The property remains with the female line until the last female heir dies, at which point it reverts to the father's heirs.
    

---

#### V. Summary Table of Differences

|**Feature**|**Male Heir**|**Female Heir**|
|---|---|---|
|**Nature of Interest**|Absolute Owner (usually).|Limited Owner (Widow's Estate).|
|**Power of Alienation**|Unrestricted (unless joint family property).|Restricted (only for Legal Necessity).|
|**Devolution on Death**|Passes to _his own_ heirs.|Reverts to the _last male owner's_ heirs.|
|**Co-heirs Rule**|Can hold as Tenants-in-Common (Dayabhaga).|Always hold as Joint Tenants with Survivorship (Two Widows/Daughters).|
|**Right by Birth**|Yes (Mitakshara Son).|No (in classical/Bangladesh law).|

---

#### VI. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Fresh Stock of Descent" Concept:** Use this phrase in exams. It means starting a new line of inheritance. Males start it; females (inheriting estate) generally continue the old line until they die.
    
- **Status of Women in Bangladesh:** Be careful **not** to apply the Indian _Hindu Succession Act, 1956_, which abolished the limited estate and made women absolute owners. In Bangladesh, the "Difference of Rules" described above is still the **active, enforceable law**.
    
- **Daughter vs. Son:** A common problem question involves a man dying leaving a son and a daughter. The son takes the whole property (Absolute Estate). The daughter takes nothing (unless there is no son/son's son, or unless she claims maintenance).
