### 14.3 Guiding Principles under Two Schools ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the philosophical heart of Hindu Law. The reason _why_ a son inherits before a nephew differs completely between the schools. If you don't grasp this, the order of succession will seem like a random list of names.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** "Distinguish between Dayabhaga and Mitakshara principles of inheritance" is arguably the most common essay question in this entire course.
    

---

#### I. Introduction: The Fundamental Divergence

While both schools rely on the same ancient texts (Smritis like Manu and Yajnavalkya), their commentators interpreted the word **"Sapinda"** (relation) differently. This difference in interpretation led to two distinct guiding principles for inheritance.

1. **Dayabhaga School (Jimutavahana):** Focuses on **Religious Efficacy** (Spiritual Benefit).
    
2. **Mitakshara School (Vijnaneswara):** Focuses on **Propinquity** (Blood Relationship).
    

---

#### II. Dayabhaga Principle: Doctrine of Spiritual Benefit

The Dayabhaga school, governing Bengali Hindus, bases the right to inherit strictly on the duty to offer funeral oblations to the deceased.

1. The Logic:

Wealth is meant for the spiritual benefit of the owner. Therefore, the person who can offer the most spiritual benefit (Pinda) to the soul of the deceased is entitled to the property.

2. The Meaning of "Sapinda" (Connected by Pinda):

Under Dayabhaga, a "Sapinda" is one who is connected by the offering of the funeral cake (Pinda).

3. Hierarchy of Spiritual Offerings:

The heirs are ranked by the "quality" of the offering they can make:

- **Sapindas (Highest Class):** Those who offer the **entire Pinda** (undivided funeral cake) to the deceased.
    
    - _Includes:_ Son, Grandson, Great-Grandson (closest agnatic males).
        
- **Sakulyas (Middle Class):** Those who offer the **Pinda-lepa** (remnants/crumbs of the cake) that remain on the hand after the offering.
    
    - _Includes:_ 4th to 6th generation ancestors and descendants.
        
- **Samanodakas (Lowest Class):** Those who offer only **Udaka** (libations of water).
    
    - _Includes:_ 7th to 14th generation relations.
        

> **Key Consequence:** Because offering Pindas is the key, cognates (relations through females, like a sister's son) are often preferred over distant agnates in Dayabhaga, because a sister's son offers Pindas to his maternal uncle, whereas a distant agnate might only offer water.

---

#### III. Mitakshara Principle: Doctrine of Propinquity

The Mitakshara school, governing the rest of the subcontinent (and non-Bengali Hindus in Bangladesh), bases inheritance on biological closeness.

1. The Logic:

One's property goes to one's own blood. The nearer the blood relationship, the stronger the claim.

2. The Meaning of "Sapinda" (Connected by Body):

Vijnaneswara revolutionized Hindu law by redefining "Sapinda." He argued that "Pinda" means "body" (Pinda = Body).

- Therefore, _Sapinda_ relationship means **"Community of particles of the same body."**
    
- If two people share common body particles from a common ancestor, they are Sapindas.
    

**3. The Rule of Preference:**

- **Consanguinity:** The primary test is: _Who is biologically closer?_
    
- **Agnates over Cognates:** While it recognizes blood ties through females (Cognates/Bandhus), it strongly prefers relations through males (**Agnates/Gotrajas**) first. A very distant agnate (e.g., a distant male cousin) will exclude a close cognate (e.g., a sister's son).
    

---

#### IV. Practical Differences Resulting from Principles

This philosophical difference leads to concrete differences in the list of heirs:

|**Feature**|**Dayabhaga (Spiritual Benefit)**|**Mitakshara (Blood Propinquity)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Cognates (Bandhus)**|Highly favored. Many cognates (like Sister's Son, Father's Sister's Son) come _very early_ in the list because they offer Pindas.|Disfavored. They inherit only after _all_ Agnates (male-line relatives) are exhausted.|
|**Father vs. Mother**|The **Father** inherits before the Mother. (Reason: Father offers Pindas to the ancestors, Mother does not).|The **Mother** inherits before the Father. (Reason: Blood propinquity—Mother is biologically closer/more certain).|
|**Exclude vs. Share**|Strictly follows "Nearer excludes the more remote."|Generally follows this, but applies the Doctrine of Survivorship in joint property.|

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Sister's Son" Example:** This is the best example to use in exams.
    
    - _Under Dayabhaga:_ The Sister's Son is a very high-ranking heir (he offers Pindas to his maternal uncle).
        
    - _Under Mitakshara:_ He is a "Bandhu" (Cognate). He will not inherit if there is even a distant agnatic cousin 7 degrees away.
        
- **Definition of Sapinda:** Memorize the two definitions:
    
    - _Dayabhaga:_ "Connected by the funeral cake."
        
    - _Mitakshara:_ "Connected by particles of the same body."
        
- **Bangladesh Context:** Always clarify that for Bengali Hindus, the _Dayabhaga_ principle (Spiritual Benefit) is the one that applies. Do not apply the blood-relationship test unless the family is verified as non-Bengali.
