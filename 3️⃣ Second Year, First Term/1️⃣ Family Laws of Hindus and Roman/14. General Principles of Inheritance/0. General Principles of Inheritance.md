## 14. General Principles of Inheritance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the bedrock of Hindu Law. Without grasping the difference between "Survivorship" and "Succession," or the philosophical difference between the Mitakshara and Dayabhaga schools, you cannot solve any problem regarding property distribution.  
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Almost every exam paper contains a problem question requiring you to calculate shares or determine the rightful heir. These principles are the formulas you must apply.


---

### Introduction

Inheritance (Succession) in Hindu Law is the method by which property passes from a deceased person to their heirs. Unlike other personal laws (like Muslim or Christian law), Hindu law in Bangladesh is not uniform; it is governed by two distinct schools—**Mitakshara** and **Dayabhaga**—which have fundamentally different rules on how property devolves.

Furthermore, the law distinguishes between two types of property: **Joint Family Property** and **Separate Property**. The rules of inheritance differ vastly depending on the nature of the property and the school of law applicable.

---

### I. The Joint and Undivided Family System

**Syllabus Covered:** _14.2 Joint and undivided family system_

To understand inheritance, one must first understand the unique Hindu institution of the Joint Family.

1. **Concept:** A Hindu Joint Family consists of all persons lineally descended from a common male ancestor, including their wives and unmarried daughters.
    
2. **Coparcenary:** Inside the joint family is a narrower body called the _Coparcenary_. This includes only those males who have a right to the joint property by birth.
    
    - It consists of the Father and his three male lineal descendants (Son, Grandson, Great-grandson).
        
3. **School Distinction:**
    
    - **Mitakshara:** Recognizes the **Right by Birth** (_Janmasvatvavada_). A son becomes a co-owner of the ancestral property the moment he is born.
        
    - **Dayabhaga (Bengal School):** Does **not** recognize the right by birth. The father is the absolute owner of all property (ancestral or self-acquired) during his lifetime. Sons have no claim until the father dies (_Uparamasvatvavada_ - right arising on death).
        

---

### II. Modes and Devolution of Property

**Syllabus Covered:** _14.1 Basics, 14.5 Modes and devolution of property_

Property devolves (passes on) in two distinct ways in Hindu Law:

**1. Survivorship (Applicable only to Mitakshara Joint Property)**

- **Principle:** In a Mitakshara Joint Family, no individual coparcener has a specific share (e.g., 1/3rd or 1/4th) while the family is undivided. His interest is fluctuating—it increases if a member dies and decreases if a member is born.
    
- **The Rule:** When a coparcener dies, his interest does _not_ pass to his own heirs (like his daughter). Instead, it passes by **Survivorship** to the remaining living coparceners.
    
- **Exception in Bangladesh:** The _Hindu Women’s Rights to Property Act, 1937_ created a massive exception. It states that even in a Mitakshara joint family, the **widow** of a deceased coparcener takes his share (as a limited owner), preventing the rule of survivorship from excluding her.
    

**2. Succession (Inheritance)**

- **Applicability:**
    
    - **Dayabhaga:** Applies to _all_ property (both joint and separate) because there is no survivorship in the Bengal school.
        
    - **Mitakshara:** Applies only to the _separate/self-acquired_ property of a male.
        
- **The Rule:** The property goes to specific heirs listed by law (e.g., Son, then Widow, then Daughter, etc.).

### III. Guiding Principles under Two Schools

**Syllabus Covered:** _14.3 Guiding principles under two schools_

When determining who inherits, the two schools use different "tests" to prioritize heirs.

**1. Dayabhaga School: Doctrine of Spiritual Benefit**

- **Principle:** The right to inherit is based on the capacity to offer funeral cakes (_Pinda_) for the spiritual salvation of the deceased.
    
- **Logic:** The person who can offer the _Pinda_ that most benefits the soul of the deceased is the nearest heir.
    
- **Hierarchy of Heirs:**
    
    - _Sapindas:_ Those who offer full Pindas (e.g., Son, Grandson).
        
    - _Sakulyas:_ Those who offer Pinda-lepa (remnants).
        
    - _Samanodakas:_ Those who offer water (Libations).
        

**2. Mitakshara School: Doctrine of Propinquity**

- **Principle:** Inheritance is based on **blood relationship** (Consanguinity).
    
- **Logic:** The person who is nearest in blood (closest genetic relationship) inherits.
    
- **Preference:** It strongly prefers **Agnates** (relations through males) over **Cognates** (relations through females), regardless of who offers the Pinda.

### IV. Difference of Rules for Male and Female

**Syllabus Covered:** _14.4 Difference of rules for male and female_

Hindu law treats males and females differently regarding _what_ they inherit and _how_ they hold it.

**1. Nature of Estate**

- **Males:** Generally take property as **Absolute Owners**. They can sell, gift, or will it away (subject to coparcenary restrictions in Mitakshara).
    
- **Females:** Take property as **Limited Owners** (Widow's Estate). They cannot alienate the corpus except for legal necessity.
    

**2. Order of Succession**

- **Succession _to_ a Male:** The primary heirs are mostly males (Son, Grandson). Females were traditionally excluded, but specific statutory exceptions (Widow, Daughter, Mother, Paternal Grandmother) are recognized as heirs.
    
- **Succession _to_ a Female (Stridhana):** Property held absolutely by a woman (Stridhana) devolves to her own heirs (e.g., daughters often take precedence over sons in Stridhana succession), which is totally different from succession to a male.

### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The 1937 Act:** In Bangladesh, you **must** mention the _Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937_. This Act is the bridge that gives the Widow a share equal to a son in both separate and agricultural land (after 1947 amendments). Without this Act, under pure Mitakshara law, a widow would get nothing from the joint estate.
    
- **Per Stirpes vs. Per Capita:**
    
    - **Per Stirpes (According to Roots/Branch):** Used for male descendants (Son, Grandson). If a son is dead, his sons take his share together.
        
    - **Per Capita (According to Heads):** Used for daughters (in some contexts) or collaterals. Everyone takes an equal share individually.
        
- **Comparison Tip:** If asked to distinguish Dayabhaga and Mitakshara inheritance:
    
    - _Dayabhaga_ = Spiritual Benefit (Pinda).
        
    - _Mitakshara_ = Blood Propinquity.
        
- **Common Exam Trap:** Do not apply the _Indian Hindu Succession Act, 1956_. That law made women absolute owners and unified the schools. In Bangladesh, we still follow the uncodified/classical law (with the 1937 Act).
