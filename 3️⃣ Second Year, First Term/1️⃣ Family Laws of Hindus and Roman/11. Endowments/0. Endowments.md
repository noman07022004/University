## 11. Endowments (_Debuttar_)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This topic introduces the legal framework where a "Deity" is treated as a property owner. Understanding the distinction between the "Ideal Owner" (the Idol) and the "Practical Manager" (_Shebait_) is the key to solving property disputes in this area.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A high-priority topic. Questions frequently ask about the "Essential requisites of a valid endowment" or the "Alienation powers of a Shebait."

### Introduction

An Endowment (_Debuttar_) is the dedication of property for a religious or charitable purpose. In Hindu Law, piety (_Dharma_) is expressed through gifts to Idols, temples, and maths. Once dedicated, the property ceases to belong to the donor and legally vests in the Idol (Deity) or the institution.

---

### 11.1 Categories

Endowments are classified based on the beneficiaries and the nature of the institution.

1. **Public vs. Private:**
    
    - **Public (_Sarvajanin_):** Dedicated for the use of the public at large. Anyone can worship there as of right (e.g., Dhakeshwari Temple).
        
    - **Private (_Pariwarik_):** Dedicated for the worship of the family deity (_Ishta Devata_) by family members only. The public has no right of entry.
        
2. **Religious vs. Charitable:**
    
    - **Religious:** For the worship of a God/Goddess (e.g., establishing a temple).
        
    - **Charitable:** For the benefit of humanity (e.g., digging tanks, establishing hospitals/Dharamshalas).
        
3. **Math vs. Temple:**
    
    - **Temple (_Debuttar_):** A place of worship where the Idol is the central element.
        
    - **Math (_Mutt_):** A monastery or place of religious instruction presided over by a _Mahant_ (spiritual head) for ascetics.
        
4. **Real vs. Illusory (Sham):**
    
    - **Real:** A genuine transfer of ownership to the Deity.
        
    - **Illusory:** A deed created merely to defraud creditors or escape taxes, where the donor acts as the owner. These are void.
        

---

### 11.2 Essential Requisites

For an endowment to be valid, four essential requirements must be met:

1. **Sankalpa (Intention):** There must be a clear resolve or determination to dedicate the property.
    
2. **Utsarga (Renunciation):** The formal renunciation of ownership by the donor. He must divest himself of the property completely.
    
3. **Property:** There must be specific property designated for the endowment.
    
4. **Object:** There must be a definite religious or charitable object (e.g., an Idol).
    

_Note:_ Unlike a secular Trust, no writing or registration is strictly necessary for a Hindu endowment (unless created by Will), but in practice, a deed is usually executed to prove the "Utsarga".

---

### 11.3 Intention and Purpose

- **Intention:** The intention to dedicate must be clear and unambiguous. A mere entry in a ledger or setting aside some income is not enough; there must be a permanent divestment of title.
    
- **Purpose:** The purpose must be either **Ishta** (sacrificial/spiritual, like temple worship) or **Purta** (charitable/social, like building a well). Both are valid under Hindu Law. The purpose must be lawful and not opposed to public policy.
    

---

### 11.4 Capacity of the Settlor

Who can create an endowment?

- **Sound Mind & Majority:** The settlor (founder) must be of sound mind and a major (18+ years).
    
- **Title:** He must have the legal title to the property.
    
    - **Dayabhaga Rule:** A father in Bangladesh has absolute power to dedicate any part of his property (ancestral or self-acquired) for religious purposes.
        
    - **Widow:** A widow can dedicate a reasonable portion of her husband's estate for his spiritual benefit, but not the whole estate.
        

---

### 11.5 Object

The "Object" refers to the beneficiary or the purpose of the trust.

- **The Idol:** In a temple endowment, the object is the **Idol** itself. The Idol is a **Juristic Person** capable of holding property.
    
- **Definiteness:** The object must be definite. A bequest for "Dharma" (religion) generally is considered too vague and void by courts, whereas a bequest for "feeding the poor" or "worship of Goddess Kali" is valid.
    

---

### 11.6 Subject of Endowment

- **Specific Property:** The property dedicated must be specific and capable of identification.
    
- **Description:** The deed must specify the boundaries or details (e.g., "The land at Plot 105"). A vague dedication like "I give some of my land" is invalid.
    
- **Scope:** Both movable (money, jewelry) and immovable (land) property can be the subject of an endowment.
    

---

### 11.7 Ascertainability

- **Beneficiary:** The beneficiary (the Idol or the charity) must be **ascertainable**.
    
- **Public Trusts:** In a public endowment, the beneficiaries are the general public. While individual members fluctuate, the "public" as a class is considered ascertainable.
    
- **Certainty:** If the trust is for "purposes which the manager thinks proper," it fails for uncertainty. It must be for a specific deity or specific charitable activity.
    

---

### 11.8 Powers, Functions, and Duties of a _Shebait_ and _Mahant_

Since the Idol is a "perpetual minor," it needs a human manager.

#### A. The Shebait (Manager of a Temple)

- **Nature:** A Shebait is the "human ministrant" of the Deity. He is **not the owner** of the property; title vests in the Idol.
    
- **Duties:**
    
    1. **Spiritual:** To conduct _Puja_ (worship) and maintain the sanctity of the temple.
        
    2. **Temporal:** To manage the property, collect rent, and repair the temple buildings.
        
- **Powers:**
    
    1. **Borrowing:** Can borrow money for the service of the Idol or repairs of the temple.
        
    2. **Suit:** Represents the Idol in all legal proceedings (e.g., filing a suit for rent).
        

#### B. The Mahant (Head of a Math)

- **Nature:** The Mahant is the spiritual head of a _Math_ (monastery).
    
- **Status:** He has a wider status than a Shebait. He is a "trustee" of the Math's property but also enjoys a beneficial interest in the income for his dignity and maintenance.
    
- **Duties:** To impart religious instruction to disciples (_Chela_) and manage the affairs of the Math.
    

---

### 11.9 Alienation of Debutter Property

Can the temple land be sold?

- **General Rule:** Debutter property is **inalienable**. The Shebait cannot sell it for his own gain or pleasure.
    
- **Exception (Legal Necessity):** The Shebait can alienate (sell/mortgage) the property **ONLY** in case of **"Legal Necessity"** or for the **"Benefit of the Estate"**.
    
    - _Example of Necessity:_ The temple is crumbling and needs urgent repairs, or government tax must be paid to save the land from auction.
        
- **Burden of Proof:** The burden lies on the **Alienee (Buyer)** to prove that there was a legal necessity or that he made bona fide inquiries into it. If he fails, the sale can be set aside.
    
- **Sanctity of the Temple:** The actual temple building (where the Idol resides) is generally absolutely inalienable, as selling it would destroy the very purpose of the endowment.
