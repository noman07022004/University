## 11.8 Powers, Functions, and Duties of a _Shebait_ and _Mahant_ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the operational heart of an endowment. The Deity cannot act on its own; the human manager acts for it. Distinguishing between a _Shebait_ (Temple Manager) and a _Mahant_ (Math Head) is crucial, as is understanding that they are **not** owners but have a special "property" in their office.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A very high-yield topic. Questions often ask: "Discuss the legal position of a Shebait" or "Can a Shebait sell temple property?"
    

---

### Introduction

Since the Hindu Idol is a "perpetual minor," it requires a human guardian to manage its property and conduct its worship. In the case of a Temple (_Debuttar_), this manager is called the **Shebait**. In the case of a Math (_Mutt_), the spiritual head is called the **Mahant**.

Their position is unique in jurisprudence. They are **not trustees** in the strict English sense (because the property does not vest in them), but their duties are similar to trustees. The Privy Council described the Shebait's position as a blend of **"Office and Property"**—it is a job (office) but also a right (property) that can sometimes be inherited.

---

### 1. The Shebait (Manager of a Temple)

#### **A. Legal Status**

- **Not an Owner:** The property vests in the Idol, not the Shebait. The Shebait is merely a manager or custodian.
    
- **Not a Mere Servant:** However, he is not just a hired priest (_Pujari_). He has a personal interest and rights in the endowment. He represents the Deity in all earthly matters.
    

#### **B. Functions and Duties**

1. **Spiritual Duties (Seva-Puja):** His primary duty is to ensure the worship of the Deity is conducted according to the Shastras and the founder's directions. He must maintain the sanctity of the temple.
    
2. **Custody of Property:** He must take care of the Idol and its valuable ornaments/property with the same care a prudent man would take of his own goods.
    
3. **Accounts:** He must keep clear and accurate accounts of the income and expenditure of the temple. In a public endowment, he is accountable to the public/devotees.
    

#### **C. Powers**

1. **Management:** He has the general power to do all things necessary for the benefit of the estate (e.g., collecting rent, repairing buildings).
    
2. **Right to Sue:** The Shebait is the only person entitled to file a lawsuit on behalf of the Deity. The Idol sues _through_ the Shebait.
    
3. **Borrowing Power:** He can borrow money, but **only** for "Legal Necessity" (e.g., to repair a collapsing temple wall or conduct an essential festival).
    
4. **Alienation:** (See Subtopic 11.9 for details). He generally cannot sell the property unless there is an unavoidable necessity.
    

---

### 2. The Mahant (Head of a Math)

#### **A. Legal Status**

- **Spiritual Head:** A _Math_ is a monastery for ascetics. The _Mahant_ is its spiritual and administrative head.
    
- **Superior Position:** His position is higher than a Shebait. While he is also a manager/trustee of the Math's property, he typically has a **beneficial interest** in the income. He is expected to support himself and his disciples (_Chelas_) from the Math's funds, consistent with his dignity.
    

#### **B. Functions and Duties**

1. **Religious Instruction:** His main function is to impart religious knowledge to disciples and propagate the specific doctrine of the Math.
    
2. **Administration:** He manages the Math's vast properties and maintains the institution.
    

#### **C. Powers**

- **Wide Discretion:** He has wide power over the disposal of the _income_ (surplus) of the Math, often for charitable/religious causes of his choice, unlike a Shebait who must strictly spend on the specific Deity.
    
- **Alienation:** Like a Shebait, he cannot sell the _corpus_ (main property) of the Math except for legal necessity.
    

---

### 3. Devolution of Office (Succession)

How does one become a Shebait or Mahant?

- **Founder's Direction:** The primary rule is the intention of the founder. The deed of dedication usually specifies the line of succession (e.g., "My eldest son, then his eldest son").
    
- **Usage/Custom:** If the deed is silent, succession is governed by the custom of the specific institution (e.g., in Maths, a Mahant often nominates his successor/Chela).
    
- **Hereditary:** In private endowments, the office of Shebait is generally hereditary and passes to the founder's heirs under the general law of inheritance.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **"Shebaitship is Property":** This is a famous legal principle laid down by the Supreme Court.
> 
> - _Meaning:_ The office of Shebait is a "property" right. It can be inherited like land.
>     
> - _Exception:_ However, it is a restricted property. It **cannot be sold** (you cannot sell your job as a Shebait to a stranger), as that would be trafficking in a religious office.
>     

> **Pujari vs. Shebait:**
> 
> - **Shebait:** The Manager/Administrator (The Boss).
>     
> - **Pujari:** The Priest hired to perform rituals (The Employee).
>     
> - _Note:_ Sometimes they are the same person, but legally they are distinct roles.
>     

> **Removal:** A Shebait can be removed by a Court (under Section 92 CPC for public temples) if he is guilty of misconduct, misappropriation of funds, or immorality.
