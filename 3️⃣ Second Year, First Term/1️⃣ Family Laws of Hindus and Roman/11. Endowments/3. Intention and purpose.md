## 11.3 Intention and Purpose ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** The validity of an endowment often hinges on the "mind" of the donor. Was it a genuine religious offering, or just a tax-evasion scheme? Understanding the legal tests for "Intention" is crucial for distinguishing between real and fake (_benami_) endowments.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Often appears as part of a larger problem question: _"X executed a deed dedicating land to a temple but kept all the income for himself. Is the endowment valid?"_ (Answer: No, lacks genuine intention).
    

---

### Introduction

The creation of a Hindu religious endowment is not merely a matter of executing a deed. The core of the transaction is the **Intention** (_Sankalpa_) of the founder. The law requires a clear, unequivocal, and genuine intention to divest ownership and dedicate the property to the Deity.

Similarly, the **Purpose** of the endowment must be one that is recognized by Hindu Law as beneficial and pious.

---

### 1. Intention (_Sankalpa_)

The mental element is paramount. A mere declaration or a registered deed is not conclusive proof of an endowment if the intention is missing.

#### **A. Genuine vs. Sham Intention**

- **Real Intention:** The donor must intend to completely transfer the beneficial interest from himself to the Idol.
    
    - _Evidence:_ The Court looks at the subsequent conduct. Did the donor stop treating the land as his own? Is the income actually spent on the _Puja_? Are accounts kept in the Idol's name?
        
- **Sham (Illusory) Intention:** Sometimes, people execute a _Debuttar_ deed to shield property from creditors or the ceiling laws of the government, while secretly continuing to enjoy the profits themselves.
    
    - _Legal Consequence:_ If the court finds the intention was not genuine, the deed is declared **Void/Sham**. The property remains secular and can be seized by creditors.
        

#### **B. Clear and Unequivocal**

- The intention must be specific. A vague statement like "I hope my sons will spend some money on religion" does not create a valid endowment. There must be a specific allocation of property.
    

---

### 2. Purpose of Endowment

The purpose for which an endowment is created must be lawful and pious. Hindu Law divides these purposes into two broad categories:

#### **A. Ishta (Sacrificial/Religious)**

These are purposes that lead to "spiritual advancement" and "heaven" for the donor.

- **Examples:**
    
    - Installation and worship of an Idol (_Thakur_).
        
    - Construction of a temple (_Mandir_).
        
    - Performance of sacrifices (_Yajna_).
        
- **Nature:** Historically, _Ishta_ works were often private (for the family soul), but can be public.
    

#### **B. Purta (Charitable/Secular)**

These are works of "public utility" that benefit humanity.

- **Examples:**
    
    - Excavating tanks and wells for public water.
        
    - Building rest houses (_Dharamshalas_) for travelers.
        
    - Establishing hospitals or schools.
        
    - Planting trees for shade.
        
    - Feeding the poor (_Sadavrat_).
        
- **Nature:** _Purta_ works are generally regarded as **Public Endowments** because the public benefits directly.
    

#### **C. Invalid Purposes**

- **Superstitious Uses:** While courts generally respect religious beliefs, a trust for a purpose totally opposed to public policy or morality (e.g., funding illegal activities in the name of religion) would be void.
    
- **Unknown to Hindu Law:** A bequest to "worship the ghost of an ancestor" (not a deity) might be challenged, though "worship of ancestors" (_Sraddha_) is generally valid. A bequest strictly for the "tomb" of a person (unlike a Samadhi of a saint) is not recognized as a religious purpose in Hindu Law (unlike Muslim Law's _Dargah_).
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Conduct" Test:** In problem questions, always check the **conduct** of the donor _after_ the deed.
> 
> - _Scenario:_ "Father deeds land to Idol in 2010. In 2015, he sells the crops and buys a personal car."
>     
> - _Verdict:_ The endowment is likely **Illusory/Sham**. The conduct proves lack of genuine intention.
>     

> **Ishta vs. Purta:** Mentioning these Sanskrit terms adds depth to your answer. It shows you understand the classical jurisprudence behind the law.
> 
> - _Ishta_ = Heaven (Vedic).
>     
> - _Purta_ = Public Welfare (Smriti/Puranic).
>     

> **Partial Dedication:** If the intention is only to spend a _part_ of the income on the deity, argue for a "Partial Dedication" (Charge) rather than a full endowment.
