## 11.5 Object of Endowment ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** You cannot create a trust for "nothing" or for a "ghost." The law requires a specific, recognizable "Object" (beneficiary or purpose). Understanding that the **Idol** is the primary object in a temple endowment is key.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often focus on the validity of specific bequests, such as "Is a bequest for 'Dharma' valid?" (Answer: No, it's too vague).
    

---

### Introduction

The "Object" of an endowment refers to the **beneficiary** or the specific **purpose** for which the property is dedicated. In Hindu Law, an endowment is not valid unless it has a lawful and definite object. The property must be given _to_ someone (like an Idol) or _for_ something (like feeding the poor).

Broadly, the objects are classified into **Religious (_Ishta_)** and **Charitable (_Purta_)** purposes.

---

### 1. Valid Objects

Hindu Law is quite liberal in recognizing various religious and charitable objects.

#### **A. The Idol (Deity) as Object**

- **Primary Object:** In a temple endowment, the primary object is the **Idol** (_Thakur_).
    
- **Juristic Person:** The property is not given to the priest; it is given to the Idol. The Idol is treated as a juristic person capable of holding the property.
    
- **Destruction of Object:** Even if the physical image (statue) is broken or lost, the "Object" (the metaphysical deity) continues to exist. The endowment does not fail; a new image can be installed.
    

#### **B. Religious Institutions (Maths)**

- **The Math:** An endowment can be created for a **Math** (Monastery). Here, the object is the promotion of religious study and the support of ascetics (_Sanyasis_).
    
- **Head of Math:** The _Mahant_ manages it, but the "Object" is the institution itself.
    

#### **C. Charitable Purposes (_Purta_)**

- **Public Benefit:** Any purpose that benefits the public is a valid object.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Excavation of tanks/wells (Water supply was a supreme pious act).
        
    - Establishing _Dharamshalas_ (Rest houses).
        
    - _Sadavrat_ (Feeding the poor/needy).
        
    - Establishing schools or hospitals.
        
    - _Vrisha-utsarga_ (dedicating a bull for public breeding/use).
        

---

### 2. The Rule of Certainty (Definiteness)

For an endowment to be valid, the object must be **specific** and **ascertainable**.

- **Vague Bequests are Void:** A bequest for "Dharma" (Religion) or "Punya-karye" (Pious acts) generally is considered **Void for Uncertainty**.
    
    - _Reason:_ "Dharma" is too broad. The court cannot determine if the money is being misspent because the trustee could spend it on anything they personally consider "pious."
        
- **Specific Bequests are Valid:** A bequest for "Worship of Goddess Kali" or "Feeding Brahmins" is valid because the court can enforce it.
    

---

### 3. Invalid Objects

Certain objects are not recognized by Hindu Law.

- **Tomb of a Private Person:** Unlike Muslim Law (which recognizes Dargahs), Hindu Law generally **does not** recognize the worship of a tomb (_Samadhi_) of an ordinary person as a religious purpose. Dedicating property to maintain a father's tomb is **Void** (unless the father was a recognized Saint/Guru).
    
- **Superstitious Uses:** A trust for a purpose totally opposed to public policy (e.g., intended for illegal activities disguised as religion) is void.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Dharma" Trap:** This is a classic objective question.
> 
> - _Question:_ "T leaves 1 Lakh Taka for 'Dharma'. Is it valid?"
>     
> - _Answer:_ **No.** It is void for uncertainty (_Runchordas v. Parvatibai_).
>     
> - _Contrast:_ "T leaves 1 Lakh for 'Feeding the Poor'." $\rightarrow$ **Valid.**
>     

> **Idol vs. Image:** Remember the distinction. The **Idol** (Deity) is the perpetual object. The **Image** (Statue) is just the physical manifestation. If the statue breaks, the endowment survives because the Deity is immortal.
