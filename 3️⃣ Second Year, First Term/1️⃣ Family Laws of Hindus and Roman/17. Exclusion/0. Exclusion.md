## 17. Exclusion ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** While the general rules of inheritance tell us _who_ inherits, the rules of exclusion tell us who is _disqualified_ despite being a valid heir. Understanding the difference between moral, physical, and religious grounds is essential, especially as statutory law has abolished many of these.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Exclusion is a favorite topic for problem-based questions (e.g., "Can a murderer inherit?" or "Can a convert inherit?"). You must distinguish between the classical rule and the current statutory position in Bangladesh.
    

---

### Introduction

In classical Hindu Law, inheritance was not just a right of blood; it was a reward for spiritual services (offering _Pinda_) or a means to continue the family line. Therefore, persons incapable of performing religious rites or managing property due to physical, mental, or moral defects were traditionally excluded from inheritance.

However, modern legislation has removed most of these disqualifications. In Bangladesh, we must carefully apply the **Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928** and the **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850**.

---

### I. Exclusion on Moral and Religious Grounds

**Syllabus Covered:** _17.1 Exclusion on moral and religious grounds_

**1. Unchastity (Moral Ground)**

- **The Rule:** This disqualification is unique to **females**. As discussed in Topic 15, a widow must be chaste _at the time the succession opens_ (husband's death) to inherit.
    
- **Scope:** In Dayabhaga law, this strictness applied to _all_ female heirs (widow, daughter, mother). In Mitakshara, it was primarily restricted to the widow.
    
- **Current Status:** This rule **still exists** in Bangladesh for widows. If a widow was unchaste before her husband died, she is excluded.
    

**2. Conversion (Religious Ground)**

- **Classical Rule:** Under pure Hindu law, if a person renounced Hinduism and converted to another religion (Islam, Christianity, etc.), they became "dead" to the family and forfeited their right to inherit.
    
- **Statutory Change (CRITICAL):** The **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850** (also known as the Freedom of Religion Act) completely **abolished** this rule.
    
- **Current Law:** A Hindu who converts to Islam or Christianity **CAN** still inherit from their Hindu relatives. The conversion does not strip them of their inheritance rights.
    
    - _Note:_ While the convert inherits, the _children_ of the convert generally do not inherit from their Hindu grandparents unless they reconvert to Hinduism.
        

**3. Asceticism (Sannyasi)**

- **The Rule:** A person who abandons all worldly affairs and enters a religious order (becomes a _Sannyasi_ or _Vanaprastha_) is excluded from inheritance. They are considered "civilly dead."
    
- **Requirement:** The renunciation must be absolute and final. Merely wearing saffron clothes is not enough; specific ceremonies of initiation must be performed.
    

---

### II. Exclusion on Physical or Mental Defects

**Syllabus Covered:** _17.2 Exclusion on physical or mental defects_

Ancient Hindu texts (Manusmriti) excluded people with various disabilities (blindness, deafness, dumbness, leprosy, lameness, idiocy) because they were seen as incapable of performing sacrifices or managing wealth.

**1. The Statutory Reform** The **Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928** drastically changed this landscape.

**2. Physical Defects (Blindness, Deafness, Dumbness)**

- **Current Law:** Under the 1928 Act, no person is excluded from inheritance by reason of any physical defect (like being born blind, deaf, or dumb) or disease (like leprosy), _unless_ the person is insane.
    
- Before 1928: Congenital (born with) blindness or deafness was a ground for exclusion.
    
- **NOW:** A blind or deaf person **inherits fully**.
    

**3. Mental Defects (Lunacy/Idiocy)**

- **The Rule:** This is the **only major disqualification remaining** under the 1928 Act.
    
- **Requirement:** The person must be a "Lunatic" or "Idiot" _at the time the succession opens_.
    
    - _Idiot:_ One with a congenital (from birth) lack of mental capacity.
        
    - _Lunatic:_ One who has a mental illness. It need not be congenital or incurable, but it must be present at the specific moment of inheritance.
        

---

### III. Other Grounds (Not explicitly in Act, but Valid)

**1. Murderer Disqualified**

- **The Rule:** A person who murders the person from whom they are to inherit is totally disqualified.
    
- **Principle:** This is based on the universal legal maxim: _"Nemo ex suo delicto meliorem suam conditionem facere potest"_ (No one can improve their condition by their own wrong).
    
- **Effect:** The murderer is treated as if they never existed. The property goes to the next heir.
    

---

### IV. Statutory Provisions for Exclusion in Bangladesh

**Syllabus Covered:** _17.3 Statutory provisions for exclusion in Bangladesh_

To summarize the position in Bangladesh specifically, you must cite these two Acts in any exam answer:

1. **The Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850:**
    
    - Prevents exclusion based on **loss of caste** or **change of religion**.
        
    - Ensures converts can inherit.
        
2. **The Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928:**
    
    - Abolished exclusion based on disease, deformity, or physical defect.
        
    - **Retained exclusion only for:** "Person who is from birth a lunatic or idiot." (Note: The text of the Act mentions "congenital lunacy," but courts often interpret lunacy broadly. However, for strict exam purposes, focus on **Congenital Lunacy/Idiocy** as the primary statutory bar).
        

---

### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Subsequent Disability:** Exclusion is determined _only_ at the time succession opens (death of the owner). If an heir inherits property and _later_ becomes insane or gets leprosy, they do **not** lose the property. Vested rights are never divested.
    
- **Maintenance:** Generally, an heir who is excluded from inheritance (e.g., a congenital idiot) is still entitled to **Maintenance** from the estate. They are not left to starve.
    
- **Scenario Question:**
    
    - _Question:_ "X, a Hindu man, dies. He has a son Y who converted to Christianity 5 years ago, and a son Z who was born blind. Who inherits?"
        
    - _Answer:_ Both Y and Z inherit.
        
        - Y inherits because the _Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_ protects his right despite conversion.
            
        - Z inherits because the _Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928_ removed blindness as a ground for exclusion.
