### 17.2 Exclusion on Physical or Mental Defects ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic demonstrates the evolution of Hindu Law from ancient ritualistic requirements (where physical perfection was needed for sacrifice) to modern statutory rights.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Problem questions frequently feature a "blind son" or a "lunatic heir." You must know which defects still disqualify an heir and which ones have been abolished by statute.
    

---

#### I. Introduction: The Classical View

In ancient Hindu Law (Dayabhaga and Mitakshara), inheritance was deeply tied to the ability to perform religious rights (_Pinda_). Consequently, persons suffering from certain physical or mental infirmities were considered incapable of performing these rites or managing property.

**The Classical List of Exclusions (Manusmriti):**

- **Mental:** Lunacy (_Unmatta_), Idiocy (_Jada_).
    
- **Physical:** Blindness, Deafness, Dumbness, Leprosy, Lameness, and other "incurable diseases."
    

If a person suffered from these defects **at the time succession opened**, they were excluded. However, this harshness was mitigated by the **Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928**.

---

#### II. Statutory Reform: The 1928 Act

The **Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928** is the governing law in Bangladesh regarding these defects.

**The General Rule of the Act:** No person, other than a person who is and has been **from birth a lunatic or idiot**, shall be excluded from inheritance or from any right to a share in joint family property by reason only of any disease, deformity, or physical or mental defect.

---

#### III. Physical Defects (Abolished)

Under the 1928 Act, practically all physical grounds for exclusion have been **abolished**.

1. **Blindness, Deafness, Dumbness:**
    
    - **Classical Law:** A person born blind or deaf was excluded.
        
    - **Current Law:** A person who is blind, deaf, or dumb (whether from birth or later) is **fully capable** of inheriting.
        
2. **Leprosy and Other Diseases:**
    
    - **Classical Law:** Virulent and incurable leprosy was a ground for exclusion.
        
    - **Current Law:** No disease (leprosy, tuberculosis, etc.) disqualifies an heir anymore.
        

---

#### IV. Mental Defects (Retained in Part)

The Act preserved the disqualification for specific severe mental conditions, but narrowed the scope significantly.

**1. Lunacy (Insanity)**

- **Requirement:** To be excluded, the person must be a lunatic **from birth** (Congenital).
    
- **Change:** Under classical Mitakshara law, _supervening_ lunacy (becoming insane later in life) was a bar. The 1928 Act changed this. Now, if a person was born sane but became insane later (e.g., at age 20), they **can inherit**.
    
- **Definition:** "Lunatic" implies a person who has lost their reason (mental illness).
    

**2. Idiocy**

- **Requirement:** The person must be an idiot **from birth**.
    
- **Definition:** An "Idiot" is a person with a congenital lack of mental capacity (profound intellectual disability). Unlike a lunatic (who has lucid intervals), an idiot generally has no understanding at all.
    

> **Key Exam Distinction:**
> 
> - **Born Blind:** Inherits (Disability removed).
>     
> - **Born Lunatic:** Excluded (Disability retained).
>     
> - **Became Lunatic at age 25:** Inherits (Disability removed).
>     

---

#### V. Maintenance for Excluded Heirs

Even if a person is excluded due to congenital lunacy or idiocy, Hindu Law is not cruel.

- **Right to Maintenance:** The excluded heir is entitled to **maintenance** (food, clothing, shelter) from the estate for their entire life.
    
- **Heirs of the Excluded:** Interestingly, the _son_ of an excluded heir (if the son himself is free from defects) can generally inherit. The disability is personal and does not block the line of descent.
    

---

#### VI. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Scope of the 1928 Act:** Strictly speaking, the Act text applies to persons governed by the _Mitakshara_ school. However, in Bangladesh exams, the principles of the 1928 Act are often applied broadly as the standard for statutory reform. If answering strictly on _Dayabhaga_ law, note that the Act technically might not cover it, but courts avoid applying ancient physical exclusions today based on equity. For exam safety, cite the 1928 Act as the primary reform.
    
- **Problem Question:** _"X has a son Y who was born blind and a son Z who was born insane. X dies. Who inherits?"_
    
    - **Answer:**
        
        - **Y (Blind):** Inherits. (Blindness is no longer a bar under the 1928 Act).
            
        - **Z (Insane):** Excluded. (Congenital insanity is still a bar). Z is entitled to maintenance.
