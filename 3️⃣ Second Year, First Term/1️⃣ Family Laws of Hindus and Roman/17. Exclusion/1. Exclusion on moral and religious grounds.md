### 17.1 Exclusion on Moral and Religious Grounds ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic highlights the intersection of law, morality, and religion in classical Hindu jurisprudence. It is essential to understand how modern statutes (like the Caste Disabilities Removal Act) have overridden ancient disqualifications.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A very popular problem question area. Examiners often test whether a student knows that "Unchastity" still disqualifies a widow in Bangladesh, while "Conversion" no longer disqualifies anyone.
    

---

#### I. Introduction

In classical Hindu Law, inheritance was not merely a birthright but was deeply tied to the capacity to perform religious duties (_Pinda_) and maintain the family honor. Consequently, ancient texts (like Manusmriti) prescribed that persons who were morally deficient or had abandoned the Hindu faith were disqualified from inheriting property.

However, the legal landscape in Bangladesh is a mix of these ancient rules and colonial-era statutes that abolished specific disqualifications.

---

#### II. Exclusion on Moral Grounds (Unchastity)

The primary moral ground for exclusion in Hindu Law is **Unchastity** (sexual infidelity).

**1. The Rule of Unchastity**

- **Applicability:** This disqualification applies **only to females**. A male heir is never excluded from inheritance on the ground of unchastity or sexual immorality.
    
- **The Principle:** A widow (or other female heir) must be **chaste** (faithful to her husband) at the time the succession opens to be competent to inherit.
    

**2. Distinction Between Schools**

- **Dayabhaga School (Bengal):** The rule is very strict. It applies to **every female heir** (Widow, Daughter, Mother, Grandmother, etc.). If any female heir is living an unchaste life (e.g., in adultery or prostitution) at the moment the owner dies, she is excluded from inheritance.
    
- **Mitakshara School:** The rule is narrower. It applies **only to the Widow**. Other female heirs (like a daughter or mother) are not disqualified by unchastity under Mitakshara law (unless a specific local custom proves otherwise).
    

**3. Timing of Unchastity (Crucial Exam Point)** The disqualification depends entirely on the status of the female **at the time the succession opens** (i.e., the moment the owner dies).

- **Pre-existing Unchastity:** If she is unchaste _at the time of death_, she gets nothing.
    
- **Subsequent Unchastity:** If she was chaste when the husband died, inherits the property, and _subsequently_ becomes unchaste, she does **not** lose the property.
    
    - **Legal Maxim:** "An estate once vested cannot be divested."
        

---

#### III. Exclusion on Religious Grounds (Conversion & Asceticism)

**1. Conversion (Change of Religion)**

- **Classical Rule:** Under ancient Hindu law, a person who renounced Hinduism and converted to another faith (Islam, Christianity, etc.) became an "outcaste" (_Patita_). They were considered "civilly dead" and forfeited all rights to inherit from Hindu relatives.
    
- **Statutory Abolition:** This rule was **abolished** by the **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850** (also known as the Freedom of Religion Act).
    
- **Current Law in Bangladesh:**
    
    - **The Convert:** A Hindu who converts to another religion **CAN** still inherit from their Hindu relatives. Their right is protected by the 1850 Act.
        
    - **The Convert’s Descendants:** However, the _children_ of the convert (born after conversion) generally do **not** inherit from their Hindu grandparents, as they are not Hindus and the Act only protects the person who actually converted.
        

**2. Asceticism (Sannyas/Vanaprastha)**

- **The Rule:** A person who enters a religious order and renounces all worldly connections is excluded from inheritance.
    
- **Requirement:** The renunciation must be absolute and final. The person must have performed the necessary ceremonies to become a _Sannyasi_ (monk) or _Yati_.
    
    - Merely wearing saffron robes or calling oneself a "Sadhu" is not enough; there must be a complete civil death in the eyes of the law.
        
- **Effect:** Since the ascetic is "civilly dead," their property (if they had any) passes to their disciples or religious order, not to their natural heirs. Conversely, they cannot inherit from their natural family.
    

---

#### IV. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Murderer" Rule:** While not strictly "religious," the disqualification of a murderer is often grouped here. A person who murders the owner cannot inherit from them (based on principles of justice, equity, and good conscience).
    
- **Comparison Tip:**
    
    - _Unchastity:_ Still valid in Bangladesh (for widows).
        
    - _Conversion:_ No longer valid (due to 1850 Act).
        
- **Problem Question Strategy:**
    
    - _Scenario:_ "H dies. His widow W is living in adultery. His son S converted to Christianity 5 years ago."
        
    - _Solution:_
        
        - **W:** Excluded due to unchastity (Moral Ground).
            
        - **S:** Inherits the property because the _Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_ protects him despite conversion.
