### 17.3 Statutory Provisions for Exclusion in Bangladesh ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the definitive law. In a Bangladesh court or exam, you cannot rely solely on ancient texts like _Manusmriti_. You must know which specific Acts of Parliament have overruled those texts.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Questions often ask: _"Discuss the statutory modifications to the rules of exclusion in Hindu Law."_ You are expected to cite the specific Acts and dates.
    

---

#### I. Introduction

While classical Hindu Law (Dayabhaga and Mitakshara) provided a long list of grounds for exclusion based on moral, religious, and physical defects, the legislature intervened during the British colonial period to modify these rules.

In Bangladesh, two primary statutes are currently in force that explicitly override the classical disqualifications.

---

#### II. The Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850

(Act XXI of 1850)

Also known as the Freedom of Religion Act, this was the first major statutory intervention.

1. Purpose

To protect the property rights of individuals who were "outcasted" or converted to another religion. Under classical law, an "apostate" (one who left Hinduism) was treated as civilly dead.

2. The Provision

The Act declares that no law or usage shall be enforced which inflicts forfeiture of rights or property on any person by reason of:

- Renouncing or being excluded from the communion of any religion (i.e., **Conversion**).
    
- Being deprived of caste (i.e., **Excommunication**).
    

**3. Impact on Exclusion**

- **Conversion:** A Hindu who converts to Islam, Christianity, or Buddhism does **not** lose their right to inherit from their Hindu relatives.
    
- **Loss of Caste:** Being "outcasted" for social or moral reasons no longer disqualifies an heir.
    

> **Crucial Limitation:** The Act protects the **convert personally**. It does _not_ protect the convert's children. If a Hindu converts to Islam and _then_ has a son, that Muslim son cannot inherit from his Hindu grandfather (due to the separate rule that inheritance requires the heir to be Hindu, which the Act didn't change for _descendants_).

---

#### III. The Hindu Inheritance (Removal of Disabilities) Act, 1928

(Act XII of 1928)

This Act modernized the law regarding physical and mental defects.

1. Purpose

To abolish the harsh ancient rules that excluded the blind, deaf, dumb, and diseased from inheritance.

2. The Provision (Section 2)

It states that:

> _"No person, other than a person who is and has been from his birth a lunatic or idiot, shall be excluded from inheritance or from any right or share in joint-family property by reason only of any disease, deformity, or physical or mental defect."_

**3. Impact on Exclusion**

- **Abolished:** Exclusion based on Blindness, Deafness, Dumbness, Lameness, Leprosy, or any other incurable disease.
    
- **Retained:** Exclusion based on **Congenital Lunacy** or **Congenital Idiocy** (born insane/intellectually disabled).
    

---

#### IV. The Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856

(Act XV of 1856)

While primarily an enabling act for marriage, Section 2 acts as a statutory exclusion/forfeiture clause.

1. The Provision (Section 2)

It provides that all rights and interests which any widow may have in her deceased husband's property shall cease and determine upon her remarriage, as if she had then died.

**2. Impact on Exclusion**

- If a widow remarries _before_ the succession opens, she is excluded.
    
- If she remarries _after_ inheriting, she forfeits the estate (it goes to the next heirs).
    

---

#### V. Summary of Statutory Changes

|**Ground of Exclusion**|**Classical Law Position**|**Statutory Law (Bangladesh)**|**Relevant Act**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Conversion**|Excluded (Civil Death)|**Not Excluded**|_Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_|
|**Loss of Caste**|Excluded|**Not Excluded**|_Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_|
|**Blindness/Deafness**|Excluded (if congenital)|**Not Excluded**|_Removal of Disabilities Act, 1928_|
|**Disease (Leprosy)**|Excluded (if virulent)|**Not Excluded**|_Removal of Disabilities Act, 1928_|
|**Insanity**|Excluded|**Excluded (only if congenital)**|_Removal of Disabilities Act, 1928_|
|**Unchastity (Widow)**|Excluded|**Excluded**|(No Statute removed this; remains valid)|

---

#### VI. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Bangladesh vs. India:** Be careful. In India, the _Hindu Succession Act, 1956_ abolished _all_ exclusions (even unchastity and conversion of descendants). In Bangladesh, we strictly follow the **1850** and **1928** Acts. Do not cite the 1956 Act.
    
- **Case Law:** You can mention _Kasturi Devi v. Chiranji Lal (1960)_, where courts interpreted that the 1928 Act was retrospective in some contexts, but mainly applies to successions opening after the Act passed.
    
- **Unchastity Remains:** Note that there is **no statute** in Bangladesh that removes the disqualification of unchastity for a widow. Therefore, that ancient rule stands firm.
