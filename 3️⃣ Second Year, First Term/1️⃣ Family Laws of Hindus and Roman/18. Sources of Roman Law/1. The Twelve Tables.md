

### 18.1 The Twelve Tables (_Lex Duodecim Tabularum_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The Twelve Tables are the cornerstone of Roman Law. As the first written code, they mark the transition from uncertain oral custom to fixed, public law. Almost all later Roman legal concepts (like _Patria Potestas_ and _Mancipatio_) find their roots here.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most frequently asked question in the Roman Law section. You are often asked to "Discuss the circumstances leading to the enactment of the Twelve Tables and their contents."
    

---

#### I. Historical Background: The Struggle of the Orders

To understand _why_ the Twelve Tables were created, one must understand the social conflict in early Rome (c. 450 BC).

**1. Patricians vs. Plebeians**

- **Patricians:** The wealthy, aristocratic class who controlled the Senate, the priesthoods, and the magistracies. They were the sole interpreters of the unwritten customary law (_Mos Maiorum_).
    
- **Plebeians:** The common citizens (farmers, traders, artisans). They were free but politically marginalized.
    

**2. The Grievance (Uncertainty of Law)** Because the law was unwritten and known only to the Patrician priests (_Pontiffs_), Plebeians felt they were treated arbitrarily. Judges could interpret "custom" however they liked to favor their own class.

- **The Demand:** The Plebeians demanded strictly defined, **written laws** so that every citizen could know their rights and punishments.
    

**3. The Commission (The Decemviri)**

- In **451 BC**, the normal government (Consuls) was suspended.
    
- A commission of ten men, the **Decemviri** ("Ten Men"), was appointed with absolute power to draft the laws.
    
- They produced Ten Tables in the first year. In **450 BC**, a second commission was appointed, adding Two Tables (which were reportedly less fair), bringing the total to Twelve.
    

---

#### II. Nature and Significance

The Twelve Tables were not a modern "Code" or "Constitution." They did not cover every aspect of life.

1. **Codification of Custom:** They were largely a compilation of existing customary laws, but writing them down "fixed" them, preventing arbitrary changes.
    
2. **Strict Formalism:** The laws were extremely rigid. Legal actions (_Legis Actiones_) required reciting exact words. A slight mistake meant losing the case.
    
3. **"Fountain of Law":** The Roman historian **Livy** famously called the Twelve Tables _"The fountain of all public and private law"_ (_Fons omnis publici privatique juris_).
    
4. **Education:** For centuries, Roman schoolboys (like Cicero) memorized the Tables by chanting them aloud.
    

---

#### III. Contents of the Tables

Although the original bronze tablets were destroyed in the Gallic invasion (390 BC), scholars have reconstructed their contents from quotes in later literature.

**Tables I, II, III: Civil Procedure**

- Focused heavily on bringing people to court.
    
- **Summons:** _"If the plaintiff summons the defendant to court, he must go. If he does not go, the plaintiff shall call a witness, then he shall take him by force."_
    
- **Execution:** Harsh rules for debt. If a debtor could not pay after 30 days, he could be chained, sold into slavery across the Tiber, or even cut into pieces (_partis secanto_) by creditors (though scholars debate if the "cutting" was literal or division of his estate).
    

**Table IV: Paternal Power (_Patria Potestas_)**

- Enshrined the absolute power of the father over his children, including the right to kill deformed infants.
    
- **Emancipation:** It provided a loophole: _"If a father sells his son three times, the son shall be free from the father."_ This became the legal method for emancipation.
    

**Table V: Guardianship and Inheritance**

- **Intestacy:** _"If a person dies intestate without a direct heir (_suus heres_), the nearest agnate shall take the estate."_
    
- **Tutela:** Rules for guardianship of women and lunatics.
    

**Table VI: Ownership and Possession**

- **Mancipatio:** Established the formal ritual for transferring important property (_Res Mancipi_).
    
- **The Rule:** _"As the tongue has spoken, so shall the right be."_ (Meaning the spoken words of the contract are binding).
    

**Table VII: Land Rights**

- Rules regarding boundaries, roads, and neighborly relations (e.g., trimming tree branches).
    

**Table VIII: Torts (_Delicts_)**

- **Talio (Retaliation):** _"If one breaks another's limb and does not settle with him, let there be retaliation (an eye for an eye)."_
    
- **Fines:** Fixed monetary penalties for lesser injuries (e.g., breaking a bone of a free man = 300 Ases; of a slave = 150 Ases).
    

**Tables IX, X: Public Law & Sacred Law**

- **Due Process:** No citizen could be executed without a trial by the great assembly (_Comitia Centuriata_).
    
- **Funerals:** Sumptuary laws limiting luxury at funerals (e.g., women were forbidden from wailing or scratching their cheeks in grief).
    

**Tables XI, XII: Supplementary Laws**

- **Intermarriage Ban:** Table XI famously forbade marriage between Patricians and Plebeians (this was repealed shortly after by the _Lex Canuleia_ in 445 BC).
    

---

#### IV. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Not a Constitution:** Do not describe the Twelve Tables as a "Constitution" in the modern sense. It was a collection of specific rules, mostly procedural and private law.
    
- **Equality:** Their greatest achievement was establishing **isonomia** (equality before the law) for Patricians and Plebeians.
    
- **Survival:** Although the physical tables were lost, the _law_ of the Twelve Tables remained technically in force for 1,000 years, until Justinian, though largely superseded by newer laws.
    
- **Quote to Memorize:** _"Salus populi suprema lex esto"_ (The safety of the people shall be the supreme law) – often associated with this era of Roman law making.
