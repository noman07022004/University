
### 19.1 Citizenship (_Status Civitatis_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** In Roman Law, simply being free (_Status Libertatis_) was not enough to enjoy full legal rights. You had to be a "Citizen" to use the Civil Law (_Jus Civile_). Without this status, you were effectively a second-class subject governed by a different system.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often ask to "Enumerate the rights of a Roman Citizen" or "Discuss the legal position of _Peregrini_ and _Latini_."
    

---

#### I. Introduction: The Concept of _Civitas_

In the Roman legal system, the **Law of Persons** determined a person's rights based on their status. Once a person was established as **Free** (not a slave), the next vital question was: _Are they a Roman Citizen?_

- **Jus Civile (Civil Law):** This was the exclusive law of Rome, applicable **only** to Roman citizens (_Cives_).
    
- **Jus Gentium (Law of Nations):** This was the broader body of law applied to foreigners (_Peregrini_) and interactions between citizens and foreigners.
    

Therefore, Citizenship (_Status Civitatis_) was the gateway to the privileged Roman legal system.

---

#### II. Classification of Free Men

Free inhabitants of the Roman Empire were divided into three distinct grades of citizenship:

1. **Cives (Roman Citizens):** They possessed full legal rights in both public and private life. They were the masters of the Roman world.
    
2. **Latini (Latins):** Originally inhabitants of Latium (the region around Rome). They held an intermediate status—they had some commercial rights (_Commercium_) but generally lacked the right to marry Romans (_Connubium_) or vote.
    
3. **Peregrini (Foreigners/Aliens):** Free inhabitants of conquered provinces who were not citizens. They had no rights under strict Roman Civil Law but were protected by the _Jus Gentium_.
    

---

#### III. Content of Citizenship (The Rights)

To be a full Roman citizen meant possessing a bundle of specific rights, divided into Public and Private categories.

**1. Public Rights (_Jura Publica_)** These rights related to the citizen's participation in the state.

- **_Jus Suffragii_** **(Right to Vote):** The right to vote in the popular assemblies (_Comitia_) to pass laws or elect magistrates.
    
- **_Jus Honorum_** **(Right to Office):** The right to stand for public office (Magistracies like Consul, Praetor, Tribune).
    
- **_Jus Militiae:_** The right (and duty) to serve in the Roman Legions.
    
- **_Provocatio ad Populum:_** The right to appeal to the people against a death sentence imposed by a magistrate (a primitive form of Due Process).
    

**2. Private Rights (_Jura Privata_)** These are the most critical for the "Family Law" section of your syllabus.

- **_Jus Connubii_** **(Right of Marriage):** The right to contract a valid Roman Civil Law marriage (_Justae Nuptiae_).
    
    - _Effect:_ Only such a marriage produced children who were **legitimate** and under the **Patria Potestas** (power) of the father.
        
- **_Jus Commercii_** **(Right of Commerce):** The right to acquire, hold, and transfer property according to the strict forms of Roman Law (like _Mancipatio_).
    
    - _Effect:_ A foreigner could buy land, but he could not use the formal _Mancipatio_ ritual, meaning his ownership was less secure under early law.
        
- **_Testamentifactio_** **(Right of Wills):**
    
    - _Active:_ The capacity to make a valid Roman Will.
        
    - _Passive:_ The capacity to be a witness to a will or receive property under a will.
        
- **_Jus Actionis_** **(Right to Sue):** The right to bring a lawsuit in Roman courts using the strict _Legis Actiones_ procedures.
    

---

#### IV. Acquisition of Citizenship

How did one become a citizen?

1. **By Birth:** A child born of a valid Roman marriage (_Justae Nuptiae_) followed the status of the **Father**. (If the marriage was invalid or involved a foreigner, the child generally took the status of the Mother).
    
2. **By Manumission:** If a Roman master freed his slave using a formal method (e.g., _Vindicta_, _Censu_, _Testamento_), the slave became a Roman Citizen instantly.
    
3. **By Grant:** The State (and later the Emperor) could grant citizenship to individuals or entire communities as a reward for service.
    

---

#### V. The Expansion and Universal Citizenship

This is a key historical development you must mention in essays.

- **Early Republic:** Citizenship was exclusive to the city of Rome.
    
- **Social War (91-88 BC):** Citizenship was extended to all free inhabitants of Italy.
    
- **The Constitutio Antoniniana (212 AD):** This is the most important date. Emperor **Caracalla** issued an edict granting Roman Citizenship to **all free inhabitants** of the Roman Empire.
    
    - _Result:_ The distinction between _Cives_ and _Peregrini_ effectively vanished for legal purposes. The _Jus Civile_ became the law of the entire empire.
        

---

#### VI. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Latin Terms:** You _must_ use the Latin terms (e.g., _Jus Connubii_, _Jus Suffragii_) in your exam. It signals academic competence.
    
- **Connubium is Key:** In problem questions about family, check if the parties had _Connubium_. If a Roman married a _Peregrine_ woman without _Connubium_, the marriage was not valid under Civil Law, and the children were not under the father's power.
    
- **Caracalla's Edict:** Mentioning the _Constitutio Antoniniana_ (212 AD) is essential when discussing the "history" or "sources" of Roman Law, as it unified the legal system.
