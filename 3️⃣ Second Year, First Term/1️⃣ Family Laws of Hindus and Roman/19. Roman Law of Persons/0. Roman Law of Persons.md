## 19. Roman Law of Persons ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The Roman legal system was not based on equality. Rights depended entirely on one's "Status." Without understanding the hierarchy of persons (Slave vs. Free, Citizen vs. Alien), you cannot understand who could own property or make a contract.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions on "Status" (_Caput_) and the legal condition of Slaves (_Servi_) are staples of Roman Law examinations.
    

---

### Introduction

The **Law of Persons** (_Jus quod ad personas pertinet_) is the first division of the Institutes of Gaius and Justinian. In Roman legal theory, not every human being was a "Person" (a subject of rights).

The term _Persona_ originally meant a theatrical mask used by actors. In law, it came to mean the "role" an individual played in the legal system. To have full legal personality, a human being had to possess three statuses: **Liberty**, **Citizenship**, and **Family Rights**.

---

### I. Status (_Caput_)

**Syllabus Covered:** _19.2 Status_

In Roman Law, _Status_ is the legal standing of an individual. Full legal personality (_Caput_) was the aggregate of three elements:

1. **Status Libertatis (Liberty):** The distinction between **Free men** (_Liberi_) and **Slaves** (_Servi_). Without liberty, one had no rights at all.
    
2. **Status Civitatis (Citizenship):** The distinction between **Roman Citizens** (_Cives_) and **Foreigners** (_Peregrini_). Only citizens had full rights under Civil Law.
    
3. **Status Familiae (Family Position):** The distinction between those who were independent (**Sui Juris**, e.g., the _Paterfamilias_) and those who were dependent (**Alieni Juris**, e.g., sons, wives).
    

**Capitis Deminutio (Loss of Status)** A change in status was called _Capitis Deminutio_. It had three degrees:

- **Maxima (Maximum):** Loss of Liberty (becoming a slave). This automatically destroyed citizenship and family rights. The person became a "thing."
    
- **Media (Intermediate):** Loss of Citizenship (exile/deportation) while retaining liberty.
    
- **Minima (Minimum):** Change in family status (e.g., a son being emancipated or adopted). He remains free and a citizen, but his agnatic family ties change.

### II. Slavery

**Syllabus Covered:** _19.3 Slavery_

Slavery was a fundamental institution of Roman society and law. It represents the negation of legal personality.

**1. Definition and Legal Condition**

- **The Rule:** A slave (_Servus_) was a thing (_Res_), not a person. They were the property of their master (_Dominus_).
    
- **No Rights:** A slave could own nothing, make no contract, and had no family rights (slave marriage was _Contubernium_, a factual cohabitation, not a legal marriage).
    
- **Power of Life and Death:** Historically, the master had _Jus Vitae necisque_ (absolute power of life and death) over the slave, though this was restricted in the later Imperial period.
    

**2. Sources of Slavery (How one becomes a slave)**

- **Birth:** The child of a female slave followed the status of the mother (_Partus sequitur ventrem_). Even if the father was free, the child was a slave.
    
- **Captivity:** Prisoners of war became slaves. (Note: Romans captured by enemies were also slaves, but if they returned, they regained their rights via _Jus Postliminii_).
    
- **Punishment:** Criminals convicted of serious crimes could be condemned to slavery (e.g., working in mines).
    

**3. Manumission (Freeing a Slave)** Slavery ended through _Manumission_—the act of the master granting freedom. There were three formal modes:

- **Vindicta:** A mock trial where a third party touched the slave with a rod (_Vindicta_) claiming he was free, and the master did not oppose it.
    
- **Censu:** Entering the slave’s name on the Censor's list of citizens (done every 5 years).
    
- **Testamento:** Granting freedom in the master’s Will.
    

**4. The Peculium** Although slaves could own nothing, masters often gave them a small amount of property or money to manage, called **Peculium**. The slave could trade with it, but legally, the _Peculium_ always belonged to the master.

### III. Citizenship

**Syllabus Covered:** _19.1 Citizenship_

If a person was free, the next question was: _Are they a Roman Citizen?_ This determined which system of law applied to them.

**1. Categories of Free Men**

- **Cives (Citizens):** Possessed full rights under Roman Civil Law (_Jus Civile_).
    
- **Latini (Latins):** Residents of Latium (and later other colonies). They had partial rights (usually commercial rights but not the right to marry Romans).
    
- **Peregrini (Foreigners):** Free inhabitants of conquered provinces. They were governed by the _Jus Gentium_ (Law of Nations), not the strict Roman Civil Law.
    

**2. Rights of a Roman Citizen** Full citizenship included two types of rights:

- **Public Rights:**
    
    - _Jus Suffragii:_ Right to vote in assemblies.
        
    - _Jus Honorum:_ Right to hold public office.
        
- **Private Rights:**
    
    - _Jus Connubii:_ Right to contract a valid Roman marriage (creating legitimate children and paternal power).
        
    - _Jus Commercii:_ Right to acquire, transfer, and hold property under Roman forms (e.g., _Mancipatio_) and make contracts.
        
    - _Testamentifactio:_ Right to make a will or be a witness to a will.
        

**3. Expansion of Citizenship** Initially exclusive to Rome, citizenship was gradually extended. The process culminated in **212 AD**, when Emperor **Caracalla** issued the **Constitutio Antoniniana**, granting Roman citizenship to almost all free inhabitants of the Empire. This effectively erased the distinction between _Cives_ and _Peregrini_ for legal purposes.

---

### IV. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Peculium (Exam Keyword):** Always mention _Peculium_ when discussing slavery. It explains how slaves conducted business despite having no property rights. It is the ancestor of the modern concept of "Limited Liability" (since the master was only liable for debts up to the value of the Peculium).
    
- **Patria Potestas:** While "Family" is in the next topic, you must understand that "Status" often refers to whether you are under _Patria Potestas_ (Power of the Father). A son, no matter how old or distinguished (even a Consul), was _Alieni Juris_ (subject to his father's rights) as long as the father lived.
    
- **Distinguish "Jus Civile" vs. "Jus Gentium":**
    
    - _Jus Civile:_ Strict law for Citizens only.
        
    - _Jus Gentium:_ Flexible law based on natural reason, applied to Foreigners (_Peregrini_) and Citizens alike.
        
- **Quote:** "All men are either free or slaves." (Institutes of Gaius). This binary distinction is the root of the Roman Law of Persons.
