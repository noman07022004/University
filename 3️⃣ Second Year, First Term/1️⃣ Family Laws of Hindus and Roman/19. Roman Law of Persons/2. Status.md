### 19.2 Status (_Caput_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The concept of _Caput_ (Legal Personality) is the skeleton of Roman Law. It explains why a slave had zero rights, a foreigner had some rights, and a father (_Paterfamilias_) had absolute rights.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** "Capitis Deminutio" (Loss of Status) is a classic exam question. You must be able to define the three degrees of loss of status with examples.
    

---

#### I. Definition of Status (_Caput_)

In Roman Law, the term **Status** refers to the legal standing or capacity of an individual. The technical Roman term for "Legal Personality" is **_Caput_** (literally meaning "Head").

To possess full legal personality (_Plena Caput_), a human being had to satisfy three distinct conditions. If any of these were missing, the person's legal rights were diminished or non-existent.

**The Three Elements of Caput:**

1. **_Status Libertatis_** **(Liberty):** The person must be free, not a slave.
    
2. **_Status Civitatis_** **(Citizenship):** The person must be a Roman citizen, not a foreigner.
    
3. **_Status Familiae_** **(Family Rights):** The person must be the head of a family (_Sui Juris_) or at least a member of a Roman family.
    

---

#### II. The Hierarchy of Status

The possession of these statuses operated like a pyramid. You could not have the higher ones without the lower ones.

1. **Liberty is the Foundation:** Without liberty, you are a "Thing" (_Res_), not a person. You cannot have citizenship or family rights.
    
2. **Citizenship requires Liberty:** A slave cannot be a citizen. However, a free man might not be a citizen (he could be a _Peregrine_).
    
3. **Family Rights require Citizenship:** Only a Roman Citizen could have Roman family rights (_Patria Potestas_). A foreigner had family relations, but they were not recognized by the Civil Law.
    

---

#### III. _Capitis Deminutio_ (Loss/Change of Status)

A change in an individual's status was technically called **_Capitis Deminutio_** (literally "Head Decrease" or "Diminution of Personality"). Even if the change was technically an "improvement" (like being adopted into a better family), the Romans viewed it as the "death" of the old legal persona and the birth of a new one.

There were three degrees of _Capitis Deminutio_:

**1. _Capitis Deminutio Maxima_ (Maximum Loss)**

- **Definition:** The loss of **Liberty**.
    
- **Effect:** This was the most severe change. When a person lost their liberty, they automatically lost their Citizenship and Family rights. They ceased to be a person in the eyes of the law and became a slave (_Res_).
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Being taken as a prisoner of war by an enemy.
        
    - A Roman citizen being sold into slavery as punishment for evading the census or defaulting on debts (in early law).
        
    - Working in the mines (_Servus poenae_) as a criminal punishment.
        

**2. _Capitis Deminutio Media_ (Intermediate Loss)**

- **Definition:** The loss of **Citizenship**, while retaining Liberty.
    
- **Effect:** The person remains free but becomes a foreigner (_Peregrinus_). They lose the rights of the Civil Law (_Jus Suffragii_, _Jus Connubii_) but retain rights under the Law of Nations (_Jus Gentium_).
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - **Exile:** A citizen banished from Rome (often "interdicted from fire and water").
        
    - Deportation to an island.
        

**3. _Capitis Deminutio Minima_ (Minimum Loss)**

- **Definition:** A change in **Family Status**, while retaining Liberty and Citizenship.
    
- **Effect:** The person’s agnatic relationship changes. They leave one family and join another, or become independent.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - **Adoption:** A person moves from their natural family to an adoptive family.
        
    - **Marriage _Cum Manu_:** A woman leaves her father’s authority to enter her husband’s authority.
        
    - **Emancipation:** A son is released from his father's power. Although he becomes independent (_Sui Juris_), he technically suffers a "loss" of his old agnatic status.
        

---

#### IV. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Civil Death:** _Capitis Deminutio Maxima_ is equivalent to "Civil Death." The person is legally dead. If they had a Will, it becomes void.
    
- **The "Mask" Metaphor:** Remember the origin of _Persona_ (a mask). _Capitis Deminutio_ is essentially changing the mask you wear on the legal stage. In _Maxima_, the mask is removed entirely.
    
- **Postliminium (Right of Return):** A unique Roman concept. If a Roman soldier was captured (suffering _Maxima_) but escaped and returned to Rome, the law pretended he **never left**. He regained all his rights as if he had never been a slave. This is called _Jus Postliminii_.
