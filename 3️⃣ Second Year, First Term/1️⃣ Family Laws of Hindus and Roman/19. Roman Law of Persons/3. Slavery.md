### 19.3 Slavery (_Servitus_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Slavery was not just a social institution but a fundamental legal category in Rome. To understand the Roman economy and the law of property, one must understand that human beings could be classified as "Things" (_Res_).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often focus on the "Sources of Slavery" (how one becomes a slave) and the "Modes of Manumission" (how one becomes free).
    

---

#### I. Definition and Legal Condition

In Roman Law, slavery is an institution of the _Jus Gentium_ (Law of Nations) by which one human being is subjected to the dominion of another, contrary to nature.

**1. The Slave as a "Thing" (_Res_)**

- **The Rule:** Legally, a slave was not a "Person" (Subject of Rights); he was a **Thing** (_Res Mancipi_).
    
- **Property:** He was the absolute property of his master (_Dominus_). He could be bought, sold, mortgaged, or bequeathed in a will just like an ox or a plow.
    
- **No Rights:** A slave had no family rights (his union was _Contubernium_, not marriage), no property rights, and no standing in court.
    

**2. Power of the Master (_Dominica Potestas_)** The authority a master held over a slave was absolute.

- **_Jus Vitae necisque:_** In early law, the master had the power of life and death. He could kill the slave without penalty. (This was restricted in the Imperial period, where killing a slave without cause became homicide).
    
- **Noxal Surrender:** If a slave committed a tort (e.g., theft), the master could either pay the fine or surrender the slave (_Noxae deditio_) to the victim to escape liability.
    

---

#### II. Sources of Slavery (How one becomes a slave)

There were two main ways a person fell into slavery: by Birth or by the Law of Nations/Civil Law.

**1. By Birth (_Partus Sequitur Ventrem_)**

- **The Rule:** The status of the child follows the status of the **Mother**.
    
- **Application:**
    
    - If the mother is a slave, the child is a slave (regardless of who the father is).
        
    - If the mother is free, the child is free.
        
- **Exception (Favor to Liberty):** If the mother was free at _any_ point during the pregnancy (e.g., she was free at conception but enslaved before birth), the child is born **Free**.
    

**2. By Capture (Jus Gentium)**

- **Prisoners of War:** Enemies captured in war became the property of the State, which usually sold them into slavery.
    
- **Note:** Roman citizens captured by enemies also became slaves of the enemy, but if they escaped and returned, they regained their rights via _Jus Postliminii_.
    

**3. By Civil Law (Punishment)**

- **Criminals:** A person convicted of a capital crime could be sentenced to death or to work in the mines. Such a person became a "Slave of Punishment" (_Servus Poenae_).
    
- **Ingratitude:** A freedman (former slave) who was grossly ungrateful to his former master could be recalled into slavery.
    

---

#### III. The _Peculium_ (The Economic Exception)

Although a slave could own nothing, commercial reality required a workaround.

- **Concept:** The master would grant the slave a sum of money or property (a shop, cattle, or even other slaves) to manage. This was called the **Peculium**.
    
- **Legal Status:** Legally, the _Peculium_ remained the property of the master. He could take it back at any time.
    
- **Practical Status:** Socially, it was treated as the slave's own funds. Slaves often used their _Peculium_ to trade, make profits, and eventually **buy their own freedom** from the master.
    

---

#### IV. Manumission (Termination of Slavery)

Slavery ended through **Manumission**—the act of granting liberty.

**1. Formal Modes (Conferred Citizenship)** These methods made the slave a full Roman Citizen (_Civis_).

- **_Vindicta:_** A mock trial. A third party (Assertor Libertatis) touched the slave with a rod (_Vindicta_) before a magistrate, claiming he was free. The master remained silent, and the magistrate declared the slave free.
    
- **_Censu:_** The master allowed the slave's name to be entered on the Censor's list of citizens (conducted every 5 years).
    
- **_Testamento:_** The master granted freedom in his Will. ("Let my slave Stichus be free").
    

**2. Informal Modes (Conferred Lesser Status)**

- Granting freedom by letter (_Per Epistolam_) or declaration among friends (_Inter Amicos_).
    
- Initially, these slaves were not legally free, but the Praetor protected them. Later, they were given the status of **Latini Juniani** (not full citizens).
    

---

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Status of Freedmen:** A manumitted slave became a **Libertinus** (Freedman). He was a citizen but had fewer political rights than a freeborn man (_Ingenuus_). He also owed specific duties of respect and service (_Obsequium_) to his former master (Patron).
    
- **Crucial Maxim:** _"Partus sequitur ventrem"_ ( The offspring follows the womb). Memorize this. It is the definitive rule for determination of status by birth.
    
- **Contradiction:** Slavery is the only institution where Roman Law explicitly admits it is **"Contrary to Nature"** (since by natural law, all men are born free), yet it upholds it as part of the "Law of Nations" (war).
