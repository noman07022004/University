## 15. Female Heirs ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** You cannot calculate inheritance shares without knowing _which_ females are eligible. The rules for female heirs are restrictive and specific compared to males.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Problem questions frequently involve a deceased Hindu leaving behind a wife, a married daughter, and a brother. You must know if the daughter or wife excludes the brother, and what the hierarchy is among daughters.

---

### Introduction

Historically, the general policy of Hindu Law (especially the Mitakshara School) was to exclude females from inheritance. The belief was that property should remain within the agnatic male line to ensure the performance of spiritual offerings (_Pinda_).

However, specific texts and statutory interventions have recognized certain female relations as heirs. In Bangladesh, the list of female heirs is much shorter than in India, as the _Hindu Succession Act, 1956_ (India) does not apply. We rely on the classical law modified by the **Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937**.

---

### I. Capacity of Female Heirs

**Syllabus Covered:** _15.1 Capacity_

"Capacity" refers to the eligibility of a female to inherit property. Unlike males, whose right is often based on birth or simple consanguinity, female heirs face stricter scrutiny.

**1. The Recognized Female Heirs** In the uncodified law applicable in Bangladesh, only **five** females are generally recognized as heirs to a male’s property. If a female relative is not on this list, she does not inherit (unless a specific custom proves otherwise).

1. **Widow (_Patni_)**
    
2. **Daughter (_Duhita_)**
    
3. **Mother (_Mata_)**
    
4. **Paternal Grandmother (_Pitamahi_)**
    
5. **Paternal Great-Grandmother (_Prapitamahi_)**
    

> **Note:** Under the _Dayabhaga_ school, this list is strict. Under _Mitakshara_ (Benares school), some other females might be admitted as _Bandhus_, but for primary succession, these five are the key players.

**2. The Condition of Chastity** A unique incapacity applies to females, particularly the widow.

- **The Rule:** A widow must be **chaste** (sexually faithful to her husband) at the time the succession opens (i.e., at the moment of her husband's death) to inherit.
    
- **Effect of Unchastity:**
    
    - If she is unchaste _at the time of death_, she is disqualified from inheriting.
        
    - If she inherits the property and _subsequently_ becomes unchaste, she does **not** lose the property (vested rights cannot be divested).
        

---

### II. Hierarchy and Rules for Specific Females

To understand capacity fully, one must know the hierarchy, particularly for daughters, which is a common exam topic.

**1. The Widow**

- **Position:** She is the first female heir.
    
- **Statutory Right:** Under the _Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937_, she inherits alongside the son, grandson, and great-grandson. She takes the **same share as a son**.
    
- **Multiple Widows:** If there are multiple widows, they inherit jointly and divide the son's share equally among themselves.
    

**2. The Daughter** The daughter inherits only if there is no male issue (Son, Grandson, Great-grandson) and no Widow.

- **Priority among Daughters (Crucial for Exams):**
    
    - **Dayabhaga School:**
        
        1. Unmarried Daughter.
            
        2. Married daughter who has a son (or is likely to have one).
            
        3. **Excluded:** Barren widowed daughters or daughters who have only female children are often excluded (because they cannot produce a male who can offer _Pinda_).
            
    - **Mitakshara School:**
        
        1. Unmarried Daughter.
            
        2. Married Daughter (indigent/poor takes precedence over wealthy in some sub-schools).
            
    - **Survivorship:** If two daughters inherit, they take jointly with rights of survivorship. If one dies, the other takes the whole; it does not pass to the deceased daughter's heirs yet.
        

**3. The Mother**

- She inherits if there are no male issues, widow, or daughters.
    
- In **Mitakshara**, the Mother is preferred over the Father.
    
- In **Dayabhaga**, the Father is preferred over the Mother.

### III. Limited or Life Interest

**Syllabus Covered:** _15.2 Limited or Life interest_

This subtopic connects directly back to the concept of "Widow's Estate" (Topic 13), but applies it to _all_ female heirs inheriting from males.

**1. The Nature of the Interest** Every female who inherits property from a male (be she widow, daughter, or mother) takes a **Limited Estate**.

- She is not an absolute owner.
    
- She cannot sell/gift the property without "Legal Necessity."
    
- Upon her death, the property reverts to the **next heir of the last male owner** (the Reversioner).
    

**2. Exception (Stridhana)** It is vital to distinguish inherited estate from _Stridhana_.

- If a woman receives property as a gift from her father or husband, or earns it herself, that is _Stridhana_. She is the **Absolute Owner** of Stridhana.
    
- However, property **inherited from a male** is **NOT** Stridhana; it is a Limited Estate.
    

> **Example:** If Mr. X dies leaving a house to his daughter D:
> 
> - D owns the house for her life.
>     
> - D cannot sell it arbitrarily.
>     
> - When D dies, the house goes to Mr. X's nephew (closest male heir), NOT to D's husband or D's daughter.
>

### IV. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Chastity" Trap:** In exam problems, look for facts mentioning a widow "living in adultery" or being "unchaste."
    
    - Check _when_ she became unchaste. If it was _after_ the husband died, she keeps the property. If _before_, she gets nothing.
        
- **Unmarried Daughter's Priority:** In Dayabhaga law, an unmarried daughter excludes a married daughter. This is a strict rule. If the question involves a Bengali Hindu family, apply Dayabhaga rules.
    
- **Step-Mother:** A step-mother is **not** a heir in the primary sense. She does not inherit from her step-son. She is entitled only to maintenance. "Mother" strictly means the biological mother.
    
- **Remarriage:** Under the _Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856_, a widow historically lost her right to her deceased husband's property upon remarriage, as she was considered "dead" to the former family.
