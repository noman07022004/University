### 15.1 Capacity (Female Heirs) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The rules for female capacity are restrictive and distinct from males. Understanding _who_ qualifies and _why_ they might be disqualified (e.g., chastity) is essential for solving succession problems.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** "Chastity" is a favorite exam trap. Problem questions often feature an unchaste widow or daughter to test if you know the specific exclusion rules for females in Bangladesh.
    

---

#### I. Introduction: The General Rule of Exclusion

Historically, the general policy of classical Hindu Law (based on Baudhayana’s text) was that **"women are devoid of the senses and are incompetent to inherit."**

- **Rationale:** Inheritance was linked to the ability to offer spiritual oblations (_Pinda_), a duty primarily performed by males.
    
- **The Exception:** Specific female relations were admitted as heirs only by special texts or statutory intervention. If a female is not on this specific list, she has **no capacity** to inherit (unlike a male relative who might inherit simply by being a distant agnate).
    

---

#### II. The Recognized Female Heirs

In the uncodified law applicable in Bangladesh (Dayabhaga and Mitakshara), only **five** females are primarily recognized as heirs to a male’s property.

1. **Widow (_Patni_):** Now protected by the _Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937_.
    
2. **Daughter (_Duhita_):** Inherits only if there is no Widow or Male Issue.
    
3. **Mother (_Mata_):** Inherits in the absence of the above.
    
4. **Paternal Grandmother (_Pitamahi_):** A much later heir.
    
5. **Paternal Great-Grandmother (_Prapitamahi_):** A remote heir.
    

> **Note:** Under the **Dayabhaga School**, this list is exhaustive. No other female (e.g., step-mother, sister, son’s daughter) can inherit. Under **Mitakshara**, some other females might theoretically be admitted as _Bandhus_ (cognates) in rare cases, but for primary succession, these five are the key figures.

---

#### III. Conditions for Capacity (Disqualifications)

The capacity of a female to inherit is subject to stricter moral conditions than that of a male.

**1. The Condition of Chastity (Sexual Purity)** This is the most critical determinant of a female's capacity.

- **The Rule:** A female heir must be **chaste** (sexually faithful to her husband) at the time the succession opens (i.e., when the owner dies) to be capable of inheriting.
    
- **School Distinction:**
    
    - **Dayabhaga (Bengal):** The requirement of chastity applies to **ALL** female heirs (Widow, Daughter, Mother, etc.). If a daughter is leading an immoral life when her father dies, she is excluded.
        
    - **Mitakshara:** The requirement of chastity is strictly limited to the **Widow**. An unchaste mother or daughter is _not_ excluded from inheritance.
        
- **Timing is Key:** The chastity must be intact **at the moment of the owner's death**.
    
    - If she is unchaste _before_ the death: **Excluded.**
        
    - If she becomes unchaste _after_ inheriting: **Not Excluded.** (Once the property vests in her, it cannot be taken away).
        

**2. Remarriage**

- **Classical Law:** A widow who remarried was considered "dead" to her first husband's family and lost her right to inherit or retain his property.
    
- **Statutory Law:** The _Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856_ legalized remarriage but confirmed the **forfeiture** of the deceased husband's estate upon remarriage.
    
- **Current Application:** If a widow remarries _before_ the succession opens, she has no capacity to inherit. If she remarries _after_ inheriting, she historically forfeited the estate (though there is legal debate, the safe academic answer in Bangladesh is that remarriage generally divests the widow's estate under the 1856 principles).
    

---

#### IV. Priority and Hierarchy Among Females

Even if they have capacity, females do not inherit simultaneously; they follow a strict hierarchy.

**1. The Widow**

- Takes precedence over all other females.
    
- Under the _1937 Act_, she inherits alongside the Son, Grandson, and Great-Grandson.
    

**2. The Daughter**

- She inherits only if there are no male issues and no widow.
    
- **Exclusion of Barren Daughters (Dayabhaga):** Since the Dayabhaga system is based on spiritual benefit, a daughter who is **barren** (cannot have children) or a widow without sons is often excluded, because she cannot produce a son to offer _Pinda_ to her father.
    
- **Preference:**
    
    - _Dayabhaga:_ Unmarried daughters exclude married daughters.
        
    - _Mitakshara:_ Unmarried daughters exclude married daughters; Indigent (poor) married daughters exclude wealthy married daughters.
        

**3. The Mother**

- "Mother" strictly means the biological mother.
    
- **Step-mother:** A step-mother has **no capacity** to inherit as a "Mother." She is entitled only to maintenance.
    

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Unchastity" Trap:** In exam problems, verify _when_ the unchastity occurred.
    
    - _Scenario:_ "W inherits her husband's land. Two years later, she becomes unchaste. Can the husband's brother evict her?"
        
    - _Answer:_ **No.** Vested rights are not divested by subsequent unchastity.
        
- **Step-Mother:** Examiners often list a "Step-Mother" as a claimant. Remember: She is **not an heir**. She gets maintenance only.
