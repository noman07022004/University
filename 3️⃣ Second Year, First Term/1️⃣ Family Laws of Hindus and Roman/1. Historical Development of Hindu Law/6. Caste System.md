## 1.6 Caste System ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Essential for understanding the historical context of Hindu society. Many ancient rules (like who can marry whom or who can adopt) were originally based on caste.  
>- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** Rarely a full essay topic on its own. However, it frequently appears as a short note or as a conceptual part of questions regarding marriage validity (inter-caste marriage) and inheritance rights.
    

---

The "Caste System" is a distinctive feature of ancient Hindu social structure. The term "Caste" is derived from the Portuguese word _Casta_ (meaning race or breed), but the Sanskrit term is **Varna** (meaning color or order). Historically, Hindu Law was not uniform for everyone; rights, duties, and punishments often varied depending on the caste to which an individual belonged.

---

### 1. The Four Varnas (classes)

Ancient texts divided society into four primary hierarchical orders. This division was originally based on occupation and qualities (_Guna_), but later became rigid and hereditary.

1. **Brahmins:** The priestly and learned class. They were the custodians of the Vedas and the law. In ancient times, they enjoyed the highest privileges and lightest punishments.
    
2. **Kshatriyas:** The warrior and ruling class. Their duty was to protect the people and the land.
    
3. **Vaishyas:** The trading, agricultural, and mercantile class. They were the economic backbone of society.
    
4. **Sudras:** The service class. Their duty was to serve the other three classes. Historically, they suffered many legal disabilities (e.g., restricted inheritance rights, inability to perform certain religious rites like _Datta Homam_ in adoption).
    

- **The "Twice-Born" (Dvija):** The first three castes (Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas) are called "Twice-Born" because they undergo the _Upanayana_ (Sacred Thread) ceremony, which is considered a second spiritual birth. Sudras do not undergo this.


### 2. Legal Significance in Ancient Law

In the strict Shastric law:

- **Marriage:** Marriage was strictly **endogamous** (within the same caste). An _Anuloma_ marriage (higher caste man + lower caste woman) was sometimes permitted, but a _Pratiloma_ marriage (lower caste man + higher caste woman) was strictly prohibited and void.
    
- **Adoption:** Sudras could not perform the _Datta Homam_ (fire sacrifice), so their adoption required only the physical giving and taking of the child.
    
- **Sanyasi (Asceticism):** Only the "Twice-Born" could renounce the world and become Sanyasis (monks). Sudras could not.
    

### 3. Modern Legal Status in Bangladesh

Today, the legal relevance of caste has significantly diminished due to democratic values and legislative intervention.

- **Inter-Caste Marriage:**
    
    - In the past, inter-caste marriages were void.
        
    - **Current Position:** In Bangladesh (following the Dayabhaga school and modern interpretation), inter-caste marriages are generally considered **valid**. The old distinctions are largely obsolete in the eyes of the court.
        
- **Constitution of Bangladesh:** Article 28 prohibits discrimination by the State on the grounds of religion, race, **caste**, sex, or place of birth. Therefore, no court can enforce caste-based penalties.
    
- **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850:** This is a vital colonial statute still in force. It ensures that no person forfeits their rights to property or inheritance by reason of renouncing or being excluded from their caste (excommunication).
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Sudra" Distinction:** In adoption problems, pay attention to the caste. If the problem states the parties are Sudras, remember that the religious ceremony (_Datta Homam_) is **not** required for a valid adoption; simple giving and taking suffices.

> **Key Statute:** Memorize the _Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_. It is the legal shield that prevents a person from losing their inheritance just because they married outside their caste or changed their religion.

> **Distinction:** Do not confuse **Caste (Varna)** with **Gotra**.
> 
> - **Caste:** Broad social class (e.g., Brahmin). Marriage _within_ caste is good (historically).
>     
> - **Gotra:** Lineage from a common ancestor. Marriage _within_ the same Gotra is prohibited (incestuous).
>
