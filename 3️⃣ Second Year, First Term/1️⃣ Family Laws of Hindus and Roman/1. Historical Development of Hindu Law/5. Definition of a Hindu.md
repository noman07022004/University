## 1.5 Definition of a Hindu ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Before applying any law, you must know _whom_ it applies to. This is the jurisdictional threshold for the entire course.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** "Who is a Hindu?" is a classic short question or the opening part of a problem question. You need to determine if the parties in a scenario are governed by Hindu Law or another personal law.

---

The term "Hindu" is not defined strictly in the ancient texts (Shastras). It was originally a geographical term used by foreign invaders to denote people living beyond the river Indus (Sindhu). Legally, however, the term has evolved to be quite broad. It does not just denote a religion in the theological sense but a **legal status** that determines which personal law applies to an individual.

The definition is generally categorized into three modes: by **Religion**, by **Birth**, and by **Law (Statutory/Negative definition)**.

---

### 1. Hindu by Religion

Any person who follows the Hindu religion in any of its forms or developments is a Hindu.

- **Core Beliefs:** This includes those who believe in the Vedas, Supreme God (Brahman), and the theory of Karma/Reincarnation.
    
- **Sects:** It covers all sects and sub-sects, such as:
    
    - **Vaishnavas** (worshippers of Vishnu)
        
    - **Shaivas** (worshippers of Shiva)
        
    - **Shaktas** (worshippers of Shakti/Mother Goddess)
        
    - **Reformist Sects:** Followers of Arya Samaj, Brahmo Samaj, and Prarthana Samaj are also legally Hindus, even if they reject idol worship or caste.
        

### 2. Hindu by Birth

A person is a Hindu by birth if they are born into a Hindu family. The degree of religious practice does not matter here.

- **Both Parents Hindu:** If both parents are Hindus, the child is essentially Hindu.
    
- **One Parent Hindu:** If only one parent is Hindu, the child is considered Hindu if they are **brought up** as a member of the Hindu tribe, group, or family.
    
    - _Example:_ If a Hindu man marries a Christian woman, and the child is raised with Hindu customs, the child is legally Hindu.
        
- **Illegitimate Children:** An illegitimate child born to a Hindu mother is generally considered a Hindu, regardless of the father's faith (unless the child is raised in the father's faith).
    

### 3. Hindu by Conversion and Reconversion

Hinduism is not historically a proselytizing religion (seeking converts), but modern law recognizes conversion.

- **Conversion:** A person of another faith (Muslim, Christian) can become a Hindu. This is usually done through a ceremony (like _Shuddhi_ performed by Arya Samaj) or by clearly expressing an intention to become Hindu and being accepted by the community.
    
- **Reconversion:** A person who was born Hindu, converted to another faith, and then decides to return to Hinduism is also considered a Hindu upon return.
    

### 4. The "Negative" or Statutory Definition

In modern legislation (like the _Hindu Marriage Act_ in India, and by extension of interpretation in Bangladesh), the term "Hindu" is defined negatively to be as inclusive as possible.

A person is considered a Hindu for legal purposes if:

1. They are a **Buddhist, Jain, or Sikh** by religion. (Historically, these groups are governed by Hindu law in personal matters unless they have their own specific statutes).
    
2. They are **NOT** a Muslim, Christian, Parsi, or Jew by religion.
    
    - _Logic:_ If a person in Bangladesh/India does not belong to one of the other distinct legal communities (Islam, Christianity, etc.), the default "residuary" law applied to them is Hindu Law.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Jains, Sikhs, and Buddhists:** Be careful in exams. While these are distinct religions theologically, for the purpose of **Personal Law** (marriage, inheritance) in the absence of specific laws, they are governed by **Hindu Law**. If a problem question features a Buddhist family dispute, you apply Hindu Law principles.

> **Loss of Caste:** Under the _Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_, even if a person is outcasted or excommunicated from their social group, they do not cease to be a Hindu for legal purposes.

> **Atheists:** Interestingly, a person born a Hindu who becomes an atheist (rejects God) remains a Hindu for legal purposes regarding marriage and inheritance, unless they formally convert to another religion (like Islam or Christianity).
