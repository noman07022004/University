## 6.2 Persons Associated with Maintenance ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This subtopic acts as the "Who's Who" of maintenance law. It defines the complete list of beneficiaries. You need to know not just _that_ a wife is entitled, but also _who else_ (like an illegitimate son or a stepmother) falls under this protective umbrella.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While the "Wife's right" is the most frequent specific question, exam problems often involve secondary characters like an "aged father" or "unmarried daughter" claiming maintenance. You must know the extent of their rights.
    

---

### Introduction

The "Persons Associated with Maintenance" refers to the specific classes of individuals who have a legal claim to be supported by another Hindu. As discussed in the previous section, this claim arises either from a direct blood/marital relationship (Personal Liability) or from the possession of family property (Estate Liability).

The persons entitled to maintenance can be broadly categorized into:

1. **The Wife**
    
2. **The Children (Legitimate and Illegitimate)**
    
3. **The Aged Parents**
    
4. **The Widowed Daughter-in-law**
    
5. **Dependents of the Deceased**
    

---

### 1. The Wife

The wife is the most significant person associated with maintenance.

- **Nature of Right:** Her right is absolute and personal against her husband.
    
- **Status:** She is considered the _Ardhangini_ (half-body). A husband is bound to maintain her even if he has no property, provided she is **chaste** and lives with him (unless she has a valid ground to live separately).

### 2. The Children

A Hindu father is personally liable to maintain his legitimate and illegitimate children.

#### **A. Legitimate Sons**

- **Minority:** A father is bound to maintain his legitimate sons as long as they are **minors** (under 18 years).
    
- **Adult Sons:** An adult son is generally **not** entitled to maintenance from his father, even if he is destitute, unless he is unable to maintain himself due to physical or mental infirmity.
    

#### **B. Legitimate Daughters**

- **Unmarried:** A father is bound to maintain his unmarried daughter. This right includes reasonable expenses for her marriage.
    
- **Married:** Once married, her maintenance becomes the primary duty of her husband. However, if she becomes widowed and destitute, she may claim maintenance from her father's family as a dependent.
    

#### **C. Illegitimate Children**

- **Sons:** Under the Shastric law (Dayabhaga), illegitimate sons of the three higher castes are not entitled to inheritance but are entitled to **maintenance for life**.
    
- **Daughters:** Illegitimate daughters are entitled to maintenance until they get married.
    

### 3. Aged Parents

The obligation to maintain parents is personal and absolute upon the son.

- **Who is included?**
    
    - **Aged Father:** If he is unable to maintain himself.
        
    - **Aged Mother:** A son must maintain his natural mother.
        
    - **Step-Mother:** A childless step-mother is also entitled to maintenance from her step-son.
        
- **Nature:** This is a pious duty. A son cannot plead "lack of funds" if he is able-bodied. Interestingly, under strict Hindu law, a daughter (before statutory reforms in India) was not personally liable to maintain parents, but in modern interpretations, if she has independent means, courts often enforce it.
    

### 4. Widowed Daughter-in-Law

This is a unique category where the liability shifts.

- **Father-in-Law's Liability:** A father-in-law has a **moral obligation** to maintain the widow of his predeceased son.
    
- **Conditions:**
    
    1. She is unable to maintain herself from her own property or earnings.
        
    2. She cannot obtain maintenance from her husband's estate, her son, or her father's family.
        
- **After Death:** Upon the death of the father-in-law, this moral obligation ripens into a **legal obligation** for his heirs (who inherit his property).
    

### 5. Dependents of the Deceased

When a Hindu dies, his estate passes to his heirs subject to the rights of his "Dependents." These are people whom the deceased was morally or legally bound to support.

- **Who are Dependents?**
    
    - Aged parents.
        
    - Unmarried daughters.
        
    - Widowed daughters (if destitute).
        
    - Illegitimate children.
        
    - Concubine (_Avaruddha Stree_): In ancient law, a faithful concubine kept permanently by the deceased was entitled to maintenance. (This is largely obsolete in modern society but technically part of the old law).
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Adult Son" Rule:** A common exam trick: _"A 25-year-old healthy son is unemployed. Can he sue his father for maintenance?"_
> 
> - **Answer:** **No.** In Hindu Law, an adult male is expected to earn his own living. The father is not a permanent welfare provider.
>     

> **Illegitimate Children:** Unlike Muslim Law (where illegitimate children have no rights against the father), **Hindu Law** is more humane. It grants them a right to maintenance (food/shelter) even if it denies them inheritance (property).

> **Step-Mother:** Remember specifically that a **childless** step-mother has a right to be maintained. If she has her own sons, she must look to them first.
