## 6. Maintenance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Maintenance is a fundamental right that ensures the survival of dependent family members. Understanding the distinction between "personal liability" (absolute duty) and "liability of the estate" (dependent on property possession) is crucial for legal practice.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often focus on the "Wife's right to separate residence and maintenance" and the "Widow's right against her father-in-law." Problem questions frequently involve calculating who is liable to pay.

---

### Introduction

In Hindu Law, the right to maintenance is not merely based on contract or relationship; it is a **duty based on humanity and social obligation**. The term "Maintenance" (_Bhrana-poshana_) generally includes the provision of food, clothing, residence, education, and medical attendance and treatment.

The liability to maintain others arises from two distinct sources:

1. **Personal Obligation:** Based on the relationship between the parties, regardless of whether the person possesses property. (e.g., A father must maintain his minor son even if the father is poor).
    
2. **Possession of Property:** Based on the possession of ancestral or joint family property. (e.g., An heir is bound to maintain the dependents of the deceased whose property he has inherited).
    

---

### 1. Liability: Personal vs. Property

The liability to maintain relatives is divided into two categories:

#### A. Personal Liability (Absolute)

- **Nature:** This obligation is personal and absolute. It exists whether the person has property or not.
    
- **Who is liable?** A Hindu is personally liable to maintain:
    
    1. His **Wife**.
        
    2. His **Minor Sons**.
        
    3. His **Unmarried Daughters**.
        
    4. His **Aged Parents**.
        

#### B. Liability Dependent on Property

- **Nature:** This obligation attaches to the property. It is not personal.
    
- **Who is liable?**
    
    - **The Manager (Karta):** The manager of a Joint Hindu Family is liable to maintain all members of the family (and their wives/children) out of the joint family property.
        
    - **The Heir:** Any person who inherits the estate of a deceased Hindu is legally bound to maintain the "dependents" of that deceased person (e.g., the deceased's widow, unmarried daughter, or illegitimate son) out of the estate.
        

---

### 2. Persons Entitled to Maintenance

#### A. Wife's Right to Maintenance (Subtopic 6.4)

The wife is the "half-body" (_Ardhangini_) of the husband. Her right to maintenance is absolute.

- **Conditions:** She must be **chaste** and generally must live with her husband.
    
- **Loss of Right:** An unchaste wife loses her right to maintenance entirely. However, if she repents and returns to a moral life, she may be entitled to "starving maintenance" (bare minimum food/clothes).
    
- **Separate Maintenance:** Generally, she must live with him. However, she can claim maintenance while living separately if she has a legal ground (discussed below under Statutory Provisions).
    

#### B. Widow's Right to Maintenance (Subtopic 6.6)

- **Against Husband's Estate:** After the husband's death, his widow is entitled to be maintained out of his estate. If the sons inherit the property, they must maintain their widowed mother.
    
- **Against Father-in-Law:** If the widow has no property and cannot maintain herself, does her father-in-law have to pay?
    
    - **Legal Rule:** The father-in-law has a **moral obligation** to maintain his widowed daughter-in-law. This moral obligation becomes a **legal obligation** _only_ if he inherits ancestral property. If he has only self-acquired property, he is not legally bound to pay.
        

#### C. Children and Parents

- **Sons:** Entitled to maintenance until majority (18 years).
    
- **Daughters:** Entitled to maintenance until they get married. The father is also liable for their marriage expenses.
    
- **Parents:** Aged and infirm parents (including a childless stepmother) are entitled to maintenance from their sons.
    

---

### 3. Maintenance in Statutory Provisions of Bangladesh (Subtopic 6.3)

While the general law is uncodified, a specific statute governs the wife's right to live separately.

**The Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946** This Act acts as a substitute for divorce in Bangladesh. It allows a wife to sue for maintenance _without_ forfeiting her right to live apart from her husband.

- **Grounds for Claiming Separate Maintenance (Section 2):** A Hindu married woman is entitled to separate residence and maintenance if her husband:
    
    1. Suffers from a loathsome disease not contracted from her.
        
    2. Treats her with such **cruelty** that it renders it unsafe for her to live with him.
        
    3. is guilty of **desertion** (abandoning her without her consent).
        
    4. **Marries again** (Polygamy).
        
    5. Ceases to be a Hindu (Conversion).
        
    6. Keeps a concubine in the house.
        
    7. For any other justifiable cause.
        

---

### 4. Amount of Maintenance (Subtopic 6.5)

How much money should be paid? There is no fixed mathematical formula (like "10% of salary").

- **Discretion of Court:** The _Quantum_ (amount) is entirely at the discretion of the Court.
    
- **Factors Considered:**
    
    1. **Status and Position:** The social standing of the parties.
        
    2. **Needs:** The reasonable wants of the claimant (food, shelter, medical).
        
    3. **Means of the Payer:** The value of the husband's/heir's estate and income.
        
    4. **Claimant's Own Income:** If the wife has her own earnings or property (_Stridhana_), the court may reduce the maintenance amount.
        
    5. **Number of Dependents:** How many other mouths the payer has to feed.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Moral to Legal" Transformation:** A classic exam concept involving the **Father-in-law**.
> 
> - While the father-in-law is alive, his duty to maintain a widowed daughter-in-law is _Moral_.
>     
> - When he dies, this duty transmits to _his_ heirs (e.g., the other brothers-in-law) as a _Legal_ duty, binding on the estate they inherit.
>     

> **Arrears of Maintenance:** A wife can sue not just for future maintenance but also for "Arrears of Maintenance" (unpaid money for past years where she was neglected).

> **Charge on Property:** Maintenance is generally a personal obligation. However, a court can declare it a **"Charge"** on specific immovable property. This means if the husband sells the land, the buyer might still have to pay the wife's maintenance from that land (if the buyer knew of the charge).
