## 6.5 Amount of Maintenance ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** The concept is straightforward—there is no fixed "price list." However, understanding the _discretionary_ nature of the court and the specific factors that influence the calculation is important for legal practice.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** In problem questions, students are often asked not just "Is she entitled?" but "How much is she entitled to?" You must be able to list the factors the court will consider.
    

---

### Introduction

A common misconception is that maintenance is a fixed percentage of the husband's income (e.g., "half the salary"). In Hindu Law, there is no such fixed mathematical formula. The determination of the **quantum** (amount) of maintenance is left entirely to the **judicial discretion** of the Court.

The guiding principle is that the amount should be such that the wife (or other dependent) can live in reasonable comfort, consistent with the status and dignity of the family. It should neither be too extravagant nor too meager.

### 1. General Rule: Discretion of the Court

The court determines the amount based on the specific facts and circumstances of each case. The decision is not arbitrary; it must be exercised on sound legal principles.

- **No Fixed Ratio:** There is no rule that the wife gets 1/3rd or 1/5th of the income.
    
- **Objective:** The goal is to place the wife in a position similar to what she would have enjoyed had she lived with her husband properly.
    

### 2. Factors Determining the Amount

While exercising its discretion, the Court considers the following factors:

#### **A. The Means and Capacity of the Husband/Payer**

- The court looks at the **total income** and the value of the estate of the person liable to pay.
    
- **Earning Capacity:** Not just what he _is_ earning, but what he is _capable_ of earning. An able-bodied man cannot plead unemployment to pay a nominal sum.
    

#### **B. The Status of the Parties**

- The court considers the **social standing**, position, and lifestyle of the family.
    
- A wife of a wealthy businessman is entitled to a higher amount than the wife of a day laborer, as she is entitled to live according to the standard she was accustomed to.
    

#### **C. The Reasonable Wants of the Claimant**

- This includes food, clothing, residence, education (for children), medical treatment, and occasional religious expenses.
    

#### **D. The Number of Persons to be Maintained**

- The court considers how many other legal dependents the husband has.
    
- _Example:_ If a man earns 50,000 Taka but has to support his aged parents and 3 minor children, the amount awarded to the estranged wife will be adjusted accordingly so strictly that he is not left destitute.
    

#### **E. The Claimant's Own Income**

- If the wife has her own separate property (_Stridhana_) or independent earnings, the court will take this into account.
    
- If her income is sufficient to maintain herself, the court may award a nominal amount or no maintenance at all. However, if her income is insufficient, the husband must make up the deficit.
    

#### **F. Conduct of the Parties**

- The behavior of the husband (cruelty, adultery) and the wife is relevant. While unchastity forfeits the right entirely, minor misconduct might reduce the amount.
    

### 3. Variation of Maintenance

The amount of maintenance fixed by a court decree or agreement is not permanent. It is **subject to variation**.

- **Change of Circumstances:** Either party can apply to the Court to alter the amount if circumstances change.
    
    - _Increase:_ If the husband’s income increases or the cost of living rises (inflation).
        
    - _Decrease:_ If the husband loses his job or the wife inherits a fortune.
        

### 4. Arrears of Maintenance

- **Past Dues:** A wife (or dependent) can sue not only for future maintenance but also for **arrears of maintenance** (unpaid money) for the period she was neglected before filing the suit.
    
- **Limitation:** Generally, the court may limit the arrears to a reasonable period to prevent a sudden crushing burden on the husband, though legally, arrears can be claimed for up to 12 years under the Limitation Act (rarely awarded in full for such long periods in practice).

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Luxury" vs. "Necessity" Argument:** In exams, do not write that maintenance is only for "bread and butter."
> 
> - _Academic Tip:_ Use the phrase **"consistent with their social status."** If the husband drives a car, the wife's maintenance should cover transport costs, not just walking money.
>     

> **Independent Income:** A trick scenario: _"Wife earns 20,000. Husband earns 1 Lakh. Can she claim maintenance?"_
> 
> - _Answer:_ **Yes.** But the amount will be calculated to bridge the gap between her 20,000 and the standard of living provided by 1 Lakh. Her earning does not absolve him completely if the status gap is high.
>     

> **Procedural Law:** In Bangladesh, these factors are weighed by the **Family Court** judge under the _Family Courts Ordinance, 1985_.
