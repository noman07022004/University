## 6.4 Wife's Right to Maintenance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the core of maintenance law. The wife is the primary beneficiary in Hindu law. Understanding that her right is "personal" (husband must pay even if poor) but "conditional" (she must be chaste) is fundamental.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Very frequent in exams. You will often get problem questions where a wife lives separately or is accused of adultery, and you must determine if she can still claim maintenance.
    

---

### Introduction

In Hindu Law, the wife is not merely a partner but is considered the _Ardhangini_ (half of the husband’s body). Consequently, the husband has a non-delegable, personal, and absolute obligation to maintain his wife. This duty arises from the very existence of the marriage relationship and exists irrespective of whether the husband possesses any property or not. As long as the marriage subsists, the right subsists, subject to certain conditions.

### 1. Nature of the Right

- **Personal Obligation:** The husband is bound to maintain his wife even if he is destitute, unemployed, or has no ancestral property. The Privy Council famously held that if necessary, he must "starve himself" to maintain her.
    
- **Duration:** The right lasts for her entire life, provided the marriage remains valid and she remains chaste.
    

### 2. Conditions for Maintenance

While the right is absolute, it is not unconditional.

#### **A. The Condition of Chastity**

- **The Rule:** Chastity is a condition precedent. An unchaste wife (one who commits adultery) forfeits her right to maintenance entirely.
    
- **Starving Maintenance:** However, if an unchaste wife repents and returns to a moral life, some schools of law and modern courts suggest she may be entitled to "Starving Maintenance" (bare minimum food and clothes to keep her alive), though this is discretionary.
    

#### **B. The Duty to Live with Husband**

- **General Rule:** A wife’s first duty is to submit herself to the authority of her husband and remain under his roof and protection. Therefore, generally, she is not entitled to separate maintenance if she refuses to live with him without a valid cause.
    

### 3. Right to Separate Maintenance

Can a wife live apart and still claim money? Yes. As detailed in the _Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946_ (see Subtopic 6.3), a wife is entitled to live separately and claim maintenance if:

1. The husband is guilty of **Cruelty** (physical or mental).
    
2. The husband suffers from a **loathsome disease** (e.g., Leprosy).
    
3. The husband is guilty of **Desertion**.
    
4. The husband **marries again** (Polygamy).
    
5. The husband keeps a **concubine**.
    
6. The husband **converts** to another religion.
    

### 4. Loss of Right (Forfeiture)

A wife loses her right to maintenance in the following scenarios:

1. **Unchastity:** If she becomes unchaste (adulterous).
    
2. **Conversion:** If she ceases to be a Hindu (converts to Islam/Christianity).
    
    - _Note:_ If the _husband_ converts, she gets separate maintenance. If _she_ converts, she gets nothing.
        
3. **Refusal to Live:** If she refuses to live with her husband without any "justifiable cause" (as listed above).
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Conversion" Double Standard:** Be careful with this distinction in exams:
> 
> - **Husband Converts:** He must still pay maintenance. (His act cannot wrong her).
>     
> - **Wife Converts:** She loses the right to maintenance. (Her act forfeits her status in Hindu law).
>     

> **Polygamy and Maintenance:** If a man has two wives, he must maintain **both**. If he lives with the second wife, the first wife has an automatic right to live separately and claim full maintenance.

> **Burden of Proof:** If a husband refuses to pay alleging his wife is "unchaste," the **burden of proof** lies heavily on the husband to prove her adultery with clear evidence. Mere suspicion is not enough.
