## 6.1 Liability for Maintenance ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the foundational concept of the entire chapter on Maintenance. You must distinguish between "maintenance as a personal duty" (which you must pay even if you are poor) and "maintenance as a charge on property" (which you only pay if you inherit assets).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Problem questions often test this distinction. For example: _"Can a father refuse to maintain his minor son because he is unemployed?"_ (Answer: No, it's a personal liability).
    

---

### Introduction

In Hindu Law, the liability to maintain others is not merely based on a contract. It arises from the concept of **Social and Moral Duty** (_Dharma_). The ancient sages, particularly Manu, declared that a man must support his closest relations—his wife, aged parents, and minor children—regardless of his financial status.

The liability to pay maintenance falls into two distinct legal categories:

1. **Personal Liability:** Arising from the existence of a relationship.
    
2. **Liability Dependent on Property:** Arising from the possession of property.
    

---

### 1. Personal Liability (Absolute Obligation)

This liability is based purely on the relationship between the parties. It is personal, absolute, and unconditional.

- **The Rule:** A Hindu is legally bound to maintain certain close relations irrespective of whether he possesses any property or not. Even if he is a pauper, legally he cannot escape this liability. As the Privy Council once observed, he must "starve himself to maintain them."
    
- **Who is Personally Liable?**
    
    - A **Husband** is liable to maintain his wife.
        
    - A **Father** is liable to maintain his minor sons and unmarried daughters.
        
    - A **Son** is liable to maintain his aged parents.
        
- **Nature of Liability:** It is legally enforceable. If a father refuses to feed his minor son, the court can attach his salary or even arrest him (under civil execution rules) to enforce the payment.
    

### 2. Liability Dependent on Property (Limited Obligation)

This liability is not based on the relationship but on the fact that a person is in possession of property that legally belongs to the family or the deceased.

- **The Rule:** A person is liable to maintain another _only_ to the extent of the property he holds. If the property is lost or insufficient, the liability ends.
    
- **Scenarios:**
    
    1. **Liability of the Heir:** An heir who inherits the estate of a deceased Hindu is bound to maintain the "dependents" of the deceased out of that estate.
        
        - _Example:_ If a man dies leaving a mistress (concubine) or an illegitimate daughter, his legitimate son who inherits the property must maintain them, not because they are the son's relatives, but because they were dependents on the estate.
            
    2. **Liability of the Manager (Karta):** In a Joint Hindu Family, the Karta is liable to maintain all members of the joint family (coparceners, their wives, and children) out of the joint family funds. He is not personally liable if the family business fails and there are no funds.
        

---

### 3. The Doctrine of "Moral turned Legal" Obligation

This is a specific and vital concept in Hindu Law regarding Liability, often appearing in exams involving a **Father-in-law**.

- **The Situation:** Does a father-in-law have to maintain his widowed daughter-in-law?
    
- **Stage 1 (Moral Liability):** While the father-in-law is alive, he has only a **moral obligation** to maintain his widowed daughter-in-law (if she is destitute). She cannot sue him in court to enforce it if he has no ancestral property.
    
- **Stage 2 (Legal Liability):** Upon the death of the father-in-law, this moral obligation transmits to his **heirs** (e.g., his surviving sons/brothers) as a **legal liability**.
    
    - _The Rule:_ An heir is legally bound to provide maintenance out of the estate to those whom the late owner was morally bound to maintain.
        

---

### Summary Table: Types of Liability

|**Feature**|**Personal Liability**|**Liability Dependent on Property**|
|---|---|---|
|**Basis**|Relationship (Blood/Marriage).|Possession of Estate/Assets.|
|**Condition**|Absolute (Must pay even if poor).|Limited (Pay only if property exists).|
|**Who is Liable?**|Husband, Father, Son.|Heirs, Karta (Manager).|
|**Beneficiaries**|Wife, Minor Children, Parents.|Widows, Dependents of deceased, Joint Family members.|

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Exam Problem Tip:** If a question says: _"Mr. X is unemployed and has no property. His wife sues for maintenance. Can he refuse?"_
> 
> - **Answer:** **No.** He is **personally liable**. The court assumes an able-bodied man can earn.
>     

> **Inheritance vs. Maintenance:** Remember the hierarchy.
> 
> - Debts of the deceased come first.
>     
> - **Maintenance of dependents** comes second.
>     
> - Inheritance (personal enjoyment of heirs) comes last.
>     
> - _Tip:_ Maintenance is a "first charge" on the inheritance.
>     

> **Moral Obligation:** Memorize the **Father-in-law** exception. It is the only instance where a "moral duty" transforms into a "legal duty" upon death.
