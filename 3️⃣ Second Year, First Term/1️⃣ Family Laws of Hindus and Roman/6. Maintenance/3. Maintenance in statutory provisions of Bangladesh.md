## 6.3 Maintenance in Statutory Provisions of Bangladesh ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Since Hindu Law in Bangladesh does not allow divorce, this specific statute is the _only_ legal lifeline for a Hindu woman trapped in an abusive marriage. Understanding this Act is essential to knowing the practical remedies available to women.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a "super-important" topic. Exam questions frequently ask: "Discuss the grounds under which a Hindu wife can claim separate residence and maintenance." Problem questions often feature a husband marrying a second wife, asking if the first wife can sue (Answer: Yes, under this Act).
    

---

### Introduction

Under the general uncodified Hindu Law, a wife’s right to maintenance is generally conditional on her living with her husband. The traditional view was that the husband and wife are one entity, and she must reside under his roof and protection.

However, to protect women from being forced to live in abusive or intolerable conditions, the Legislature enacted specific statutes. In Bangladesh, the primary statutory provision governing this is **The Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946**. Additionally, the **Family Courts Ordinance, 1985** provides the procedural forum for enforcing these rights.

---

### 1. The Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946

This Act is the Magna Carta for distressed Hindu wives in Bangladesh. It statutorily recognizes that a woman has the right to live separately from her husband and still claim maintenance under specific justifiable circumstances.

#### **Grounds for Separate Maintenance (Section 2)**

A Hindu married woman shall be entitled to separate residence and maintenance from her husband on any of the following grounds:

1. **Disease:** If the husband is suffering from any loathsome disease (e.g., Leprosy or virulent venereal disease) which has not been contracted from her.
    
2. **Cruelty:** If the husband renders it unsafe or undesirable for her to live with him by his behavior. This includes both physical violence and mental cruelty.
    
3. **Desertion:** If the husband is guilty of abandoning her without her consent or against her wish.
    
4. **Polygamy:** If the husband **marries again**.
    
    - _Significance:_ This is the most frequently used ground. Since polygamy is legal for men, the law gives the first wife the option to leave and claim maintenance if he brings a second wife. She is not forced to share the house.
        
5. **Conversion:** If the husband ceases to be a Hindu by conversion to another religion (e.g., Islam or Christianity).
    
6. **Concubinage:** If the husband keeps a concubine (mistress) in the house or habitually resides with a concubine elsewhere.
    
7. **Other Justifiable Cause:** A "residuary clause" allowing the court to grant relief for any other valid reason not listed above (e.g., persistent demand for dowry).
    

#### **The Proviso (Disqualification)**

The Act contains a strict condition: A Hindu married woman shall **not** be entitled to separate residence and maintenance if:

- She is **unchaste** (adulterous).
    
- She converts to another religion.
    
- She fails to comply with a decree for restitution of conjugal rights without sufficient cause.
    

---

### 2. The Family Courts Ordinance, 1985

While the 1946 Act provides the _Substantive Law_ (the right), the _Family Courts Ordinance, 1985_ provides the _Procedural Law_ (the remedy).

- **Jurisdiction:** Section 5 of the Ordinance grants the Family Courts exclusive jurisdiction to try suits relating to **Maintenance**.
    
- **Applicability:** Crucially, this Ordinance applies to **all citizens** of Bangladesh, including Hindus.
    
- **Significance:** Before 1985, a Hindu woman had to file a regular civil suit (which took years) to claim maintenance. Now, she can file a suit in the Family Court for a faster and cheaper remedy.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Polygamy" Remedy:** In exam problems, if a man marries a second wife, the first wife cannot sue for _Bigamy_ (because it's legal), nor for _Divorce_ (because it's not allowed). Her **ONLY** remedy is to sue for **Separate Maintenance** under the 1946 Act.

> **Cruelty Standard:** The definition of "Cruelty" under this Act is interpreted liberally by Bangladeshi courts. It is not limited to physical beating; it includes mental torture, neglecting the wife, or forcing her to live in degrading conditions.

> **Conversion:** Note the difference:
> 
> - Husband converts $\rightarrow$ Wife can claim separate maintenance.
>     
> - Wife converts $\rightarrow$ Wife **loses** her right to maintenance (under the Proviso).
