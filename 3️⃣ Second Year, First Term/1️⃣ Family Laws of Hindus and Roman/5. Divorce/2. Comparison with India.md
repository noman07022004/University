## 5.2 Comparison with India ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This comparison is the single best way to understand "what could have been." It highlights the static nature of Hindu Law in Bangladesh by contrasting it with the dynamic, reformed law of India.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often ask for a "Comparative study of Divorce laws" or "Why the position of a Hindu wife in Bangladesh is different from that in India."
    

---

### Introduction

The history of Hindu Law is a tale of two paths. Until 1947, the law was the same across the subcontinent. However, after independence, India chose to aggressively reform and codify its personal laws to ensure gender justice. In contrast, East Pakistan (now Bangladesh) adopted a policy of non-interference, leaving the law in its traditional, uncodified state.

The result is that **India** follows the "Modern Statutory Hindu Law," while **Bangladesh** follows the "Traditional Shastric Hindu Law." The difference is most glaring in the law of Divorce.

### 1. The Legal Basis

- **India:** The law is governed by **The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955**. This Act overrode the ancient texts and introduced uniform rules for all Hindus.
    
- **Bangladesh:** The law is governed by the ancient **Shastras (Dayabhaga)**, Custom, and the **Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946**. The 1955 Indian Act has **NO application** here.
    

### 2. Concept of Marriage

- **India:** Marriage is still a sacrament, but the 1955 Act introduced significant **contractual features**. It can be dissolved, essentially treating it as a contract that can be terminated under specific conditions.
    
- **Bangladesh:** Marriage remains a **pure Sacrament** (_Samskara_). It is an eternal, indissoluble union of souls. The law does not recognize the concept that a "holy union" can be broken by a court decree.
    

### 3. Availability of Divorce

- **India:** Divorce is available to **both husband and wife** on specific statutory grounds.
    
- **Bangladesh:** Divorce is **generally not recognized**.
    
    - It is available _only_ if a valid **custom** exists in the specific caste (usually lower castes).
        
    - For higher castes, there is absolutely no provision for divorce.
        

### 4. Grounds for Divorce vs. Separate Residence

The remedy for a distressed wife differs fundamentally.

- **In India (Divorce):** Under Sec 13 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, a spouse can file for **Divorce** on grounds like:
    
    - Adultery
        
    - Cruelty
        
    - Desertion (2 years)
        
    - Conversion to another religion
        
    - Incurable Insanity or Leprosy
        
    - Renunciation of the world
        
    - Presumed death (missing for 7 years)
        
- **In Bangladesh (Separate Residence):** A wife cannot sue for divorce on these grounds. She can only sue for **Separate Residence and Maintenance** under the 1946 Act on similar grounds (Cruelty, Desertion, Polygamy, etc.).
    
    - _Crucial Difference:_ In India, the marriage ends. In Bangladesh, she lives apart, but the marriage continues legally (she cannot remarry).
        

### 5. Mutual Consent

- **India:** Introduced **Divorce by Mutual Consent** (Sec 13B). If both parties agree that the marriage is not working, they can separate amicably after a cooling-off period.
    
- **Bangladesh:** There is **no concept** of divorce by mutual consent. Even if both husband and wife desperately want to separate, the law does not allow them to break the bond.
    

### Comparative Summary Table

|**Feature**|**India (Modern Law)**|**Bangladesh (Traditional Law)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Governing Statute**|_Hindu Marriage Act, 1955_|None (Custom / Shastras apply).|
|**Is Divorce Allowed?**|**Yes** (Statutory Right).|**No** (Except by Custom).|
|**Mutual Consent**|**Yes** (Sec 13B).|**No.**|
|**Nature of Remedy**|Dissolution of Marriage (Ends the tie).|Separate Residence (Tie remains).|
|**Right to Remarry**|Yes, after divorce decree.|No, as the first marriage subsists.|
|**Polygamy**|Abolished (Ground for divorce/jail).|**Allowed** (Ground for separate residence only).|

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "1955 Act" Error:** The most common mistake students make is citing the _Hindu Marriage Act, 1955_ in a Bangladesh law exam. **Never do this** unless you are specifically asked to compare. That law stops at the Indian border.

> **"Judicial Separation" vs. "Separate Residence":**
> 
> - In **India**, "Judicial Separation" is a specific decree under the 1955 Act.
>     
> - In **Bangladesh**, we use the term "Separate Residence" under the 1946 Act. While the effect is similar (living apart), the legal terminology differs.
>     

> **Social Impact:** In your essay conclusion, you can argue that the lack of divorce law in Bangladesh often forces Hindu women to endure abusive marriages or forces Hindu men to convert to Islam solely to get a divorce, which creates complex social problems.
