## 5. Divorce ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This topic highlights the starkest difference between the "Modern" Hindu Law of India and the "Traditional" Hindu Law of Bangladesh. To understand the plight of Hindu women in Bangladesh regarding marital oppression, you must grasp the legal impossibility of divorce here.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A highly probable exam topic. Questions often ask to "Compare the law of Divorce in Bangladesh and India" or "Discuss the grounds on which a Hindu wife in Bangladesh can dissolve her marriage." (The trick answer being: generally, she can't).

---

### Introduction

In the ancient Shastric Hindu Law, marriage is a **Sacrament** (_Samskara_) that creates an indissoluble bond between husband and wife. It is a union of souls that supposedly persists even after death. Consequently, the concept of **Divorce** (_Moksha_ or release from marriage) was absolutely unknown to the general Hindu law. Once the _Saptapadi_ (seven steps) is taken, the knot is tied forever.

While India completely overhauled this concept in 1955 by introducing statutory divorce, **Bangladesh** continues to follow the traditional Shastric law. Therefore, strictly speaking, **there is no recognized law of divorce** for the vast majority of Hindus in Bangladesh, creating a significant legal and social deadlock.

---

### 5.1 Divorce in Bangladesh

The law of divorce in Bangladesh is characterized by its absence.

#### A. The General Rule: Indissolubility

- **No Statutory Provision:** Unlike Muslims (who have the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act) or Christians (Divorce Act), there is **no statute** in Bangladesh that allows a Hindu couple to file for divorce in the family court.
    
- **Indissoluble Bond:** A Hindu marriage cannot be dissolved by the act of parties (mutual consent) or by the decree of a court. Even if the husband is cruel, adulterous, or insane, the marriage legally subsists.
    
- **Effect:** A Hindu wife who is abused effectively has no "exit door" from the marriage. She can live separately, but she remains legally married (meaning she cannot remarry).
    

#### B. The Exception: Customary Divorce

While the general Shastric law forbids divorce, it allows **Custom** to override the text.

- **Lower Castes:** Divorce is recognized as valid **only** among certain lower castes (e.g., Sudras, tribal communities) if it is proved to be an ancient, continuous, and established custom in their community.
    
- **Method:** The mode of divorce depends on the specific custom (e.g., tearing a leaf, breaking a straw, or mutual consent before the village _Panchayat_).
    
- **High Castes:** For the "higher castes" (Brahmins, Kayasthas, etc.), customary divorce is generally **not recognized**. They are bound by the strict orthodox rule of indissolubility.
    

#### C. The Confusion with "Special Marriage Act, 1872"

- **Clarification:** The _Special Marriage Act, 1872_ allows for civil marriage and divorce. However, this Act applies only if the couple specifically chose to marry **under this Act** (civil registry marriage) and renounced their religion (historically) or declared they don't profess the standard faith.
    
- **Reality:** 99% of Hindu marriages in Bangladesh are religious marriages (Sacramental). Therefore, the divorce provisions of the 1872 Act **do not apply** to them.
    

#### D. Limited Remedy: Separate Residence

Since a wife cannot get a divorce, her only legal relief is to claim **Separate Residence and Maintenance** under the _Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946_.

- **Grounds:** She can live separately and claim money if the husband:
    
    1. Suffers from a loathsome disease.
        
    2. Is guilty of cruelty.
        
    3. Abandons her without consent.
        
    4. Marries a second wife (Polygamy).
        
    5. Converts to another religion.
        
    6. Keeps a concubine.
        
- **Crucial Note:** This provides separation, **not dissolution**. The marriage bond remains intact.

### 5.2 Comparison with India

The divergence between the two legal systems is most visible here.

|**Feature**|**Bangladesh (Traditional Law)**|**India (Modern Statutory Law)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Governing Law**|Uncodified Shastric Law / Custom.|_The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955_.|
|**Availability of Divorce**|**Generally No.** (Except by custom in lower castes).|**Yes.** Available to all Hindus.|
|**Nature of Marriage**|Strictly a **Sacrament** (Indissoluble).|A Sacrament, but with **Contractual** features (Dissoluble).|
|**Grounds for Divorce**|None (Statutorily).|Adultery, Cruelty, Desertion, Conversion, Insanity, Leprosy, Renunciation, Presumed Death.|
|**Mutual Consent**|Not recognized.|**Divorce by Mutual Consent** is allowed (Sec 13B).|
|**Remarriage**|Impossible (since divorce is impossible).|Possible after the divorce decree.|
|**Polygamy as Ground**|Wife can claim _separate maintenance_ only.|Wife can claim _divorce_.|

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Conversion" Consequence:** A common exam scenario: _"A Hindu husband converts to Islam. Is the marriage automatically dissolved?"_
> 
> - **In India:** No, but it is a ground for the wife to sue for divorce.
>     
> - **In Bangladesh:** **No.** Conversion does _not_ automatically dissolve a Hindu marriage. The wife remains his wife (though she can claim separate maintenance). However, under pure Hindu law, he may become an "outcaste," but the marriage bond persists.
>     

> **Law Commission Recommendation:** When writing an essay, mention that the **Law Commission of Bangladesh** has recommended introducing statutory divorce for Hindus to protect women's rights, but this has faced opposition from orthodox groups like the _Hindu Bibaho Registry O Hindu Ain Bastobayon Parishad_.

> **Key Distinction:**
> 
> - **Judicial Separation:** A court order allowing the couple to live apart (Possible in Bangladesh via maintenance suits).
>     
> - **Divorce:** Breaking the marriage tie (Impossible in Bangladesh).
