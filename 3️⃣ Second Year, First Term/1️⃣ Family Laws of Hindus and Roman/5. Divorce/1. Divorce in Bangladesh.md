## 5.1 Divorce in Bangladesh ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most defining feature of Hindu Law in Bangladesh. It represents the "frozen" state of the law compared to India. You must understand that for a Hindu woman in Bangladesh, marriage is legally a life sentence, regardless of abuse, unless she falls under specific exceptions.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A critical topic. Questions like "Is divorce permitted in Hindu Law in Bangladesh?" or "Discuss the remedies available to a Hindu wife facing cruelty" are standard.
    

---

### Introduction

In the legal landscape of Bangladesh, the concept of **Divorce** (_Vivaha-Viccheda_) for Hindus is largely non-existent. Since Bangladesh follows the traditional, uncodified Shastric law (primarily Dayabhaga), marriage is viewed strictly as a **Sacrament** (_Samskara_). It is a holy union that creates an indissoluble bond between husband and wife, designed to last not just for this life, but for eternity.

Consequently, unlike in Muslim Law (where marriage is a contract dissolvable by Talaq or court decree) or Western law, **there is no general statutory provision for divorce for Hindus in Bangladesh**.

### 1. The General Rule: Doctrine of Indissolubility

The orthodox Hindu view, which is the current law in Bangladesh, maintains that a valid marriage cannot be dissolved.

- **No Dissolution by Act of Parties:** A husband and wife cannot dissolve their marriage by mutual consent or agreement. Even if they sign a "divorce deed" on a stamp paper, it has **no legal value** in the eyes of the court.
    
- **No Dissolution by Court Decree:** There is no law authorizing a Family Court in Bangladesh to grant a decree of divorce to a Hindu couple, regardless of the grounds (adultery, cruelty, desertion).
    
- **Consequence:** Even if a husband abandons his wife and lives with another woman for 20 years, the first wife remains his legal wife. She cannot remarry because her first marriage legally subsists.
    

### 2. The Exception: Customary Divorce

While the _Shastras_ (holy texts) forbid divorce, Hindu Law accepts that **"Custom overrides the Text."**

- **Applicability:** Divorce is recognized as valid **only** if it is a long-standing, established custom within a specific caste or community.
    
- **Target Groups:** This is typically found among the **lower castes** (Sudras) or specific **tribal communities** (e.g., Santhals, Oraons). High-caste Hindus (Brahmins, Kayasthas) generally do not have this custom.
    
- **Modes of Customary Divorce:**
    
    - **Mutual Consent:** In some communities, separation by mutual agreement is recognized.
        
    - **Panchayat:** Divorce granted by the village council or caste tribunal.
        
    - **Symbolic Acts:** Breaking a straw, tearing a leaf, or cutting a thread in the presence of witnesses.
        
- **Legal Burden:** If a person claims they are divorced by custom, they bear the heavy burden of proving to the court that such a custom exists and is ancient, reasonable, and continuous.
    

### 3. Statutory Remedy: Right to Separate Residence

Since strict divorce is unavailable, the legislature provided a "halfway house" remedy. The **Hindu Married Women's Right to Separate Residence and Maintenance Act, 1946** allows a wife to live separately from her husband without losing her right to maintenance.

- **Nature:** This is **Judicial Separation**, not Divorce. The marriage bond remains intact, but the wife is not forced to live with the husband.
    
- **Grounds for Separate Residence (Section 2):** A Hindu wife can claim separate residence and maintenance if her husband:
    
    1. Suffers from a loathsome disease (e.g., Leprosy) not contracted from her.
        
    2. Is guilty of such cruelty that renders it unsafe for her to live with him.
        
    3. Is guilty of desertion (abandoning her without consent).
        
    4. **Marries again** (Polygamy).
        
    5. Ceases to be a Hindu (Conversion).
        
    6. Keeps a concubine in the house or habitually resides with one.
        
    7. For any other justifiable cause.
        

### 4. Divorce under Special Marriage Act, 1872

There is often confusion regarding this Act.

- **Civil Marriage:** This Act allows for a civil marriage registration (Court Marriage) which is secular in nature.
    
- **Divorce Availability:** If a Hindu couple chooses to marry **under this specific Act** (signing the civil register instead of just performing religious rites), they represent a "Civil Marriage." The Divorce Act, 1869 applies to them, and they **can** obtain a legal divorce.
    
- **Reality:** The vast majority of Hindu marriages in Bangladesh are religious (Sacramental), not civil. Therefore, this Act's divorce provisions do **not** apply to them.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Conversion" Trick:** A common exam problem: _"A Hindu husband converts to Islam. Is the marriage dissolved?"_
> 
> - **Answer:** **No.** Under traditional Hindu law in Bangladesh, conversion ("Apostasy") does _not_ automatically dissolve the marriage. The wife is still his wife.
>     
> - **Remedy:** She can claim _Separate Residence_ under the 1946 Act, but she cannot claim divorce to marry someone else.
>     

> **Polygamy vs. Divorce:** Note that "Husband marrying again" is a ground for the first wife to **leave** and claim money (Separate Residence), but it is **not** a ground to end the marriage.

> **Crucial Case Law:** You can cite the case of _Nellie Sen Gupta vs. The State_ (though old) or recent High Court observations where judges have expressed that the absence of divorce is a violation of fundamental rights, yet ruled that they cannot legislate new law—only Parliament can.
