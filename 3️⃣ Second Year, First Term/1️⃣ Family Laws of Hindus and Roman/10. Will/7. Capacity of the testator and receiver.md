## 10.7 Capacity of the Testator and Receiver ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** A Will is legally void if the person making it lacks the mental or legal capacity to do so. Similarly, understanding who can _receive_ property (e.g., can an Idol or a minor receive?) is fundamental to estate planning.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often arise regarding the validity of a Will made by a minor or a person of unsound mind. The "Dayabhaga father's absolute capacity" is a specific point of interest for Bangladesh.
    

---

### Introduction

For a Will to be valid, the law requires not only that the document be in the correct form but also that the parties involved possess the necessary legal **Capacity** (Competence). This involves two sides: the **Testator** (who makes the Will) and the **Legatee** (who receives the property).

If the testator lacks capacity, the entire Will is _Void ab initio_. If a specific legatee lacks capacity, the bequest to that person fails, though the rest of the Will may stand.

### 1. Capacity of the Testator (_Who can make a Will?_)

Under **Section 59** of the **Succession Act, 1925** (applicable to Hindus), strict rules define who is competent to make a Will.

#### **A. Sound Mind**

- **The Rule:** The testator must be of **sound mind** at the time of making the Will. He must understand the nature of his act and its effect.
    
- **Intoxication/Illness:** A person who is in such a state of mind (due to intoxication, illness, or other causes) that they do not know what they are doing cannot make a valid Will.
    
- **Lucid Intervals:** A generally insane person _can_ make a valid Will during a "lucid interval" (a period when they temporarily regain sanity). Conversely, a generally sane person _cannot_ make a Will while temporarily delirious from fever.
    

#### **B. Age of Majority**

- **The Rule:** The testator must have attained the age of **Majority**.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** Under the _Majority Act, 1875_, this is **18 years** (or 21 if a certified guardian exists).
    
- **Minor's Will:** A Will made by a minor is **Void**. Even if the minor dies after becoming an adult, the Will made during minority remains void unless he ratified or re-executed it after turning 18.
    

#### **C. Freedom from Fraud/Coercion**

- A Will caused by fraud, coercion, or undue importunity (pressure that destroys free agency) is void. The testator must be a free agent.
    

#### **D. The Dayabhaga Father (Specific to Bangladesh)**

- **Absolute Capacity:** Unlike the Mitakshara school (where a father cannot will away ancestral coparcenary property), under the **Dayabhaga School** in Bangladesh, a Hindu father has **absolute capacity** to dispose of **all** his property (ancestral or self-acquired) by Will. He can even disinherit his competent adult sons entirely.
    

### 2. Capacity of the Receiver / Legatee (_Who can take under a Will?_)

The "Legatee" is the beneficiary named in the Will. The rules for receivers are far more liberal than for testators.

#### **A. General Rule**

- Any person capable of holding property can be a legatee.
    
- **Minors and Lunatics:** A minor or a person of unsound mind **can** be a legatee. They cannot _manage_ the property, but they can _own_ it (usually managed by a guardian until majority).
    

#### **B. Juristic Persons (Idol/Deity)**

- A bequest to a Hindu **Idol** (_Debuttar_) or a religious institution is valid. The Idol is considered a "juristic person" capable of owning property.
    

#### **C. The Outsider/Stranger**

- A Hindu can leave his property to anyone—a friend, a servant, or a stranger—excluding his own relatives, provided the Will is validly executed.
    

#### **D. The Attesting Witness (Disqualification)**

- **Section 67 Rule:** This is a crucial incapacity.
    
    - A Will is valid if attested by a beneficiary, **BUT** the bequest to that witness (or their spouse) is **Void**.
        
    - _Logic:_ A person cannot be a witness to their own benefit to prevent fraud.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Lucid Interval:** This is a high-value concept. If a problem says _"X, a lunatic, wrote a Will on a day he was behaving normally,"_ the Will is **Valid**. The burden of proof is on the person propounding the Will to prove the "Lucid Interval."

> **Disinheriting Sons:** In Bangladesh (Dayabhaga), always remember the father's power is absolute. If a problem says _"Father wills 100% of ancestral land to a charity, leaving sons penniless,"_ the Will is **Valid**. (Though the sons might claim the father was "unduly influenced" if the charity guru controlled him).
