## 10.4 Essential Requirements (Formalities) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** A Will is a highly technical document. Unlike a contract where intent often matters more than form, a Will is strictly "formalistic." If you fail to get two witnesses or if the testator signs in the wrong place, the entire Will is **Void**. Understanding these strict formalities is the difference between a valid inheritance and a legal disaster.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is the single most tested area regarding Wills. Problem questions invariably involve a Will that was signed without witnesses, or where witnesses signed at different times. You must know **Section 63** of the Succession Act inside out.
    

---

### Introduction

For a Will to be valid in Bangladesh, it is not enough for the testator to simply write down his wishes. He must strictly comply with the formalities prescribed by the Legislature. For unprivileged Wills (ordinary civilian Wills), these requirements are governed by **Section 63 of the Succession Act, 1925**. Courts are extremely strict in enforcing these rules to prevent fraud, as the main witness (the testator) is dead when the document takes effect.

The essential requirements can be broken down into three strict steps: **Writing**, **Signature**, and **Attestation**.

### 1. Writing

- **Mandatory:** The Will must be in **writing**. An oral Will by a civilian Hindu is **Void ab initio**.
    
- **Material:** It can be written on any material (paper, cloth, wall) using any instrument (ink, pencil, blood).
    
- **Language:** It can be in any language intelligible to the testator.
    
- **Format:** No specific legal form is necessary. It does not need to be on a judicial stamp paper. A Will written on a plain sheet of paper is perfectly valid.
    

### 2. Signature of the Testator

- **The Act:** The testator must **sign** or affix his **mark** (thumb impression) to the Will.
    
- **Proxy Signature:** If the testator is too weak to sign, some other person may sign it in his presence and by his direction.
    
- **Placement:** The signature must be placed in such a way that it appears intended to give effect to the writing as a Will. Usually, this means at the end or bottom of the document. A signature at the top or in the margin might be questionable but is sometimes accepted if the intention is clear.
    

### 3. Attestation (The Most Critical Step)

Attestation means witnessing the execution of the Will. This is where most Wills fail in court. Under **Section 63(c)**, the following rules apply:

- **Number of Witnesses:** The Will must be attested by **two or more witnesses**. One witness is not enough.
    
- **The Process of Witnessing:**
    
    - Each witness must have **seen the testator sign** (or affix his mark), OR
        
    - Each witness must have received from the testator a **personal acknowledgment** of his signature (e.g., The testator says to the witness, "I have already signed this, please witness it").
        
- **Signing by Witnesses:** Each witness must sign the Will in the **presence of the testator**.
    
    - _Reason:_ This ensures the testator knows who is witnessing his Will and prevents fraud.
        
- **Simultaneous Presence Not Required:** Unlike English Law, in Bangladesh (and India), it is **NOT necessary** that both witnesses be present at the same time. One witness can sign on Monday, and the other on Tuesday, provided each witnessed the testator's signature/acknowledgment separately.
    

### 4. Mental Capacity (_Animus Testandi_)

While not a "formality" of the document, it is a prerequisite for validity.

- **Sound Mind:** The testator must be of sound mind at the precise moment of execution.
    
- **Intention:** He must have the **Animus Testandi** (Intention to make a Will). If he thought he was signing a deed of gift or a power of attorney, the Will is void.
    

### 5. Registration (Optional)

- **Status:** Registration of a Will is **Optional** under the _Registration Act, 1908_.
    
- **Validity:** A non-registered Will is perfectly valid.
    
- **Benefit:** However, a registered Will has higher evidentiary value because the Registrar (a public officer) verifies the testator's sanity and signature, making it harder to challenge in probate court.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Beneficiary Witness" Trap:** A very common objective question: _"A testator leaves his house to his son. The son signs as one of the two witnesses. Is the Will valid?"_
> 
> - **Answer:**
>     
>     - The **Will is Valid**.
>         
>     - The **Bequest to the Son is VOID** (Section 67, Succession Act).
>         
> - _Logic:_ A person cannot witness a benefit to themselves. To save the Will, the law strikes out the gift to the witness but keeps the rest of the Will alive.
>     

> **Comparison with Gift:**
> 
> - **Gift:** Needs 2 witnesses + Registration (Mandatory).
>     
> - **Will:** Needs 2 witnesses + Registration (Optional).
>     

> **English vs. Bangladeshi Law:** In English Law, witnesses _must_ be present together. In Bangladesh, they can attest _separately_. Mentioning this distinction impresses examiners.
