## 10. Will (_Ichha-patra_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** A "Will" is the legal tool that allows a person to bypass the general rules of inheritance. Understanding it is crucial because a valid Will overrides the natural succession. You must understand how a Hindu father can legally disinherit his sons using this instrument.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often focus on "Capacity to make a Will," "Formalities (Attestation)," and the specific rules regarding "Bequest to an Unborn Person." The distinction regarding revocation by marriage (which does _not_ happen in Hindu Law) is a favorite objective question.
    

---

### Introduction

A **Will** (Testament) is the legal declaration of the intention of a testator with respect to his property, which he desires to be carried into effect after his death.

Interestingly, the concept of a Will was **unknown to ancient Hindu Law**. The joint family system and the religious duty of the son to inherit made the idea of "willing away" property foreign to the Smritis. However, contact with European culture introduced this concept. It was firmly established by judicial decisions (notably the _Tagore v. Tagore_ case) and is now governed by the **Succession Act, 1925**, which applies to Hindus in Bangladesh.

---

### 10.1 Definition and Characteristics

**Definition:** "Will" means the legal declaration of the intention of a testator with respect to his property which he desires to be carried into effect after his death. (Section 2(h), Succession Act, 1925).

**Key Characteristics:**

1. **Legal Declaration:** It must be in conformity with the law (signed and attested).
    
2. **Disposition of Property:** It must relate to the disposal of property.
    
3. **Post-Mortem Effect:** It takes effect **only after the death** of the testator. During his lifetime, it is a mere piece of paper.
    
4. **Revocability:** It is inherently **revocable**. The testator can change it 100 times before he dies. The last one prevails.
    

---

### 10.2 & 10.3 Classification and Forms

While specific forms like "Holograph Wills" (handwritten) exist, the law generally classifies them based on privilege.

1. **Privileged Wills:**
    
    - Oral or informal wills permitted for **soldiers/airmen** employed in an expedition or warfare.
        
    - _Relevance:_ Rare in general civil practice.
        
2. **Unprivileged Wills (Ordinary Wills):**
    
    - The standard form for all ordinary Hindus.
        
    - **Form:** It **Must be in Writing**. An oral will by a civilian Hindu is **Void**.
        

---

### 10.4 Essential Requirements (Formalities)

For a Will to be valid in Bangladesh, it must strictly comply with **Section 63 of the Succession Act, 1925**.

1. **Writing:** The will must be in writing.
    
2. **Signature:** The testator (maker) must sign or affix his mark (thumbprint) to the will.
    
3. **Attestation (Crucial):**
    
    - The Will must be attested by **two or more witnesses**.
        
    - Each witness must have seen the testator sign.
        
    - **Beneficiary cannot be a witness:** If a person who is receiving property under the Will signs as a witness, the Will is valid, but the **bequest to that witness is VOID**.
        

---

### 10.7 Capacity of Testator and Receiver

#### A. The Testator (Who can make a Will?)

- **Sound Mind:** He must be capable of understanding the nature of his act. A lunatic generally cannot make a will (unless during a lucid interval).
    
- **Majority:** He must be a **Major** (18+ years). A minor cannot make a valid will, even of his self-acquired property.
    
- **Hindu Father (Dayabhaga):** In Bangladesh, a father has **absolute power** to dispose of all his property (ancestral or self-acquired) by Will, even to the exclusion of his son.
    

#### B. The Receiver (Legatee)

- Any person capable of holding property can be a legatee.
    
- **Minor/Lunatic:** Can receive property (a guardian accepts it).
    
- **Unborn Person:** Subject to specific restrictions (see below).
    

---

### 10.8 Scope of Property

What property can be willed away?

- **Dayabhaga School (Bangladesh):** A Hindu governed by Dayabhaga law can dispose of **all his property** (ancestral, joint, or self-acquired) by Will. Since the son has no right by birth, the father is the absolute owner and can disinherit his heirs.
    
- **Widow:** A widow holding a "Widow's Estate" **cannot** dispose of that property by Will. It must revert to her husband's heirs. She can only will away her _Stridhana_.
    

---

### 10.9 Will to Unborn Person

Can you leave property to a child who is not yet conceived?

- **The Rule (_Tagore v. Tagore_):** Originally, a bequest to a person not in existence at the testator's death was void.
    
- **Statutory Modification (Sec 113, Succession Act):** A bequest to an unborn person is **Valid**, provided:
    
    1. **Prior Interest:** The property is given to a living person first (Life Estate).
        
    2. **Whole Remainder:** The **absolute interest** (not just life interest) is transferred to the unborn person when the prior interest ends.
        
- _Perpetuity:_ The vesting cannot be delayed beyond the life of living persons + minority (18 years) of the unborn.
    

---

### 10.10 Revocation

A Will can be revoked at any time when the testator is competent.

**Modes of Revocation:**

1. **By Execution of a New Will:** The latest Will automatically revokes earlier ones ("This is my last will and testament").
    
2. **By Destruction:** Burning, tearing, or destroying the Will with the _intention_ of revoking it (_Animus Revocandi_).
    
3. **Marriage (The Big Exception):**
    
    - **General Law:** Under the Succession Act (Section 69), marriage usually revokes a Will (e.g., for Christians).
        
    - **Hindu Law Exception:** **Marriage does NOT revoke a Will of a Hindu.**
        
    - _Scenario:_ If a Hindu man makes a Will giving everything to his brother, then marries and dies, the **Brother gets everything**, and the Widow gets nothing (except maintenance). The marriage did not cancel the old Will.
        

---

### 10.5 Effectiveness (Probate)

- **Probate:** A "Probate" is a copy of the Will certified under the seal of a Court of competent jurisdiction.
    
- **Necessity:** Under Section 213 of the Succession Act, no right as an executor or legatee can be established in any Court unless a Court has granted **Probate** of the Will.
    
- **Effect:** The probate proves the genuineness of the Will. It is conclusive evidence of the testator's capacity and the Will's validity.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Disinheritance" Power:** In Bangladesh (Dayabhaga), a father can legally write a Will saying: _"I give all my property to the Temple, and my worthless son gets nothing."_
> 
> - **Validity:** This is **Valid**. The son cannot challenge it on grounds of "right by birth."
>     
> - _Limitation:_ He cannot defeat the wife's right to _maintenance_, but he can defeat her right to _inheritance_.
>     

> **Revocation by Marriage Trap:** This is the #1 trick question.
> 
> - _Question:_ "H makes a will. H marries W. H dies. Is the will void?"
>     
> - _Answer:_ **No.** For Hindus, marriage does not revoke an existing will.
>     

> **Witness Rule:** Ensure you mention that a **Beneficiary cannot be a witness**. If the son signs as a witness to the Will, he loses his inheritance under that Will.
