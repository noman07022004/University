## 10.2 Classification of Wills ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Knowing the types of Wills is essential to understand the varying degrees of formality required. For instance, knowing that a soldier at war can make an oral Will while a civilian cannot is a key legal distinction.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While less frequent than questions on "Essentials," short notes on "Privileged Will" or "Holograph Will" appear occasionally.
    

---

### Introduction

The **Succession Act, 1925** classifies Wills primarily based on the status of the testator and the circumstances under which the Will is made. The law recognizes that sticking to strict formalities (like finding two witnesses) might be impossible in certain dangerous situations (like a battlefield). Therefore, Wills are broadly classified into two categories:

1. **Privileged Wills**
    
2. **Unprivileged Wills**
    

Apart from this statutory classification, there are other descriptive types of Wills recognized by courts, such as Joint Wills, Mutual Wills, and Holograph Wills.

---

### 1. Statutory Classification

#### **A. Privileged Wills (Section 65 & 66)**

This is a special class of Wills designed for people serving in the armed forces who face imminent danger and may not have access to legal advice or stationery.

- **Who can make it?**
    
    1. Any soldier (or airman) being employed in an expedition or engaged in actual warfare.
        
    2. Any mariner (sailor) being at sea.
        
- **Relaxed Formalities:**
    
    - It may be in **writing** or even **oral** (by word of mouth).
        
    - It does **not** need to be attested by witnesses.
        
    - If written by the testator himself, it doesn't even need a signature.
        
    - _Note:_ This privilege is **rare** in ordinary civil practice but exists to protect those defending the country.
        

#### **B. Unprivileged Wills (Ordinary Wills)**

This category covers the vast majority of Wills. Every Will that is not a Privileged Will is an Unprivileged Will.

- **Who makes it?** Ordinary citizens (Civilians) who are not currently fighting a war or at sea.
    
- **Formalities:** Strict compliance with **Section 63** of the Succession Act is mandatory.
    
    - Must be in **Writing**.
        
    - Must be **Signed** by the testator.
        
    - Must be **Attested** by at least two witnesses.
        
- **Validity:** An oral Unprivileged Will is **Void**.
    

---

### 2. General/Academic Classification

Beyond the statute, legal practice recognizes several specific forms of Wills based on their content and execution.

#### **A. Holograph Will**

- **Definition:** A Will that is written entirely in the **handwriting** of the testator himself.
    
- **Legal Status:** It is the safest form of Will because it is difficult to forge. In Unprivileged Wills, it still requires attestation, but courts presume it to be genuine more easily than a typed document.
    

#### **B. Conditional or Contingent Will**

- **Definition:** A Will expressed to take effect only in the event of the happening of a certain contingency.
    
- **Example:** "This Will applies only if I die during my trip to London."
    
- **Effect:** If the testator returns safely from London, the Will becomes invalid and does not operate upon his eventual death years later.
    

#### **C. Joint Will**

- **Definition:** A single testamentary instrument made by **two or more persons** (usually husband and wife) agreeing to dispose of their property.
    
- **Effect:** It operates as the separate Will of each testator. Upon the death of the first person, it is probated as their Will.
    

#### **D. Mutual (Reciprocal) Will**

- **Definition:** Two testators make separate Wills conferring reciprocal benefits on each other.
    
- **Example:** Husband leaves everything to Wife; Wife leaves everything to Husband.
    
- **Revocability:** Generally revocable during their lifetime, but may become irrevocable after the death of one party if there was an agreement not to revoke.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Oral Will" Limit:** Students often think Hindus can make oral Wills because of ancient custom.
> 
> - **Correction:** Under the Succession Act, an ordinary Hindu **CANNOT** make an oral Will. Only a soldier in war can.
>     

> **Holograph Will:** This is a good term to drop in an essay. It refers to a handwritten Will.
> 
> - _Tip:_ Even if a Will is Holograph (Handwritten), for an ordinary person, it **STILL needs 2 witnesses**. Handwriting does not excuse the need for attestation.
>     

> **Joint Will vs. Joint Tenancy:** A Joint Will does not prevent the survivor from revoking their part of the Will later, unless there is a specific contract binding them (Mutual Will).
