## 10.8 Scope of Property ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most crucial distinction between the Dayabhaga and Mitakshara schools regarding Wills. You must understand _why_ a Bangladeshi Hindu father can disinherit his son from ancestral property (Dayabhaga rule) while a traditional Mitakshara father could not.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Problem questions frequently feature a scenario where a father leaves his entire ancestral estate to a stranger. You must determine if this is valid. (Hint: In Bangladesh, it is).
    

---

### Introduction

The "Scope of Property" refers to the extent of a testator's power to dispose of his property by Will. Can a Hindu will away _everything_ he possesses, or are there restrictions based on the nature of the property (Ancestral vs. Self-Acquired)?

The answer depends entirely on the **School of Law** governing the testator. Since Bangladesh is primarily governed by the **Dayabhaga School**, the scope here is significantly wider than under the traditional Mitakshara law.

### 1. Under Dayabhaga Law (Bangladesh Rule)

The Dayabhaga school takes a modern, individualistic approach to property ownership.

- **The Rule:** A Hindu governed by Dayabhaga law has the power to dispose of **all his property** by Will.
    
- **Types of Property Included:**
    
    1. **Self-Acquired Property:** Property earned by his own exertion.
        
    2. **Ancestral Property:** Property inherited from father, grandfather, etc.
        
    3. **Joint Family Property:** His share in the family estate.
        
- **Reasoning:** Under Dayabhaga law, the doctrine of **Right by Birth** (_Janmasvatvavada_) does not exist. The father is the absolute owner of all property (including ancestral) during his lifetime. The sons have no legal claim until the father dies. Therefore, since the father is the absolute owner, he has the absolute right to sell it or will it away to anyone he pleases, even effectively disinheriting his sons.
    

### 2. Under Mitakshara Law (Exception in Bangladesh)

For communities in Bangladesh governed by the Mitakshara school (e.g., migrant families), the traditional restrictions still apply (as the modern Indian reforms of 1956 do not apply here).

- **Self-Acquired Property:** A Mitakshara Hindu **can** dispose of his separate/self-acquired property by Will.
    
- **Coparcenary Interest (Ancestral):** Under strict uncodified Mitakshara law, a coparcener **CANNOT** dispose of his undivided interest in the joint family property by Will.
    
    - _Reason:_ Upon his death, his share immediately merges with the other coparceners by the doctrine of **Survivorship**. There is nothing left to "Will" away.
        
    - _Note:_ This is a vital difference from Dayabhaga.
        

### 3. Property of a Hindu Female

The testamentary power of a woman depends on the nature of the property she holds.

#### **A. Stridhana (Absolute Property)**

- **Scope:** Property that a woman owns absolutely (e.g., gifts from parents, husband, or her own earnings).
    
- **Power:** She has absolute power to dispose of her **Stridhana** by Will. She can leave it to her daughter, son, or a stranger.
    

#### **B. Widow's Estate (Limited Estate)**

- **Scope:** Property inherited by a widow (or other limited female heir) from her husband (or male relations) before the statutory reforms (or under current BD law).
    
- **Power:** She **CANNOT** dispose of a Widow's Estate by Will.
    
    - _Reason:_ She is not the full owner; she is a "Limited Owner" for life. Upon her death, the property must go to the **Reversioners** (the next heirs of her husband). She cannot divert this line of succession by making a Will.
        
    - _Exception:_ She might be able to will away the _savings_ or _income_ accumulated from the estate, but not the land (corpus) itself.
        

### 4. Property Incapable of Bequest

Certain rights cannot be transferred by Will because they are personal or restricted by law.

- **Right to Maintenance:** A person cannot transfer their right to future maintenance by Will.
    
- **Spes Successionis:** The "chance" of inheriting property cannot be willed away.
    
- **Office of Dignity:** Religious offices (like a Shebaitship) generally follow the line of succession and cannot always be willed away unless custom allows.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Disinheritance" Power:** In Dayabhaga law (Bangladesh), a father can legally write: _"I give all my ancestral land to the Temple, and my son gets nothing."_ This Will is **VALID**. The son cannot challenge it based on "Right by Birth." This illustrates the absolute power of the Dayabhaga father.

> **Widow's Will:** If an exam problem says: _"A widow makes a Will giving her husband's house to her brother,"_ the bequest is **VOID** regarding the house (Widow's Estate). The house goes to her husband's heirs (e.g., husband's brother) after her death, not her own brother.
