## 10.3 Forms of Will ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** While the standard Will is a simple document, knowing the specific forms (like Mutual or Concurrent Wills) is crucial for handling complex estate planning, such as for couples or people with international property.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Short notes on "Holograph Will" or distinguishing between "Joint and Mutual Wills" are common academic questions.
    

---

### Introduction

While the law prescribes that an unprivileged Will must be in writing, it does not prescribe any specific rigid "template" or legal form. A Will does not need to be written on stamp paper (it can be on plain paper), nor does it require technical legal language. The only requirement is that the **intention** of the testator to dispose of his property after death must be clear.

However, legal practice and academia recognize several specific "Forms" of Wills based on their structure, authorship, and operation.

### 1. Form of Expression

#### **A. Written Will (The Rule)**

- **Requirement:** For ordinary Hindus in Bangladesh, a Will must be in writing.
    
- **Language:** It can be written in any language (Bengali, English, Hindi, etc.) that the testator understands.
    
- **Technical Terms:** No technical legal terms (like "bequeath" or "devise") are necessary. Simple language like "I give my house to my son" is legally sufficient.
    
- **Stamp Paper:** A Will does **not** need to be written on judicial stamp paper. It can be written on a simple piece of paper, provided it is signed and attested.
    

#### **B. Oral Will (Nuncupative Will)**

- **Validity:** As discussed, this is **Void** for civilians. It is valid **only** for soldiers in warfare or mariners at sea (Privileged Wills).
    

---

### 2. Forms Based on Authorship

#### **A. Holograph Will**

- **Definition:** A Will that is entirely written, signed, and dated in the **handwriting of the testator** himself.
    
- **Evidentiary Value:** Courts place great value on holograph wills because there is a strong presumption against forgery. Since the whole document is in the testator's own hand, it strongly proves his knowledge and approval of the contents.
    
- **Legal Requirement:** Even a holograph will **MUST be attested** by two witnesses under the Succession Act. Being handwritten does not exempt it from the formalities of Section 63.
    

#### **B. Joint Will**

- **Definition:** A single testamentary instrument made by **two or more persons** (usually husband and wife) conveying their property to a beneficiary.
    
- **Nature:** It is technically two separate Wills written on one piece of paper for convenience.
    
- **Revocability:** It is revocable at any time by either of the testators during their joint lives, or after the death of one, by the survivor.
    
- **Probate:** Upon the death of the first person, the Will is admitted to probate as their Will. Upon the death of the second, it is admitted again as their Will.
    

#### **C. Mutual (Reciprocal) Will**

- **Definition:** Two testators make separate Wills (or a joint will) conferring **reciprocal benefits** on each other.
    
    - _Example:_ Husband says "I leave everything to Wife." Wife says "I leave everything to Husband."
        
- **The Binding Nature:** Usually, mutual wills are the result of an agreement/contract.
    
- **Revocability:** If the survivors accepts the benefit after the first death (e.g., Wife inherits Husband's property), she generally **cannot revoke** her mutual will later to cut out the agreed secondary beneficiaries. Equity binds her conscience.
    

---

### 3. Forms Based on Operation

#### **A. Conditional or Contingent Will**

- **Definition:** A Will that is expressed to take effect **only** in the event of the happening of a certain contingency.
    
- **Example:** "This Will is to operate only if I die during my surgery next week."
    
- **Effect:** If the contingency fails (e.g., he survives the surgery), the Will becomes void automatically. If he dies years later of natural causes, this specific Will cannot be used.
    

#### **B. Concurrent Wills**

- **Definition:** A testator can make **two or more independent Wills** dealing with different properties.
    
- **Usage:** Usually used when a person has property in different countries.
    
    - _Example:_ One Will for property in Bangladesh (governed by BD law) and another independent Will for property in London (governed by English law).
        
- **Caution:** They must be drafted carefully so that one does not revoke the other.
    

#### **C. Duplicate Will**

- **Definition:** A Will executed in duplicate (two copies signed and attested as originals). One is kept by the testator, the other by the executor/bank.
    
- **Revocation Rule:** If the testator destroys the copy in his possession with the intent to revoke, the other copy is also deemed revoked.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Joint vs. Mutual:** This is the key distinction for exams.
> 
> - **Joint Will:** Just a format (2 people, 1 paper). Revocable.
>     
> - **Mutual Will:** A contract/arrangement (Reciprocal benefits). Often becomes Irrevocable after one party dies and the other accepts the benefit.
>     

> **Drafting Tip:** If a problem says _"A wrote his Will on a page of his diary and signed it."_
> 
> - **Analysis:** This is a valid **Holograph Will** (if attested by 2 witnesses). The informality of the paper (diary) does not invalidate it.
>     

> **Stamp Duty:** Unlike Gifts (which require stamp duty and registration fees), a Will requires **NO Stamp Duty** during the testator's life. It is the cheapest way to transfer property.
