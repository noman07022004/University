## 10.5 Effectiveness (Probate) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** You can write a perfect Will, but it is useless in the real world unless it is "Probated." Understanding the difference between a "Valid Will" (Substantive) and an "Effective Will" (Procedural) is the key to legal practice.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A very practical exam topic. Questions often ask: _"Is it mandatory to obtain Probate for a Hindu Will in Bangladesh?"_ or _"Distinguish between Probate and Letters of Administration."_
    

---

### Introduction

A Will is described as "Ambulatory," meaning it walks or wanders during the testator's life and only stops (becomes fixed/effective) upon his death. Therefore, the first condition for the effectiveness of a Will is the **Death of the Testator**. Until the testator dies, the beneficiaries (legatees) have no rights; they only have a hope of succession (_Spes Successionis_).

However, even after death, a Will does not automatically transfer the legal title in the eyes of the public/state. To make the Will legally effective and enforceable in a court of law, it must go through a judicial process called **Probate**.

---

### 1. Probate: The Seal of Validity

- **Definition:** "Probate" means the copy of a Will certified under the seal of a Court of competent jurisdiction with a grant of administration to the estate of the testator (Section 2(f), Succession Act, 1925).
    
    - Simply put, it is a certificate from the District Judge saying: _"We have checked this Will; it is genuine, the testator was sane, and the Executor is authorized to manage the property."_
        
- **Necessity (Section 213):**
    
    - **The Rule:** No right as an **Executor** or **Legatee** (beneficiary) can be established in any Court of Justice unless a Court has granted Probate of the Will.
        
    - **Implication:** If a Hindu father leaves a Will giving his house to his son, the son generally cannot sue a tenant or sell the house using just the Will paper. He must first get Probate to prove his title.
        
- **Effect of Probate (Section 227):**
    
    - Probate renders valid all intermediate acts of the executor.
        
    - It acts as **Conclusive Proof** of the Will's validity. Once Probate is granted, no one can challenge the mental capacity or signature of the testator in any other legal proceeding.
        

### 2. Letters of Administration (Section 232)

What happens if there is a valid Will, but no one is named to carry it out?

- **Scenario:** A Will exists, but:
    
    - The testator failed to name an Executor.
        
    - The named Executor is dead, refuses to act, or is incapable.
        
- **The Remedy:** The Court grants **Letters of Administration with the Will annexed**.
    
- **Effect:** The person appointed (Administrator) performs the same duties as an Executor but is appointed by the Court, not the testator.
    

### 3. Executor vs. Administrator

The effectiveness of a Will is managed by these two key figures.

|**Feature**|**Executor**|**Administrator**|
|---|---|---|
|**Appointment**|Named by the **Testator** in the Will.|Appointed by the **Court** (District Judge).|
|**Source of Authority**|Derives authority from the **Will** itself.|Derives authority from the **Court Order**.|
|**Vesting of Property**|Property vests in him from the moment of **Death**.|Property vests only from the date of the **Grant**.|
|**Probate?**|He applies for **Probate**.|He applies for **Letters of Administration**.|

### 4. Limitations on Effectiveness

Even a valid, probated Will cannot violate substantive Hindu Law restrictions.

- **Maintenance:** A Will cannot defeat the right of maintenance of the testator's wife or minor children. The estate remains liable for their support.
    
- **Illegal Bequests:** A bequest to a person for an illegal purpose (e.g., "to promote gambling") or a bequest that violates the rule against perpetuity is void, even if the rest of the Will is effective.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Section 213" Rule:** This is the most cited section in Will problems.
> 
> - _Scenario:_ "Son sues for rent based on Father's Will. Tenant objects that the Will is not probated."
>     
> - _Judgment:_ The suit will be dismissed (or stayed) because **Section 213** bars the court from recognizing a right without Probate.
>     

> **Executor de son tort:** (Executor of his own wrong). A person who is not the appointed executor but intrudes into the estate and acts as one. He makes himself liable for the assets he touches but does not gain the rights of a proper executor.

> **Probate vs. Succession Certificate:**
> 
> - **Probate:** For Wills (Testamentary Succession).
>     
> - **Succession Certificate:** For Debts/Securities where there is **NO Will** (Intestate Succession) or for debts in a Will where probate is not strictly mandatory (rare in BD for immovable property).
