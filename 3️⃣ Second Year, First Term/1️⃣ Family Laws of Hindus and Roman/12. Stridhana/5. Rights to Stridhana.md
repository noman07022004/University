## 12.5 Rights to Stridhana ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the functional core of Stridhana. It answers the practical question: "Can a woman sell her property?" Understanding the "Husband's Veto" over certain types of property during marriage is essential for legal practice in Bangladesh.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often ask: "Discuss the rights of a Hindu woman over her Stridhana property during coverture" or "Can a husband alienate his wife's Stridhana in times of distress?"
    

---

### Introduction

The term "Stridhana" implies absolute ownership. However, in the uncodified Hindu Law applicable in Bangladesh, the woman's power to exercise this ownership is not always absolute. It depends on two key factors:

1. **Her Status:** Is she a Maiden, Married (_Coverture_), or a Widow?
    
2. **The Nature of Property:** Is it _Saudayika_ (from kin) or _Non-Saudayika_ (from strangers/earnings)?
    

While she is always the _owner_, her power of **alienation** (selling/gifting) fluctuates.

---

### 1. Rights During Maidenhood and Widowhood

During these two periods, a Hindu woman is independent of male control regarding her Stridhana.

- **Absolute Power:** Whether the property is _Saudayika_ (gifts from family) or _Non-Saudayika_ (gifts from strangers/own earnings), she has **absolute power** to dispose of it. She can sell, mortgage, gift, or will it away to anyone she pleases.
    
- **No Guardian Control:** Her father (as a maiden) or her sons (as a widow) have no legal right to stop her or demand an account of how she spends her Stridhana.
    

---

### 2. Rights During Coverture (Married Life)

This is where the law becomes complex. The husband's authority imposes restrictions on certain types of property.

#### **A. Over _Saudayika_ Stridhana (Gifts from Kin)**

- **Absolute Power:** She retains **absolute power** of disposal. She can sell it or gift it without her husband's consent.
    
- **Husband's Interference:** The husband cannot control it, use it, or sell it. If he wrongfully takes it, she can sue him to recover it (though traditionally such suits were discouraged, modern courts allow them).
    

#### **B. Over _Non-Saudayika_ Stridhana (Earnings/Gifts from Strangers)**

- **The "Husband's Veto":** Under the Dayabhaga law, while she is the owner, she **CANNOT** alienate (sell/gift) this property without her **husband's consent**.
    
- **Reasoning:** Ancient texts sought to preserve the unity of the family and prevent the wife from becoming financially independent enough to defy the husband.
    
- **After Husband's Death:** If the husband dies, the restriction is lifted. She regains absolute power over this property as a widow.
    

---

### 3. The Husband's Right of "Distress"

While the husband generally has no ownership over his wife's Stridhana, the Mitakshara and Dayabhaga schools grant him a specific emergency power.

- **Right to Take:** The husband may take and use his wife's Stridhana (even _Saudayika_) in times of **extreme distress** (_Apatkale_).
    
- **Valid Grounds for Distress:**
    
    1. Famine or inability to procure food.
        
    2. Acute illness needing expensive treatment.
        
    3. Imprisonment.
        
    4. Performance of indispensable religious duties.
        
- **Legal Obligation:**
    
    - **Moral or Legal?** Initially, it was a moral duty to repay. However, courts now hold that he is **legally bound** to repay the amount with interest when his financial condition improves.
        
    - **Wife's Right:** She cannot object to his taking it during the emergency, but she can claim it back later.
        

---

### 4. Effect of Unchastity

Does a woman lose her Stridhana if she becomes unchaste (adulterous) or is excommunicated?

- **The Rule:** **No.** Unchastity does **not** divest a woman of property she has already inherited or acquired as Stridhana. Once the property vests in her, it remains hers regardless of her moral conduct.
    
- **Exception (Maintenance):** Unchastity _does_ forfeit her right to be maintained by her husband, but it does not confiscate her existing separate property.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Earnings" Problem:** A common exam scenario:
> 
> - _Facts:_ "Mrs. X, a teacher, buys land with her salary. Her husband is a drunkard. She wants to sell the land to pay for her son's college. Husband refuses consent."
>     
> - _Legal Position:_ Under strict uncodified Hindu Law in Bangladesh, she **cannot** sell it without his consent because salary is _Non-Saudayika_.
>     
> - _Advice:_ She might have to argue "Legal Necessity" or appeal to the court's equity jurisdiction, but the strict rule is against her.
>     

> **Comparison with Widow's Estate:**
> 
> - **Stridhana:** Restrictions depend on _Husband's Consent_ (only during his life).
>     
> - **Widow's Estate:** Restrictions depend on _Legal Necessity_ (permanent limitation).
>
