## 12.4 Maintenance and Inheritance (Stridhana) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This section contains the most critical legal distinction in uncodified Hindu Law: the difference between money given _for_ support (which becomes absolute property) and property received _by_ inheritance (which becomes limited property).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a high-yield exam area. A standard trick question is: _"A widow inherits a house from her husband. Is it her Stridhana?"_ The answer in Bangladesh is a firm **NO**.
    

---

### Introduction

In the classification of women's property, the source of acquisition determines the extent of her ownership. Two common sources are **Maintenance** (money given for her support) and **Inheritance** (property received on the death of a relative).

In the uncodified Dayabhaga law applicable in Bangladesh, these two sources lead to very different legal results.

---

### 1. Property Given for Maintenance

When a woman receives money or property specifically for her maintenance, the law generally treats this as her absolute property.

- **Arrears of Maintenance:** If a husband or his family fails to pay maintenance and the court later awards a lump sum as "arrears," this money is **Stridhana**. She has absolute control over it and can gift or will it away.
    
- **Savings from Maintenance:** If a woman lives frugally and saves money from her maintenance allowance, those savings are her **Stridhana**.
    
    - _Example:_ A widow receives 5000 Taka/month. She spends 3000 and saves 2000. After 10 years, she buys a gold necklace with the savings. That necklace is her Stridhana (Absolute Property).
        
- **Lump Sum Grant:** If she is given a specific plot of land _absolutely_ in lieu of maintenance (where the deed explicitly grants ownership), it becomes Stridhana. However, if she is just given the _right to enjoy_ the land for maintenance, it is a limited interest.
    

---

### 2. Property Acquired by Inheritance

This is the most crucial concept in Bangladeshi Hindu Law. The nature of inherited property depends on _whom_ she inherited it from.

#### **A. Inheritance from a Male (The Dayabhaga Rule)**

- **The Rule:** Under the Dayabhaga school, a woman who inherits property from a **male** (e.g., husband, father, son, or grandson) does **NOT** acquire it as Stridhana.
    
- **Status:** She takes it as a **Woman's Estate** (also called Widow's Estate or Limited Estate).
    
- **Restrictions:**
    
    1. **No Alienation:** She is the owner, but she **cannot sell or mortgage** the property except for "Legal Necessity" (strict need).
        
    2. **No Will:** She cannot dispose of it by Will.
        
    3. **Reversion:** On her death, the property does not go to her own heirs. It "reverts" (goes back) to the next heirs of the male she inherited it from (called **Reversioners**).
        
    
    - _Example:_ A widow inherits her husband's house. She holds it for life. When she dies, the house goes to her husband's brother (Reversioner), not to her own daughter (unless the daughter is also an heir of the father).
        

#### **B. Inheritance from a Female**

- **The Rule:** Property inherited by a woman from another **female** (e.g., a daughter inheriting her mother's Stridhana) is generally treated as **Stridhana**.
    
- **Status:** In this case, she becomes the absolute owner with full powers of disposal.

### 3. Comparison: Maintenance vs. Inheritance

|**Feature**|**Savings from Maintenance**|**Inherited Property (from Male)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Nature**|**Stridhana** (Absolute).|**Woman's Estate** (Limited).|
|**Power to Sell**|Absolute (Can sell anytime).|Restricted (Only for Necessity).|
|**Devolution**|Goes to her own Stridhana heirs.|Goes to the Male's heirs (Reversioners).|
|**Control**|She is the full mistress.|She is a limited owner.|

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "India vs. Bangladesh" Trap:**
> 
> - **In India:** Under Section 14 of the _Hindu Succession Act, 1956_, any property possessed by a female (whether maintenance or inheritance) is **Stridhana** (Absolute).
>     
> - **In Bangladesh:** This Act does not apply. We follow the old Dayabhaga rule. **Inherited property is NOT Stridhana.** Be very careful not to apply Indian law in your exam answers.
>     

> **Accumulations:** Distinguish between:
> 
> - **Income:** The income (rents/crops) from a Woman's Estate is her absolute property (Stridhana). She can spend it or save it.
>     
> - **Corpus:** The land itself is the Limited Estate. She cannot touch it.
