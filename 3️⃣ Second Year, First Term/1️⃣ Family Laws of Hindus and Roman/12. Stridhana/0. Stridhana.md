## 12. Stridhana ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** "Stridhana" (Woman's Property) is the only area in traditional Hindu Law where a woman has absolute ownership. Understanding the difference between "Stridhana" (Absolute) and "Widow's Estate" (Limited) is the most critical concept for female property rights in Bangladesh.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent topic. Questions often ask to "Distinguish between Stridhana and Woman's Estate" or "Discuss the line of succession to Stridhana property under Dayabhaga Law."

---

### Introduction

The term **Stridhana** literally means "Woman's Property" (_Stri_ = Woman, _Dhana_ = Property). In Hindu Law, it refers to that property which a Hindu woman possesses with **absolute ownership**. She can sell, gift, or will it away at her own pleasure, and on her death, it descends to her own heirs (not her husband's heirs).

This is in sharp contrast to the **"Widow's Estate"** (or Woman's Estate), where she has only a limited interest and cannot sell the property (except for legal necessity).

### 12.1 Property Received from Relatives (_Saudayika_)

The most common and "purest" form of Stridhana is property given to a woman by her affectionate relations.

- **Definition:** Gifts received from parents, brothers, husband, or other affectionate relations are called **Saudayika** (Gifts from affectionate kindred).
    
- **Time of Gift:** These gifts can be given:
    
    - **Adhyagni:** Gifts given before the nuptial fire during the wedding ceremony.
        
    - **Adhyavahanika:** Gifts received during the bridal procession (from parental home to husband's home).
        
    - **Pritidatta:** Gifts made out of love by parents-in-law or elders.
        
- **Legal Status:** A woman has **absolute power of disposal** over _Saudayika_ Stridhana at all times, even during marriage. Her husband has no control over it.
    

### 12.2 Gifts from Strangers (_Non-Saudayika_)

- **Definition:** Gifts received from non-relatives (strangers) or earnings from her own mechanical arts/labor.
    
- **Status during Maidenhood/Widowhood:** If received while she is unmarried or a widow, it is **Stridhana**, and she has absolute control.
    
- **Status during Coverture (Marriage):** Under the Dayabhaga school (applicable in Bangladesh), gifts from strangers received _during marriage_ are **NOT Stridhana** in the absolute sense.
    
    - **Husband's Control:** The property is subject to her husband's control during his lifetime. She cannot sell it without his consent. However, if she survives him, it becomes her absolute Stridhana after his death.
        

### 12.3 Property by Partition

- **The Scenario:** When a partition takes place in a joint family (e.g., between sons), a share may be allotted to a female (like a mother or grandmother) in lieu of maintenance.
    
- **Status in Bangladesh (Dayabhaga):** Such a share obtained on partition is **NOT Stridhana**. It is considered a **Woman's Estate**.
    
    - _Effect:_ She can enjoy the income for her life, but she has no right to sell the land. On her death, the share goes back to the heirs of the male from whom she received it (e.g., her husband's heirs), not her own heirs.
        

### 12.4 Maintenance and Inheritance

- **Maintenance:** Money paid to a woman for her maintenance (lump sum or periodic) and the savings made from it are her **Stridhana**. She has absolute control over it.
    
- **Inheritance:**
    
    - **From a Male:** Property inherited by a woman from a male (e.g., husband, father, son) is **NOT Stridhana** under Dayabhaga law. It is a **Woman's Estate**. She holds it for life, and after her death, it reverts to the _Reversioners_.
        
    - **From a Female:** Property inherited from another female is generally treated as Stridhana.
        

### 12.5 Rights to Stridhana

The extent of a woman's power over her property depends on her status and the source of the property.

1. **Absolute Disposal:** She has the absolute right to sell, gift, mortgage, or will away her **Saudayika Stridhana** (gifts from relations) without her husband's consent.
    
2. **Husband's Distress:** The husband has no right to use his wife's Stridhana, with one exception. In times of **extreme distress** (e.g., famine, illness, imprisonment), he can take and use it. He is morally bound to repay it, but technically she cannot sue him for it during his lifetime (though modern courts are stricter).
    
3. **Unchastity:** Unchastity does not strip a woman of her Stridhana rights. Once the property vests in her, it remains hers regardless of her moral conduct.
    

### 12.6 Inheritance of Stridhana Property (Dayabhaga Succession)

If a woman dies leaving Stridhana, who inherits it? The rules are distinct from male inheritance and favor female heirs.

**Order for _Yautaka_ (Nuptial Gifts) under Dayabhaga:**

1. **Unbetrothed Daughter**.
    
2. **Betrothed Daughter**.
    
3. **Married Daughter** who has (or is likely to have) a son.
    
4. **Son**.
    
5. **Daughter's Son**.
    
6. **Son's Son**.
    
7. **Husband**.
    

_Note:_ The key feature is that **daughters** are preferred over sons for Nuptial Stridhana, ensuring wealth stays in the female line.

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Woman's Estate" Trap:** This is the most common mistake.
> 
> - _Inherited Land:_ If a widow inherits land from her husband, is it Stridhana? **NO.** It is a **Woman's Estate** (Limited Interest). She cannot sell it except for legal necessity.
>     
> - _Gifted Gold:_ If her father gifts her gold, is it Stridhana? **YES.** She can sell it freely.
>     

> **Section 14 of Hindu Succession Act (India):** In Indian law (post-1956), _all_ property held by a woman is Stridhana. **THIS DOES NOT APPLY IN BANGLADESH.** In Bangladesh, you must stick to the old Dayabhaga distinction: Inheritance = Limited Estate; Gifts = Absolute Stridhana.
