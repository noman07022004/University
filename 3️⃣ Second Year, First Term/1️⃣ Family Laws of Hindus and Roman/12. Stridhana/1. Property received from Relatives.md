## 12.1 Property Received from Relatives (_Saudayika_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the "purest" form of Stridhana. Understanding _Saudayika_ is critical because it is the _only_ category of property where a married Hindu woman in Bangladesh has absolute, independent power of disposal at all times, free from her husband's control.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Examiners often set problems asking: "Can a husband stop his wife from selling the gold given by her father?" (Answer: No, it is _Saudayika_). You must know the specific Sanskrit terms for these gifts.
    

---

### Introduction

The Sanskrit term **Saudayika** is derived from _Sudaya_, meaning "affectionate kindred." It refers to gifts received by a woman from her affectionate relations (parents, husband, brothers, or parents-in-law) out of love and affection.

These gifts are considered **Stridhana** in the strict sense. The distinguishing feature of _Saudayika_ property is that the woman has **absolute power of disposal** over it, even during her marriage (coverture). Her husband has no right to control or spend it.

### 1. Classification of Gifts from Relatives

Ancient Smriti writers (like Manu and Katyayana) classified these gifts based on _when_ and _by whom_ they were given. You should memorize these technical terms for the exam:

#### **A. Adhyagni (Fire-Witnessed Gifts)**

- **Definition:** Gifts given to the bride before the **Nuptial Fire** (_Agni_) which witnesses the marriage ceremony.
    
- **Source:** Usually from the father, mother, or brother during the actual wedding rituals.
    

#### **B. Adhyavahanika (Procession Gifts)**

- **Definition:** Gifts received by the bride during the **Bridal Procession**, while she is being led from her father’s house to her husband’s house.
    
- **Scope:** It includes gifts from both her paternal and maternal relations during the _Bidai_ (farewell).
    

#### **C. Pritidatta (Token of Affection)**

- **Definition:** Gifts given to the bride out of genuine affection (_Priti_) by her **parents-in-law** (father-in-law or mother-in-law).
    
- **Occasion:** Often given when she bows at their feet (_Padavandanika_) or enters the new house.
    

#### **D. Bhatrudatta (Gifts from Husband)**

- **Definition:** Gifts given by the husband to his wife out of love.
    
- **Dayabhaga Exception (Crucial):** Under the Dayabhaga school (Bangladesh), gifts of **immovable property** (land/house) given by the husband to the wife are **NOT** _Saudayika_ Stridhana.
    
    - She takes them as a "Woman's Estate" (Limited Interest) and cannot sell them without his consent.
        
    - _Movable_ gifts (jewelry/cash) from the husband _are_ absolute Stridhana.
        

### 2. Legal Rights over _Saudayika_ Stridhana

The legal status of _Saudayika_ is unique because it grants the woman autonomy that was otherwise rare in ancient law.

- **Absolute Ownership:** She is the absolute owner. She can spend, sell, mortgage, gift, or will it away to anyone she likes.
    
- **Independence from Husband:** Her husband has **no control** over it. He cannot bind her property for his debts.
    
    - _Contrast:_ For _Non-Saudayika_ property (e.g., gifts from strangers), the husband _does_ have control during his lifetime.
        
- **Husband's Limited Right (Distress):** The husband can take this property **only** in times of extreme "Distress" (_Apatkale_), such as famine, acute illness, or imprisonment. Even then, he is morally bound to repay it later.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Dayabhaga Exception:** This is the most likely trick question.
> 
> - _Scenario:_ "Husband gifts a piece of land to Wife. Wife sells it to X. Is the sale valid?"
>     
> - _Answer:_ Under **Dayabhaga Law**, the sale is **Void/Voidable**. Why? Because land given by a husband is _not_ absolute Stridhana; she cannot sell it. (Under Mitakshara, she could).
>     

> **Succession:** Remember that _Saudayika_ property devolves to the woman's **own heirs** (primarily daughters), not her husband's heirs. This preserves the wealth within the female line.
