## 12.3 Property by Partition ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This is a tricky concept. While "Partition" usually implies dividing property among owners (coparceners), in Hindu Law, certain females (like the Mother) are given a share _during_ partition not because they are owners, but "in lieu of maintenance." Understanding that this share is _not_ absolute property is vital.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A favorite objective and short-question topic. "Is the share obtained by a Hindu mother on partition her Stridhana?" (Answer: No, under Dayabhaga law, it is a Woman's Estate).
    

---

### Introduction

In the Hindu Joint Family system, "Partition" refers to the division of joint family property among the coparceners (usually sons). While females are generally not coparceners in the uncodified law, they are sometimes allotted a share during this division.

The critical question for this section is: _What is the nature of this share?_ Does she become the absolute owner (Stridhana), or does she hold it only for life (Woman's Estate)?

### 1. When does a Woman get a Share?

Under the Dayabhaga school (applicable in Bangladesh), a female does not have a right to _demand_ partition. However, if the male members (sons) decide to partition the property, certain females are entitled to a share "in lieu of maintenance" (as a substitute for their maintenance support).

- **Mother:** If a partition takes place between the sons, the mother is entitled to a share equal to that of a son.
    
- **Grandmother:** Similarly, a grandmother may be entitled to a share if partition takes place between grandsons.
    

### 2. Legal Status: Stridhana or Woman's Estate?

This is the most important legal point.

- **The Rule:** Property obtained by a woman on partition of joint family property is **NOT Stridhana**.
    
- **Status:** It is considered a **Woman's Estate** (Limited Estate).
    
    - **Meaning:** She holds the property only for her lifetime to support herself. She is the owner for life, but she is **not** the full/absolute owner.
        

### 3. Consequences of this Status

Because it is a Woman's Estate and not Stridhana, strictly restrictive rules apply:

- **Alienation:** She **CANNOT** sell, gift, or mortgage this share merely for her own pleasure. She can alienate it **only** for "Legal Necessity" (e.g., her own survival, medical treatment, or religious acts for her husband).
    
- **Devolution (Inheritance):** On her death, this property does **not** go to her own heirs (Stridhana heirs). Instead, it **reverts** to the heirs of the male from whom the property came (usually her husband's heirs or the sons who partitioned it).
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The Rationale:** Why is it not Stridhana?
> 
> - The law views this share as a "capitalized provision for maintenance." Since maintenance is for her life only, the property given in exchange for it is also essentially for her life.
>     

> **Crucial Exam Distinction:**
> 
> - **Gift from Father:** = Stridhana (Absolute).
>     
> - **Share on Partition:** = Woman's Estate (Limited).
>     
> - _Scenario:_ "Mother receives a plot of land when her sons divide the estate. She writes a Will giving it to her daughter."
>     
> - _Result:_ The Will is **Void** regarding that land. She cannot will away a Woman's Estate. It goes back to the sons.
