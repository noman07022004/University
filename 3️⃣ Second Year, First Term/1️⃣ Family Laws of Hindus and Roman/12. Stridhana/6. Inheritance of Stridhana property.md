## 12.6 Inheritance of Stridhana Property ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most complex part of Stridhana. Unlike male property (which follows a uniform agnatic line), Stridhana succession changes based on the _type_ of property (_Yautaka_ vs. _Ayautaka_) and the _status_ of the female heirs.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a standard essay question: "Discuss the general rules of succession to Stridhana under the Dayabhaga School." You must be able to list the order of heirs correctly.
    

---

### Introduction

The succession to Stridhana is very different from the succession to the property of a male.

1. **Female Preference:** Generally, female heirs (daughters) are preferred over male heirs (sons), especially for nuptial gifts.
    
2. **Source Matters:** Under the Dayabhaga school (Bangladesh), the heirs vary depending on _how_ and _when_ the woman received the property.
    

The law divides Stridhana into three classes for succession purposes:

1. **Yautaka:** Gifts given at the time of marriage (before the nuptial fire).
    
2. **Ayautaka:** Gifts not given at the time of marriage (e.g., from father before marriage).
    
3. **Pritidatta:** Gifts given by the husband or his relations.
    

---

### 1. Succession to _Yautaka_ Stridhana (Nuptial Gifts)

This refers to gifts received by the woman during the marriage ceremony (_Adhyagni_ and _Adhyavahanika_). The Dayabhaga law strongly prefers daughters here to keep the "female wealth" in the female line.

**Order of Succession:**

1. **Unbetrothed Daughter** (Unmarried and not engaged).
    
2. **Betrothed Daughter** (Engaged but not married).
    
3. **Married Daughter** who has, or is likely to have, a son.
    
    - _Note:_ Barren daughters or widowed daughters without sons are excluded.
        
4. **Son**.
    
5. **Daughter's Son**.
    
6. **Son's Son**.
    
7. **Son's Son's Son**.
    
8. **Husband**.
    

_Key Takeaway:_ The son inherits only if all categories of eligible daughters are exhausted.

### 2. Succession to _Ayautaka_ Stridhana (Non-Nuptial Gifts)

This category includes gifts and bequests from the father or other relations made _before_ or _after_ marriage (not during the ceremony).

**Order of Succession:**

1. **Son and Maiden (Unmarried) Daughter** take together in equal shares.
    
    - _Note:_ This is a unique feature of Dayabhaga law—brother and sister inheriting together.
        
2. **Married Daughter** who has, or is likely to have, a son.
    
3. **Son's Son**.
    
4. **Daughter's Son**.
    
5. **Barren/Sonless Widowed Daughter**.
    

### 3. Succession to _Pritidatta_ (Gifts from Husband/In-laws)

If the property was given by the husband or his family (father-in-law), the order slightly shifts to favor the heirs of the husband earlier.

**Order of Succession:**

1. **Son and Maiden Daughter** (Equally).
    
2. **Married Daughter** (with son).
    
3. **Son's Son**.
    
4. **Daughter's Son**.
    

---

### 4. Special Rules for Degraded/Unchaste Women

- **Heirs of Prostitutes:** If a Hindu woman becomes a prostitute or lives an unchaste life and dies leaving Stridhana, her property passes to her own heirs (children, etc.) and not necessarily to the Crown. Unchastity does not sever the tie of blood relationship.
    
- **Illegitimate Children:** In some interpretations, illegitimate children can inherit the Stridhana of their mother if she has no legitimate issue.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Son vs. Daughter" Conflict:**
> 
> - **Yautaka (Nuptial):** Daughter excludes Son.
>     
> - **Ayautaka (Non-Nuptial):** Son and Maiden Daughter share equally.
>     
> - _Exam Trick:_ "A widow dies leaving a gold necklace given by her father at her wedding (_Yautaka_) and a plot of land given by her father years later (_Ayautaka_). She has one son and one unmarried daughter."
>     
> - _Distribution:_
>     
>     - Necklace goes to **Daughter only**.
>         
>     - Land goes to **Son and Daughter equally**.
>         

> **Exclusion of Childless Widowed Daughter:** Under Dayabhaga law, a married daughter is usually only an heir if she has a son or is capable of having one. A daughter who is a childless widow or barren is often excluded because she cannot offer spiritual benefit (_Pinda_) to the ancestors.

> **Spiritual Benefit:** The entire Dayabhaga scheme is based on _Pinda_ (spiritual offering). Daughters inherit because they produce sons who can offer Pinda. A barren daughter cannot, so she is excluded.

>Branch 1: Yautaka -> Unmarried Daughter -> Married Daughter -> Son. 
Branch 2: Ayautaka -> Son + Unmarried Daughter (Equally) -> Married Daughter.
