### 16.4 Succession _per stirpes_ and succession _per capita_ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** These are the two fundamental mathematical formulas used to distribute an estate. If you confuse them, you will give a grandson the same share as a son, which is legally incorrect.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Almost every calculation problem in a Hindu Law exam requires you to explicitly state: _"Here, the division is per stirpes."_
    

---

#### I. Definitions

These Latin terms describe the **mode of distribution** when there are multiple heirs.

1. **_Per Stirpes_** **(By Roots/Branches):**
    
    - The estate is divided according to the "branches" or stocks of the family.
        
    - The share depends on which branch you belong to, not just your individual existence.
        
    - This applies when heirs inherit by **Representation**.
        
2. **_Per Capita_** **(By Heads):**
    
    - The estate is divided equally among the number of individuals (heads).
        
    - Every heir gets an identical share, regardless of which branch they come from.
        
    - This applies when heirs inherit in their **own right**.
        

---

#### II. Succession _Per Stirpes_ (The Rule for Male Descendants)

This is the primary rule for the **male agnatic line** (Son, Grandson, Great-grandson) under both Dayabhaga and Mitakshara schools.

How it Works:

The estate is first divided by the number of original sons (the "Roots"). If a son is dead, his share passes to his specific branch of sons.

> Example:
> 
> $P$ (Propositus) has two sons: $A$ (living) and $B$ (dead).
> 
> - $B$ (the dead son) left 3 sons: $X, Y, Z$.
>     
> 
> **Calculation:**
> 
> 1. **Count the Roots:** There are 2 roots ($A$ and $B$).
>     
> 2. **Divide Estate:** Estate is divided into 2 halves.
>     
> 3. **Distribution:**
>     
>     - **Son A** takes **1/2** (50%).
>         
>     - **B's Branch ($X, Y, Z$)** takes the other **1/2** collectively.
>         
> 4. **Sub-division:** $X, Y,$ and $Z$ divide their father's half equally.
>     
>     - $X = 1/6$
>         
>     - $Y = 1/6$
>         
>     - $Z = 1/6$
>         
> 
> **Result:** Although $A$ and $X$ are both heirs, $A$ gets 50% while $X$ gets only roughly 16%. This is _Per Stirpes_.

---

#### III. Succession _Per Capita_ (The Rule for Others)

This rule generally applies to heirs who stand in the same degree of relationship but are not male agnatic descendants claiming by representation. It is most commonly seen with female heirs or collaterals.

How it Works:

The total estate is divided simply by the total number of eligible claimants.

> Example 1 (Daughters):
> 
> $P$ dies leaving two daughters, $D1$ and $D2$.
> 
> - **Rule:** They inherit _Per Capita_.
>     
> - **Calculation:** Total Heirs = 2. Each takes **1/2**.
>     
> 
> Example 2 (Daughter's Sons - Dayabhaga):
> 
> $P$ dies leaving two daughters ($D1, D2$) who are both dead.
> 
> - $D1$ left 1 son ($S1$).
>     
> - $D2$ left 3 sons ($S2, S3, S4$).
>     
> - **Rule:** Under Dayabhaga law, Daughter's Sons inherit _Per Capita_ because they do not have the right of representation in the agnatic sense (they offer Pindas individually).
>     
> - **Calculation:** Total Heirs = 4 ($S1, S2, S3, S4$).
>     
> - **Result:** The estate is divided by 4. **Everyone gets 1/4.**
>     
>     - _(Note: This contrasts with Per Stirpes, where S1 would have taken half)._
>         

---

#### IV. Summary Distinction

|**Feature**|**Per Stirpes (By Roots)**|**Per Capita (By Heads)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Basis**|Branch/Stock|Individual|
|**Logic**|Doctrine of Representation|Equality of individuals|
|**Primary Beneficiaries**|Son, Grandson, Great-grandson|Daughters, Widows, some Collaterals|
|**Inequality**|Individual shares vary (Uncles get more than Nephews)|Individual shares are equal|
|**Formula**|Share = (Total Estate ÷ Number of Branches)|Share = (Total Estate ÷ Number of Persons)|

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Grandson" Trap:** If a problem says _"P dies leaving 4 grandsons from 4 different deceased sons,"_ calculate it.
    
    - Since all branches are equal (1 son each), the result looks like Per Capita (everyone gets 1/4).
        
    - **However**, technically it is still _Per Stirpes_. You must write "Per Stirpes" to get full marks for legal accuracy.
        
- **Stridhana:** Succession to _Stridhana_ (woman's property) often follows _Per Stirpes_ rules in some schools for her issue, but this is advanced. Stick to the male succession examples for general exams.
    
- **Dayabhaga Nuance:** Remember that Dayabhaga uses _Per Capita_ more often for cognates (like daughter's sons) than Mitakshara would in similar (theoretical) contexts, because Dayabhaga values the individual Pinda offering.
