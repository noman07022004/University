

### 16.3 Co-heirs ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** It is vital to distinguish how different heirs hold property together. Are they individuals sharing a plot (Tenants-in-Common), or a single legal entity (Joint Tenants)? The answer changes their ability to sell or leave property to their children.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent short question asks to "Distinguish between Joint Tenancy and Tenancy-in-Common in Hindu Law."
    

---

#### I. Definition and Concept

When two or more persons inherit property simultaneously from the same deceased owner (_Prepositus_), they are called **Co-heirs**.

The crucial legal question is: What is the nature of their joint ownership?

Depending on the school of law and the gender of the heirs, they may hold the property in two ways:

1. **Tenancy-in-Common:** Each heir has a defined, specific share (e.g., 50%).
    
2. **Joint Tenancy:** The heirs hold the property as a collective body with no defined shares and the **Right of Survivorship**.
    

---

#### II. Male Co-heirs (Sons, Grandsons)

The rules for male co-heirs differ sharply between the two schools.

**1. Dayabhaga School (Bangladesh)**

- **Nature:** When multiple male heirs (e.g., brothers) inherit property, they hold it as **Tenants-in-Common**.
    
- **Defined Shares:** Each brother has a fixed, mathematical share (e.g., 1/3rd) from the moment of death, even if they haven't physically divided the land.
    
- **Result:** If one brother dies without a partition, his defined share passes to **his own heirs** (e.g., his widow or daughter), not to his surviving brothers.
    
- **Alienation:** A co-heir can freely sell or mortgage his specific share without the consent of the others.
    

**2. Mitakshara School**

- **Nature:**
    
    - **Ancestral Property:** Male descendants inheriting ancestral property hold it as **Coparceners** (Joint Tenants). There are no defined shares. The Doctrine of Survivorship applies.
        
    - **Separate Property:** If sons inherit their father's _self-acquired_ property, they hold it as **Tenants-in-Common** (according to the Privy Council ruling in _Jogeswar v. Ram Chandra_). Each takes a separate share.
        

---

#### III. Female Co-heirs (Widows, Daughters)

This is a unique exception in Hindu Law where the rules are uniform across both schools.

The Rule:

When two or more females inherit property together (e.g., two widows of the same husband, or two daughters of the same father), they invariably take as Joint Tenants with Rights of Survivorship.

**How it Works:**

1. **Joint Enjoyment:** They are entitled to enjoy the property together. They can divide the _income_ or possession for convenience, but they cannot destroy the joint title.
    
2. **Survivorship:** If one female co-heir dies, her interest does **not** pass to her own heirs. It passes by survivorship to the **surviving female co-heir**.
    
3. **Reversion:** The property does not revert to the husband’s/father’s heirs (Reversioners) until the **last** surviving female dies.
    

> **Example:** A Hindu man dies leaving two widows, $W1$ and $W2$.
> 
> - They inherit the estate together.
>     
> - If $W1$ dies, her share goes to $W2$ (not to $W1$'s parents).
>     
> - Only when $W2$ dies does the property go to the husband's brother/nephew.
>     

---

#### IV. Rights of Co-heirs

Regardless of the type of tenancy, co-heirs generally have the following rights:

1. **Right to Joint Possession:** Every co-heir has a right to possess and enjoy every part of the joint property. One heir cannot exclude the others.
    
2. **Right to Partition:** Any co-heir (in Dayabhaga or Mitakshara separate property) can demand a partition (division) of the property at any time to separate their specific share.
    
3. **Right to Account:** A co-heir managing the property must account for the profits to the other co-heirs (strict in Dayabhaga, less strict in Mitakshara Karta management).
    

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Daughter vs. Son" Contrast:**
    
    - _Two Sons (Dayabhaga):_ Tenants-in-Common. If one dies, his share goes to his family.
        
    - _Two Daughters (Any School):_ Joint Tenants. If one dies, her share goes to her sister.
        
- **Alienation by Female Co-heir:** A female co-heir cannot alienate her share to a stranger in a way that defeats the survivorship right of the other female co-heir. She can only sell her _life interest_ in that share.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** In problem questions set in Bangladesh, assume the family is Dayabhaga. Therefore, brothers are always **Tenants-in-Common**. They can sell their shares freely, and "Survivorship" does not apply to them.
