
### 16.2 Doctrine of _Spes Successionis_ ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This doctrine defines the boundary between "property you own" and "property you hope to own." It is crucial for understanding why a reversioner cannot sell their future interest.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A favorite topic for short notes and problem questions. Examiners often ask: _"X, a reversioner, transfers his share to Y while the widow is still alive. Is the transfer valid?"_
    

---

#### I. Definition and Meaning

The term **_Spes Successionis_** is Latin for **"Hope of Succession"** or "Expectation of Succession."

In law, it refers to the interest of a person who is currently the apparent heir but whose right to inherit has not yet **vested** (become fixed/secured). It is a mere possibility of getting property in the future, which may or may not happen.

**Contrast with Vested Interest:**

- **Vested Interest:** A fixed right to property (e.g., A son’s right in Mitakshara ancestral property by birth). It is transferable and heritable.
    
- **Spes Successionis:** A mere chance. The person might die before the owner, or the owner might sell the property (if they have the power) or make a will (if allowed).
    

---

#### II. Application in Hindu Law: The Reversioner

The primary application of this doctrine in classical Hindu Law (Bangladesh) concerns the **Reversioner** standing behind a **Widow's Estate**.

1. **The Situation:** When a Hindu widow holds a "Limited Estate" (Topic 13), the husband's heirs (Reversioners) wait for her death to inherit.
    
2. **The Nature of Right:** While the widow is alive, the reversioner has **no right or interest** in the property _in praesenti_ (at present).
    
    - He is not an owner.
        
    - He does not have a "contingent interest" (in the strict English sense).
        
    - He has only a **Spes Successionis**—a hope that he will survive the widow and take the property.
        
3. **Uncertainty:** If the reversioner dies _before_ the widow, his heirs get nothing. The right does not pass to them because he never "owned" it.
    

---

#### III. Transferability (The Legal Prohibition)

Since a _Spes Successionis_ is not property, but merely a possibility of getting property, it cannot be the subject of a valid transfer.

**1. Statutory Bar in Bangladesh** **Section 6(a) of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882** explicitly states:

> _"The chance of an heir-apparent succeeding to an estate, the chance of a relation obtaining a legacy on the death of a kinsman, or any other mere possibility of a like nature, cannot be transferred."_

**2. Effect of Transfer** If a reversioner purports to sell, mortgage, or gift his "future share" while the widow is still alive:

- The transfer is **Void _ab initio_** (invalid from the very beginning).
    
- Whatever contract was signed is a nullity in the eyes of the law.
    
- Even if he survives the widow later, the transfer signed earlier does not automatically become valid (though the Doctrine of Estoppel might arise, see below).
    

---

#### IV. Exception: Doctrine of Estoppel

While the transfer itself is void, the **Law of Contracts** and **Equity** may sometimes intervene against the reversioner personally.

- **The Scenario:** If a reversioner represents to a buyer that he has a right to sell, accepts money (consideration), and then later actually inherits the property, he may be stopped (**Estopped**) from claiming "My previous sale was void."
    
- **The Principle:** He cannot take advantage of his own fraud. The court may compel him to hand over the property to the buyer, not because the transfer was valid, but because he is personally bound by his contract and receipt of money.
    
- **Limit:** This applies only if the transfer was for **value** (money/consideration). A gift of _Spes Successionis_ is totally void and cannot be cured by estoppel.
    

---

#### V. Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Case Law:** The Privy Council decision in **_Amrit Narayan v. Gaya Singh (1917)_** is the leading authority. It established that a reversioner's right is a mere chance and cannot be assigned or relinquished.
    
- **Guardian's Power:** A guardian cannot bind a minor reversioner by dealing with their _Spes Successionis_. Since it's not property, the guardian has no power over it.
    
- **Problem Solving:**
    
    - _Question:_ "R is the nearest reversioner to W's estate. R sells his chance of succession to P for 1 Lakh Taka. W dies. R refuses to give the land to P. Can P sue?"
        
    - _Analysis:_ The sale under Transfer of Property Act s. 6(a) is void. However, P can sue for the return of his money, or arguably invoke estoppel if R has now inherited the land. In a strict property law exam, start by saying the **transfer is void**.
