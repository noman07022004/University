## 7.5 Consequences of Adoption ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This section explains the _result_ of the legal process. It answers the fundamental question: "What happens to the boy's rights?" Understanding the concept of "Civil Death" in one family and "Rebirth" in another is the essence of Hindu adoption law.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a high-yield area for problem questions. Examiners love scenarios where an adopted son tries to claim property from his biological father (he can't) or where a widow adopts a son and the son claims immediate ownership of the estate (he can).
    

---

### Introduction

The legal effect of a valid _Dattaka_ adoption is drastic and permanent. It operates as a "legal transplantation." The moment the ceremony of giving and taking is completed, the adopted boy is completely severed from his natural family and is grafted into the adoptive family. The texts describe this change of status as a **Civil Death** in the family of birth and a **Rebirth** in the family of adoption.

This change of status brings about a complete shift in rights, duties, lineage, and inheritance.

---

### 1. Effect on the Natural Family (Severance)

From the moment of adoption, all ties between the adopted son and his natural family are severed for civil purposes.

- **Loss of Inheritance:** He loses all rights to inherit property from his natural father, mother, or other relations in the natural family. He can no longer perform the _Sraddha_ (funeral rites) for his natural parents.
    
- **Loss of Gotra:** He loses the _Gotra_ (lineage) of his natural father.
    
- **Exception (Prohibited Degrees):** There is one vital tie that is _never_ severed: the blood tie for the purpose of marriage.
    
    - _The Rule:_ He cannot marry anyone in his natural family whom he could not have married before adoption. For example, he still cannot marry his natural sister or natural mother’s sister, because the blood relationship (_Consanguinity_) persists even if the legal relationship ends.
        

### 2. Effect on the Adoptive Family (Admission)

The adopted son is legally deemed to be the son of the adoptive father, born on the day of adoption (though rights may relate back).

- **Same Rights as Natural Son:** He generally enjoys the same rights and privileges as a legitimate natural-born son (_Aurasa_).
    
- **Inheritance:**
    
    - **Lineal:** He inherits from his adoptive father.
        
    - **Collateral:** He inherits from his adoptive father’s relations (e.g., uncles, grandfather).
        
    - **Maternal:** He inherits from his adoptive mother and her relations.
        
- **Guardianship:** The adoptive father becomes his natural guardian.
    
- **Maintenance:** He is entitled to be maintained by the adoptive father.
    

### 3. Rights of Adopted Son vs. After-born Son

What happens if, after adopting a son, the adoptive father has a natural legitimate son (_Aurasa_) born to him?

- **Co-existence:** The adoption remains valid. The adopted son does not lose his status.
    
- **Reduction of Share:** However, in matters of inheritance, the adopted son’s share is reduced because the natural-born son is considered spiritually superior.
    
- **The Dayabhaga Rule (Bangladesh):** In Bengal, the rule is that the adopted son gets **one-third (1/3)** of the share of the natural son.
    
    - _Example:_ If an estate is worth 4 Lakhs. The natural son gets 3 Lakhs, and the adopted son gets 1 Lakh.
        

### 4. Divesting of Estate (The Widow Scenario)

This is a specific consequence relevant to the **Dayabhaga** school in Bangladesh.

- **Scenario:** A Hindu man dies leaving a widow. The widow inherits his property (Widow's Estate). Two years later, she adopts a son (using the authority/permission given by her husband).
    
- **Consequence:** The moment the adoption is valid, the son **divests** (strips) the widow of the estate.
    
    - **Ownership:** The adopted son immediately becomes the owner of the property (as the heir of his deceased father).
        
    - **Widow's Status:** The widow loses her ownership and is reduced to the status of a mother entitled only to **Maintenance**.
        
- **Doctrine of Relation Back:** The adoption relates back to the date of the husband's death. It is deemed that the son was living when the father died.
    

### 5. Irrevocability

- **The Rule:** A valid adoption, once made, cannot be cancelled by either party.
    
    - The father cannot "disown" the adopted son and send him back to his natural family.
        
    - The adopted son cannot renounce his adoptive status and return to his natural family.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Civil Death" Trap:** In exams, if a problem says: _"X was adopted by Y. Later, X's natural father died leaving millions. Can X inherit?"_
> 
> - **Answer:** **No.** He is legally "dead" to his natural father.
>     

> **Marriage Prohibition:** Remember the exception. Even though he is "dead" to the natural family, he **cannot marry his natural sister**. This is a frequent objective question.

> **Widow's Property:** Crucial for Dayabhaga problems: When a widow adopts, she essentially **disinherits herself**. She swaps her "Life Estate" for a mere "Right of Maintenance." This explains why widows historically were sometimes reluctant to exercise the power of adoption quickly.
