## 7.4 Requirements for a Valid Adoption ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the checklist that determines the legal fate of an adoption. If any one of these requirements is missing (e.g., the widow didn't have permission), the adoption is void _ab initio_.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most "problem-heavy" area of the syllabus. You will frequently face scenarios like: "A widow adopts a son without her husband's consent" or "A man adopts his sister's son." You must know the specific prohibitions to solve these.
    

---

### Introduction

For an adoption to be valid under the uncodified Hindu Law of Bangladesh, it must satisfy strict conditions regarding the **capacity** of the persons involved and the **eligibility** of the child. Unlike a contract where any two adults can agree, adoption changes the lineage and religious destiny of a family, so the rules are rigid.

The requirements are broadly categorized into three heads:

1. **Capacity to Take** (Who can adopt?)
    
2. **Capacity to Give** (Who can give?)
    
3. **Capacity to be Adopted** (Who can be the son?)
    

---

### 1. Capacity to Take in Adoption (The Adopter)

The rules differ significantly for men and women.

#### **A. Male Hindu**

A male Hindu has the inherent right to adopt a son, provided he satisfies these conditions:

- **Sound Mind:** He must be of sound mind.
    
- **Age:** He must have attained the "age of discretion." While the Majority Act sets 18, under pure Hindu law, a minor who has reached the age of discretion (usually 15 or 16) can validly adopt.
    
- **No Male Issue:** He must **not** have a son, grandson, or great-grandson (legitimate or adopted) living at the time of adoption. If he already has a son, the adoption is void.
    
- **Wife's Consent:** Under the uncodified law in Bangladesh, a husband **does not need** his wife's consent to adopt. He can adopt even against her wishes.
    

#### **B. Female Hindu (The Widow's Authority)**

This is the most critical area of difference between Bangladeshi and Indian law.

- **Unmarried Woman:** A spinster cannot adopt.
    
- **Wife:** A married woman cannot adopt while her husband is alive. The right belongs solely to the husband.
    
- **Widow:** A widow can adopt a son **only if** she has the express authority or permission (**Anumati-patra**) from her husband.
    
    - **Bangladesh Position:** A widow in Bangladesh **cannot** adopt on her own volition. If her husband died without giving her permission (verbal or written), she has no power to adopt, even to save his soul. This strict Dayabhaga rule still applies here.
        

---

### 2. Capacity to Give in Adoption (The Giver)

The power to give a boy in adoption is exclusively parental.

- **The Father:** He has the primary and absolute right to give his son in adoption.
    
- **The Mother:** She cannot give her son in adoption while her husband is alive and capable of consenting, unless he has renounced the world or is insane. After the father's death, she can give the son in adoption (provided there is no prohibition from the husband).
    
- **Others:** No other person (brother, uncle, step-mother, or orphanage guardian) can give a child in adoption. An orphan cannot be adopted under strict Hindu law because there is no parent to "give" him.
    

---

### 3. Capacity to be Adopted (The Subject)

Who can be chosen as a son?

#### **A. Gender**

- **Must be a Male:** Since the object is to offer _Pinda_ (funeral cakes), only a son can be adopted. The adoption of a daughter is invalid in general Hindu law (unless supported by a specific custom, e.g., Naikins).
    

#### **B. Caste**

- **Same Caste:** The boy must belong to the **same caste** as the adopting father. A Brahmin cannot adopt a Sudra, and vice versa.
    

#### **C. Age**

- **General Rule:** The boy must be a minor.
    
- **Ceremonial Limit:** The adoption must take place before the boy's **Upanayana** (Sacred Thread) ceremony (for higher castes) or marriage (for Sudras). Once a boy is married, he generally cannot be adopted (unless there is a custom to the contrary).
    

#### **D. Relationship (Niyoga Rule)**

- **The Rule:** The boy must be one whose mother the adopting father could have legally married in her maiden state.
    
    - **Meaning:** You cannot adopt a boy who is the son of a woman you are prohibited from marrying (e.g., sister, daughter, or aunt).
        
    - **Result:** A man **cannot** adopt his **daughter's son**, **sister's son**, or **mother's sister's son**.
        
- **Exception for Sudras:** This prohibition does _not_ apply to Sudras. A Sudra in Bangladesh can validly adopt his sister's son.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Orphan" Problem:** A frequent trick question: _"A rich Hindu wants to adopt an orphan from a shelter. Is it valid?"_
> 
> - **Answer:** **No.** Under uncodified law, there must be a "giving" by a natural parent. An orphan has no parents to give him; thus, he cannot be adopted (Dattaka).
>     

> **Widow's Power (Anumati):** In exams, strictly apply the rule: **No Permission = No Adoption.** Do not use the Indian rule (where a widow can adopt freely).

> **Only Son:** Can a father give away his _only_ son?
> 
> - **Ancient Texts:** Vasistha prohibited it ("Let no man give or receive an only son").
>     
> - **Legal Position:** The Courts have held this text is _directory_ (moral advice), not _mandatory_ (legal prohibition). Therefore, the adoption of an **only son is VALID** (Factum Valet applies).
>
