## 7.3 Ceremony of Adoption ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** You must understand that adoption is not a "paper transaction." Unlike modern Western adoption which is a court process, Hindu adoption is a physical and religious act. A registered deed without the ceremony is void.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent problem question scenario: "A deed was signed, but the boy was not physically handed over. Is the adoption valid?" You need to know the answer is "No."
    

---

### Introduction

Adoption in Hindu Law is the "civil death" of a boy in his natural family and his "rebirth" in the adoptive family. Such a drastic change in status, which alters the line of inheritance, cannot be done secretly. Therefore, the law requires specific ceremonies to ensure **publicity** and distinct **proof** of the transfer.

Under the uncodified law of Bangladesh, the mere signing of a registered document (Deed of Adoption) is **not sufficient** to constitute a valid adoption. There must be an actual act of transfer.

### 1. The Essential Ceremony: Physical Giving and Taking

This is the operative part of the ceremony. Without this, there is no adoption.

- **The Act:** The natural parent (usually the father) must physically hand over the child to the adoptive parent, and the adoptive parent must physically receive the child.
    
- **The Declaration:** This act must be accompanied by a declaration (verbal or implied) that the child is being given in adoption and taken as a son.
    
- **Intent:** There must be a clear intention to transfer the child from one family to another. A mere temporary foster care arrangement does not amount to adoption.
    
- **Constructive Delivery:** If the boy is old enough (e.g., a teenager), he need not be physically lifted. However, there must be some overt act showing the transfer of power and custody.
    

### 2. The Religious Ceremony: _Datta Homam_

- **Definition:** _Datta Homam_ is the sacrifice of burning clarified butter (ghee) in the sacred fire, accompanied by the recitation of holy mantras. It invokes the gods to witness the acceptance of the son.
    
- **Necessity:**
    
    - **For "Twice-Born" Castes:** For the higher castes (Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas), the performance of _Datta Homam_ is generally considered **essential** to validate the adoption. Without it, the religious transfer is incomplete.
        
        - _Exception:_ If the adoptive father and the natural father belong to the _same Gotra_, _Datta Homam_ may be dispensed with.
            
    - **For Sudras:** The performance of _Datta Homam_ is **NOT required**. For Sudras (and by extension, many general communities in Bangladesh not claiming high-caste status), the simple giving and taking of the child is sufficient to complete the adoption.
        

### 3. Evidentiary Value of a Deed

In modern times, people often execute a "Deed of Adoption" on non-judicial stamp paper and register it.

- **Legal Status:** A deed is merely **evidence** of the adoption. It records the fact that the adoption took place.
    
- **Validity:** The deed _itself_ does not create the adoption.
    
    - If there is a Deed but **no giving and taking**, the adoption is **VOID**.
        
    - If there is **giving and taking** but no Deed, the adoption is **VALID**.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Deed" Trap:** In an exam problem, if it says: _"The father signed a registered adoption deed but died before the ceremony could take place."_
> 
> - **Verdict:** The adoption is **Invalid**.
>     
> - **Reasoning:** Adoption requires the _corporeal_ (physical) delivery of the child. A paper cannot deliver a child.
>     

> **Sudra Exception:** Always check the caste in the problem. If the parties are Sudras, _Datta Homam_ is not needed. If the problem is silent on caste, you can argue both sides but mention that the _Presumption_ in Bangladesh is often towards the necessity of rites unless low caste is proven.

> **Pollution:** The ceremony cannot be performed when the parties are in a state of ritual impurity (e.g., immediately after a death in the family).
