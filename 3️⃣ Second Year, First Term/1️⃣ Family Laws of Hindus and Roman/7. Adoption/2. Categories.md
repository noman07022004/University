## 7.2 Categories of Adoption ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** It is important to know that "Adoption" is not a single monolithic concept. Historically, there were many ways to acquire a son. Knowing the difference between the standard form (_Dattaka_) and the rare form (_Kritrima_) prevents legal confusion, especially regarding inheritance rights in the natural family.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While the _Dattaka_ form is the primary focus of exams, questions occasionally ask for a "Distinction between Dattaka and Kritrima forms of adoption" or short notes on the obsolete types of sons.
    

---

### Introduction

In the ancient Vedic society, the need for a son (for spiritual salvation and secular protection) was so intense that the law recognized various methods of acquiring one. The ancient _Smritis_ listed as many as **12 types of sons**. These included the legitimate son (_Aurasa_), the son of the wife by another man (_Kshetraja_), the son of an appointed daughter (_Putrika-putra_), and the son rejected by his parents (_Apaviddha_).

However, as society advanced and moral codes tightened, most of these forms became obsolete. In the modern uncodified Hindu Law applicable in Bangladesh, only **two forms** of adoption are legally recognized:

1. **Dattaka** (The Given Son).
    
2. **Kritrima** (The Made Son).
    

---

### 1. The Dattaka Form (The Given Son)

This is the prevalent and standard mode of adoption throughout Bangladesh and most of India. When we use the word "Adoption" in a general legal sense, we refer to the _Dattaka_ form.

- **Definition:** It is the adoption of a son by a gift from his natural parents to the adoptive parents.
    
- **Key Feature: Transplantation:** The central legal effect of Dattaka adoption is the complete **transplantation** of the boy.
    
    - He is severed from his natural family (civil death in the family of birth).
        
    - He is grafted into the adoptive family (rebirth in the family of adoption).
        
- **Rights:**
    
    - He loses all rights of inheritance in his natural family.
        
    - He gains all the rights of a natural-born son in the adoptive family, including the right to inherit collaterally (from uncles, etc.).
        
- **Formalities:** It requires the ceremony of actual giving and taking. For the "Twice-Born" castes, the religious rite of _Datta Homam_ is also generally required.
    

### 2. The Kritrima Form (The Made Son)

This is a rare and ancient form of adoption that is legally recognized today only in the **Mithila School** (a sub-school of Mitakshara). However, it is relevant in Bangladesh because families migrating from the Mithila region (Bihar/Nepal border) may still follow this custom.

- **Definition:** It is an adoption where a man "makes" a son by mutual consent with the adoptee.
    
- **Key Feature: Contractual:** Unlike Dattaka (which is a gift of a child), Kritrima is a contract between the adopter and the adoptee.
    
    - _Consent:_ The consent of the **adoptee** (the boy himself) is the most essential factor.
        
    - _Adult Adoption:_ Consequently, a Kritrima son must be an **adult** (or at least of an age of discretion) to give consent. A minor cannot be adopted in this form.
        
- **No Severance:** The Kritrima son does **not** lose his rights in his natural family. He keeps his original identity and inheritance rights there.
    
- **Limited Rights:** In the adoptive family, he inherits _only_ from the person who adopted him (the father). He does not inherit from the father's relatives (collaterals) or the father's wife.
    

---

### Comparison: Dattaka vs. Kritrima

|**Feature**|**Dattaka (Standard Adoption)**|**Kritrima (Mithila Form)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Prevalence**|Universal (All over Bangladesh).|Rare (Mithila region/families).|
|**Nature**|A religious gift of a child.|A personal contract/agreement.|
|**Consent**|Consent of natural parents is required.|Consent of the **adoptee** is required.|
|**Age of Son**|Must be a minor (generally).|Must be an adult (to give consent).|
|**Status**|Transferred from Natural to Adoptive family.|Belongs to **both** families.|
|**Inheritance**|Inherits from adoptive father and all relations.|Inherits **only** from the adoptive father.|
|**Rights in Natural Family**|**Lost completely.**|**Retained.**|

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Only Two" Rule:** In exams, if asked "How many types of adoption exist?", answer **Two** (Dattaka and Kritrima). Do not list the ancient 12 types unless asked for "Historical Development."

> **Bangladesh Context:** For 99% of problem questions in Bangladesh, you will apply the rules of **Dattaka** adoption. Kritrima is the exception. If the facts do not specify the form, **presume Dattaka**.

> **Adopting an Adult:** If a problem asks _"Can a Hindu man adopt a 25-year-old man?"_
> 
> - Under **Dattaka**: Generally **No** (unless custom allows).
>     
> - Under **Kritrima**: **Yes**, in fact, he _must_ be an adult.
