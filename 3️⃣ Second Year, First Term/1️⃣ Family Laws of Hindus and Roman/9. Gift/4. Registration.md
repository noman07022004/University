## 9.4 Registration ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the bridge between "Religious Intent" and "Legal Title." You must understand that a Hindu gift is not just a pious act; it is a secular legal transaction governed by the strict procedural rules of the _Transfer of Property Act_. Without registration, even the most solemn vow to gift land is legally worthless.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a definitive exam topic. Problem questions routinely feature scenarios where a donor hands over possession of land but fails to register a deed. You must be able to state clearly that such a transfer is void.
    

---

### Introduction

In the ancient Shastric law, the formalities of a gift (_Dan_) were simple: a declaration by the donor (_Sankalpa_) and physical delivery of the property (_Pradan_). Writing or registration was not required.

However, in modern Bangladesh, the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882** (specifically Section 123) has superseded these ancient rules. Today, the validity of a Hindu gift depends entirely on statutory compliance. The law draws a sharp distinction between the mode of transferring **Immovable Property** (Land/Buildings) and **Movable Property** (Cash/Jewelry).

### 1. Gift of Immovable Property (Mandatory Registration)

For any gift of immovable property, regardless of its value (even if it is worth 1 Taka), the law mandates strict formalities.

- **Section 123 of TPA:** A gift of immovable property must be effected by a **Registered Instrument** (Deed).
    
- **Requirements for a Valid Deed:**
    
    1. **Written & Signed:** The instrument must be in writing and signed by or on behalf of the donor.
        
    2. **Attested:** It must be attested by at least **two witnesses** who saw the donor sign.
        
    3. **Registered:** The document must be registered under the _Registration Act, 1908_.
        
- **Effect of Non-Registration:**
    
    - An oral gift of immovable property is **Void Ab Initio**.
        
    - Even if the donor hands over possession and the donee builds a house on the land, the legal title **does not pass** to the donee without a registered deed. The donor (or his heirs) can legally evict the donee.
        

### 2. Gift of Movable Property (Optional Registration)

For movable property (like ornaments, cars, cash, or furniture), the law provides two alternative modes of transfer.

- **Mode A: Registered Instrument:** The donor may choose to execute a registered deed, just as with land.
    
- **Mode B: Delivery of Possession:** Alternatively, the transfer may be effected simply by **delivery** of the property to the donee.
    
    - _How to Deliver:_ Delivery can be actual (handing over the object) or constructive (handing over the keys to a car).
        
- **Conclusion:** For movables, registration is **optional**. A father can gift a gold ring to his son simply by putting it on his finger; no paperwork is needed.
    

### 3. The Conflict: Ancient Custom vs. Modern Statute

- **The "Oral Gift" Myth:** Many students (and laypeople) incorrectly believe that because Hindu Law is "personal," ancient customs apply.
    
- **Legal Supremacy:** The _Transfer of Property Act_ is a general law that overrides personal law in procedural matters. Section 123 applies specifically to Hindus.
    
- **Precedent:** Courts in Bangladesh have consistently held that a gift of immovable property by a Hindu is invalid unless registered, notwithstanding any custom to the contrary.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **Hindu "Dan" vs. Muslim "Hiba":** This is the most crucial comparative point.
> 
> - **Muslim Law:** Historically, an oral _Hiba_ of land was valid if accompanied by delivery of possession. (Note: The Registration Act was amended in 2004 to make Heba registration mandatory too, but conceptually they differ).
>     
> - **Hindu Law:** Section 123 of TPA has _always_ applied. An oral gift of land by a Hindu has been void since 1882.
>     

> **Attestation is Key:** A gift deed is one of the few documents that _requires_ attestation by two witnesses to be valid (unlike a Sale Deed, where attestation is a formality for registration but not strictly for validity under TPA s.54).
> 
> - _Exam Trap:_ "Donor signed the deed and registered it, but no witnesses signed." $\rightarrow$ **Void Gift**.
>     

> **Registration Fees:** Mentioning that the registration requires payment of stamp duty adds practical value to your answer, showing you understand the administrative side of the law.
