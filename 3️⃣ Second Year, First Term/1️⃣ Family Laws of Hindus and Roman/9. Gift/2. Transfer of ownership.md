## 9.2 Transfer of Ownership ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the mechanical core of property law. You may have the _intention_ to gift, but without the correct legal _mode_ of transfer, the ownership does not move. Understanding the difference between "Oral Gift" (Ancient) and "Registered Gift" (Modern) is critical.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A massive area for problem questions. A standard scenario is: _"A father hands over the keys of a house to his daughter as a gift, but no deed is written."_ You must know that ownership has **not** transferred.
    

---

### Introduction

In Hindu Law, the "Transfer of Ownership" refers to the procedural mechanism by which the title of the property passes from the Donor to the Donee. In ancient times, the mere delivery of possession (_Sankalpa_ and _Sampradana_) was sufficient to transfer ownership.

However, in modern Bangladesh, the transfer of ownership via Gift is governed strictly by **Section 123 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882**. This statute overrides the ancient Hindu texts. Therefore, to determine _how_ ownership is transferred, we must look at the nature of the property: **Immovable** or **Movable**.

### 1. Transfer of Immovable Property

For land, houses, or benefits arising out of land, the law is strict. Ownership **cannot** be transferred by oral declaration or mere physical delivery.

- **The Statutory Rule (Sec 123 TPA):** A gift of immovable property must be effected by a **Registered Instrument** (Deed).
    
- **Requirements for the Deed:**
    
    1. **Signed:** It must be signed by or on behalf of the Donor.
        
    2. **Attested:** It must be attested by at least **two witnesses**.
        
    3. **Registered:** The document must be registered under the _Registration Act, 1908_.
        
- **Effect of Non-Registration:** If a Hindu makes an oral gift of land and even hands over possession to the donee, the ownership does **not** transfer. The donee remains a trespasser or licensee in the eyes of the law, and the donor (or his heirs) can reclaim the land.
    

### 2. Transfer of Movable Property

For assets like cash, jewelry, cars, or furniture, the law is more flexible. Ownership can be transferred in two ways:

- **Mode A: Registered Instrument:** The donor can execute a registered deed (same as above).
    
- **Mode B: Delivery of Possession:** The transfer can be effected by simple **delivery** of the property to the donee.
    
    - _Example:_ A father puts a gold necklace around his daughter's neck with the intention of gifting it. Ownership transfers immediately. No writing is needed.
        

### 3. The Conflict: Shastra vs. Statute

This is a vital concept for legal history and application in Bangladesh.

- **Ancient Hindu Law:** Stated that a gift is valid if there is _Sankalpa_ (intention) and _Pradan_ (delivery). Writing was not required even for land.
    
- **Modern Law:** The _Transfer of Property Act_ (TPA) generally supersedes personal laws in matters of property transfer procedure.
    
- **The Rule:**
    
    - **TP Act Applies:** In Bangladesh, the provisions of Section 123 of the TP Act apply to Hindus. Therefore, the ancient rule of "Oral Gift of Land" is abolished.
        
    - **Possession Rule:** While the TP Act requires registration, Hindu Law principles still emphasize **possession**. A gift where the donor retains possession and only gives a paper deed might be challenged as "sham" (lacking intention). However, strictly legally, registration completes the transfer of title.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Oral Gift" Trap:** In exams, never validate an oral gift of land for a Hindu.
> 
> - _Contrast:_ A Muslim **can** make an oral gift (_Hiba_) of land if possession is delivered.
>     
> - _Hindu:_ **Cannot**. He must register a deed.
>     
> - This distinction is a favorite of examiners.
>     

> **Delivery of Title Deeds:** Merely handing over the old title deeds of a house to the donee does **not** constitute a gift of the house. A _new_ deed must be written and registered.

> **Actionable Claim:** A gift of an "Actionable Claim" (e.g., a right to recover a debt or an insurance policy) must be made by an instrument in writing signed by the donor, but registration is not strictly mandatory under Sec 130 TPA (though advisable).
