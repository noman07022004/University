## 9.3 Consideration ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This is the defining line between a "Gift" (_Dan_) and a "Sale" (_Vikraya_). If you don't understand that a gift must be absolutely free of monetary return, you cannot legally classify a transaction.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent objective question or problem component. Questions often test whether "spiritual benefit" or "love and affection" counts as consideration (it does not).
    

---

### Introduction

The essence of a Gift (_Dan_) in Hindu Law, as well as under the _Transfer of Property Act, 1882_, is its **gratuitous** nature. "Gratuitous" means "without expectation of return." If property is transferred in exchange for money or money's worth, the transaction ceases to be a gift and becomes a **Sale** or **Exchange**.

Therefore, the **absence of consideration** is not just a feature; it is a mandatory condition for the validity of a gift.

### 1. Definition of Consideration

To understand what must be _absent_, we must define what "Consideration" means in this context.

- **Secular Meaning:** As per the Contract Act, consideration is a _quid pro quo_—something in return. It usually refers to money, goods, or services.
    
- **Rule for Gift:** For a valid gift, there must be **ZERO** monetary consideration. The transfer must be an act of pure liberality.
    

### 2. What is NOT Consideration? (Valid Motives)

Students often confuse the "motive" of the gift with "consideration." In Hindu Law, certain intangible returns are **not** treated as legal consideration, and thus, a transfer based on them remains a valid gift.

#### **A. Natural Love and Affection**

- If a father gives land to his daughter out of "love and affection," is that consideration?
    
- **Legal Position:** **No.** Love is a motive, not consideration. Therefore, the transfer is a valid gift.
    

#### **B. Spiritual or Religious Benefit (_Punya_)**

- If a widow gifts land to a priest or temple to secure "spiritual salvation" for her deceased husband, is that consideration?
    
- **Legal Position:** **No.** The expectation of spiritual merit in the afterlife is not "valuable consideration" in the eyes of the law. Such a transfer is a valid gift (_Dan_).
    
    - _Note:_ This is a unique feature of Hindu Law where "spiritual benefit" validates the act rather than invalidating it as a trade.
        

### 3. Difference between Gift and Sale

The presence of consideration changes the entire nature of the transaction.

|**Feature**|**Gift (Dan)**|**Sale (Vikraya)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Consideration**|**None** (Gratuitous).|**Price** (Money) is mandatory.|
|**Motive**|Love, Affection, Spiritual Merit.|Commercial gain / Profit.|
|**Applicable Law**|Section 122, TP Act.|Section 54, TP Act.|
|**Revocation**|Can be revoked in rare cases (e.g., fraud).|Generally Irrevocable once registered.|

### 4. Onerous Gifts (A Burdened Gift)

Is it possible to attach a burden (debt) to a gift?

- **Section 127, TP Act:** Yes. This is called an **Onerous Gift**.
    
- **The Rule:** If a gift comes with a burden (e.g., "I give you this house, but you must pay off the mortgage of 5 Lakhs"), the donee must accept the **entire** gift—he cannot take the house and reject the debt.
    
- **Consideration Analysis:** Even though the donee pays the debt, it is still treated as a form of gift (or a mixed transaction), provided the value of the property exceeds the debt.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Sham" Transaction:** A common problem scenario: _"A father 'sells' land to his son for 100 Taka, but the land is worth 1 Crore."_
> 
> - **Analysis:** This is often treated by courts as a **Gift** in the guise of a Sale. The "price" is illusory.
>     
> - **Why it matters:** If it's a Gift, it requires attestation by 2 witnesses. If it's a Sale, it does not.
>     

> **Services as Consideration:** If a person transfers land to a servant in exchange for "past services," it might be argued as a "Remunerative Gift." However, strictly under TPA, if it is a payment for service, it is not a pure gift. In exams, assume "Love and Affection" = Gift.
