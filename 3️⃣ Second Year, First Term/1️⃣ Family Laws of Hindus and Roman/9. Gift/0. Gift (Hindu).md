## 9. Gift (_Dan_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** "Gift" is one of the primary modes of transferring property. Understanding how a Hindu _Dan_ differs from a Muslim _Hiba_ (especially regarding registration and delivery of possession) is a fundamental comparative legal concept in Bangladesh.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Problem questions frequently test the validity of a gift. Common scenarios include: "Can a father gift the entire ancestral property?" or "Is an oral gift of land valid if possession is delivered?" (Answer: No, registration is mandatory).


---

### Introduction

In Hindu Law, a Gift is known as **Dan**. It is defined as the renunciation of property by one person (Donor) in favor of another (Donee) without any consideration. Ancient texts regarded _Dan_ not just as a legal transfer but as a pious act (_Ishta_) leading to spiritual merit.

However, the law of Gift in Bangladesh is no longer governed solely by the ancient Shastras. It is heavily regulated by the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882**. While the _substantive_ right to gift comes from Hindu Law, the _procedural_ formalities (like registration) are governed by the statutory law.

---

### 9.1 Elements, Consideration, and Transfer of Interest

For a gift to be valid, specific elements must be present. These elements combine the ancient requirements with modern statutory rules.

#### 1. The Parties

- **The Donor (Giver):** Must be a person of **sound mind** and **major** (18+ years). He must have the legal title to the property.
    
    - _Dayabhaga Rule:_ A father has absolute power to gift both ancestral and self-acquired property. A coparcener can gift his share of the joint family property.
        
    - _Widow:_ A widow holding a "Widow's Estate" generally cannot gift the property except for "Legal Necessity" or religious acts conducive to her husband's spiritual welfare.
        
- **The Donee (Receiver):** Any person capable of holding property can be a donee. A gift can be made to a minor, but it must be accepted on their behalf by a guardian.
    

#### 2. Subject Matter

- The property must be **existing** property. A gift of "future property" is void.
    
- It can be movable or immovable.
    

#### 3. Transfer of Interest (Absence of Consideration)

- **Gratuitous:** The essence of a gift is that it must be **voluntary** and **without consideration**.
    
- **Ownership Transfer:** The donor must completely divest himself of all ownership rights and transfer them to the donee immediately. If the donor keeps control (e.g., "I give it to you, but I will keep the title deed until I die"), it is not a valid gift.
    

#### 4. Acceptance

- The gift must be **accepted** by the donee during the lifetime of the donor. If the donee dies before acceptance, the gift is void.
    

---

### 9.2 Transfer of Ownership: Registration and Possession

This is the most critical area where statutory law overrides traditional law.

#### A. Registration (Subtopic 9.4)

- **Ancient Rule:** Under pure Hindu Law, an oral gift accompanied by delivery of possession was valid. Writing was not required.
    
- **Modern Rule (Bangladesh):** Section 123 of the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882** applies to Hindus in Bangladesh.
    
    - **Immovable Property:** A gift of immovable property (land, house) must be effected by a **Registered Instrument** signed by the donor and attested by at least two witnesses.
        
    - **Consequence:** An oral gift of land by a Hindu is **VOID**, even if possession is handed over. This is a major difference from Muslim _Hiba_, which can be oral.
        

#### B. Possession (Subtopic 9.5)

- **Movable Property:** For movables (money, jewelry), the gift can be completed either by a registered deed OR by **Delivery of Possession**.
    
- **Immovable Property:** While registration is the primary requirement, delivery of possession is still relevant as proof of acceptance. However, registration without possession is valid, whereas possession without registration is invalid for land.
    

---

### 9.3 Perpetuity and Conditions

- **Rule Against Perpetuity:** A gift cannot be made to tie up property forever (e.g., "To A, then to his son, then to his son's son..."). Hindu gifts are subject to **Section 14** of the Transfer of Property Act, which restricts transfers that delay vesting beyond the life of living persons plus the minority of an unborn person.
    
- **Gift to Unborn Person:**
    
    - _Ancient Law:_ Gift to an unborn person was void.
        
    - _Modern Law:_ Valid, provided it complies with statutory rules (Section 13, TPA), meaning the interest must extend to the whole of the remaining interest of the donor.
        

---

### 9.4 Termination (Revocation)

Can a gift be taken back? (Subtopic 9.8)

- **General Rule:** A gift, once accepted, is **Irrevocable**. The donor cannot take it back just because he changed his mind or quarreled with the donee.
    
- **Exceptions (Section 126, TPA):**
    
    1. **Agreement:** If the donor and donee agreed _at the time of the gift_ that it would be revoked on the happening of a specified event (which does not depend on the donor's will).
        
        - _Example:_ "I give this land to you, but if you die before me without children, it returns to me." (Valid).
            
    2. **Rescission (Fraud/Coercion):** A gift can be revoked on any ground on which a contract can be rescinded (e.g., if the signature was obtained by fraud or undue influence).
        
    3. **Will of Donor:** A gift revocable at the mere "pleasure/will" of the donor is **Void**.
        

---

### 9.5 _Donatio Mortis Causa_ (Gift in Contemplation of Death)

This is a unique hybrid concept—half gift, half will.

- **Definition:** A gift made by a person who is ill and **expects to die shortly** of his illness, delivering possession of movable property to another to keep as a gift _in case_ the donor dies.
    
- **Applicability in Bangladesh:** Section 191 of the **Succession Act, 1925** recognizes this concept.
    
- **Conditions:**
    
    1. Must be **Movable Property** only. (Land cannot be given this way because of strict registration rules).
        
    2. Must be made in contemplation of death.
        
    3. Must be conditional: It takes effect only if the donor dies.
        
- **Revocability:** It is **revocable**. If the donor recovers from the illness, the gift is automatically void and the property returns to him.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Hiba" vs. "Dan" Distinction:** This is the most common exam trap.
> 
> - **Muslim Hiba:** Oral gift of land is **Valid** (if 3 essentials met).
>     
> - **Hindu Dan:** Oral gift of land is **Void** (Registration is mandatory under TPA s.123).
>     
> - _Tip:_ If a problem says "A Hindu father orally gifted land to his daughter at her marriage," the gift is invalid. She gets no title.
>     

> **Subject Matter:** A "Widow's Estate" cannot be gifted away by the widow (except for necessity). If she gifts it to her brother, the reversioners (husband's heirs) can recover it after her death.

> **Revocation:** Remember that "Ungratefulness" is **not** a ground for revoking a gift in court. Even if the son beats the father after receiving the house, the father cannot revoke the gift unless he reserved a right of maintenance in the deed itself.
