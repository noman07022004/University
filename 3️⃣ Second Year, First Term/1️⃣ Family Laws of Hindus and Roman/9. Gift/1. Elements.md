## 9.1 Elements of Gift (_Dan_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** To identify if a transaction is a "Gift" or a "Sale" or a "Trust," you must strictly identify its elements. If any element (like the mental capacity of the donor or the existence of the property) is missing, the entire transaction is void.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A foundational exam topic. Problem questions often involve a "Gift to an unborn person" or a "Gift by a minor," requiring you to check the validity against these specific elements.
    

---

### Introduction

In Hindu Law, a Gift is technically known as **Dan**. It is defined as the creation of another person's proprietary right after the extinction of one's own proprietary right in the subject matter, without any consideration.

For a gift to be legally valid in Bangladesh, it must satisfy the requirements of both the ancient Shastric law and the modern **Transfer of Property Act, 1882** (Section 122). A gift is not merely a transfer of a thing; it is a contract consisting of specific constituent elements.

---

### 1. The Parties (Donor and Donee)

A gift essentially requires two distinct parties. A person cannot give a gift to themselves.

#### **A. The Donor (The Giver)**

The person giving the gift must have the legal capacity to do so.

- **Capacity:** The donor must be a person of **sound mind** and must have attained **majority** (18 years). A gift by a minor or a lunatic is **Void**.
    
- **Right to Dispose:** The donor must have the legal title/ownership of the property.
    
    - **Father’s Power:** Under the Dayabhaga school (Bangladesh), a father has absolute power to gift away all his property (ancestral or self-acquired) without the consent of his sons.
        
    - **Coparcener’s Power:** A coparcener (under Dayabhaga) can validly gift his specific share of the joint family property.
        
    - **Widow’s Limitation:** A widow holding a "Widow's Estate" cannot gift the corpus of the property, except for religious acts conducive to her husband's spiritual welfare.
        

#### **B. The Donee (The Receiver)**

The person accepting the gift.

- **Capacity:** Any person is capable of being a donee. This includes a minor, a lunatic, or even a deity (Idol).
    
    - _Note:_ If the donee is a minor or lunatic, the gift must be accepted on their behalf by a competent guardian.
        
- **Existence:** The donee must be a generally **existing person** at the time of the gift.
    
    - _Gift to Unborn Person:_ Historically void in Hindu Law. However, under modern statutes (Transfer of Property Act, Sec 13), a gift can be made to an unborn person provided a "prior interest" is created for a living person and the absolute interest passes to the unborn person upon birth.
        

### 2. Subject Matter (The Property)

What can be gifted?

- **Existing Property:** The property must be in existence at the time of the gift. A gift of **"future property"** (e.g., "I give you the crops that will grow in my field next year") is **Void** (Void _ab initio_).
    
- **Transferability:** The property must be transferable. One cannot gift a mere "right to sue" or a "chance of an heir succeeding to an estate" (_Spes Successionis_).
    
- **Types:** It can be movable (money, jewelry) or immovable (land, house).
    

### 3. Voluntariness (Free Consent)

- The gift must be made **voluntarily**.
    
- If the consent of the donor is obtained by coercion, undue influence (e.g., by a spiritual guru), fraud, or misrepresentation, the gift is **Voidable** at the option of the donor.
    

### 4. Absence of Consideration

This is the defining feature that separates a "Gift" from a "Sale" or "Exchange."

- **Gratuitous:** The transfer must be made **without consideration** (money or money's worth).
    
- **Spiritual Benefit:** Interestingly, "spiritual merit" or "love and affection" is _not_ considered "consideration" in the legal sense. Therefore, a transfer made for "love" is a valid gift. If money is exchanged, it becomes a Sale.
    

### 5. Acceptance

A gift is a bilateral transaction, not unilateral. Giving is not enough; taking is essential.

- **Requirement:** The gift must be **accepted** by or on behalf of the donee.
    
- **Timing:** The acceptance must be made:
    
    1. During the **lifetime** of the donor.
        
    2. While the donor is still **capable** of giving (i.e., before he loses his mind or becomes insolvent).
        
- **Consequence:** If the donee dies before accepting the gift, the gift is **Void**.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

> **The "Future Property" Trap:** A common objective question: _"X gifts Y the inheritance he expects to receive when his father dies."_
> 
> - **Result:** **Void.** This is a gift of _Spes Successionis_ (expectation of succession) and future property. You cannot gift what you do not yet own.
>     

> **Donation to an Idol:** A gift to a Hindu Idol (_Debuttar_) is valid. The Idol is considered a "Juristic Person" (a legal entity) capable of holding property. The acceptance is made by the _Shebait_ (manager/priest) on behalf of the Idol.

> **Acceptance by Silence:** Acceptance does not always need to be a formal speech. Mere silence or taking possession of the property acts as implied acceptance.
