### Introduction

**Sampling** is the process of selecting a subset of individuals from a population to estimate characteristics of the whole population.

In a perfect world, we would measure every individual (a **Census**). However, due to constraints on time, money, and feasibility, we must rely on samples. This section addresses the theoretical foundations that allow a small sample to represent a large population and covers a specific technique used for estimating populations that are impossible to count directly (like wild animals or software bugs).

---

### 1. Random Sampling Theory

This subtopic covers the "Golden Rule" of data collection: **Randomness**. Without randomness, statistical probability laws (like the Central Limit Theorem) simply do not apply.

- Simple Random Sampling (SRS):
    
    The purest form of sampling. A sample of size $n$ is selected such that every possible sample of size $n$ has an equal probability of being chosen.
    
    - _Method:_ Assign every member of the population a number and use a Random Number Generator (RNG) to select them.
        
    - _Theory:_ SRS minimizes systematic bias. It ensures that the sample mean $\bar{x}$ is an **unbiased estimator** of the population mean $\mu$.
        
- Sampling Error:
    
    Even with perfect random sampling, $\bar{x}$ will rarely equal $\mu$ exactly. This difference ($\bar{x} - \mu$) is called Sampling Error.
    
    - _Note:_ This is not a "mistake." It is the natural variation expected by chance. We quantify this error using the **Standard Error** ($SE = \sigma / \sqrt{n}$).
        
- Knowledge Gap Fill: Other Sampling Methods
    
    To understand SRS, you must understand what it is not:
    
    - **Stratified Sampling:** Dividing population into subgroups (strata) and sampling from each. (More precise than SRS).
        
    - **Cluster Sampling:** Dividing population into clusters (e.g., schools) and picking entire clusters. (Cheaper, but less precise).
        
    - **Convenience Sampling:** Picking whoever is easiest to reach. (Statistically invalid).

### 2. Capture/Recapture

This is a specialized sampling method used to estimate the **total size ($N$)** of a population where individuals are mobile or hidden, making a census impossible.

- **The Scenario:** How do you count the number of fish in a lake? You cannot drain the lake. Instead, you use probability.
    
- **The Method (Lincoln-Petersen Index):**
    
    1. **Capture:** Catch a first sample of size **$M$**.
        
    2. **Tag:** Mark these animals (tags, paint, etc.) and release them back into the population to mix.
        
    3. **Recapture:** Later, catch a second sample of size **$n$**.
        
    4. **Count:** Count how many in this second sample are already marked (**$m$**).
        
- The Formula:
    
    The proportion of marked animals in the second sample should equal the proportion of marked animals in the entire population.
    
    $$\frac{m}{n} = \frac{M}{N}$$
    
    Rearranging to solve for the Total Population ($N$):
    
    $$\hat{N} = \frac{M \times n}{m}$$
    
- Assumptions (Crucial for Exams):
    
    The formula is only valid if:
    
    1. The population is **Closed** (no births, deaths, immigration, or emigration during the study).
        
    2. Tags do not fall off or make the animal more likely to be eaten/caught.
        
    3. The marked animals have enough time to mix randomly back into the population.

### Important Notes, Tips & Facts

1. **Finite Population Correction (FPC):**
    
    - In Topic 1 (Standard Deviation), we assumed the population was infinite.
        
    - If you sample a significant portion of a small population (specifically, if $n > 5\%$ of $N$), you must adjust your Standard Error using the FPC factor:
        
        $$FPC = \sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}}$$
        
    - _Effect:_ This reduces your standard error (because you have measured a large chunk of the reality).
        
2. **Bias vs. Error:**
    
    - **Sampling Error:** Random, unavoidable, decreases as $n$ increases.
        
    - **Non-Sampling Error (Bias):** Systematic mistakes (e.g., broken survey questions, non-response bias). Increasing sample size does _not_ fix this; it just gives you a more "precisely wrong" answer.
        
3. **Capture/Recapture in IT:** This method is also used in software engineering to estimate the number of bugs in a code. Two independent testers check the code; the overlap in bugs found allows you to estimate the total bugs remaining.
