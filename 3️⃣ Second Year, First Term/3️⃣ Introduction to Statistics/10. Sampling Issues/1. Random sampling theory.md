---

### **1. The Goal of Sampling Theory**

Sampling theory deals with the statistical bridge between a **Population** (the Total Reality) and a **Sample** (the Observed Subset).

- **The Problem:** We want to know a parameter (e.g., Population Mean $\mu$), but measuring the entire population (a **Census**) is usually too expensive, time-consuming, or destructive.
    
- **The Solution:** We measure a sample statistic ($\bar{x}$) and use probability theory to estimate how close it is to $\mu$.
    
- **The Requirement:** For the laws of probability (like the Central Limit Theorem) to apply, the sample must be drawn using a **probability mechanism** (randomness). Convenience sampling (picking whoever is nearby) invalidates statistical inference.
    

---

### **2. Simple Random Sampling (SRS)**

This is the purest form of sampling and the theoretical baseline for all statistics courses.

- **Definition:** A sampling method where every possible sample of size $n$ has an **equal probability** of being selected from the population $N$.
    
- **Method:**
    
    1. Assign a unique number to every member of the population (Sampling Frame).
        
    2. Use a Random Number Generator (RNG) or table to select $n$ unique numbers.
        
- Theoretical Implication: SRS ensures that the sample mean $\bar{x}$ is an Unbiased Estimator of the population mean $\mu$.
    
    $$E[\bar{x}] = \mu$$
    
    (This means that if you took infinite samples and averaged their results, you would hit the exact truth).
    

---

### **3. Sampling Error vs. Non-Sampling Error (Bias)**

It is critical to distinguish between "Error" (Randomness) and "Mistake" (Bias).

- **Sampling Error:**
    
    - The natural, expected difference between the sample statistic and the population parameter ($\bar{x} - \mu$) that occurs simply because we didn't measure everyone.
        
    - _Control:_ Reduced by **increasing sample size ($n$)**.
        
- **Non-Sampling Error (Systematic Bias):**
    
    - Flaws in the design that push the result in one specific direction.
        
    - _Examples:_
        
        - **Selection Bias:** Using a phone book to survey people (excludes those without phones).
            
        - **Non-Response Bias:** People who refuse to answer surveys often think differently than those who do.
            
    - _Control:_ Increasing $n$ does **not** fix this. It just gives you a "precisely wrong" answer.
        

---

### **4. The Finite Population Correction (FPC)**

In most introductory statistics (and Topic 4.3), we assume the population is infinite (or at least huge compared to the sample). We assume sampling is effectively "with replacement" (independent trials).

However, if you sample a **large fraction** of a **finite population** (specifically, if $n > 5\%$ of $N$), you are depleting the population. The trials become dependent (Hypergeometric logic), and the variance _decreases_ (because there are fewer unmeasured people left to surprise you).

You must adjust the Standard Error formula by multiplying it by the **FPC Factor**:

$$SE_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \times \sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}}$$

- **Effect:** The term $\sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}}$ is always less than 1. It **reduces** the standard error, making your confidence intervals narrower (more precise).
    
- _Note:_ If $N$ is huge (e.g., 1,000,000) and $n$ is small (100), the FPC is $\approx 1$, so we ignore it.

### **5. Knowledge Gap Fill: Other Probability Sampling Designs**

While SRS is the theory standard, real-world research often uses variations to improve efficiency.

1. **Stratified Random Sampling:**
    
    - Divide population into subgroups (strata) like Male/Female, then sample randomly from _each_ group.
        
    - _Theory:_ This reduces sampling error (Standard Error) because it ensures perfect representation of key groups.
        
2. **Cluster Sampling:**
    
    - Divide population into clusters (e.g., Schools), then randomly select _entire clusters_ to survey.
        
    - _Theory:_ Cheaper/easier, but **increases** sampling error (higher variance) because people in the same cluster tend to be similar.
        
3. **Systematic Sampling:**
    
    - Select every $k$-th person (e.g., every 10th file in a drawer).
        
    - _Theory:_ Statistically equivalent to SRS _unless_ there is a hidden pattern in the list (periodicity).
        

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **The "Random" Misconception:** "Random" does not mean "Haphazard." Walking down the street and picking 10 people is _not_ random sampling (it is Convenience Sampling). Random sampling requires a formal randomized mechanism (like dice or a computer).
    
- **Precision vs. Accuracy:**
    
    - **Precision:** Low Standard Error (tight cluster of data). Achieved by high $n$.
        
    - **Accuracy:** Unbiased estimator (centered on the truth). Achieved by proper Random Sampling design.
        
    - _Goal:_ We want both.
