# 4.5 Hypergeometric Distribution ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** While the Binomial distribution assumes independence (sampling _with_ replacement), the real world often involves sampling _without_ replacement (e.g., dealing cards, selecting a committee, quality control testing). The Hypergeometric distribution models these dependent events.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions specifically asking for Hypergeometric calculations are less frequent than Binomial ones because the formula is computationally heavy. However, identifying _when_ to use it (small population, no replacement) is a key conceptual skill.
    

---

### **1. The Scenario: Sampling Without Replacement**

The defining characteristic of the Hypergeometric distribution is that the probability of success changes with each draw.

- **Context:** Imagine a deck of 52 cards.
    
    - Probability of an Ace on the 1st draw = $4/52$.
        
    - If you draw an Ace and _don't put it back_, the probability of an Ace on the 2nd draw = $3/51$.
        
    - Because the denominator changes ($N \to N-1$), the trials are **Dependent**. This violates the "Independence" condition of the Binomial distribution.
        

---

### **2. The Parameters**

We define the setup using three numbers:

- **$N$:** The total size of the population.
    
- **$K$:** The total number of "Success" items in the population.
    
- **$n$:** The sample size drawn (without replacement).
    
- **$x$:** The number of successes observed in the sample (Random Variable).
    

---

### **3. The Probability Mass Function (Formula)**

The probability of getting exactly $x$ successes is calculated using combinations.

$$P(X=x) = \frac{\binom{K}{x} \binom{N-K}{n-x}}{\binom{N}{n}}$$

- **Numerator Part 1 $\binom{K}{x}$:** Choose $x$ successes from the available $K$.
    
- **Numerator Part 2 $\binom{N-K}{n-x}$:** Choose the remaining $(n-x)$ failures from the available $(N-K)$ failures.
    
- **Denominator $\binom{N}{n}$:** The total number of ways to choose a sample of size $n$ from population $N$.
    

---

### **4. Mean and Variance**

- Mean (Expected Value):
    
    $$\mu = n \left( \frac{K}{N} \right)$$
    
    - _Intuition:_ This is effectively $n \times p$, where $p$ is the initial proportion of successes in the population.
        
- Variance:
    
    $$\sigma^2 = n \left( \frac{K}{N} \right) \left( \frac{N-K}{N} \right) \left( \frac{N-n}{N-1} \right)$$
    
    - _Note:_ This looks like the Binomial variance $np(1-p)$ multiplied by an extra term: $\frac{N-n}{N-1}$.
        
    - **Finite Population Correction Factor (FPC):** This term $\frac{N-n}{N-1}$ accounts for the shrinking population. As you sample more (as $n \to N$), the variance drops to zero because you have measured almost everyone.
        

---

### **5. Binomial Approximation**

Calculating combinations for large numbers (e.g., $N=10,000$) is tedious.

- **Rule of Thumb:** If the sample size $n$ is less than **5%** of the population $N$ ($n/N < 0.05$), the trials are "almost" independent.
    
- **Action:** You can approximate the Hypergeometric distribution using the **Binomial Distribution** with $p = K/N$. The error will be negligible.
    

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- Range of $x$: Be careful with the bounds. The number of successes $x$ cannot exceed the sample size $n$, nor can it exceed the total available successes $K$.
    
    $$max(0, n - (N-K)) \le x \le min(n, K)$$
    
- **Exam Identifier:** Look for the phrase **"Without Replacement"** or scenarios involving **"Committees"** or **"Batches."**
    
    - _Example:_ "A batch of 50 parts contains 5 defectives. A sample of 10 is tested..." $\rightarrow$ Hypergeometric.
        
- **Fisher's Exact Test:** The Hypergeometric distribution is the mathematical engine behind Fisher's Exact Test (Topic 5), used for analyzing $2 \times 2$ contingency tables with small samples.
