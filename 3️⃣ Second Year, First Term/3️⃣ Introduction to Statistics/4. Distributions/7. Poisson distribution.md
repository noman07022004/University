# 4.7 Poisson Distribution ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the second most important discrete distribution (after Binomial). It models "arrival" processes—how often events happen over time or space. It is foundational for Queuing Theory, Telecommunications, and Epidemiology.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A staple of statistics exams. You will likely face a word problem describing a rate (e.g., "calls per hour") and be asked to calculate the probability of a specific count.
    

---

### **1. The Poisson Process**

The Poisson distribution describes the number of events occurring within a **fixed interval of time or space**. It is often called the law of "rare events."

- **Examples:**
    
    - Number of emails received in one hour.
        
    - Number of potholes per kilometer of road.
        
    - Number of decay events from a radioactive source per second.
        
    - Number of customers arriving at a bank teller in 10 minutes.
        

---

### **2. The Poisson Conditions**

For a random variable to follow a Poisson distribution, the process must meet specific criteria (often called the Poisson Assumptions):

1. **Independence:** The occurrence of one event does not affect the probability of another event occurring (e.g., one car crash doesn't make the next one more likely).
    
2. **Homogeneity (Constant Rate):** The average rate at which events occur ($\lambda$) is constant throughout the interval.
    
3. **Non-Simultaneity:** Two events cannot occur at the exact same instant (the probability of two events in a tiny interval approaches zero).
    

---

### **3. The Parameter: Lambda ($\lambda$)**

The Poisson distribution has only one parameter: $\lambda$ (Lambda).

- **Definition:** $\lambda$ represents the **average number of events** expected in the given interval.
    
- **Scalability:** $\lambda$ is proportional to the size of the interval.
    
    - _Example:_ If a store gets 12 customers per _hour_ ($\lambda = 12$), then in _15 minutes_, the rate is $\lambda = 3$.
        

---

### **4. The Probability Mass Function (Formula)**

The probability of observing exactly $x$ events in an interval is given by:

$$P(X=x) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{x!}$$

- $e$: Euler's number ($\approx 2.71828$).
    
- $x$: The specific number of successes we want to find (must be a non-negative integer: $0, 1, 2...$).
    
- $\lambda$: The average rate.
    

---

### **5. Mean and Variance (The Unique Property)**

The defining mathematical characteristic of the Poisson distribution is that the mean and variance are identical.

- Mean (Expected Value):
    
    $$E[X] = \lambda$$
    
- Variance:
    
    $$Var(X) = \lambda$$
    
- Standard Deviation:
    
    $$\sigma = \sqrt{\lambda}$$
    
- **Diagnostic Utility:** If you are analyzing count data and calculate that $s^2 \approx \bar{x}$, it is strong evidence that the data follows a Poisson distribution. If Variance > Mean, the data is "Overdispersed" (suggesting Negative Binomial might be better).
    

---

### **6. Poisson Approximation of Binomial**

The Poisson distribution is actually a limiting case of the Binomial distribution.

- **When to use:** If $n$ is very large ($n \to \infty$) and $p$ is very small ($p \to 0$), calculating binomial factorials becomes impossible.
    
- **The Method:** Set $\lambda = np$. You can then use the Poisson formula to approximate the Binomial probability.
    
- **Rule of Thumb:** Use this approximation if $n \ge 100$ and $np \le 10$.
    

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **Unit Consistency:** The most common mistake in exams is **mismatched units**.
    
    - _Problem:_ "Calls arrive at 6 per hour. What is the probability of 2 calls in 10 minutes?"
        
    - _Error:_ Using $\lambda = 6$.
        
    - _Correction:_ You must adjust $\lambda$ to the new interval. $\lambda = 6 \times (10/60) = 1$. Use $\lambda = 1$ in the formula.
        
- **Cumulative Probability:** Questions often ask for "at least 1" or "at most 3."
    
    - "At most 3" = $P(0) + P(1) + P(2) + P(3)$.
        
    - "At least 1" = $1 - P(0)$.
        
- **Shape:**
    
    - Small $\lambda$ (e.g., 1): Strongly right-skewed.
        
    - Large $\lambda$ (e.g., 10+): Approaches a Normal Distribution (Symmetric).
