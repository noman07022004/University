

# 5.3 Confidence Intervals for Proportions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** While Hypothesis Testing (Topic 5.2) gives a "Yes/No" answer to "Is there a difference?", Confidence Intervals (CIs) answer the more important scientific question: _"How big is the difference?"_ Understanding why we do **not** pool variances here (unlike in the Z-test) is a critical nuance.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a high-yield calculation topic. You will often be asked to construct a 95% CI for the difference between two groups and interpret whether the result implies a significant difference.
    

---

### **1. Introduction: From Testing to Estimation**

In Subtopic 5.2, we calculated a Z-score to reject or fail to reject the Null Hypothesis. However, a P-value doesn't tell us the **magnitude** of the effect.

- _Example:_ "Drug A is significantly better than Drug B ($P < 0.05$)."
    
- _Question:_ "Okay, but how much better? Is it 1% better or 50% better?"
    

A **Confidence Interval (CI)** provides a range of plausible values for the true population difference ($p_1 - p_2$) with a specified level of confidence (usually 95% or 99%).

---

### **2. The Standard Error (The "No Pooling" Rule)**

This is the most common mistake students make in this topic.

- **In Hypothesis Testing ($H_0: p_1 = p_2$):** We assume the groups are equal, so we calculate a **Pooled Proportion** ($\hat{p}$) to estimate the variance.
    
- **In Confidence Intervals:** We do **NOT** assume $H_0$ is true. We are trying to estimate the _actual_ difference, which we suspect is not zero. Therefore, we must use the **individual sample proportions** to calculate the Standard Error.
    

Standard Error for the Difference:

$$SE = \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1 (1 - \hat{p}_1)}{n_1} + \frac{\hat{p}_2 (1 - \hat{p}_2)}{n_2}}$$

---

### **3. The Formula (Wald Interval)**

To construct the interval for the difference in population proportions ($p_1 - p_2$), we use the point estimate plus/minus the margin of error.

$$(\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2) \pm Z_{\alpha/2} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1 (1 - \hat{p}_1)}{n_1} + \frac{\hat{p}_2 (1 - \hat{p}_2)}{n_2}}$$

- **$\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2$:** The observed difference in the samples (Point Estimate).
    
- **$Z_{\alpha/2}$:** The critical value from the Standard Normal distribution.
    
    - For 95% Confidence: $Z = 1.96$.
        
    - For 99% Confidence: $Z = 2.576$.
        
- **The Margin of Error (ME):** The entire term after the $\pm$ sign.
    

---

### **4. Interpretation: The "Zero" Rule**

Once you calculate the interval (e.g., $[ -0.02, 0.08 ]$), how do you interpret it in the context of "Statistical Significance"?

You must check if the interval contains the value **0**.

1. **Interval includes 0 (e.g., -0.02 to +0.08):**
    
    - _Meaning:_ It is plausible that the true difference is zero.
        
    - _Conclusion:_ There is **No Statistically Significant Difference** between the groups (at that confidence level). This corresponds to "Fail to Reject $H_0$."
        
2. **Interval does NOT include 0 (e.g., 0.05 to 0.15):**
    
    - _Meaning:_ 0 is not a plausible value. The difference is strictly positive.
        
    - _Conclusion:_ Group 1 is **Significantly Higher** than Group 2. This corresponds to "Reject $H_0$."
        
3. **Interval is entirely Negative (e.g., -0.20 to -0.10):**
    
    - _Meaning:_ Group 1 is **Significantly Lower** than Group 2.
        

---

### **5. Knowledge Gap Fill: The "Plus Four" Method**

The standard formula above (Wald Interval) is known to be inaccurate when sample sizes are small or proportions are extreme (near 0 or 1).

- _Academic Solution:_ The **Agresti-Caffo Interval** (or "Plus Four" method).
    
- Method: Add 1 success and 1 failure to Sample 1, and 1 success and 1 failure to Sample 2 (adding 4 "fake" observations total). Then use the standard formula.
    
    $$\tilde{p} = \frac{x+1}{n+2}$$
    
- _Result:_ This simple trick drastically improves the accuracy of the confidence interval coverage for small samples.
    

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **Rounding:** Do not round intermediate steps. Proportions are sensitive. If $\hat{p} = 0.512$, do not round to 0.5. Keep 3-4 decimal places until the final answer.
    
- **Compatibility:** A 95% Confidence Interval gives the same conclusion as a Two-Tailed Hypothesis Test at $\alpha = 0.05$. If the test rejects $H_0$, the CI will not contain 0.
    
- **Notation:**
    
    - $\hat{q} = 1 - \hat{p}$.
        
    - Some texts write the formula as: $(\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2) \pm Z \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1\hat{q}_1}{n_1} + \frac{\hat{p}_2\hat{q}_2}{n_2}}$.
