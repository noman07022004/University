

# 5.2 Chi-squared and z-score tests for equality of two proportions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the "bread and butter" of comparative statistics. If you want to know if a vaccine works (Treatment vs. Control) or if a marketing campaign increased sales (Before vs. After), this is the math you use.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is one of the most common calculation questions in Section A exams. You must know _when_ to pool your variance (Z-test) and _how_ to calculate Expected counts (Chi-squared).
    

---

### **1. The Two Approaches**

When sample sizes are **large** (typically $n > 30$ and expected counts $\ge 5$), we move away from Fisher's Exact Test and rely on asymptotic approximations. We have two mathematically related ways to test the Null Hypothesis $H_0: p_1 = p_2$.

1. **The Z-Score Test:** Focuses on the _difference between proportions_ ($\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2$).
    
2. **The Chi-Squared ($\chi^2$) Test:** Focuses on the _difference between counts_ (Observed vs. Expected).
    

- **Crucial Fact:** For a $2 \times 2$ table, these tests are identical. Mathematically, **$Z^2 = \chi^2$**.
    

---

### **2. The Z-Score Test (Two-Sample z-test)**

This test relies on the Normal Approximation of the Binomial distribution.

- **Hypotheses:**
    
    - $H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 0$ (No difference).
        
    - $H_1: p_1 - p_2 \neq 0$ (Difference exists).
        
- The Pooled Proportion ($\hat{p}$):
    
    This is the most critical conceptual step. Since $H_0$ assumes the two groups are identical, we combine them into one "super-sample" to estimate the true proportion.
    
    $$\hat{p} = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2}$$
    
    - $x$: Number of successes.
        
    - $n$: Sample size.
        
    - _Why Pool?_ It provides a more precise estimate of the standard error under the Null assumption.
        
- The Test Statistic:
    
    $$Z = \frac{(\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2) - 0}{\sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p}) \left( \frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2} \right)}}$$
    
    - Numerator: The observed difference.
        
    - Denominator: The Standard Error (SE) using the **pooled** $\hat{p}$.
        

---

### **3. The Chi-Squared ($\chi^2$) Test of Homogeneity**

This test compares the observed frequency counts in a contingency table to what we would expect if $H_0$ were true.

- Calculating Expected Counts ($E$):
    
    For each of the 4 cells in the table, we calculate:
    
    $$E_{ij} = \frac{(\text{Row Total}) \times (\text{Column Total})}{\text{Grand Total}}$$
    
    - _Logic:_ If 50% of the total sample are Women, and 20% of the total sample are Smokers, then (under independence) we expect $0.50 \times 0.20 = 10\%$ of the sample to be Women Smokers.
        
- The Test Statistic:
    
    $$\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}$$
    
    - Sum this value over all 4 cells.
        
    - **Degrees of Freedom ($df$):** $(Rows - 1)(Cols - 1)$. For a $2 \times 2$ table, $df = 1$.
        

---

### **4. Yates' Continuity Correction**

Both the Z-test and Chi-Squared test approximate discrete data (counts) with a continuous distribution (Normal/Chi-square). This introduces a small error.

- **The Correction:** To be more conservative (and accurate), we shrink the numerator towards zero.
    
    - **Z-Test:** $| (\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2) | - \frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2})$
        
    - **Chi-Squared:** $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(|O - E| - 0.5)^2}{E}$
        
- **Exam Note:** Check your specific course guidelines. Some introductory courses require Yates' correction always; others ignore it unless asked.
    

---

### **5. Comparison Strategy**

|**Feature**|**Z-Test**|**Chi-Squared Test**|
|---|---|---|
|**Variable Type**|Proportions ($0.45$)|Frequencies / Counts ($45$)|
|**Direction**|Can be One-Tailed or Two-Tailed|Always Two-Tailed (Non-directional)|
|**Extension**|Only for 2 Groups|Can handle 3+ Groups ($r \times c$ table)|
|**Math**|Uses Standard Error|Uses Sum of Squared Errors|

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **The "Pooling" Trap:**
    
    - **Hypothesis Testing ($H_0: p_1 = p_2$):** You **MUST Pool** the variance (use $\hat{p}$) because you assume the groups are the same.
        
    - **Confidence Intervals (Topic 5.3):** You do **NOT Pool**. You assume the groups might be different (that's why you are estimating the difference!), so you use individual variances ($\hat{p}_1$ and $\hat{p}_2$).
        
- **The 2x2 Identity:** If you calculate $Z = 1.96$ in the first test, and then run a Chi-Squared test on the same data, you will get $\chi^2 = (1.96)^2 = 3.84$. Both result in $P = 0.05$.
    
- **Minimum Count Rule:** If any Expected Count ($E$) is $< 5$, both these tests are invalid. You must switch back to **Fisher's Exact Test** (Topic 5.1).
