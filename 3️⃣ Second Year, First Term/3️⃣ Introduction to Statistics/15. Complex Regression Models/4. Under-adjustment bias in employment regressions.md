# 15.4 Under-Adjustment Bias in Employment Regressions ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This is a specialized application of regression logic found in Labor Economics and Forensic Statistics. It addresses a specific flaw when using statistics to sue for discrimination: _"Does the math prove discrimination, or did we just forget to measure a valid reason for the pay gap?"_
    
- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** Questions are typically conceptual or essay-based, asking you to explain why a "significant" gender coefficient might not actually prove discrimination due to omitted variables.
    

---

### **1. The Context: Discrimination Lawsuits**

In employment discrimination cases (e.g., "Women are paid less than Men"), statisticians build a regression model to test for a wage gap.

- The Model:
    
    $$Wage = \beta_0 + \beta_1(Gender) + \beta_2(Education) + \beta_3(Experience) + \epsilon$$
    
- **The Goal:** To see if $\beta_1$ (the Gender coefficient) is statistically significant.
    
- **The Ideal:** If $\beta_1$ is significant and negative, it implies that _after adjusting for_ Education and Experience, women are still paid less. This is often taken as evidence of discrimination.
    

---

### **2. The Concept of Under-Adjustment**

**Under-adjustment Bias** occurs when the regression model fails to include _all_ relevant productivity-related variables (or measures them poorly). As a result, the model "under-adjusts" for legitimate differences between employees.

- The Mechanism (Omitted Variable Bias):
    
    If there is a valid predictor of wage (e.g., "Uninterrupted Work History") that is omitted from the model, and that variable is correlated with Gender, the Gender coefficient ($\beta_1$) will absorb that effect.
    
- **The Result:** The model essentially blames "Gender" for differences that are actually caused by the missing variable. It typically **overestimates** the level of discrimination.
    

---

### **3. The "Proxy" Problem (Measurement Error)**

In the real world, "Productivity" is invisible. We use **Proxies** instead.

- _Real Variable:_ "Skill / Capability."
    
- _Proxy Used:_ "Years of Education."
    

If the proxy is imperfect (which it always is), the regression cannot fully "scrub" the productivity differences out of the equation.

- **Scenario:** Men and Women both have "10 Years Experience" (the proxy). But if the Men worked 60-hour weeks and the Women worked 40-hour weeks, the _quality_ of experience differs.
    
- **Bias:** Because the model treats "10 years" as identical for both, it fails to adjust for the intensity of work. The resulting pay difference is labeled "Discrimination" by the model, even though it might be "Hours Worked."
    

---

### **4. Reverse Regression**

To combat Under-adjustment Bias, statisticians sometimes use **Reverse Regression**.

- **Standard Regression:** Predicts Wage based on Qualifications.
    
    - _Question:_ "Do men and women with the same qualifications earn the same wage?"
        
- **Reverse Regression:** Predicts Qualifications based on Wage.
    
    - _Question:_ "Do men and women with the same wage have the same qualifications?"
        
- **Logic:** If discrimination exists, a woman should need _higher_ qualifications to earn the same salary as a man. If she has _lower_ qualifications for the same salary, the employer might argue they are actually favoring her (or that unmeasured factors are driving the pay).
    

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **The "Catch-22" (Over-adjustment):** The opposite error is also dangerous. If you control for variables that are _themselves_ outcomes of discrimination (like "Job Title"), you mask the discrimination.
    
    - _Example:_ If a company refuses to promote women, controlling for "Job Title" will make wages look equal (within the low title), hiding the fact that the discrimination happens at the promotion stage.
        
- **Legal Standard:** In court, a regression analysis doesn't need to be perfect (include _every_ variable), but it must include the major, obvious ones. The defense usually argues Under-adjustment ("You forgot variable X"), while the plaintiff argues validity.
