# 15.1 Time Series ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** Time Series analysis breaks the cardinal rule of standard regression: "Independence." In a time series, today's value is almost certainly related to yesterday's value. This requires a shift in thinking from "Causal" modeling to "Historical" modeling.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often focus on identifying the four components (Trend, Seasonality, Cyclicality, Irregularity) or interpreting a "Moving Average" graph.
    

---

### **1. Definition and Uniqueness**

A **Time Series** is a sequence of data points collected at regular time intervals (e.g., daily stock prices, monthly rainfall, yearly GDP).

- **The Problem:** Standard regression ($Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X$) assumes errors are independent. In time series, errors are **Autocorrelated** (correlated with themselves over time).
    
- **The Goal:** To use past patterns to forecast future values.
    

---

### **2. The Four Components of Time Series**

We decompose a time series into four distinct underlying forces:

1. **Trend (T):**
    
    - The long-term movement or direction of the data (up, down, or flat) over many years.
        
    - _Example:_ The steady increase in Global Temperature over 50 years.
        
2. **Seasonality (S):**
    
    - Short-term, repetitive patterns that occur at fixed intervals (e.g., within a year).
        
    - _Example:_ Retail sales spiking every December (Christmas) or Ice cream sales peaking every July.
        
3. **Cyclicality (C):**
    
    - Long-term wave-like fluctuations that occur over multi-year periods, often tied to the business cycle (Recession vs. Expansion).
        
    - _Difference from Seasonality:_ Cycles do not have a fixed period (a recession might last 1 year or 5 years).
        
4. **Irregularity (I) / Random Noise:**
    
    - The unpredictable, random residual left over after removing T, S, and C.
        
    - _Example:_ A sudden drop in sales due to a hurricane strike.
        

---

### **3. Smoothing Techniques**

Because raw time series data is noisy, we "smooth" it to see the underlying trend.

- **Simple Moving Average (SMA):**
    
    - Averages the last $k$ periods.
        
    - _Formula:_ $SMA_t = \frac{x_t + x_{t-1} + ... + x_{t-k+1}}{k}$
        
    - _Effect:_ It lags behind reality (reacts slowly to changes).
        
- **Exponential Smoothing:**
    
    - Gives more weight to recent data and less weight to old data.
        
    - _Formula:_ $F_{t+1} = \alpha Y_t + (1-\alpha) F_t$
        
    - $\alpha$ (Alpha): The smoothing constant (0 to 1). High $\alpha$ tracks changes quickly; low $\alpha$ makes the line very smooth.
        

---

### **4. Autocorrelation (Serial Correlation)**

This is the statistical measure of the relationship between a variable and its past self.

- **Lag:** A "Lag 1" autocorrelation measures the correlation between $Y_t$ (today) and $Y_{t-1}$ (yesterday).
    
- **Diagnostic:** The **Durbin-Watson Test** (Topic 14.8) is used to detect this.
    
    - If Autocorrelation exists, OLS regression is invalid, and we must use specialized Time Series models (like ARIMA).
