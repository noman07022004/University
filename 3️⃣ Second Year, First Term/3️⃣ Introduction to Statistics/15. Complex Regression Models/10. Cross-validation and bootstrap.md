### **1. Cross-Validation (CV)**

**Cross-Validation** is a technique used to assess how well a predictive model will perform on "unseen" data.

- **The Problem:** **Overfitting**. If you train a model on a specific dataset, it will learn that data perfectly—including the random noise. It will have a great $R^2$ on the training data but fail miserably when used on new patients or customers.
    
- **The Solution:** We pretend we don't have all the data. We hide a piece of it, train the model on the rest, and then test it on the hidden piece.
    

#### **A. K-Fold Cross-Validation (The Gold Standard)**

This is the standard method used in professional research.

1. **Split:** Randomly divide the dataset into **$K$ equal-sized groups** (folds). (Commonly $K=5$ or $K=10$).
    
2. **Iterate:**
    
    - **Round 1:** Hold out Fold 1 as the "Test Set." Train on Folds 2–10. Calculate Error on Fold 1.
        
    - **Round 2:** Hold out Fold 2 as the "Test Set." Train on Folds 1, 3–10. Calculate Error on Fold 2.
        
    - ... Repeat $K$ times.
        
3. **Average:** The final performance metric is the average of the $K$ error scores.
    

#### **B. Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)**

This is the extreme version where $K = N$ (the total sample size).

- **Method:** You train on $N-1$ points and test on the **single** remaining point. Repeat $N$ times.
    
- **Connection:** This is computationally identical to the **Jackknife** technique (Subtopic 15.9), but applied to prediction error rather than parameter bias.
    
- **Pros:** Uses almost all data for training.
    
- **Cons:** Extremely slow (you must run the regression 1,000 times for 1,000 data points).

### **2. The Bootstrap**

**The Bootstrap** is a resampling method used to estimate the **Standard Error** and **Confidence Intervals** of a statistic, especially when the theoretical distribution is unknown or complex.

- **The Name:** Comes from the phrase "Pulling yourself up by your bootstraps." It implies doing the impossible: learning about the Population without actually collecting more data.
    
- **The Logic:** If the Sample is representative of the Population, then **resampling from the Sample** is mathematically equivalent to resampling from the Population.
    

#### **The Procedure**

1. **Original Sample:** Start with your dataset of size $n$.
    
2. **Resample:** Create a "Bootstrap Sample" by drawing $n$ observations from the original data **With Replacement**.
    
    - _Note:_ Because it is _with replacement_, some data points will appear twice or thrice, and others will be missing. This variation mimics natural sampling error.
        
3. **Calculate:** Compute the statistic of interest (e.g., the Median, the Slope, the Odds Ratio) for this new sample.
    
4. **Repeat:** Do this 1,000 or 10,000 times.
    
5. **Distribution:** You now have a histogram of 1,000 estimated slopes. The Standard Deviation of this histogram is your **Bootstrap Standard Error**. The middle 95% is your **Bootstrap Confidence Interval**.
    

---

### **3. Comparison: CV vs. Bootstrap vs. Jackknife**

|**Feature**|**Cross-Validation**|**Bootstrap**|**Jackknife**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Primary Goal**|**Model Selection**: Checking how well a model predicts new data.|**Inference**: Calculating Standard Errors and Confidence Intervals.|**Bias Correction**: Checking if a statistic is biased.|
|**Method**|Split data into Training vs. Testing.|Random sampling **with replacement**.|Deterministic deletion (**Leave-one-out**).|
|**Exam Key**|"Use this to prevent overfitting."|"Use this to find the CI of a complex metric."|"Use this to estimate bias."|

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **Assumption of Independence:** Both methods assume the data points are independent. You cannot blindly bootstrap Time Series data (Topic 15.1) because breaking the order destroys the time trend. You must use "Block Bootstrapping."
    
- **The "Free Lunch":** Bootstrap feels like magic, but it cannot fix bad data. If your original sample was biased (e.g., you only surveyed rich people), your bootstrap estimates will be **precisely biased**. It quantifies random error, not systematic bias.
