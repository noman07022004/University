# 15.5 System of Equations (Simultaneous Equation Models) ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** This topic introduces a "chicken and egg" problem. In standard regression, we assume X causes Y. But what if Y also causes X? This creates a feedback loop that breaks standard OLS regression.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** You are unlikely to be asked to solve a simultaneous system by hand (calculus/matrix algebra required). Exam questions usually focus on the **concept of Endogeneity** and identifying examples where a system is needed (e.g., Supply and Demand).
    

---

### **1. The Problem: Simultaneity (Feedback Loops)**

Standard regression assumes Unidirectional Causality:

$$X \rightarrow Y$$

(Changes in X cause changes in Y, but Y does not affect X).

However, in many real-world systems (especially Economics and Biology), causality is Bi-directional:

$$X \leftrightarrow Y$$

- **Classic Example (Supply and Demand):**
    
    - **Demand:** Higher Price $\rightarrow$ Lower Quantity bought.
        
    - **Supply:** Higher Demand (Quantity) $\rightarrow$ Higher Price charged.
        
    - _Problem:_ If you just regress `Quantity` on `Price`, you aren't measuring the Demand curve _or_ the Supply curve. You are measuring a meaningless mix of both, known as **Simultaneity Bias**.
        

---

### **2. Structural vs. Reduced Form Equations**

To handle this, we write a **System** of equations rather than a single line.

#### **A. Structural Equations**

These represent the underlying theory of how the world works. They describe the causal relationships.

- _Eq 1 (Consumption):_ $C = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Income} + \epsilon_1$
    
- _Eq 2 (Income):_ $\text{Income} = C + \text{Investment} + \text{GovSpending}$
    
- _Note:_ `Income` is on the Right Hand Side of Eq 1, but it is also determined by `C` in Eq 2. This circularity is the defining feature of the system.
    

#### **B. Reduced Form Equations**

To solve the system mathematically, we rearrange the algebra so that the **Endogenous** variables (the ones inside the loop, like $C$ and $Income$) are strictly on the left, and **Exogenous** variables (outside forces, like $Investment$) are on the right.

- _Goal:_ Express $Y$ solely as a function of Exogenous variables.
    

---

### **3. Why OLS Fails (Endogeneity)**

If you try to run a standard OLS regression on a single equation from a simultaneous system, it fails.

- **The Assumption Violation:** OLS requires that the predictor ($X$) is uncorrelated with the error term ($\epsilon$).
    
- **The Reality:** In a system, because Y feeds back into X, a random shock ($\epsilon$) to Y _changes_ X.
    
- **Result:** $Cov(X, \epsilon) \neq 0$. The estimates are **Biased** and **Inconsistent** (even with infinite data, they remain wrong).
    

---

### **4. The Solution: Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS)**

Since we cannot use OLS directly, we use a multi-step method. This is the standard "fix" for systems of equations.

1. **Stage 1:** Regress the problematic Endogenous variable ($X$) on a valid **Instrumental Variable (IV)** (a variable that affects $X$ but is not part of the feedback loop).
    
    - _Goal:_ Isolate the "clean" variation in $X$ that is not contaminated by the feedback loop.
        
2. **Stage 2:** Use this predicted "clean" version of $X$ ($\hat{x}$) in the original equation to predict $Y$.
    

---

### **5. The Identification Problem**

Before trying to solve a system, statisticians must ask: _"Is it even possible to solve this?"_

- **Unidentified:** Not enough information to distinguish the equations (e.g., Supply and Demand look identical).
    
- **Identified:** We have unique "shifters" (Instrumental Variables) for each equation that allow us to tease them apart.
    
    - _Example:_ If we have a variable `Weather` that affects Supply (farming) but not Demand, we can use Weather to trace out the Demand curve.
        

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **Terminology:**
    
    - **Endogenous Variable:** Determined _inside_ the system (e.g., Price, Quantity).
        
    - **Exogenous Variable:** Determined _outside_ the system (e.g., Rainfall, Tax Rate).
        
- **Recursive Models:** A special type of system where the chain goes $A \to B \to C$. Since there is no feedback ($C$ doesn't go back to $A$), OLS **can** be used step-by-step. Systems only require special math if there is a **Loop**.
