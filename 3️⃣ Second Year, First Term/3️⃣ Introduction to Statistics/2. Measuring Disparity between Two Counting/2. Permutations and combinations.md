
## 2.2 Permutations and Combinations ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This is the mathematical engine of probability. You cannot calculate "The probability of winning the lottery" if you cannot first calculate "How many total lottery combinations exist." It teaches you how to count outcomes in complex sample spaces.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While direct questions (e.g., "Calculate $^5C_2$") are rare in advanced exams, word problems requiring these formulas to find the denominator for a probability question are standard.

---

### 1. The Fundamental Counting Principle

Before specific formulas, we must understand the core axiom of discrete mathematics.

- The Multiplication Rule: If a task consists of a sequence of choices in which there are $n_1$ ways to do the first task, $n_2$ ways to do the second, and so on, then the total number of ways to perform the sequence is:
    
    $$\text{Total Ways} = n_1 \times n_2 \times ... \times n_k$$
    
- Factorial Notation ($n!$):
    
    To simplify counting formulas, we use factorials.
    
    $$n! = n \times (n-1) \times (n-2) \times ... \times 1$$
    
    - _Crucial Definition:_ $0! = 1$. This is defined algebraically to make the formulas work.
        

---

### 2. Permutations (Order Matters)

A **Permutation** is an ordered arrangement of distinct items. The key characteristic is that **Sequence/Order is important**.

- _Scenario:_ "A, B, C" is considered different from "C, B, A".
    

**Types of Permutations:**

1. Permutation of $n$ distinct items:
    
    Arranging all items in a set.
    
    $$P = n!$$
    
    - _Example:_ How many ways can 5 books be arranged on a shelf? $5! = 120$ ways.
        
2. Permutation of $r$ items selected from $n$ ($^nP_r$):
    
    Arranging a subset of items.
    
    $$^nP_r = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$$
    
    - Example: In a race with 10 sprinters, how many ways can we award Gold, Silver, and Bronze?
        
        Since Gold $\neq$ Silver, order matters.
        
        $$^{10}P_3 = \frac{10!}{7!} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 = 720 \text{ ways}$$
        

---

### 3. Combinations (Order Does Not Matter)

A **Combination** is a selection of distinct items where the order of selection is irrelevant. We are looking for **Groups** or **Subsets**.

- _Scenario:_ "A, B, C" is considered identical to "C, B, A" (they are the same group of letters).
    

The Formula ($^nC_r$):

Because order doesn't matter, we have fewer unique outcomes than permutations. We divide the permutation formula by $r!$ to remove the redundant arrangements.

$$^nC_r = \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$$

- Example: A class has 10 students. The teacher needs to select a committee of 3 students.
    
    Being picked 1st or 3rd doesn't change your membership in the committee. Order does not matter.
    
    $$^{10}C_3 = \frac{10!}{3!7!} = \frac{10 \times 9 \times 8}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 120 \text{ ways}$$
    

---

### 4. Distinguishing Between the Two

The hardest part of this topic in an exam is not the math, but deciding which formula to use. You must analyze the "physicality" of the problem.

|**Feature**|**Permutation (nPr​)**|**Combination (nCr​)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Key Question**|Does the sequence/ranking change the outcome?|Does the membership of the group change?|
|**Keywords**|Arrange, Schedule, Rank, Order, Titles (Pres/VP).|Select, Choose, Group, Sample, Committee.|
|**Analogy**|A Locked Safe (4-2-1 is different from 1-2-4).|A Fruit Salad (Apple-Banana is the same as Banana-Apple).|
|**Magnitude**|Result is always larger (or equal).|Result is always smaller.|

---

### Important Notes, Tips & Facts

- Symmetry of Combinations:
    
    $$^nC_r = ^nC_{n-r}$$
    
    Choosing 3 people to join a team from a group of 10 is mathematically identical to choosing 7 people to leave the group.
    
    - _Example:_ $^{10}C_3 = ^{10}C_7$.
        
- **The Binomial Connection:** The values calculated by $^nC_r$ are the coefficients in the expansion of $(x+y)^n$. This is why they are often called "Binomial Coefficients" in Topic 4 (Distributions).
    
- **Calculator proficiency:** Locate the `nCr` and `nPr` buttons on your calculator immediately. You will almost never calculate factorials by hand in a statistics exam; you will use these functions.
