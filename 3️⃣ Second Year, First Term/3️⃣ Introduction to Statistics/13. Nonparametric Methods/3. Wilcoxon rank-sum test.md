# 13.3 Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test (Mann-Whitney U Test) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This is the nonparametric alternative to the **Independent Samples T-Test** (Topic 8.1). It is used when comparing two _independent_ groups (e.g., Treatment vs. Control) that do not meet the assumptions of normality.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Like the Signed-Rank test, calculation questions are common. You must be careful not to confuse the **Signed-Rank** test (for paired data) with the **Rank-Sum** test (for independent data).
    

---

### **1. Terminology Clarification**

In textbooks and exams, this test goes by two names which are mathematically equivalent:

1. **Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test:** Focuses on the sum of ranks ($W$).
    
2. **Mann-Whitney U Test:** Focuses on the number of pairs where one group exceeds the other ($U$).
    

- _Note:_ Most syllabi treat them as the same procedure. We will focus on the **Rank-Sum ($W$)** approach as it is more intuitive for manual calculation.
    

---

### **2. When to Use It**

Use this test when:

1. You have **two independent groups** (Sample sizes $n_1$ and $n_2$).
    
2. The outcome variable is continuous or ordinal.
    
3. The data is **non-normal** (skewed) or the sample size is small, violating the T-test assumption.
    

---

### **3. The Calculation Procedure (Step-by-Step)**

Step 1: Pool and Rank

Combine the data from both groups into one single list. Sort them from smallest to largest.

- Assign Rank 1 to the smallest value, Rank 2 to the next, etc.
    
- **Ties:** If values are tied, assign the average rank (e.g., if ranks 4 and 5 are tied, both get 4.5).
    

Step 2: Sum the Ranks

Separate the data back into their original groups.

- Calculate $R_1$: The sum of ranks for Group 1.
    
- Calculate $R_2$: The sum of ranks for Group 2.
    

Step 3: Calculate the Statistic ($U$)

The test statistic $U$ measures the degree of separation between the groups. Calculate $U$ for both groups and pick the smaller one.

$$U_1 = R_1 - \frac{n_1(n_1+1)}{2}$$

$$U_2 = R_2 - \frac{n_2(n_2+1)}{2}$$

$$U_{stat} = \min(U_1, U_2)$$

- _Logic:_ If Group 1 has all the tiny values, its Rank Sum ($R_1$) will be very small (close to the minimum possible sum), leading to a small $U$.
    

---

### **4. Hypothesis Testing**

- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** The two populations have the same distribution (specifically, the same median). $P(X > Y) = 0.5$.
    
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$):** One population tends to have larger values than the other.
    
- **Decision Rule:**
    
    - Consult the **Mann-Whitney Table** (or Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Table).
        
    - **Critical Value:** Based on $n_1$ and $n_2$.
        
    - **Rejection:** If $U_{stat} \le U_{critical}$, Reject $H_0$. (Again, **Small is Significant**).
        

---

### **5. Large Sample Approximation ($Z$)**

If both $n_1$ and $n_2$ are large (usually $> 10$ or $> 20$), the sampling distribution of $U$ becomes Normal.

- Mean ($\mu_U$):
    
    $$\mu_U = \frac{n_1 n_2}{2}$$
    
- Standard Deviation ($\sigma_U$):
    
    $$\sigma_U = \sqrt{\frac{n_1 n_2 (n_1 + n_2 + 1)}{12}}$$
    
- Z-Score:
    
    $$Z = \frac{U - \mu_U}{\sigma_U}$$
    

---

### **Important Notes, Tips & Facts**

- **Relation to T-Test:** The Rank-Sum test is about 95% as efficient as the T-test even if the data _is_ normal. If data is highly skewed, it is often _more_ powerful than the T-test.
    
- **Assumption of Homogeneity:** While it doesn't assume Normality, it does assume the **shapes** of the two distributions are similar (e.g., both have the same variance). If Group A is narrow and Group B is wide, the test may be invalid.
    
- Check Formula: A useful check for your math in exams:
    
    $$U_1 + U_2 = n_1 \times n_2$$
    
    If your calculated Us don't sum to the product of the sample sizes, you made an error.
