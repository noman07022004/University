## 8. Use of Search Engines ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** Critical. In the information age, the ability to find _correct_ information quickly is the most valuable skill a student can possess. It separates a "user" from a "researcher."
    
- **Exam (3/5):** Moderate Frequency. Exams may ask "How does a search engine work?", "Explain Boolean Operators," or "Difference between a Web Browser and a Search Engine."
    

---

### The Gateway to Information

A **Search Engine** is a software system designed to carry out web searches. It searches the World Wide Web in a systematic way for particular information specified in a textual web search query.

While you see a simple box where you type "Khulna University," the backend is a massive engineering marvel.

#### How It Works (The "Hidden" Mechanism)

*To understand search engines, you must understand what happens before you type.*

1. **Crawling (The Spider):** The search engine sends out automated programs called "Spiders" or "Crawlers" to browse the web. They follow links from one page to another (like a spider on a web) to discover new content.
    
2. **Indexing (The Library):** The data found by the spiders is analyzed and stored in a massive database called an **Index**. Think of this as the index at the back of a book—it lists which page contains which keyword.
    
3. **Ranking (The Algorithm):** When you type a query, the search engine doesn't scan the whole internet _live_ (that would take too long). It scans its _Index_. Then, it uses a secret **Algorithm** to decide which result is most relevant to show you first (based on keywords, site speed, and reliability).
    

---

### 8.1 General Searches (The Surface Web)

This refers to standard queries used for daily information—news, definitions, shopping, or images.

- **Examples:** **Google** (90%+ market share), **Bing** (Microsoft), **DuckDuckGo** (Privacy-focused).
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:** In Bangladesh, Google is dominant. However, specialized search portals like **Pipilika** (the first Bangla search engine) attempted to organize local content, though Google remains the standard.
    

#### _Additional Necessary Information:_ Advanced Search Techniques (Boolean Operators)

_This is the "secret weapon" for academic searching. It is often missing in basic texts but is essential for getting 5/5 in exams._

You can filter results using **Boolean Operators**:

1. **AND:** Narrows results. `Climate AND Bangladesh` finds pages containing _both_ words.
    
2. **OR:** Broadens results. `Laptop OR Tablet` finds pages with _either_ word.
    
3. **NOT ( - ):** Excludes words. `Jaguar -Car` finds the animal, removing results about the luxury car.
    
4. **Exact Phrase (" "):** `"Khulna University CSE"` finds pages with that _exact_ sentence order.

### 8.2 Publication and Resources (The Academic Web)

For university assignments, "Googling" is often not enough because standard results include blogs, Wikipedia, and unverified opinions. You need **Academic Search Engines** that index only peer-reviewed journals, theses, and books.

**1. Google Scholar**

- The most accessible tool for students. It filters out commercial websites and shows only legal opinions, patents, and academic papers.
    
- _Feature:_ It shows you "Cited by" numbers—telling you how influential a paper is.
    

**2. ResearchGate / Academia.edu**

- These are social networking sites for scientists. Researchers upload their papers here for free access.
    

**3. Institutional Resources (Bangladeshi Context)**

- **BanglaJOL (Bangladesh Journals Online):** A database of scientific journals published in Bangladesh.
    
- **UGC Digital Library (UDL):** The University Grants Commission of Bangladesh provides universities (including Khulna University) access to expensive international journals (like IEEE, Springer) for free or low cost. You usually need to be on the university Wi-Fi to access these.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Browser vs. Search Engine:** This is the most common "trap" question.
    
    - **Web Browser:** The application you install on your PC to view webpages (e.g., Chrome, Firefox, Edge).
        
    - **Search Engine:** The website you visit _inside_ the browser to find other websites (e.g., Google, Bing).
        
    - _Analogy:_ Browser = The Car; Search Engine = The Map/GPS.
        
- **The "CRAAP" Test:** When evaluating if a search result is good for your assignment, check:
    
    - **C**urrency (Is it new?)
        
    - **R**elevance (Is it on topic?)
        
    - **A**uthority (Who wrote it? A professor or a blogger?)
        
    - **A**ccuracy (Is it supported by evidence?)
        
    - **P**urpose (Is it trying to sell you something?)
