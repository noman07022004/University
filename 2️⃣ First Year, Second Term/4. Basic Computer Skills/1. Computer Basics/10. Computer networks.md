**Rating Justification:**

### The Connected World

A **Computer Network** is a collection of two or more computers connected together to share information and resources.

When your **ASUS Strix** connects to the Wi-Fi at Khulna University, it joins a network. This allows it not just to access the Internet, but to share printers, exchange files with other students, and communicate efficiently. The device sending data is the **Sender** (Source), the one receiving is the **Receiver**, and the path they use (wire or air) is the **Transmission Medium**.

#### 1. Classification by Geographical Span

The most common way to classify networks is by how large of an area they cover.

**A. Personal Area Network (PAN)**

- **Range:** Smallest range (approx. 10 meters). Organized around an individual person.
    
- **Example:** Connecting your Bluetooth headphones or smartphone to your laptop. When you use "Shareit" or Bluetooth to transfer a file from your phone to your PC, you have created a temporary PAN.
    

**B. Local Area Network (LAN)**

- **Range:** Covers a small, specific area like a room, an office, or a single building.
    
- **Ownership:** Privately owned (e.g., by the university or your home).
    
- **Speed:** Very high data transfer rates (100 Mbps to 1 Gbps+).
    
- **User Context:** The Wi-Fi in your home is a WLAN (Wireless LAN). Your laptop connects to a central router, creating a network of your phone, laptop, and smart TV.
    

**C. Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)**

- **Range:** Covers a larger geographic area, like an entire city. It connects multiple LANs.
    
- **Example:** The cable TV network in Khulna city or a city-wide Wi-Fi provided by an ISP (Internet Service Provider) like Carnival or Amber IT.
    

**D. Wide Area Network (WAN)**

- **Range:** Spans a large physical distance—a country, continent, or the entire globe.
    
- **Mechanism:** It uses satellites, microwave signals, and undersea cables.
    
- **Example:** The **Internet** is the world's largest WAN.
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:** Bangladesh connects to the global WAN primarily through the **SEA-ME-WE 4** and **SEA-ME-WE 5** submarine cables landing in Cox's Bazar and Kuakata. This infrastructure is what allows your laptop to load Google (servers in the US) or Facebook (servers in Singapore).

### _Additional Necessary Information:_ Network Architecture & Topology

_To fully understand how networks are built and structured, you need two more concepts often asked in exams._

#### 2. Network Architecture (Logical Design)

- **Client-Server Network:**
    
    - A powerful central computer (Server) provides resources to less powerful computers (Clients).
        
    - _Example:_ When you access Khulna University's website, their computer is the Server; your laptop is the Client.
        
- **Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Network:**
    
    - No central server. All computers have equal status and share resources directly.
        
    - _Example:_ A home network where your laptop shares a printer directly with another laptop.
        

#### 3. Network Topology (Physical Layout)

Topology refers to the geometric arrangement of the computers (nodes) and cables.

- **Bus Topology:** All devices connect to a single central cable (backbone). If the backbone breaks, the whole network fails.
    
- **Star Topology:** All devices connect to a central device (Hub or Switch). _This is the most common modern topology._ If one cable breaks, only that computer is affected.
    
- **Ring Topology:** Computers are connected in a closed loop. Data travels in one direction.
    
- **Mesh Topology:** Every device connects to every other device. Highly reliable but expensive.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **LAN vs. WAN:** This is a classic 5-mark question.
    
    - _Area:_ LAN = Small (Building); WAN = Large (Country/World).
        
    - _Speed:_ LAN = High Speed; WAN = Lower Speed (generally).
        
    - _Cost:_ LAN = Cheap to set up; WAN = Expensive (Satellites/Cables).
        
- **Router vs. Switch:**
    
    - **Switch:** Connects devices _within_ a single network (e.g., creates a LAN).
        
    - **Router:** Connects _different_ networks together (e.g., connects your Home LAN to the Internet WAN).
        
- **Essay Tip:** In a broad question about "ICT in Bangladesh," mention that the expansion of **4G networks (WAN)** by Grameenphone/Robi has brought internet access to rural areas, fulfilling the "Digital Bangladesh" vision.
