### The Moral Code of the Digital World

**Cyber Ethics** (or Computer Ethics) is the set of moral principles that govern the behavior of individuals and groups when using computers, the internet, and digital media. Just as we have social norms in the physical world (like not stealing or not shouting in a library), we have **"Netiquette"** (Network Etiquette) in the digital world.

As a student, you are a **"Netizen"** (Internet Citizen). This title comes with rights (freedom of speech, access to information) and responsibilities (respecting others, obeying laws).

#### 1. Netizen Community Standards & Netiquette

"Accessing the internet" is technical; accessing it _ethically_ is social. Community Standards are the rules set by platforms (like Facebook, Google, or Khulna University’s network policy) regarding what is acceptable.

**Core Principles of Netiquette (Virginia Shea’s Rules):**

- **Remember the Human:** Do not be rude or abusive just because you cannot see the other person's face. If you wouldn't say it to their face, don't type it.
    
- **Respect Privacy:** Do not forward private emails or photos without permission.
    
- **Forgive Mistakes:** If someone makes a typo or asks a "stupid" question, be patient.
    
- **Don't "Flame":** Avoid "Flaming"—expressing strong opinions without holding back emotion (online shouting matches).
    

#### 2. Cyber Security: The CIA Triad

**Cyber Security** is not just about installing antivirus; it is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks. In academia, we define security using the **CIA Triad** (Crucial for exams):

- **Confidentiality:** Keeping data secret. Only authorized people should see it. (e.g., Only _you_ should see your password).
    
- **Integrity:** Ensuring data is not altered. (e.g., A hacker shouldn't be able to change your grades in the university database).
    
- **Availability:** Ensuring data is accessible when needed. (e.g., The university website shouldn't crash during exam registration due to an attack).
    

#### 3. Privacy Rights & Intellectual Property

- **Privacy Rights:** This is the right of individuals to determine for themselves when, how, and to what extent information about them is communicated to others.
    
    - _Digital Footprint:_ Every click, like, and search you make leaves a permanent trail. Companies track this to build a profile of you.
        
- **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR):** This protects the creations of the mind.
    
    - **Copyright:** The legal right of the creator to print, publish, and sell their work.
        
    - **Plagiarism:** Presenting someone else's work (code, essay, idea) as your own. In university, this leads to immediate failure or expulsion.
        
    - **Software Piracy:** The illegal copying or distribution of software. Using a "Cracked" version of Windows or MS Office is technically unethical and illegal.

### Key Ethical Issues and Threats (The "Missing" Link)

_To fully understand Cyber Ethics, you must know what you are fighting against._

**A. Cyber Crimes (Offenses)**

1. **Hacking:** Gaining unauthorized access to data in a system.
    
    - _White Hat:_ Ethical hackers (Security experts).
        
    - _Black Hat:_ Criminals.
        
2. **Phishing:** Sending fraudulent emails (looking like a bank or university) to trick you into revealing passwords.
    
    - _Example:_ An email saying "Urgent: Your Khulna University account will be suspended. Click here to login." (This is a trap).
        
3. **Identity Theft:** Stealing someone's personal info (NID, Date of Birth) to commit fraud in their name.
    
4. **Cyberbullying:** Using digital platforms to harass or intimidate others.
    

**B. Malware (Malicious Software)**

- **Virus:** Attaches to a file and spreads when you open it.
    
- **Worm:** Spreads automatically through the network without human action.
    
- **Trojan Horse:** Looks like useful software (e.g., a free game) but contains malware inside.
    
- **Ransomware:** Locks your files (encrypts them) and demands money (crypto) to unlock them.

### Bangladeshi Context: The Legal Framework

_You must mention this in essays regarding Cyber Ethics in Bangladesh._

The primary law governing this in Bangladesh was the **Digital Security Act (DSA) 2018**, which has recently been replaced/reformed into the **Cyber Security Act (CSA) 2023**.

- It penalizes hacking, identity theft, and spreading propaganda/hate speech online.
    
- **Section 17:** Punishment for illegal access to critical information infrastructure.
    
- **Relevance:** As a student, be very careful about sharing unverified political news or sensitive images, as these can fall under "cyber offenses" in Bangladesh.

### Practical Security Measures (How to Protect Yourself)

- **Strong Passwords:** Use a mix of symbols, numbers, and cases (e.g., `Tr!ckP@ss24`).
    
- **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** Always enable this (OTP on phone) for your Email and Facebook. Even if a hacker steals your password, they can't get in without your phone.
    
- **HTTPS:** Look for the "Lock" icon in the browser address bar. It means the connection is encrypted. Never enter credit card info on a site without HTTPS.
    
- **Update Your OS:** Your **Windows 11** receives "Security Patches" every month. Do not delay these updates; they fix holes that hackers use.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Ethics vs. Law:**
    
    - _Ethics:_ "What you _should_ do" (Moral/Social). Breaking it leads to social rejection.
        
    - _Law:_ "What you _must_ do" (Legal). Breaking it leads to jail/fines.
        
- **Essay Topic:** "The impact of Social Media on Youth Ethics." Discuss cyberbullying, fake news, and addiction.
    
- **Exam Terminology:** If asked about "Spam," define it as "Unsolicited junk email."
    
- **The "Delete" Myth:** Nothing on the internet is ever truly deleted. Even if you delete a post, screenshots or server backups likely exist. This is the concept of **Data Permanence**.
