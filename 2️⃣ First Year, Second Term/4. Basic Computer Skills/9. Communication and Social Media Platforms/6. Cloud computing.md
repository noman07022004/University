### The Computer in the Sky

**Cloud Computing** is the delivery of computing services—including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, and intelligence—over the Internet ("the cloud") to offer faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale.

In simple terms: Instead of doing the work on your own computer's hard drive (Local Computing), you use the Internet to connect to a massive computer owned by a company like Google or Microsoft, and do the work there.

When you edit a document in **Google Docs**, the file is not on your laptop. It is on a server in a data center (perhaps in Singapore or the USA), and your screen is just a window into that server.

#### 1. Why is it called "The Cloud"?

In network diagrams, engineers used to draw a fluffy cloud symbol to represent the Internet because the complex details of the connections didn't matter—it just "worked." The name stuck.

#### 2. Key Models of Cloud Computing

_To understand this fully for exams, you must know the three layers of service:_

**A. SaaS (Software as a Service) – _What You Use_**

- **Definition:** You use software hosted on the cloud via a web browser. You manage nothing.
    
- **Examples:** **Gmail**, **Google Drive**, **Google Classroom**, **Zoom**, **Facebook**.
    
- **Relevance:** As a Basic Computer Skills student, this is your primary interaction layer.
    

**B. PaaS (Platform as a Service) – _For Developers_**

- **Definition:** A platform that allows developers to build and run applications without worrying about the underlying servers.
    
- **Example:** Google App Engine, Heroku.
    

**C. IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service) – _For IT Admins_**

- **Definition:** You rent the raw hardware (virtual machines, storage) and manage the OS yourself.
    
- **Example:** Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure, DigitalOcean.

### Practical Application: Cloud Storage (Your Digital Backpack)

For a university student, the most immediate application of cloud computing is **Cloud Storage**. This allows you to save files online and access them from anywhere.

**1. Google Drive (The Standard)**

- **Capacity:** 15 GB Free (shared across Gmail and Photos).
    
- **Integration:** Works seamlessly with Google Classroom. When you upload an assignment in Classroom, it is actually stored here.1
    
- **Collaboration:** Allows "Real-time Co-authoring." You and your group partner can type in the same Word document at the same time from different houses.2
    

**2. Microsoft OneDrive (The Window3s Native)**

- **Capacity:** 5 GB Free.
    
- **Integration:** Built deeply into your **Windows 11** OS.
    
- **Feature:** On your ASUS laptop, you likely see a folder named "OneDrive." Anything you put there is automatically uploaded to the cloud. This acts as an instant backup.
    

**3. Dropbox**

- The pioneer of modern cloud storage, known for its fast syncing speed.

### _Additional Necessary Information:_ Advantages & Disadvantages

_This comparison is a standard exam essay topic._

**Advantages:**

1. **Accessibility:** Access your files from any device (Phone, Laptop, Library PC) via the internet.
    
2. **Data Security (Backup):** If your laptop is stolen or your hard drive crashes, your data is safe in the cloud.
    
3. **Cost:** You don't need to buy expensive hard drives; free tiers are sufficient for most students.
    
4. **Collaboration:** Multiple users can work on one file simultaneously.
    

**Disadvantages:**

1. **Internet Dependency:** You strictly need an internet connection. If the Wi-Fi at Khulna University goes down, you might lose access to your files (unless you enabled "Offline Mode").
    
2. **Privacy:** You are entrusting your data to a third party (Google/Microsoft).
    
3. **Security Risks:** If someone hacks your password, they have access to _everything_. (Hence, the need for 2FA).

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Local vs. Cloud" Distinction:**
    
    - **Local:** Stored on your physical Hard Drive/SSD. (Fast, works offline, risky if device breaks).
        
    - **Cloud:** Stored on Internet Servers. (Accessible anywhere, requires internet, safe from device breakage).
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** Bangladesh has its own "National Data Center" (Tier-IV) in **Kaliakair**, which is a form of government cloud infrastructure ("Meghna Cloud"). Mentioning this adds local relevance.
    
- **Practical Tip:** Always install the **Google Drive for Desktop** or ensure **OneDrive** is active on your laptop. It creates a "Drive letter" (like a USB stick) on your PC that syncs automatically.
