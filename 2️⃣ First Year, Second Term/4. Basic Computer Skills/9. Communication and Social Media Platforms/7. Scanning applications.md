## 9.7 Scanning Applications ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** Highly practical. As a student, you will frequently need to submit handwritten assignments digitally. Knowing how to create a clean, professional PDF from a notebook page is a daily survival skill.
    
- **Exam (2/5):** Low Frequency. You might get a short note on "OCR" or "Digitization," or a practical viva question like "How do you scan a document using your phone?"
    

---

### Digitizing the Physical World

**Scanning Applications** are mobile or desktop software tools that use a camera and image processing algorithms to convert physical documents (hard copies) into high-quality digital files (soft copies).

Before smartphones, you needed a bulky flatbed scanner to digitize a paper. Now, scanning apps use **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** to replicate that quality. They don't just "take a picture"; they perform a sequence of image enhancements to make the document look like it was digitally created.

#### The Scanning Workflow (How it differs from a Photo)

When you take a normal photo of a page, it has shadows, the table might look visible in the background, and the page might be trapezoidal (tilted). A Scanning App fixes this:

1. **Edge Detection:** The app identifies the four corners of the paper automatically.
    
2. **Perspective Correction (De-skewing):** It flattens the image. Even if you took the photo from an angle, the app stretches it to look like a flat, rectangular scan.
    
3. **Binarization/Enhancement:** It removes shadows and turns the background pure white and the text dark black (High Contrast). This makes the text readable and drastically reduces file size.
    
4. **Format Conversion:** It saves the final result as a **PDF** (Portable Document Format), which is the standard for academic submission, rather than a heavy JPG image.
    

#### Popular Scanning Applications

- **CamScanner:** The most famous app in Bangladesh. It offers powerful editing features but places a "Scanned with CamScanner" watermark on the free version.
    
- **Adobe Scan:** Excellent for text. It automatically detects text and runs **OCR** (see below) to make it searchable. It integrates with the Adobe Acrobat ecosystem.
    
- **Microsoft Lens:** Great for capturing whiteboards in class. It removes glare and integrates directly with OneNote and Word.
    
- **Google Drive App:** Your Android phone and iPhone have a hidden scanner inside the Google Drive app. (Tap the `+` button > `Scan`). It saves directly to the cloud.

### _Additional Necessary Information:_ OCR (Optical Character Recognition)

_This is the "secret sauce" of modern scanning and a key technical concept for exams._

**OCR** is the technology that converts _images_ of typed, handwritten, or printed text into _machine-encoded text_.

- **Without OCR:** A scan is just a picture. You cannot select, copy, or search for a specific word inside it.
    
- **With OCR:** The software recognizes the shapes of letters (e.g., sees a circle and calls it 'o'). It turns the image into a text document.
    
- **Relevance:** When you scan a book page using **Adobe Scan**, you can literally copy-paste the text from the image into your Microsoft Word assignment.

### Practical Guide: Scanning for Assignments

Since you must submit assignments to Google Classroom, follow these rules to ensure your teachers can read them:

1. **Lighting:** Always scan near a window or bright light. Do not cast your own shadow on the paper.
    
2. **Contrast:** Use the "Magic Color" or "B&W" filter. Do not submit "Original" photos unless it's a color drawing. Gray pages are hard to print and read.
    
3. **Multi-page PDF:** Do not upload 10 separate images for a 10-page assignment. Use the **"Batch Mode"** in the app to scan all pages and save them as a **single PDF file**.
    
4. **Naming:** Rename the file _before_ uploading. (e.g., `CSE1251_ID190201_LabReport1.pdf`).

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Hard Copy vs. Soft Copy:**
    
    - **Hard Copy:** Physical paper output (Tangible).
        
    - **Soft Copy:** Digital file stored on a computer (Intangible). Scanning converts Hard $\to$ Soft. Printing converts Soft $\to$ Hard.
        
- **Resolution:** Scanned documents are often measured in **DPI** (Dots Per Inch). Standard academic scans should be around **200-300 DPI** for a balance between clarity and file size.
    
- **Exam Question:** "What are the advantages of using a scanning app over a normal camera?"
    
    - _Answer:_ Perspective correction, shadow removal, lower file size, PDF creation, and OCR capabilities.
