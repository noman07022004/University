## 15.8 Miscellaneous Rules ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** These rules cover the "edge cases" of inheritance. While less common than the standard Father/Son calculations, knowing these rules prevents you from getting stuck on tricky problem questions involving pregnancy or unknown heirs.
    
- **Exam (3/5):** Often tested as short notes (e.g., "Rights of an unborn child" or "Escheat").
    

---

### Special Cases & Calculations

These rules address specific situations that do not fit neatly into the standard Class I, II, or III categories.

#### 1. The Unborn Child (_Hamal_)

If a person dies while his wife (or a female heir) is pregnant, the distribution of the estate cannot be fully finalized until the child is born. The child in the womb is competent to inherit if born alive.

- **The Rule of Reservation:** The other heirs can choose to wait or distribute the estate immediately. If they distribute immediately, they must **reserve a share** for the unborn baby.
    
- **How much to reserve?** Opinions vary, but the standard Hanafi practice is to reserve the share of **one son** (since a son takes the largest possible share).
    
    - _If born a boy:_ He takes the reserved share.
        
    - _If born a girl:_ She takes her legal share (1/2 of a son's), and the surplus is returned to the other heirs.
        
    - _If born dead:_ The reserved share is distributed among the other heirs as if the child never existed.
        

#### 2. The Hermaphrodite (_Khuntha_)

A _Khuntha_ is a person whose gender is ambiguous or indeterminate. Since Muslim law relies heavily on the 2:1 (Male:Female) ratio, determining their sex is crucial.

- **The Rule:** Until their gender can be determined (usually at puberty), the _Khuntha_ is given the **lesser of the two shares**.
    
    - _Example:_ If being a male gives them 100 taka and being a female gives them 50 taka, they are given **50 taka** (the female share) to be safe. The remaining amount is held in suspense until their gender is clarified.
        

#### 3. Step-Children and Adopted Children

This is a definitive rule often confused by students familiar with Western law.

- **Step-Children:** A step-child (child of a spouse from a previous marriage) is **not an heir**. They have no blood relation to the step-parent.
    
    - _Example:_ If a man dies leaving a wife and her son from a previous marriage, the wife inherits, but her son gets **Zero** from the step-father.
        
- **Adopted Children:** The formal legal adoption does not create inheritance rights in Sharia. An adopted child (_Mutabanna_) is considered a stranger for inheritance purposes.
    
    - _Remedy:_ The only way to provide for a step-child or adopted child is through a **Will (_Wasiyyat_)**, which is limited to 1/3rd of the estate.
        

#### 4. The Missing Person (_Mafqud_)

If an heir has disappeared and their whereabouts are unknown:

- **Inheriting FROM others:** The missing person is presumed **alive** until declared dead. A share is reserved for them and held in custody. If they do not return within the statutory period (7 years under Evidence Act), that share is returned to the other heirs.
    
- **Others inheriting FROM them:** Their own property cannot be distributed until they are proven dead or the 7-year period elapses.
    

#### 5. Doctrine of Escheat (_Bait-ul-Mal_)

What happens if a person dies with absolutely **NO** heirs?

- No Sharers (Class I).
    
- No Residuaries (Class II).
    
- No Distant Kindred (Class III).
    
- No Successor by Contract.
    
- **The Final Rule:** The property escheats (reverts) to the State. In Islamic terms, it goes to the **Bait-ul-Mal** (Public Treasury) to be used for the benefit of the poor Muslim community.
    

#### 6. Primogeniture (The Eldest Son)

- **Sunni Law Rule:** Sunni law strictly **rejects** the right of the eldest son to take extra privileges. All sons share equally.
    
- _Comparison:_ This is a key difference with Shia Law, where the eldest son is entitled to the father's personal items (_Habua_ - sword, ring, Quran). In Sunni exams, **never** give the eldest son extra items.
