## 📌 15.5 Anomalous Cases of 'Awl and Radd ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (5/5):** Essential. In the real world, inheritance fractions rarely add up perfectly to 1. Without these two doctrines, you cannot solve about 30-40% of inheritance cases.
>- **Exam (5/5):** Highly frequent. Examiners love these problems because they test if you can do the math, not just recall the rules.
    

---

### 1️⃣ The Doctrine of 'Awl (Increase)

**Scenario:** When the sum of the fixed shares is **greater than unity (1)**.

- **The Problem:** The heirs are claiming more pie than exists. (e.g., Total claims = 1.25 of the estate).
    
- **The Solution:** We **increase the denominator** of the fractions to equal the sum of the numerators. This effectively reduces everyone's share proportionately to fit the estate.
    

**Classic Example: The "Unlucky Husband" Case**

- **Heirs:** Husband and 2 Full Sisters.
    
- **Step 1 (Assign Shares):**
    
    - Husband = $1/2$ (since no child).
        
    - 2 Sisters = $2/3$ (collective share).
        
- **Step 2 (Common Denominator):** Convert to 6.
    
    - Husband = $3/6$
        
    - Sisters = $4/6$
        
    - **Total:** $3 + 4 = 7$. (So we have $7/6$).
        
- **Step 3 (Apply 'Awl):** Change the denominator from 6 to **7**.
    
    - Husband's Share becomes **$3/7$**.
        
    - Sisters' Share becomes **$4/7$**.
        
- **Result:** $3/7 + 4/7 = 7/7 = 1$. Everyone took a slight cut, but the ratio remains fair.
    

---

### 2️⃣ The Doctrine of Radd (Return)

**Scenario:** When the sum of the shares is **less than unity (1)**, and there is **NO Residuary** to take the leftover.

- **The Problem:** After giving everyone their fixed share, there is money left over ($Surplus$).
    
- **The Solution:** The surplus is "Returned" to the Sharers in proportion to their original shares.
    

The Critical Exception (Spouse Rule):

Under classical Sunni law, the Husband and Wife do NOT share in the Return (Radd) if there is any blood relation present. They take their fixed share ($1/4$, $1/8$, etc.) and step aside. The surplus goes entirely to the blood relatives (Mother, Daughter, etc.).

**Classic Example: Mother and Daughter**

- **Heirs:** Mother and Daughter. (No Son, No Father).
    
- **Step 1 (Assign Shares):**
    
    - Mother = $1/6$ (due to daughter).
        
    - Daughter = $1/2$.
        
- **Step 2 (Common Denominator):** Convert to 6.
    
    - Mother = $1/6$
        
    - Daughter = $3/6$
        
    - **Total:** $1 + 3 = 4$. (So we have $4/6$). The Surplus is $2/6$.
        
- **Step 3 (Apply Radd):** Since there is no Residuary, we ignore the old denominator (6) and use the **Sum of Numerators (4)** as the new denominator.
    
    - Mother gets **$1/4$**.
        
    - Daughter gets **$3/4$**.
        
- **Result:** The Mother's share increased from $16.6\%$ to $25\%$, and the Daughter's from $50\%$ to $75\%$.
    

---

### 🔷 Summary of Mathematical Rules

|**Situation**|**Condition**|**Action**|
|---|---|---|
|**Perfect**|Shares sum = 1|Distribute as is.|
|**'Awl** (Increase)|Shares sum > 1|**Increase the Denominator** to match the Numerator.|
|**Radd** (Return)|Shares sum < 1 (No Residuary)|**Decrease the Denominator** to match the Numerator (excluding Spouse).|
|**Residuary**|Shares sum < 1 (Residuary exists)|Give the surplus to the Residuary (No Radd).|

### 🔷 Important Exam Tips

- **Check for Residuaries First:** Before you ever apply _Radd_, check if there is a Paternal Uncle, Brother, or distant male cousin. If _any_ Residuary exists, _Radd_ is impossible because the Residuary eats all the surplus.
    
- **Spouse in Radd:** If a problem has "Husband and Daughter," calculating Radd is tricky.
    
    - Husband gets his fixed $1/4$.
        
    - Daughter gets her fixed $1/2$ **PLUS** the remaining $1/4$ by Return.
        
    - Final: Husband ($1/4$), Daughter ($3/4$).
