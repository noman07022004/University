## Sunni Law of Inheritance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is widely considered the most difficult yet "mathematically beautiful" part of Muslim Law. The Prophet (PBUH) called it "half of all knowledge." It involves precise fractional calculations where every heir’s share is predetermined by God.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Absolutely critical. You will almost certainly face a mathematical problem question (e.g., "A dies leaving a wife, two daughters, and a father. Distribute the shares.").
    

### Introduction

The Sunni Law of Inheritance (specifically the Hanafi school) is a system of "compulsory rules." Unlike Western systems where a person can disinherit their children via a Will, in Islam, the moment a person dies, their property automatically vests in their heirs in fixed shares. The law balances the rights of the immediate family (spouse, children, parents) with the extended agnatic family (male tribal relatives).

### 1. General Principles and Competence

**Subtopics Covered:** _General Principles, Competence, Miscellaneous rules._

Before distribution begins, the estate must be "cleaned." The following steps occur in order:

1. **Funeral Expenses:** Reasonable costs for burial.
    
2. **Debts:** Payment of all unsecured and secured debts.
    
3. **Will (_Wasiyat_):** Execution of valid legacies (up to 1/3 of the remainder).
    
4. **Inheritance:** The residue is distributed among heirs.
    

**Competence (Who can inherit?):**

- **Survivorship:** The heir must be alive at the moment of the _propositus'_ (deceased's) death. A child in the womb inherits if born alive.
    
- **Exclusion (Impediments):**
    
    - **Homicide:** A person who kills the deceased (intentionally or accidentally) is totally excluded from inheritance.
        
    - **Difference of Religion:** Traditionally, a non-Muslim cannot inherit from a Muslim. However, in Bangladesh/India, the **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850** technically removed this bar, allowing a convert to inherit, though this remains a contentious point in strict Sharia practice.
        
    - **Illegitimacy:** An illegitimate child cannot inherit from the father, only from the mother.
        

### 2. Classes of Heirs

**Subtopics Covered:** _Classes of heirs, Agnatic heirs, Uterine heirs._

Sunni law divides heirs into three strict hierarchical classes. You move to the next class _only_ if the previous one doesn't exhaust the estate.

1. **Sharers (_Quranic Heirs_):**
    
    - They are the "Primary Heirs" mentioned specifically in the Quran.
        
    - They take their specific fraction (e.g., 1/2, 1/4, 1/8) first.
        
2. **Residuaries (_Asaba_ / Agnatic Heirs):**
    
    - These are usually male agnates (related wholly through males, like Son, Brother, Uncle).
        
    - They take **"what is left"** after the Sharers are paid.
        
    - _Note:_ If there are no Sharers, Residuaries take everything.
        
3. **Distant Kindred (_Dhul-Arham_ / Uterine Heirs):**
    
    - These are blood relations who are neither Sharers nor Residuaries (e.g., daughter's children, sister's children).
        
    - They inherit **only** if there are no Sharers and no Residuaries.
        

### 3. The Quranic Heirs (Sharers)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Quranic heirs._

There are **12 Sharers**. Their shares vary based on the presence of children or other relatives.

|**Heir**|**Share Scenario 1**|**Share Scenario 2**|**Condition for Change**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Husband**|**1/4**|**1/2**|Gets 1/4 if wife has children; 1/2 if no children.|
|**Wife**|**1/8**|**1/4**|Gets 1/8 if husband has children; 1/4 if no children. (Multiple wives share this).|
|**Daughter**|**1/2** (if one)|**2/3** (if 2+)|Gets specific share only if **no Son**. If Son exists, she becomes a Residuary (gets half of Son).|
|**Father**|**1/6**|**1/6 + Residue**|Gets 1/6 if there is a child (son/grandson). Gets 1/6 + Remainder if only daughters exist.|
|**Mother**|**1/6**|**1/3**|Gets 1/6 if there are children or siblings. Gets 1/3 if no children/siblings.|

_(Other Sharers include: True Grandfather, True Grandmother, Son's Daughter, Uterine Brother/Sister, Full Sister, Consanguine Sister)._

### 4. Agnatic Heirs (Residuaries)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Agnatic heirs._

The "Residuaries" are the backbone of the tribal inheritance system. They are classified into:

1. **Residuaries in their own right:** All male agnates (Son, Son's son, Father, Brother, Uncle).
    
2. **Residuaries by another:** Females who become residuaries when they coexist with a male of the same degree (e.g., Daughter becomes residuary with Son; Sister with Brother).
    
    - **The 2:1 Rule:** When inheriting as residuaries, the **male gets double the portion of the female**.
        

### 5. Anomalous Cases: 'Awl and Radd

**Subtopics Covered:** _Anomalous cases of 'awl and radd._

Sometimes, the fractions don't add up perfectly to "1" (the whole estate).

#### A. Doctrine of 'Awl (Increase)

- **Problem:** The sum of shares exceeds unity (1).
    
    - _Example:_ Husband (1/2) + 2 Sisters (2/3). Total = 7/6. (More than the property exists).
        
- **Solution ('Awl):** The denominator is increased to equal the numerator.
    
    - The shares are reduced proportionately.
        
    - _Result:_ 7/6 becomes 7 shares total. Husband gets 3/7, Sisters get 4/7.
        

#### B. Doctrine of Radd (Return)

- **Problem:** The sum of shares is less than unity (1), and there are **no Residuaries** to take the leftover.
    
    - _Example:_ Mother (1/6) + Daughter (1/2). Total = 4/6. (2/6 is left over).
        
- **Solution (Radd):** The residue is "returned" to the Sharers in proportion to their shares.
    
    - _Exception:_ The **Spouses (Husband/Wife)** do _not_ participate in the Return if other blood relations exist.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Son" Factor:** The Son is a "Super-Heir." His presence excludes many distant relatives (brothers, uncles) and converts the Daughter from a Sharer into a Residuary.
    
- **Section 4 of MFLO (The Orphaned Grandchild):** This is the **most crucial statutory modification**.
    
    - _Traditional Sharia:_ If a son dies before his father, the son's children (grandchildren) get nothing from the grandfather.
        
    - _Sec 4 MFLO:_ The orphaned grandchildren **inherit the share their parent would have received** if alive.
        
    - _Exam Warning:_ Always check if the problem mentions "Son died during father's lifetime." If yes, apply Section 4!
        
- **Spouse Rule:** The Husband or Wife is **never** excluded. They always get something (share varies, but never zero).
    
- **Math Tip:** In exams, convert everything to a common denominator (usually 12 or 24) to solve the problem easily.
