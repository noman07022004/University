## 15.6 📌 Agnatic Heirs (_Asaba_ / Residuaries) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (5/5):** The concept of "Agnates" (Male relations) defines the backbone of the tribal inheritance system Islam reformed. You must understand that after the Quranic Sharers take their small cuts, the Agnates take the bulk of the wealth.
>- **Exam (5/5):** Essential. You cannot solve a problem without knowing who the "Residuary" is. A common exam mistake is giving the residue to a Distant Kindred (like a daughter's son) when a remote Agnate (like a 2nd cousin) is present.


### 1. Definition and Nature 
"Agnatic Heirs" are legally synonymous with **Residuaries** (_Asaba_).

- **Who are they?** They are relations connected to the deceased entirely through **males**. (e.g., Son, Father, Brother, Paternal Uncle).
    
- **Role:** They do not have fixed shares (like 1/2 or 1/4). Instead, they take **"The Residue"**—whatever is left after the Sharers have been paid.
    
- **If No Sharers:** If there are no Sharers (e.g., no Wife, no Mother, no Daughter), the Agnatic Heir takes the **Whole Estate**.
    

### 2. Classification (The 4 Groups) 
The Agnates are divided into four classes. The rule of succession is strict: **Class 1 excludes** Class 2, Class 2 excludes Class 3, and so on.

#### Group I: Male Descendants (The Strongest)

1. **Son:** The primary heir. He excludes almost everyone (siblings, uncles, grandparents).
    
2. **Son's Son:** Inherits only if the Son is dead.
    

#### Group II: Male Ascendants

3. **Father:** Inherits as a Residuary if there are no descendants.
    
4. **True Grandfather:** Inherits if the Father is dead.
    

#### Group III: Male Descendants of Father (Siblings)

5. **Full Brother.**
    
6. **Consanguine Brother** (Father's side).
    
7. **Full Brother's Son** (Nephew).
    
8. **Consanguine Brother's Son.**
    
    - _Note:_ A Brother excludes a Nephew. A Full Brother excludes a Consanguine Brother.
        

#### Group IV: Male Descendants of Grandfather (Uncles)

9. **Full Paternal Uncle.**
    
10. **Consanguine Paternal Uncle.**
    
11. **Full Paternal Uncle's Son** (Cousin).
    
12. **Consanguine Paternal Uncle's Son.**
    

---

### 3. The Rules of Preference (Who blocks whom?)

When you have multiple Agnates (e.g., a Brother and an Uncle), who wins?

1. **Rule of Class:** A lower class is excluded by a higher class.
    
    - _Example:_ A **Son** (Class I) blocks a **Brother** (Class III). A **Father** (Class II) blocks an **Uncle** (Class IV).
        
2. **Rule of Degree:** Within the same class, the one nearer in degree to the deceased wins.
    
    - _Example:_ A **Son** (1 degree) blocks a **Son's Son** (2 degrees). A **Brother** blocks a **Brother's Son** (Nephew).
        
3. **Rule of Blood:** Within the same class and degree, the **Full Blood** excludes the **Half Blood** (Consanguine).
    
    - _Example:_ A **Full Brother** excludes a **Consanguine Brother**. A **Full Uncle** excludes a **Consanguine Uncle**.
        

---

### 4. The "Female Agnates" (Residuary by Another)

Normally, agnates are males. However, four specific females (who are usually Sharers) become Agnatic Heirs if they have a male counterpart of the same degree.

- **Who are they?** Daughter, Son's Daughter, Full Sister, Consanguine Sister.
    
- **The Effect:** They lose their fixed share and become Residuaries with the male.
    
- **The Ratio:** They split the residue in a **2:1 ratio** (Male gets double the female).
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Uterine Brother is NOT an Agnate:** The Uterine brother (mother's side) is a **Sharer**, not a Residuary. He gets a fixed 1/6. He never takes the residue.
    
- **The "Uncle" Trap:** In exams, "Maternal Uncle" (Mother's brother) is **Class III Distant Kindred**. "Paternal Uncle" (Father's brother) is **Class II Residuary**.
    
    - _Scenario:_ Deceased leaves Paternal Uncle and Maternal Uncle.
        
    - _Result:_ Paternal Uncle takes **Everything** (as Residuary). Maternal Uncle gets **Zero** (blocked by Residuary).
        
- **No "Representation" in Residuaries:** The rule "Nearer excludes remote" is absolute here. If a man leaves a Son and a Grandson (from a dead son), the **Son takes 100%** of the residue. The Grandson gets nothing (unless MFLO Sec 4 applies).
