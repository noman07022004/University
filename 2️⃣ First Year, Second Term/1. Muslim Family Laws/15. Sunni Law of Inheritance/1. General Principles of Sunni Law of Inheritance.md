## 📌 15.1. General Principles of Sunni Inheritance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the absolute bedrock. If you do not understand these principles (e.g., when inheritance opens, the nature of property), you will make fundamental errors in every calculation problem.
>- **Exam (5/5):** High priority. Exam questions often ask: "Discuss the general principles of Muslim Law of Inheritance and how they differ from Hindu Law" or short notes on "Vested Inheritance" vs. "Contingent Inheritance."

The Sunni Law of Inheritance (Hanafi School) is often called **'Ilm-ul-Faraiz** (The Science of Duties/Shares). It is a rigid, divine system where the shares are fixed by the Quran and cannot be altered by human will. Unlike Western or Hindu law, it operates on a specific set of juridical rules that define _what_ can be inherited and _when_.

#### 1. Nature of Heritable Property (No Distinction)

In many legal systems (like Hindu Mitakshara Law), there is a sharp distinction between "Ancestral Property" (grandfather's land) and "Self-Acquired Property" (father's salary).

- **The Muslim Law Rule:** There is **NO distinction** between ancestral and self-acquired property, or between movable and immovable property.
    
- **The Estate:** All property belonging to the deceased—whether he bought it yesterday or inherited it from his great-grandfather—is lumped together into one single "Heritable Estate."
    
- **Deductions First:** The "Heritable Estate" is what remains _only after_ three things are paid:
    
    1. Funeral expenses.
        
    2. Debts (including unpaid Dower/Mahr).
        
    3. Valid Wills (up to 1/3rd).
        

#### 2. When Inheritance Opens (_Nemo est haeres viventis_)

This is the most critical conceptual difference from Hindu Law.

- **The Rule:** Inheritance opens **only upon the death** of the ancestor.
    
- **No Birthright:** A child has **no right** to the property of the parents merely by birth. While the father is alive, the son cannot claim a share or prevent the father from selling the property.
    
- **Spes Successionis:** The expectation of an heir to succeed to property (e.g., "I will inherit this when Dad dies") is a mere hope (_Spes Successionis_). It is not a legal right and cannot be sold or transferred. If a son sells his "future share" while his father is alive, the sale is **void**.
    

#### 3. Doctrine of Representation (The Conflict)

Does a grandchild inherit if their parent died before the grandparent?

- **Classical Sharia Rule (The Exclusion):** Muslim law traditionally rejects the "Doctrine of Representation." The rule is: **The nearer excludes the more remote.**
    
    - _Example:_ If A has a son (S1) and a grandson (GS) from a deceased son (S2). When A dies, S1 takes the whole property. GS gets **nothing** because S1 is nearer in degree.
        
- **Bangladeshi Statutory Change (MFLO 1961):**
    
    - **Section 4 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961** has overridden the general principle in Bangladesh.
        
    - **Current Rule:** Grandchildren (children of a predeceased son or daughter) **DO inherit**. They step into the shoes of their deceased parent and take the share that parent would have received if alive.
        

#### 4. Joint Tenancy vs. Tenancy in Common

When multiple heirs inherit property, how do they hold it?

- **Tenancy in Common:** Heirs in Muslim law are **Tenants in Common**, not Joint Tenants.
    
- **Meaning:** Each heir has a specific, defined share (e.g., 1/8th, 1/4th) in every particle of the property. There is **no right of survivorship**.
    
    - _Contrast:_ In a Joint Tenancy (English concept), if one co-owner dies, his share goes to the other co-owners. In Muslim law, if an heir dies, his share passes to _his own_ heirs, not to his siblings.
        

#### 5. Gender Disparity (Rule of _Tasib_)

- **The 2:1 Ratio:** The general principle is that a male gets **double the share** of a female of the same degree and class.
    
    - _Quranic Verse 4:11:_ "Allah charges you concerning (the provision for) your children: to the male the equivalent of the portion of two females."
        
    - _Reasoning:_ In Islamic social structure, the male has the sole financial obligation to maintain the family (wife, children, parents), whereas the female has no financial burden and keeps her property/dower for herself.
        
- **Exception:** This applies only to residuary heirs (Son/Daughter, Brother/Sister). Uterine brothers and sisters (same mother, different father) share **equally** (1:1).
    

#### 6. Rule of Primogeniture (Rejected)

- **Equality of Sons:** Muslim law strictly **rejects** Primogeniture (the right of the eldest son to inherit the estate or a larger share).
    
- All sons, whether eldest or youngest, whether born of a first wife or fourth wife, inherit **equally**.
    

#### 7. Total vs. Partial Exclusion

General principles dictate that heirs can be blocked in two ways:

- **Total Exclusion:** Being completely blocked from inheritance.
    
    - _By Person:_ A son blocks a grandson. A father blocks a brother.
        
    - _By Impediment:_ A murderer is totally excluded. A non-Muslim is totally excluded (subject to statutory laws).
        
- **Partial Exclusion:** Having one's share reduced.
    
    - _Example:_ A husband's share reduces from 1/2 to 1/4 if there is a child. A mother's share reduces from 1/3 to 1/6 if there are children.

### 🔷 Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Vested" Trick:** If a question asks, "When does the right of inheritance vest?", the answer is **"At the moment of death."** Even if the distribution happens 10 years later, the ownership theoretically transferred the second the ancestor died. If an heir dies _after_ the ancestor but _before_ distribution, their share still passes to their own heirs.
    
- **Illegitimacy:** Under Sunni general principles, an illegitimate child inherits **only from the mother** and her relations, never from the father. (Under Shia law, they inherit from neither).
    
- **Step-Children:** Step-children (children of a spouse from a previous marriage) are **not heirs**. They fall under no category of inheritance. They can only receive property via Will (up to 1/3rd) or Gift.
