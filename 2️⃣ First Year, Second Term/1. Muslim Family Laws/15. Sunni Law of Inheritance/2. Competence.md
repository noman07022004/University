

## 📌 15.2. Competence (Impediments to Inheritance) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (5/5):** You cannot distribute the estate until you filter out the disqualified heirs. Understanding who is "barred" is just as important as knowing who is an heir.
>- **Exam (4/5):** Problem questions often include a "trick" character—like a son who killed the father or a non-Muslim relative—to test if you know they should be excluded before doing the math.

### 🔷 Who is Qualified to Inherit?

In Sunni law, for a person to be competent to inherit, two primary conditions must be met:

1. **Existence:** The heir must be **alive** at the moment the ancestor dies. (This includes a child in the womb, provided they are born alive later).
    
2. **No Impediment:** The heir must not be disqualified by any specific legal bar (_Mani_).
    

If a legal heir is disqualified, they are treated as **non-existent** (civilly dead). They do not inherit, nor do they block others (in most cases).

### 🔷 The Major Impediments (Disqualifications)

#### 1. Homicide (_Qatl_)

The Prophet (PBUH) said: _"The killer shall not inherit."_ This rule prevents someone from killing a relative to hasten their inheritance.

- **Hanafi Law (Strict Rule):** A person who causes the death of the propositus—whether **intentionally or unintentionally** (e.g., through negligence)—is excluded from inheritance.
    
    - _Example:_ A son accidentally runs over his father with a car. Under strict Hanafi law, the son is excluded.
        
- **Exception:** If the killing was purely accidental (with no negligence) or justifiable (e.g., self-defense or carrying out a legal execution), some jurists allow inheritance, but the safest exam answer for Hanafi law is **exclusion**.
    
- **Effect on Others:** The killer is treated as dead. He does not inherit, nor does he exclude others.
    

#### 2. Difference of Religion (_Ikhtilaf al-Din_)

- **Strict Sharia Rule:** A non-Muslim cannot inherit from a Muslim, and a Muslim cannot inherit from a non-Muslim.
    
- **Statutory Override (Bangladesh):** The **Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850** changed this landscape specifically for _apostasy_.
    
    - **The Rule:** If a Muslim converts to another religion (apostasy), this Act protects their property rights. They do **not** lose their right to inherit from their Muslim relatives.
        
    - **Clarification:** However, the law generally remains that a person _born_ and raised in a different faith does not inherit from a Muslim relative under Muslim personal law. The Act primarily protects the _convert_.
        

#### 3. Illegitimacy (_Walad-uz-Zina_)

- **Father's Side:** An illegitimate child has **no right** to inherit from the putative father or the father's relations. The father also cannot inherit from the child.
    
- **Mother's Side:** Under Sunni law, an illegitimate child **CAN inherit** from the mother and her relations (and vice versa). The law recognizes the maternity, but not the paternity.
    
    - _Contrast with Shia Law:_ In Shia law, an illegitimate child is "Nullius Filius" (child of no one) and inherits from _neither_ parent.
        

#### 4. Slavery

Historically, a slave could not inherit property. This is now obsolete in Bangladesh due to the abolition of slavery.

### 🔷 Special Cases regarding Competence

#### A. The Unborn Child (Child in the Womb)

A child in the womb (_Hamal_) is considered competent to inherit, provided:

1. The child was conceived **before** the death of the propositus.
    
2. The child is **born alive**.
    

- **Procedure:** A share is reserved for the unborn child during distribution. Usually, the share of **one son** or **one daughter** (whichever is larger/safer) is set aside until birth. If born dead (stillborn), the share is returned to the other heirs.
    

#### B. Missing Person (_Mafqud_)

If an heir is missing at the time the inheritance opens:

- **The Rule:** Their share is **reserved**. It is not given to others immediately.
    
- **Presumption of Death:** Under the **Evidence Act, 1872 (Section 108)**, a person is presumed dead only if unheard of for **7 years**. Until then, they are presumed alive for the purpose of receiving inheritance, but presumed dead regarding their _own_ property (i.e., their own heirs cannot inherit from them yet).
    

#### C. Simultaneous Death (_Commorientes_)

If two relatives die in a common disaster (e.g., plane crash, boat sinking) and it is impossible to determine who died first:

- **The Rule:** Neither inherits from the other. Both are treated as if they died at the exact same instant. The estate of each is distributed to their _other_ living heirs.
    
    - _Example:_ Father and Son die in a fire. The Father's property goes to the Mother/Daughter, not to the Son (because we can't prove the Son survived the Father).

### 🔷 Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Killer" Trick:** If an exam problem says "A dies, leaving a Son who murdered him and a Brother," do not give the Son a share.
    
    - _Calculation:_ Son = Excluded (0). Brother = Inherits the whole estate. (Normally, a Son would block the Brother, but since the killer is treated as non-existent, the Brother inherits).
        
- **Insanity is NOT a Bar:** Mental unsoundness or physical deformity (blindness, deafness) is **not** an impediment in Muslim law. An insane son inherits just like a sane son.
    
- **Step-Relations:** A step-son is not "incompetent" in the sense of a bar; he is simply **not an heir** at all. There is a difference between being an heir who is disqualified (like a killer) and not being an heir in the first place.
