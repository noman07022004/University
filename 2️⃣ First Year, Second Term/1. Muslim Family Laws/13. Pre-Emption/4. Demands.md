## 13.4 Demands (_Talabs_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** Pre-emption is legally described as a "feeble" (weak) right. It stands or falls entirely on procedure. If you understand the _concepts_ of claimant and priority but fail to understand the _formalities_ of the demands, the entire claim is void.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** The single most frequent problem question area. Examiners love scenarios like: _"He heard the news, finished his lunch, and then claimed pre-emption. Is the claim valid?"_ (Answer: No).
    

---

###  The Three Formalities

Because the right of pre-emption restricts the freedom of contract (stopping an owner from selling to whom they please), Muslim Law insists on the strictest possible observance of formalities. The intention to claim must be declared clearly and instantly.

These formalities consist of three specific demands, known as **Talabs**. They must be performed in a specific chronological order.

#### 1. First Demand: _Talab-i-Mowasibat_ (The Immediate Demand)

- **Meaning:** "Mowasibat" literally means "jumping up." It implies an act performed with extreme urgency.
    
- **The Rule:** The pre-emptor must assert his claim **immediately** upon receiving the information of the sale.
    
- **Timing:** The demand must be made instantaneously.
    
    - If he is sitting, he must declare it before he stands up.
        
    - If he is reading a letter containing the news, he must declare it before he folds the letter.
        
    - _Case Law:_ A delay of even **12 hours** or waiting to verify the price has been held to invalidate the claim.
        
- **Form:** No specific formula is necessary, but the intention must be clear. Words like _"I do claim my right of pre-emption"_ or _"I am the Shafi"_ are sufficient.
    
- **Witnesses:** Witnesses are **not** strictly required for this first demand (though helpful for proof), but the act itself must be immediate.
    

#### 2. Second Demand: _Talab-i-Ishhad_ (The Confirming Demand)

- **Meaning:** "Ishhad" means "invocation of witnesses."
    
- **The Rule:** The pre-emptor must, with the least practicable delay after the first demand, affirm his intention in the presence of **two witnesses**.
    
- **Distinct Requirements (Crucial for Exams):**
    
    1. **Venue:** This demand must be made in one of three places:
        
        - On the premises (the land being sold).
            
        - In the presence of the Seller.
            
        - In the presence of the Buyer.
            
    2. **Reference:** The pre-emptor **MUST explicitly refer** to his first demand (_Talab-i-Mowasibat_). He must say: _"I have already claimed pre-emption, and I now call upon you to bear witness."_
        
- **Fatal Error:** If the pre-emptor makes the demand before witnesses but **forgets to mention** that he already made the first immediate demand, the entire claim fails.
    

#### 3. Third Demand: _Talab-i-Tamlik_ (Demand by Suit)

- **Meaning:** "Tamlik" means "possession."
    
- **The Rule:** If the buyer refuses to hand over the property after the first two demands, the pre-emptor must enforce his right by filing a formal **Civil Suit** in court.
    
- **Limitation:** Under the **Limitation Act, 1908** (Article 10), the suit must be filed within **1 year** from the date the purchaser takes possession of the property (or from the date of registration).

### Statutory Conflict: The "No Talab" Rule in Bangladesh

This is the most critical distinction for a Bangladeshi law student.

**A. Under the State Acquisition and Tenancy Act (SAT Act), 1950**

- **Context:** For **Agricultural Land**.
    
- **The Change:** The strict rule of _Talabs_ (jumping up, shouting immediately) is **abolished**.
    
- **Procedure:** The right is exercised simply by filing an **application** to the Court within the statutory period (2 months from notice or 2 months from knowledge).
    
- **Implication:** You do not need to make _Talab-i-Mowasibat_ or _Ishhad_ for agricultural land. The application itself is the demand.
    

**B. Under the Non-Agricultural Tenancy Act, 1949**

- **Context:** For **Urban/Non-Agri Land**.
    
- **Procedure:** Similar to the SAT Act, it requires an application within the limitation period (4 months). The formalities of Muslim Law Talabs are generally replaced by the statutory application procedure.
    

**C. When do Talabs still apply?**

- They apply in cases where the statutory laws (SAT Act/NATA) are silent or inapplicable, or in specific urban scenarios involving pre-emption between Muslim co-sharers on grounds not covered by the tenancy acts (though these are rare and legally complex). For academic purposes, you must know the Talabs for "Pure Muslim Law" questions.

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Valid Sale" Prerequisite:** The demands are only valid if the sale is **complete**.
    
    - _Scenario:_ A hears a rumor that B _intends_ to sell. A shouts "I claim Shufa!" Next week, B actually sells.
        
    - _Result:_ A's claim is **void**. The demand was premature. He must make the demand _after_ the sale is complete and he hears of it.
        
- **Agent:** The demands can be made by the pre-emptor's authorized agent or manager if the pre-emptor is away or unable.
    
- **Death:** If the pre-emptor dies after making the demands but _before_ filing the suit (_Talab-i-Tamlik_):
    
    - _Hanafi Law:_ The right of pre-emption is **extinguished** (it dies with the person).
        
    - _Shafi/Shia Law:_ The right descends to the heirs.
