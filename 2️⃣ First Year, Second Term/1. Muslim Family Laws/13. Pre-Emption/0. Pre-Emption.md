## Pre-Emption (_Shufa_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic introduces a unique concept in Islamic property law—restricting a person's right to sell their own land to whomever they please. It balances individual property rights against community cohesion and privacy.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Frequently tested, especially the procedural "Demands" (_Talabs_) and the conflict between traditional Muslim Law and the Statutory Law (SAT Act) in Bangladesh.
    

### Introduction

Pre-emption, or _Shufa_, is the right of a person to step into the shoes of a stranger who has purchased some immovable property. Essentially, if A sells his land to C (a stranger), B (A's neighbor or partner) has the right to force C to sell the land to him for the same price. The objective is to prevent the introduction of a stranger into a shared property or neighborhood, thereby preserving privacy and preventing annoyance (_Drar_). It is a "weak right" in the eyes of the law, meaning it is lost if the strict formalities are not followed instantly.

### 1. Concept and Claimants

**Subtopics Covered:** _Concept, Claimant, Priority._

The person who claims the right of pre-emption is called the _Shafi_. Under traditional Muslim Law, there are three categories of claimants, listed in order of priority:

#### A. Shafi-i-Sharik (Co-sharer in the Corpus)

- **Definition:** A person who is an owner of an undivided share in the property itself (e.g., two brothers owning a house jointly).
    
- **Priority:** They have the **highest** right. No one can defeat their claim.
    

#### B. Shafi-i-Khalit (Participator in Appendages)

- **Definition:** A person who does not own a share in the property but shares a special right or "appendage" connected to it, such as a **Right of Way** (shared private road) or **Right of Water** (shared drain/irrigation channel).
    
- **Priority:** They come **second**. They can claim only if no _Shafi-i-Sharik_ claims.
    

#### C. Shafi-i-Jar (Owner of Adjoining Property)

- **Definition:** A mere neighbor whose property touches the property being sold, but who shares no common amenities.
    
- **Priority:** They have the **lowest** right. They can claim only if the first two categories do not.
    
- **Significance:** This category is unique to Muslim Law and is generally _not_ recognized under modern statutory laws in Bangladesh (discussed below).
    

### 2. The Formalities: Demands (_Talabs_)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Demands._

Since Pre-emption restricts the seller's freedom of contract, the law imposes incredibly strict procedural hurdles. If the claimant delays even for a second, the right is lost. There are three mandatory demands:

1. **Talab-i-Mawasibat (The Jumping Demand/Immediate Demand):**
    
    - The moment the pre-emptor hears the news of the sale, he must declare his intention to claim **immediately** (e.g., shouting "I claim Shufa!").
        
    - _Delay:_ Even a delay of 12 hours or waiting to verify the price is fatal to the claim. It must be instantaneous.
        
2. **Talab-i-Ishhad (The Confirming Demand):**
    
    - After the immediate demand, the pre-emptor must confirm it with witnesses.
        
    - He must go to the seller, the buyer, or the land itself, and declare: _"I have claimed Shufa, and I now confirm it in the presence of these witnesses."_
        
    - This must be done "as soon as practicable" after the first demand.
        
3. **Talab-i-Tamlik (The Demand of Possession/Suit):**
    
    - If the buyer refuses to hand over the land, the pre-emptor must file a civil suit in court to enforce his right.
        

### 3. Statutory Provisions (The Bangladeshi Context)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory Provisions._

In Bangladesh, the practical application of Pre-emption is governed more by statute than by pure Muslim Law.

#### A. The State Acquisition and Tenancy Act (SAT Act), 1950 (Section 96)

This applies to **Agricultural Land**.

- **Who can claim:** Only **Co-sharer tenants** (by inheritance or purchase) and tenants holding contiguous land.
    
- **Difference from Muslim Law:**
    
    - It creates a statutory right irrespective of religion.
        
    - It outlines a specific application procedure (filing within 2 months of notice or 4 months of knowledge).
        
    - The strict "Jumping Demand" (_Talab-i-Mawasibat_) is **not required**.
        

#### B. The Non-Agricultural Tenancy Act, 1949 (Section 24)

This applies to **Urban/Non-Agricultural Land**.

- **Who can claim:** Only **Co-sharer tenants**.
    
- **Neighbours Excluded:** Unlike Muslim Law (_Shafi-i-Jar_), a mere neighbor generally cannot claim pre-emption under this statute unless they are a co-sharer.
    

#### C. Conflict: Muslim Law vs. Statute

- If a case is filed under **Muslim Law**, the strict _Talabs_ must be proven.
    
- If a case is filed under the **SAT Act**, the statutory limitation periods apply.
    
- **Judicial View:** For agricultural land, Section 96 of the SAT Act is the complete code, effectively superseding Muslim Law pre-emption rules.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Talab" Trap:** In problem questions involving _Muslim Law_ pre-emption, look for the "delay." If the problem says: _"A heard the news of the sale, went home, thought about it, and then claimed pre-emption,"_ the claim is **VOID**. It must be instant (_Mawasibat_).
    
- **Neighbour's Right:** Be careful with "Neighbours" (_Shafi-i-Jar_).
    
    - Under **Muslim Law**, a neighbor _can_ pre-empt.
        
    - Under **Statutory Law** (SAT Act/NAT Act), a mere neighbor usually _cannot_ pre-empt (unless they are a contiguous tenant under SAT Act s.96, but this is technical).
        
    - _Exam Tip:_ Unless the question specifically mentions "Under the State Acquisition Act," apply the Muslim Law principles of _Shafi-i-Jar_.
        
- **Subject Matter:** Pre-emption applies only to the sale of full ownership (_Milk_). It does not apply to a lease, gift (without exchange), or charity.
