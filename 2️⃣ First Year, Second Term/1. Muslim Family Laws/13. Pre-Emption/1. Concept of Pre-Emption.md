

## 13.1 Pre-Emption (_Shufa_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (3/5):** A highly technical topic. It represents a restriction on the free transfer of property, designed to prevent strangers from entering a neighborhood or family estate.
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Frequently tested on the _procedure_ ("Did he make the demand fast enough?") and the conflict between Muslim Law and Bangladeshi Statutes (SAT Act).
    


***1. The Concept and Juridical Nature***

- **Definition:** _Shufa_ (Pre-emption) literally means "conjunction" or "adjaceny." Legally, it is a right which the owner of an immovable property possesses to acquire by purchase another immovable property which has been sold to another person.
    
- **Substitution, Not Repurchase:** It is legally defined as a right of **substitution**, not re-purchase. The pre-emptor (_Shafi_) stands in the shoes of the original buyer. They take the property for the _same price_ and on the _same terms_ that the buyer paid.
    
- **Objective:** The primary object is to prevent the introduction of a stranger/outsider into the co-ownership or neighborhood, preserving privacy and preventing the fragmentation of property.
    
- **Nature of Right:** It is described as a "feeble" (weak) right. Because it restricts the owner's freedom to sell to whom they please, the law requires strict adherence to formalities. A slight delay or error in procedure defeats the right.
    

***2. The Three Classes of Claimants (Who can claim?)***

Under classical Muslim Law, the right belongs to three categories of persons, in strictly hierarchical order:

1. **Shafi-i-Sharik (Co-sharer in the property):** A person who owns an undivided share in the property itself (e.g., two brothers owning a field jointly). This is the strongest claim.
    
2. **Shafi-i-Khalit (Participator in Appendages):** A person who does not own the land itself but shares a specific right or appendage connected to it, such as a **Right of Way** (common road) or **Right of Water** (common drain/source).
    
3. **Shafi-i-Jar (Owner of Adjoining Property):** A simple neighbor whose property shares a fixed boundary wall or fence with the sold property. This is the weakest claim.
    

***3. Priority of Claim***

- **The Rule:** The right is exercised in the order listed above.
    
    - A _Sharik_ excludes a _Khalit_.
        
    - A _Khalit_ excludes a _Jar_.
        
- **Equal Class:** If there are multiple claimants of the _same_ class (e.g., two neighbors on different sides), they generally share the right equally (_per capita_) in Sunni Law, regardless of the size of their own properties.

### The Three Demands (_Talabs_) – The Procedural Strictness

The most critical exam point is the **procedure**. Since the right is "weak," it is lost if these demands are not performed instantly and correctly.

**1. First Demand: _Talab-i-Mowasibat_ (Jumping Demand)**

- **Timing:** It must be made **immediately** upon hearing the news of the sale. "Immediately" is literal—if you are sitting, you must declare it before you stand up. If you read the letter of news, you must declare it before folding the letter.
    
- **Form:** A clear declaration like "I claim Shufa!" or "I am the Pre-emptor!".
    
- **Effect:** A delay of even a few seconds without excuse can invalidate the claim.
    

**2. Second Demand: _Talab-i-Ishhad_ (Demand with Witnesses)**

- **Timing:** Must be made as soon as practicable after the first demand.
    
- **Procedure:** The pre-emptor must assert his claim in the presence of **two witnesses**.
    
- **Venue:** This demand must be made either:
    
    - On the premises (the land sold), OR
        
    - In the presence of the seller, OR
        
    - In the presence of the buyer.
        
- **Crucial Reference:** He must explicitly refer to his First Demand ("I have already claimed Shufa..."). If he forgets to mention the first demand, the second demand is void.
    

**3. Third Demand: _Talab-i-Tamlik_ (Enforcement by Suit)**

- If the buyer refuses to hand over the land, the pre-emptor must file a civil suit in court to enforce the right.

### Statutory Provisions in Bangladesh (The Conflict)

In Bangladesh, statutory laws have largely superseded classical Muslim Law for most lands. You must distinguish between **Agricultural** and **Non-Agricultural** land.

**1. The State Acquisition and Tenancy Act (SAT Act), 1950 (Section 96)**

- **Scope:** Applies to **Agricultural Land**.
    
- **Major Modification:** It drastically restricts the claimants.
    
    - **Who can claim?** ONLY a **co-sharer tenant by inheritance**.
        
    - **Who CANNOT claim?** Neighbors (_Shafi-i-Jar_) and strangers who bought into the property (co-sharers by purchase) are **totally excluded**.
        
- **Time Limit:** Application must be made within **2 months** of notice (or 2 months from knowledge if no notice, subject to a 3-year cap from registration).
    
- **Compensation:** The pre-emptor must deposit the sale price + **25% compensation** (formerly 10%) + 8% simple interest.
    

**2. The Non-Agricultural Tenancy Act, 1949 (Section 24)**

- **Scope:** Applies to **Non-Agricultural Land** (urban plots, etc.).
    
- **Claimants:** Only **co-sharer tenants**. Neighbors generally have no right under this section.
    
- **Time Limit:** **4 months** from the date of knowledge.
    
- **Saving Clause:** Section 24(10) states that "Nothing in this section shall take away the right of pre-emption conferred on any person by Muhammadan Law". This implies that in urban areas, if the Act doesn't apply (e.g., for a neighbor claim), one _might_ arguably fall back on Muslim Law, though courts are reluctant.
    

### Summary of Application in Bangladesh

|**Feature**|**Classical Muslim Law**|**SAT Act, 1950 (Agri Land)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Claimant**|Co-sharer, Participator, **Neighbor**|**Co-sharer by Inheritance ONLY**|
|**Formalities**|3 Talabs (Instant + Witnesses)|Statutory Application (No Talabs)|
|**Neighbor Right**|**Yes** (Shafi-i-Jar)|**NO** (Abolished)|

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Sale" Requirement:** Pre-emption arises **only** on a valid, complete, and permanent **Sale** or exchange.
    
    - It does _not_ arise in cases of Gift (_Hiba_), Charity (_Sadaqah_), Inheritance, or Lease.
        
    - _Exam Trick:_ "A gifts his land to B. Can the neighbor claim pre-emption?" $\rightarrow$ **No.**
        
- **The "Delay" Trap:** In an exam problem, if the claimant hears the news, goes home to check his bank balance, and _then_ claims Shufa $\rightarrow$ **The Right is Lost.** The First Demand must be instantaneous.
    
- **Sectarian Conflict:** If the Seller is Sunni and the Neighbor is Shia (who doesn't recognize neighbor pre-emption), the **Law of the Seller** usually prevails in the subcontinent. Claim fails.
