## 13.3 Priority of Pre-Emption ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** Crucial for solving "competition" problems where multiple people want to buy the same land. You need to know who stands first in line.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A favorite for problem questions. Example: _"A stranger buys land. The neighbor and the co-owner both claim pre-emption. Who wins?"_
    


When multiple persons claim the right of pre-emption (_Shufa_) over the same sale, the law does not give them equal standing. There is a strict hierarchy. The right is exercised in accordance with the **Class** of the claimant and their **Degree** of relationship to the property.

#### 1. Priority Among Different Classes (The General Rule)

Under classical Sunni Law, the priority follows the order of the three classes we discussed. The "Stronger" right completely excludes the "Weaker" right.

1. **First Priority:** **Shafi-i-Sharik** (Co-sharer in the property).
    
    - _If a Co-sharer claims, the Neighbor gets NOTHING._
        
2. **Second Priority:** **Shafi-i-Khalit** (Participator in Appendages/Easements).
    
    - _Inherits only if no Co-sharer claims._
        
3. **Third Priority:** **Shafi-i-Jar** (Neighbor/Adjacent Owner).
    
    - _Inherits only if no Co-sharer AND no Khalit claims._
        

- **Scenario:** A sells his share of a house to a stranger. B is a co-sharer. C is a neighbor. Both claim pre-emption.
    
    - **Result:** B (Co-sharer) takes the **whole** property. C (Neighbor) gets **zero**.
        

---

#### 2. Priority Within the Same Class (Competition)

What happens if there are multiple claimants from the _same_ class? (e.g., Two different neighbors or two co-sharers).

**A. Sunni Law Rule: Per Capita (By Heads)**

- **The Rule:** If there are multiple claimants of the same class, the right of pre-emption is divided **equally among them (Per Capita)**, regardless of the size of their property shares.
    
- **Example:**
    
    - A sells his share.
        
    - Claimants: B (owns 1/2 of property) and C (owns 1/6 of property).
        
    - **Result:** B and C get **equal shares** (1/2 of the sold portion each). B's larger ownership stake does _not_ give him a larger pre-emption right.
        

**B. Shia Law Rule: Pro Rata (By Shares)**

- **The Rule:** If there are two co-sharers (remember, Shia law generally rejects pre-emption if there are more than two), they claim in **proportion to their existing shares** in the property.
    
- **Example:** B (owns 2/3) and C (owns 1/3). B would get 2/3 of the sold land, and C would get 1/3.
    

**C. Special Rule for "Khalit" (Easements)**

- Within the class of _Shafi-i-Khalit_, a person with a **Specific/Special Right** is preferred over a person with a **General Right**.
    
    - _Example:_ A person who shares a private right of way (closer connection) takes precedence over a person who only shares a water drain.
        
- **The "Nearer" Principle:** The participator "nearer" to the property sold excludes the more remote one.

#### 3. Statutory Priority in Bangladesh (The Conflict)

In Bangladesh, statutes have codified distinct priority rules that override Muslim Law for specific land types.

**A. Agricultural Land (SAT Act, 1950)**

- **Current Law (Post-2006 Amendment):**
    
    - The right is restricted **strictly** to **Co-sharer tenants by inheritance**.
        
    - Neighbors and Co-sharers by purchase are **excluded**.
        
- **Competition:** If multiple co-sharers by inheritance apply, the Court apportions the land among them in a manner it deems **"equitable"** (often based on their existing possession or necessity).
    

B. Non-Agricultural Land (NATA, 1949)

For urban land governed by this Act, there is a clear statutory hierarchy:

1. **First:** Co-sharer tenants by **Inheritance**.
    
2. **Second:** Co-sharer tenants by **Purchase**.
    
3. **Third:** Contiguous tenants (Neighbors) – _Note: This right is limited and often contested in urban areas._

### Summary Table: Who Wins?

|**Scenario**|**Sunni Law Winner**|**Bangladesh Statute (Agri Land) Winner**|
|---|---|---|
|**Co-sharer vs. Neighbor**|**Co-sharer** takes all.|**Co-sharer** (Neighbor has no right).|
|**Two Neighbors (Equal)**|**Split Equally** (50/50).|**N/A** (No right).|
|**Big Owner vs. Small Owner**|**Split Equally** (Per Capita).|**Court decides** (Equitable/Proportional).|

### Important Exam Tip

- **The "Stranger" Factor:** A common exam question asks: _"A sells land to B (a Co-sharer). Can C (a Neighbor) pre-empt?"_
    
    - **Answer:** **NO.** Pre-emption arises to stop a _stranger_. If the buyer is already a co-sharer (who has a superior right), a lower-class claimant (Neighbor) cannot kick him out.
