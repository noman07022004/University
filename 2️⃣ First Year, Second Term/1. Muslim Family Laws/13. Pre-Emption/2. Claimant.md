## 13.2 Claimant (_Shafi_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** Identifying the correct claimant is the first step in any pre-emption problem. If you identify the wrong person (e.g., a neighbor in a statutory case), the entire answer fails.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Essential. Exam scenarios almost always involve multiple people (a brother, a neighbor, and a stranger) trying to buy land, and you must identify who has the legal standing to claim.
    

---

### Who Can Claim Pre-Emption?

In Muslim Law, the right of pre-emption (_Haq-i-Shufa_) is not open to everyone. It is a strictly limited right belonging only to specific persons who are inconvenienced by the sale of property to a stranger. The person entitled to claim this right is called the **Shafi** (Claimant).

Under classical Sunni (Hanafi) Law, there are **three distinct classes** of claimants. To be a valid claimant, a person must hold a specific interest in the property being sold or the property adjoining it.

#### 1. Shafi-i-Sharik (Co-sharer in the Property)

This is the strongest class of claimant.

- **Definition:** A person who is a co-owner of an **undivided share** in the subject property itself.
    
- **Scenario:** A and B jointly own a piece of land (e.g., inherited from their father). The land has not been partitioned (divided by metes and bounds). If A sells his share to a stranger, B is the _Shafi-i-Sharik_.
    
- **Rationale:** If a stranger enters a joint property, they can disrupt the privacy and management of the co-owner.
    

#### 2. Shafi-i-Khalit (Participator in Immunities/Appendages)

This class is unique to Muslim Law and often confused with neighbors.

- **Definition:** A person who is **not** a co-owner of the land itself but shares a specific **right of easement** or appendage connected to the property, such as:
    
    - **Right of Way (_Haq-i-Marur_):** Sharing a private road or alleyway.
        
    - **Right of Water (_Haq-i-Majra_):** Sharing a private water channel or drain.
        
- **Scenario:** House X and House Y are separate properties, but they both use the same private alleyway to reach the main road. If House X is sold, the owner of House Y can claim pre-emption as a _Shafi-i-Khalit_.
    
- **Note:** Sharing a public road or public river does not create this right.
    

#### 3. Shafi-i-Jar (Owner of Adjoining Property)

This is the weakest class, often referred to simply as the "Neighbor."

- **Definition:** A person who owns property that is strictly **adjacent** (sharing a boundary wall or fence) to the property being sold, but has no joint ownership or shared easements.
    
- **Scenario:** House A backs onto House B. They share a back fence. If House A is sold, the owner of House B is the _Shafi-i-Jar_.
    
- **Constraint:** This right extends only to immovable property (houses, small plots). It generally does not apply to large estates (_Zamindaris_) or large tracts of land.

### Statutory Modification in Bangladesh (Crucial Context)

While the above three classes exist in Sharia, the laws of Bangladesh have significantly narrowed who can be a claimant for certain types of land.

A. Agricultural Land (State Acquisition & Tenancy Act, 1950)

For agricultural land, Section 96 of the SAT Act overrides Muslim Law.

- **Eligible Claimant:** **ONLY** a "Co-sharer tenant by inheritance."
    
- **Excluded:**
    
    - Neighbors (_Shafi-i-Jar_) are totally excluded.
        
    - Co-sharers by purchase (strangers who bought in) are excluded.
        
    - Participators in appendages (_Shafi-i-Khalit_) are excluded unless they are also co-sharers.
        

**B. Non-Agricultural Land (Non-Agricultural Tenancy Act, 1949)**

- **Eligible Claimant:** Generally only **Co-sharer tenants**.
    
- **Neighbor Status:** The right of a simple neighbor is generally not recognized under this statute, though in urban areas not covered by specific tenancy rules, one might argue Muslim Law applies (a complex legal grey area).

### Shia Law Distinction

If the exam problem specifies the parties are **Shia**, the list of claimants changes:

1. **Co-sharers Only:** Shia law **only** recognizes the _Shafi-i-Sharik_ (Co-sharer).
    
2. **No Neighbors:** It explicitly **rejects** the right of the _Shafi-i-Khalit_ and _Shafi-i-Jar_.
    
3. **Two-Person Limit:** Some Shia schools argue pre-emption only arises if there are exactly _two_ co-sharers. If there are three or more, the right is lost.

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The Tenant Trap:** A "Tenant" (renter) is **NOT** a _Shafi_. Only the **owner** of the property can claim pre-emption. If you rent the house next door, you cannot pre-empt the sale.
    
- **Stranger Purchaser:** If the buyer is _also_ a _Shafi_ (e.g., a co-sharer sells to another co-sharer), a neighbor cannot jump in. The right of pre-emption is usually meant to stop a **stranger**, not to stop an existing insider from buying more.
