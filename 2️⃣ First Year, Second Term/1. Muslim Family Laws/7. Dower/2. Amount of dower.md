
## 7.2 Amount of Dower ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Knowing the limits (or lack thereof) is essential for drafting a _Kabinnama_. It protects clients from agreeing to invalid amounts (too low) or realistic amounts (too high).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3.5/5):** Questions often focus on the "Minimum Limit" in Hanafi law or whether a court can reduce an "Excessive Dower."
    

---

### The Financial Quantum

One of the most unique features of Islamic Law regarding Dower is the vast freedom given to the parties to fix the amount. Unlike modern statutes that might set fixed tariffs, Sharia allows the amount to range from a token sum to a king's ransom, subject to very few restrictions.

#### 1. The Minimum Limit

The law sets a floor to ensure the dower has some value and is not made a mockery of.

- **Hanafi Law (Bangladesh):** The minimum amount of dower is **10 Dirhams**.
    
    - _Historical Context:_ A Dirham was a silver coin. 10 Dirhams roughly equaled 3–4 tolas of silver.
        
    - _Legal Effect:_ If the parties agree to a sum _less_ than 10 Dirhams (e.g., 5 Dirhams), the agreement is not void, but the law automatically raises it to **10 Dirhams**.
        
- **Maliki Law:** The minimum is **3 Dirhams**.
    
- **Shia Law:** There is **no fixed minimum** amount. It must simply be something of value (_Mutaqawwam_). Even a Quranic verse taught to the wife or a handful of dates can serve as Mahr.
    

**Bangladeshi Context:** In modern Bangladesh, 10 Dirhams is a negligible amount (a few hundred Taka). Therefore, in practice, there is effectively no minimum barrier, but it must be money or property with commercial value.

#### 2. The Maximum Limit

- **Sunni Law (General Rule):** There is **NO Maximum Limit**. The husband can agree to pay millions of Taka, effectively more than his entire net worth.
    
    - _The Precedent:_ When Caliph Umar (RA) tried to cap the dower amount to prevent social difficulty, a woman stood up and quoted the Quran (4:20), which allows giving a "heap of gold" (_Qintar_). Umar withdrew his proposal, saying, " The woman is right and Umar is wrong."
        
- **Shia Law:** There is also no strict maximum. However, it is considered "Disliked" (_Makruh_) to exceed the **Mahr-e-Sunnat** (the amount the Prophet paid for his wives), which is **500 Dirhams**. If it exceeds this, it is valid but pious Muslims avoid it.
    

#### 3. Excessive Dower and Legislation

Since there is no maximum limit, families often fix astronomical sums (e.g., 1 Crore Taka) for a groom who earns a modest salary.

- **Purpose:** This is usually done for "Show" (_Sumat_) or as a "Deterrent" against divorce (financial blackmail).
    
- **Legal Enforceability:**
    
    - **General Rule (Bangladesh):** The court is bound to award the **entire agreed amount**, no matter how excessive or beyond the husband's means. The contract is binding.
        
    - **Exception (Oudh Laws Act / Some Jurisdictions):** In some specific jurisdictions (like Oudh in India), courts have statutory power to reduce the dower to a "reasonable amount" based on the husband's income. **Bangladesh does NOT have such a statute.** Here, the contract acts strictly.
        

#### 4. Fictitious Dower (_Mahr-e-Sumat_)

Sometimes, parties secretly agree on a small real amount (e.g., 50,000 Taka) but publicly announce a huge amount (e.g., 50 Lakh Taka) for social prestige.

- **The Conflict:** If the wife sues for the 50 Lakh, and the husband claims it was just for show.
    
- **The Ruling:** The burden of proof is on the husband to prove that the "Real Dower" was different from the "Public Dower." If he cannot prove the secret agreement with strong evidence, the Court will enforce the **Public (Written) Amount** in the _Kabinnama_.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Exam Strategy:** If a problem says: _"A husband agreed to pay 5 Taka as dower. Is this valid?"_
    
    - **Answer:** Under Hanafi Law, the _stipulation_ is modified. The wife is entitled to the minimum legal dower of **10 Dirhams** (or its monetary equivalent), not just 5 Taka.
        
- **Calculations:**
    
    - **10 Dirhams** $\approx$ 30.6 grams of silver.
        
    - In Bangladesh, usually, cash amounts are written directly (e.g., 5,00,000 BDT).
        
- **Comparison:**
    
    - _Minimum:_ Hanafi (10) vs Maliki (3) vs Shia (None).
        
    - _Maximum:_ None for all (but Shia prefers 500).
