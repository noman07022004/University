## 7.1 Concept of Dower (_Mahr_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** You must understand that Dower is not a "price" for the woman, but a "token of respect." This conceptual shift is crucial to distinguishing Islamic marriage from a sale or a pre-Islamic transaction.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** While specific questions solely on the "concept" are rare, almost every essay question on Dower requires you to write an introductory paragraph defining its concept and nature.

### The Token of Respect

In the legal and social framework of Islam, **Dower (_Mahr_)** represents a unique financial right of the wife. It is often misunderstood by Western observers as a "purchase price," but legally and spiritually, it is defined as a **Mark of Respect** (_Ikram-un-Nisa_).

#### 1. Definition

- **Literal Meaning:** The word _Mahr_ in Arabic implies a "reward" or a "gift" given voluntarily.
    
- **Legal Definition:** It is a sum of money or other property which the wife is entitled to receive from the husband **in consideration of the marriage**.
    

#### 2. Historical Evolution (The Conceptual Shift)

To understand the concept, one must look at what it replaced.

- **Pre-Islamic Era (_Jahiliyya_):** A sum called _Mahr_ was paid by the husband to the **bride's father**. It was effectively a "Bride Price" or sale—the father sold the daughter, and he kept the money.
    
- **Islamic Reform:** The Quran (4:4) commanded: _"And give the women (on marriage) their dower as a free gift."_
    
    - **The Shift:** Islam took the money _away_ from the father and gave it **directly to the wife**.
        
    - **The Result:** It transformed from a "Price of the Bride" to the "Property of the Bride." It became her financial security, which her husband or father cannot touch.
        

#### 3. Legal Nature: Consideration vs. Obligation

There is a famous debate in jurisprudence regarding the nature of Dower.

- **Is it Consideration?** In the famous case of **_Abdul Kadir v. Salima (1886)_**, Justice Mahmood argued that Dower is the "consideration" (exchange) for the contract of marriage, just as price is the consideration for a sale.
    
    - _Critique:_ Modern scholars argue this analogy is flawed because marriage is not a trade of goods.
        
- **The Correct View:** Dower is not the price for the _woman_, but it is technically the consideration for the **enjoyment of conjugal rights** (_Buz_). More accurately, it is an **obligation imposed by law**. Even if the husband and wife agree to delete Dower from the contract, the law ignores their agreement and imposes "Proper Dower" anyway.
    

#### 4. The Object and Rationale

Why does the law insist on Dower?

1. **Check on Divorce:** In a system where the husband has unilateral power of divorce (_Talaq_), a high "Deferred Dower" acts as a financial brake. If he divorces her, he must pay a massive sum instantly.
    
2. **Financial Independence:** It provides the wife with independent capital she can use for investment or emergencies.
    
3. **Token of Honor:** It symbolizes that the woman is entering the union as a dignified partner, not a subordinate.

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Crucial Distinction (Dowry vs. Dower):**
    
    - **Dower (_Mahr_):** **Islamic**. Money paid by **Groom to Bride**. Mandatory and legal.
        
    - **Dowry (_Joutuk_):** **Cultural/Hindu influence**. Money/Gifts demanded by **Groom's family from Bride's family**. This is **Prohibited** in Islam and is a criminal offense in Bangladesh under the _Dowry Prohibition Act, 2018_.
        
    - _Exam Tip:_ Never confuse these two terms. Mahr is a Right; Joutuk is a Crime.
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** In Bangladesh, the concept of Mahr often serves as "Social Insurance." Families demand high Mahr (e.g., 20 Lakh Taka) not expecting it to be paid during the marriage, but to ensure the husband is too scared of the debt to divorce the wife.
