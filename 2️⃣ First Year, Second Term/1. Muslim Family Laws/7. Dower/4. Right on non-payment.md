## 7.4 Right on Non-Payment (Remedies) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** A right without a remedy is useless. This topic defines the "teeth" of the dower law—how a wife actually forces the husband to pay. The "Right of Retention" is a complex property law concept essential for inheritance disputes.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Very frequent. Questions often focus on: _"Can a wife refuse to live with her husband if dower is unpaid?"_ or _"Discuss the nature of the widow's right of retention."_
    

---

### The Wife's Legal Arsenal

If the husband fails to pay the dower when it is due, the wife is not helpless. Muslim Law provides her with three distinct remedies to enforce her claim. These remedies vary depending on whether the marriage is subsisting or dissolved.

#### 1. The Right of Refusal (Conjugal Rights)

This is a preventive remedy used while the marriage is intact.

- **The Rule:** The wife has the right to **refuse to live with her husband** or admit him to sexual intercourse until the **Prompt Dower** (_Mu'ajjal_) is paid.
    
- **Before Consummation:** This right is **Absolute**. If she refuses, the husband cannot force her back, and he must still maintain her.
    
- **After Consummation:** Can she refuse _after_ sexual relations have already happened?
    
    - _Abu Hanifa's View:_ Yes, she can still refuse.
        
    - _The Court's View (Bangladesh/India):_ Following the leading case of **_Abdul Kadir v. Salima_**, the courts hold that once she has surrendered herself, the "absolute" right is lost. She cannot strictly refuse cohabitation.
        
    - _The Equitable Remedy:_ However, if the husband sues for "Restitution of Conjugal Rights," the Court will pass a **Conditional Decree**: _"The husband is entitled to his wife ONLY IF he pays the dower first."_ This effectively enforces payment.
        

#### 2. The Right of Enforcement (Suit for Debt)

The wife can sue the husband or his heirs in a Civil Court (Family Court in Bangladesh) to recover the dower as a simple money debt.

- **Nature:** It is an **unsecured debt**. If the husband is bankrupt, she is paid pro-rata with other creditors.
    
- **Limitation Period:**
    
    - _Prompt Dower:_ 3 years from demand and refusal.
        
    - _Deferred Dower:_ 3 years from death or divorce.
        

#### 3. The Widow's Right of Retention (Lien)

This is the most powerful and complex remedy, typically arising after the husband's death.

- **Definition:** If a widow is in **lawful possession** of her deceased husband's property, and her dower remains unpaid, she has the right to **retain possession** of that property against the other heirs until her dower debt is satisfied.
    
- **Nature of the Right:**
    
    - It is a form of **Lien** (security), not ownership. She does _not_ become the owner of the property.
        
    - She cannot sell or gift the property (unless she has a separate title). She can only hold it and enjoy its income (rents/profits) to pay off her debt.
        
- **Conditions for Retention:**
    
    1. **Possession:** She must have actual possession of the property.
        
    2. **Lawfulness:** The possession must be obtained lawfully and without force or fraud (usually with the consent, express or implied, of the husband or other heirs).
        
    3. **Existence of Debt:** The dower must be unpaid.
        
- **Accountability:** She acts like a trustee. She must keep accounts of the income she generates from the property (e.g., rent). This income is deducted from the dower debt. Once the debt is paid off (from the rent), she must return the property to the heirs.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Leading Case Law:**
    
    - **_Maina Bibi v. Chaudhri Vakil Ahmad (1925):_** The Privy Council clarified that the widow's right of retention is **heritable**. If the widow dies while holding the property, her heirs can continue to hold it until the debt is paid.
        
- **Exam Distinction (Possession vs. Title):**
    
    - If a widow is "Retaining" property, she is NOT the owner. She cannot sell it. If she sells it, the sale is voidable by the other heirs.
        
    - However, she _is_ an heir herself (getting 1/8 or 1/4). So she has dual capacity: **Heir (Owner of share)** + **Creditor (Retaining the rest)**.
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** In rural Bangladesh, this right is often the only protection for a widow. If the husband's brothers try to kick her out, she can legally say, _"I am not leaving until you pay my 5 Lakh Taka dower."_ Since they usually can't pay, she effectively keeps the house for life.
