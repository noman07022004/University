## Dower (_Mahr_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Dower is a _sine qua non_ (essential condition) of Muslim marriage. It is not merely a financial transaction but a mark of respect for the wife and a check on the husband's power of divorce. Understanding Dower is essential to understanding the economic rights of women in Islam.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a high-yield topic. Problem questions frequently involve calculations of "Prompt" vs. "Deferred" dower, or a widow retaining her deceased husband's property in lieu of unpaid dower (_Right of Retention_).
    

### Introduction

Dower, or _Mahr_, is a sum of money or other property which the wife is entitled to receive from the husband in consideration of the marriage. While often compared to "consideration" in contract law, in Islamic law, it is theoretically a token of respect offered by the husband to the wife. It is an exclusive right of the wife; her parents or guardians have no claim over it. It is a debt upon the husband that must be paid.

### 1. Concept and Amount of Dower

**Subtopics Covered:** _Concept, Amount of dower._

- **Concept:**
    
    - The Quran terms it _Sadaqa_ (a free gift) or _Nihla_, implying it should be given willingly.
        
    - It serves two practical purposes:
        
        1. To impose an obligation on the husband as a mark of respect.
            
        2. To act as a deterrent against capricious divorce (since the husband must pay the full amount upon divorce).
            
    - **Legal Status:** Even if the marriage contract says "No Dower shall be paid," the stipulation is void, and the wife is still entitled to Proper Dower (_Mahr-i-Mithl_).
        
- **Amount:**
    
    - **Maximum:** There is **no maximum limit** in Sunni or Shia law. A husband can settle his entire fortune as dower.
        
    - **Minimum:**
        
        - **Hanafi Law:** 10 Dirhams (historically small, now nominal).
            
        - **Maliki Law:** 3 Dirhams.
            
        - **Shia Law:** No fixed minimum (anything of value).
            
    - **Statutory Reality:** In Bangladesh, courts often encounter excessive dower amounts fixed to prevent divorce. If the husband claims the amount was beyond his means and only for show, the burden of proof is on him, but generally, the written contract prevails.
        

### 2. Types of Dower

**Subtopics Covered:** _Types._

Dower is primarily classified based on whether the amount is fixed in the contract or determined by custom.

#### A. Specified Dower (_Mahr-i-Musamma_)

This is the amount expressly agreed upon by the parties at the time of marriage (or even after). It is further divided into:

1. **Prompt Dower (_Mu’ajjal_):**
    
    - Payable **immediately** upon demand.
        
    - The wife can refuse to enter the marital home (_conjugal domicile_) until this is paid.
        
2. **Deferred Dower (_Mu’wajjal_):**
    
    - Payable upon the **dissolution of marriage** (by death or divorce).
        
    - It serves as a security for the wife in her later life.
        

- **Rule of Presumption:** If the marriage deed (_Kabinnama_) does not specify how much is Prompt and how much is Deferred:
    
    - **Traditional Law:** Part is prompt, part is deferred (based on custom).
        
    - **Statutory Law (MFLO 1961):** The **entire amount** is presumed to be **Prompt** (payable on demand).
        

#### B. Proper or Customary Dower (_Mahr-i-Mithl_)

If the amount was **not fixed** in the contract, or if the marriage was contracted on the condition that no dower would be paid, the wife is entitled to "Proper Dower."

- **Calculation:** It is determined by looking at the dower fixed for other female members of her father's family (aunts, sisters) who are equal to her in:
    
    - Beauty
        
    - Age
        
    - Education/status
        
    - Virginity
        

### 3. Rights and Remedies regarding Dower

**Subtopics Covered:** _Right on non-payment, Widow's claim, Limitation._

Since Dower is a debt, the wife has several remedies if it is not paid.

#### A. Right of Refusal (Non-payment)

- If the **Prompt Dower** is unpaid, the wife has the right to **refuse to live with the husband** or admit him to sexual intercourse.
    
- If she is a minor, her guardian can refuse to send her to the husband's house.
    
- **Crucial Condition:** Once the marriage is consummated (sexual intercourse has happened), she technically loses the absolute right to refuse (in classical law), but courts in the subcontinent often rule that she can still refuse if the prompt dower remains unpaid, provided she acts in good faith.
    

#### B. Widow's Right of Retention

This is a specific "Self-Help" remedy for a widow.

- **The Right:** If a widow is in lawful, peaceful possession of her deceased husband's property, and her dower remains unpaid, she has the right to **retain possession** of that property until her dower debt is satisfied from the income of that property.
    
- **Nature of Right:**
    
    - It is **not a title** (she doesn't own the land).
        
    - She cannot sell or mortgage the property (unless she has a distinct share as an heir).
        
    - It is a shield, not a sword (she uses it to defend her possession against other heirs).
        

#### C. Limitation Period

When must the wife sue for her dower?

- **During Marriage:** She can sue anytime.
    
- **After Divorce/Death:** Under the **Limitation Act, 1908**:
    
    - The suit must be filed within **3 years**.
        
    - Time starts ticking from the date of **dissolution** (death/divorce) OR from the date the dower was **demanded and refused**.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Prompt vs. Deferred Presumption:** In exams, if the problem doesn't specify the split, always cite **Section 10 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961**: _"Where no mode of payment is specified in the Nikah Nama, the entire amount is presumed to be Prompt."_ This overrides the old Hanafi rule.
    
- **Retention is NOT Ownership:** Be careful in problem questions. If a widow sells the land she is holding for dower, the sale is **voidable** (or valid only to the extent of her inheritance share, not the dower debt). She only has the right to _hold_ it.
    
- **Inflation:** A common modern legal issue is whether dower fixed 30 years ago (e.g., 500 Taka) should be adjusted for inflation. Traditional law says "No" (a debt is fixed), but some modern judgments are exploring equity arguments. For standard exams, stick to the fixed amount rule.
    
- **Remission:** A wife can remit (forgive) the dower, but it must be done with free consent. Remission made under emotional duress (e.g., while the husband is dying) or by a minor wife is invalid.
