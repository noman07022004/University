## 7.6 Limitation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Knowing the substantive law (that you have a right) is useless if you miss the procedural deadline to enforce it. This topic explains _when_ the clock starts ticking for a wife to sue for her dower.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Extremely frequent in problem questions. A classic scenario: _"A wife demands dower in 2010. The husband refuses. She sues in 2015. Is the suit barred?"_ You must know the Limitation Act rules to answer.
    

---

### The Time Bar for Legal Action

In legal procedure, a right must be enforced within a specific time frame. If the wife waits too long, her remedy is barred by the **Limitation Act, 1908** (which is applicable in Bangladesh).

The period of limitation for a suit to recover Dower is **3 Years**. However, the crucial legal question is: _When does this 3-year clock start ticking?_ This depends entirely on whether the dower is **Prompt** or **Deferred**.

The relevant provisions are found in **Schedule I** of the Limitation Act, 1908.

#### 1. Limitation for Prompt Dower (_Mu'ajjal_)

- **Relevant Law:** **Article 103** of the Limitation Act, 1908.
    
- **Period:** **3 Years**.
    
- **Starting Point (Cause of Action):** The time begins to run from:
    
    1. The date when the dower is **demanded and refused**; OR
        
    2. If no demand is made during the marriage, from the date of **dissolution** of marriage (by Death or Divorce).
        

**Critical Analysis (The "No Demand" Rule):** This rule is very favorable to the wife.

- _Scenario A:_ If a wife lives with her husband for 20 years and never demands the Prompt Dower, the limitation period _has not started_. If he divorces her after 20 years, she still has 3 more years to sue.
    
- _Scenario B:_ If she demands it in Year 1, and he refuses, she _must_ sue within 3 years (by Year 4). If she waits until Year 10, her claim is time-barred.
    

#### 2. Limitation for Deferred Dower (_Mu'wajjal_)

- **Relevant Law:** **Article 104** of the Limitation Act, 1908.
    
- **Period:** **3 Years**.
    
- **Starting Point:** The time begins to run strictly from the date of the **dissolution of marriage** (by Death or Divorce).
    
    - _Note:_ Since Deferred Dower is _not_ payable on demand, making a demand during the marriage is legally irrelevant. The clock can only start when the marriage ends.
        

#### 3. Special Cases for Starting Time

- **Divorce by Notice:** If the divorce is pronounced in the wife's absence (e.g., by letter or affidavit), the limitation period starts from the date when **she becomes aware** of the divorce, not necessarily the date he signed the paper.
    
- **Death:** In case of the husband's death, the limitation starts from the date of death.
    

#### 4. The Exception: Widow in Possession (Right of Retention)

This is a vital exception to the limitation rule.

- **The Rule:** If a widow is in **lawful possession** of her husband's property in lieu of her unpaid dower (exercising her Right of Retention), there is **NO Limitation Period** to recover her debt from the income of the property.
    
- **Logic:** She is not suing for money; she is holding security. As long as she holds the property, she is enforcing her right. The heirs must sue _her_ to recover the property, and in that suit, she can claim her dower deduction regardless of how many years have passed.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Exam Strategy (The "Demand & Refusal" Trap):**
    
    - In exams, look carefully for the word **"Refused"**.
        
    - If the wife demands and the husband says _"I will pay later"_ or stays silent, this is **NOT** a refusal. It must be an unambiguous denial for the clock to start.
        
    - _Tip:_ Always argue that the limitation has not expired unless there is clear evidence of refusal, to protect the wife's claim.
        
- **Procedural Maxim:** _"Limitation bars the remedy, but does not extinguish the right."_
    
    - Even if 3 years pass and she cannot sue in court, the debt still morally exists. If the husband pays it voluntarily after 5 years, it is a valid payment and cannot be recovered by him.
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** In family courts, judges are very lenient regarding limitation for dower. If a woman claims she didn't know about the divorce until recently, the court usually accepts her version to save her dower claim from being time-barred.
