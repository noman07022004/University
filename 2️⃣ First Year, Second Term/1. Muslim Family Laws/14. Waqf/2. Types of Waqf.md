

## 14.2 Types of Waqf ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** Essential to distinguish between purely charitable endowments and those used to protect family property.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** One of the most frequent exam topics. You will almost certainly be asked to "Distinguish between Public and Private Waqf" or explain the legal validity of _Waqf-al-Awlad_ (Family Waqf).
    

---

### Classification by Object

Waqfs are primarily classified based on the **object** or purpose for which the property is dedicated.

#### 1. Public Waqf (_Waqf-al-Khair_)

This is a dedication made strictly for **public, religious, or charitable purposes**.

- **Beneficiaries:** The general public or a specific section of the community (e.g., "The poor of Khulna").
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Building a Mosque, Madrasa, or Bridge.
        
    - Digging a well for public use.
        
    - Establishing a graveyard (_Kabarstan_).
        
- **Management:** Usually managed by a Mutawalli, but often subject to stricter government supervision (under the Waqfs Ordinance, 1962) because public money/interest is involved.
    

#### 2. Private/Family Waqf (_Waqf-al-Awlad_)

This is a dedication where the income is primarily reserved for the **benefit of the dedicator’s own family and descendants**.

- **The Condition:** It is valid _only_ if there is an **ultimate reservation** for the poor.
    
    - _Structure:_ "I dedicate this house. The income shall go to my children, then their children, and when my line of descendants becomes extinct, the income shall go to the poor."
        
- **Purpose:** Historically used to protect family property from being squandered by heirs or seized by creditors, while keeping it within the bloodline.
    

**The Historical Legal Conflict (Crucial Exam Point)**

- **The Privy Council Rule (1894):** In the famous case of _Abul Fata Mahomed Ishak v. Russomoy Dhur_, the Privy Council declared Family Waqfs **VOID**. They argued that "charity begins at home" was not a valid legal principle for perpetuity and that the gift to the poor was "illusory" (too remote).
    
- **The Legislative Correction (1913):** The Muslim community in India protested this decision as an attack on their religious law. The British government passed the **Mussalman Wakf Validating Act, 1913**.
    
- **Current Law:** This Act overturned the Privy Council judgment. Today, a Family Waqf is **VALID** in Bangladesh, provided the ultimate benefit is expressly or impliedly reserved for the poor.
    

#### 3. Mixed Waqf (_Waqf-al-Mushtarak_)

This is a dedication that serves both public and private purposes simultaneously.

- _Example:_ "50% of the income goes to my children, and 50% goes to the maintenance of the local Mosque."
    

---

### Other Classifications (By Nature)

#### 1. Testamentary Waqf

A Waqf created by a **Will** (to take effect after death).

- **Validity:** Valid, but subject to the **1/3rd rule** of Wills. It cannot exceed 1/3rd of the net estate unless heirs consent.
    

#### 2. Contingent Waqf

A Waqf that depends on a future uncertain event (e.g., "If my son returns, I will make this a Waqf").

- **Validity:** **VOID.** A Waqf must be immediate and unconditional.
    
- _Exception:_ A Waqf conditioned on the death of the dedicator (Testamentary Waqf) is valid.
    

---

### Comparison Table: Public vs. Private Waqf

|**Feature**|**Public Waqf (Waqf-al-Khair)**|**Private Waqf (Waqf-al-Awlad)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Primary Object**|Benefit of the public/poor.|Benefit of the dedicator's family.|
|**Reservation**|Immediate benefit to charity.|Ultimate benefit to charity (after family line ends).|
|**Motivation**|Pure charity (_Sadaqah_).|Family security + Pious intention.|
|**Statutory Control**|Strictly supervised by the Administrator of Waqfs.|Less interference; Mutawalli (family member) has more freedom.|

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Mosque is Special:** Once a property is dedicated as a Mosque, it becomes a special class of Public Waqf. It cannot be reverted, sold, or used for any other purpose. Access cannot be restricted to a specific sect/group.
    
- **The "Illusory" Test:** If a Family Waqf does _not_ mention the poor at all (e.g., "To my family forever"), it is **VOID** even under the 1913 Act. There _must_ be an ultimate charitable destination.
