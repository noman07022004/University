
## 14.3 Statutory Provisions of Waqf ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** While the _concept_ of Waqf is religious, the _management_ is statutory. You cannot understand how a Waqf operates in modern Bangladesh without knowing the powers of the Administrator.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Highly frequent. Questions often focus on the **"Administrator of Waqfs"** and the restrictions on **transferring Waqf property** (Section 32).
    

---

### The Legislative Framework

In Bangladesh, the administration of Waqf properties is not left solely to the Mutawalli. The state intervenes to prevent corruption, mismanagement, and the loss of religious property.

#### 1. The Mussalman Wakf Validating Act, 1913

- **Purpose:** This is the "Savior Act" for Family Waqfs.
    
- **Context:** Before 1913, the Privy Council had declared Family Waqfs (_Waqf-al-Awlad_) void.
    
- **Key Provision:** It explicitly declared that a Waqf is **valid** even if the income is dedicated to the support of the dedicator's family, provided there is an ultimate reservation for the poor. This Act effectively overruled the judicial decision in the _Abul Fata_ case.
    

#### 2. The Waqfs Ordinance, 1962 (The Main Law)

This is the primary statute governing the administration and management of Waqf properties in Bangladesh today. It replaced the earlier Bengal Wakf Act of 1934.

**Key Features & Provisions:**

- **A. The Administrator of Waqfs:** The Ordinance established the office of the **Administrator of Waqfs**, a government official with supreme supervisory power over all Waqf estates in Bangladesh. The Administrator acts as the ultimate guardian of these properties.
    
- **B. Enrolment (Registration) of Waqfs:**
    
    - **Requirement:** All Waqfs (whether Public or Private) must be enrolled at the Office of the Administrator.
        
    - **Duty:** It is the legal duty of every _Mutawalli_ to apply for enrolment. Failure to do so is a punishable offense.
        
- **C. Restriction on Transfer (Section 32 - Crucial):** This is the most important practical section.
    
    - **The Rule:** A Mutawalli cannot transfer Waqf property by sale, mortgage, exchange, or lease (for more than a short period) **without the prior sanction (permission)** of the Administrator.
        
    - **Effect:** Any transfer made without this permission is **voidable** (and often declared void) by the Administrator. The Administrator can recover such property.
        
- **D. Removal of Mutawalli:** The Administrator has the power to **remove** a Mutawalli from their position if they:
    
    - Misappropriate Waqf funds.
        
    - Fail to submit accounts.
        
    - Neglect the property or the religious duties.
        
    - Are convicted of a criminal offense.
        
- **E. 5% Contribution:** Every Waqf estate (with a net income above a certain threshold) must pay **5%** of its net income to the Administrator's office annually to cover the costs of the administration.
    

---

### 3. The Religious Endowments Act, 1863

- **Historical Context:** This was one of the earliest laws. It relieved the British Government from the direct management of religious institutions (Mosques/Temples) and handed them over to local committees.
    
- **Relevance:** While largely superseded by the 1962 Ordinance for general administration, it is still relevant for the history of committee management in older shrines.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Mutawalli's Power" Trap:** If an exam question asks, _"Can a Mutawalli sell the Waqf land to pay for repairs?"_
    
    - **Answer:** **NO**, not on his own. He _must_ obtain the **prior sanction of the Administrator** under Section 32 of the Waqfs Ordinance, 1962. If he sells it without permission, the sale is illegal.
        
- **Administrator vs. Mutawalli:**
    
    - **Mutawalli:** The manager on the ground (day-to-day).
        
    - **Administrator:** The "Super-Manager" (Government oversight).
        
- **Family Waqf Taxes:** Even private Family Waqfs must pay the 5% contribution to the government and submit accounts. They are not exempt from supervision.
