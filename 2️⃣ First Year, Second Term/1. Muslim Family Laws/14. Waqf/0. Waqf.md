## Waqf ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Waqf represents the Islamic institution of "Trust." It explains how religious and charitable institutions (Mosques, Madrasahs, Orphanages) are sustained financially. It is unique because it permanently "locks" property, preventing it from ever being sold or inherited.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent exam topic. Questions often focus on the validity of "Family Waqf" (_Waqf-ul-Aulad_) and the historical conflict between the Privy Council and the Legislature (1913 Act). The powers and removal of a _Mutawalli_ are also common short notes.
    

### Introduction

The literal meaning of _Waqf_ is "detention" or "stoppage." In the legal sense, it implies the **permanent dedication** of property by a Muslim for a religious, pious, or charitable purpose. Once a Waqf is created, the property no longer belongs to the founder (_Waqif_); its ownership is metaphorically transferred to **Allah**, and the profits (_Usufruct_) are used for the benefit of mankind. Unlike a Trust in English law where the "legal ownership" is with the trustee, in Waqf, the "manager" (_Mutawalli_) has no ownership at all—only management duties.

### 1. Concept and Essentials

**Subtopics Covered:** _Concept._

For a Waqf to be valid under Muslim Law, the following essentials must be met:

1. **Permanent Dedication:** The dedication must be perpetual. A "Waqf for 10 years" is void.
    
2. **Competency of the Waqif:** The founder must be an adult, sane Muslim (or a non-Muslim for a secular public purpose).
    
3. **Subject Matter:** The property must be tangible and capable of use without being consumed (e.g., land, money for investment).
    
4. **Ownership of God:** The founder's right is extinguished. The property cannot be sold, mortgaged, or inherited.
    
5. **Object:** The purpose must be Islamic and charitable (e.g., building a mosque, feeding the poor, or supporting one's own family).
    

### 2. Types of Waqf

**Subtopics Covered:** _Types._

Waqf is generally classified based on the beneficiary:

#### A. Waqf-ul-Khair (Public Waqf)

- **Purpose:** Created solely for the public benefit or charity from the start.
    
- **Examples:** Building mosques, digging wells, funding schools, or feeding travelers.
    

#### B. Waqf-ul-Aulad (Private/Family Waqf)

- **Purpose:** Created for the benefit of the **founder's own family** and descendants.
    
- **Condition:** There must be an "ultimate bequest" to charity. Meaning, the deed must say: _"The income is for my children, then their children, but if my line goes extinct, it goes to the poor."_
    
- **Significance:** This serves as a social security system for the family, protecting the property from being squandered by future generations.
    

#### C. Waqf-bil-Wasiyat (Testamentary Waqf)

- **Creation:** Created by a Will (_Wasiyat_), taking effect after the founder's death.
    
- **Restriction:** Like any Will, it is valid only up to **one-third (1/3)** of the net estate, unless heirs consent to more.
    

### 3. Statutory Provisions

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory provisions._

The history of Waqf legislation is a story of colonial conflict and correction.

#### A. The Mussalman Waqf Validating Act, 1913

- **Background:** In the famous case _Abul Fata v. Russomoy (1894)_, the British Privy Council declared **Family Waqf (_Waqf-ul-Aulad_) void**, arguing it was just a trick to keep property in the family and not "true charity."
    
- **Reaction:** This caused outrage among Muslims. The legislature passed the **1913 Act** to override the Privy Council judgment.
    
- **Current Law:** The Act explicitly validates Family Waqfs, provided there is an ultimate reservation for the poor (even if that reservation is small or remote).
    

#### B. The Waqf Ordinance, 1962 (Bangladesh)

This is the primary law governing Waqf administration in Bangladesh today.

- **Waqf Administrator:** It established the office of the "Waqf Administrator" to oversee all Waqfs in the country.
    
- **Registration:** Every Waqf property must be enrolled/registered at the Administrator's office.
    
- **Supervision:** The Administrator acts as a "Super-Mutawalli." They can audit accounts, remove corrupt Mutawallis, and ensure the income is spent correctly.
    
- **Section 32:** Grants the Administrator the power to remove a Mutawalli for misappropriation or incompetence.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The Mutawalli:** Remember, the _Mutawalli_ is merely a **Manager/Superintendent**. He is **NOT a Trustee** (in the English sense) because the property is not vested in him. He cannot sell the land without the permission of the Court (or now, the Waqf Administrator).
    
- **The "Abul Fata" Case:** In exams, if you are asked about the "Historical Development of Waqf" or "Family Waqf," you **must** cite _Abul Fata v. Russomoy_ and the subsequent _1913 Act_. This is the classic "Judiciary vs. Legislature" moment in Muslim Law history.
    
- **Shia vs. Sunni:**
    
    - **Sunni:** A Waqf can be completed by mere declaration (according to Abu Yusuf).
        
    - **Shia:** Delivery of possession to the Mutawalli is **mandatory** for the Waqf to be valid.
        
- **Mosque as Waqf:** Once a mosque is consecrated and public prayer is offered, it becomes Waqf forever. No deed is strictly necessary if long usage is proved.
