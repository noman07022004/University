### 14.1 Concept of Waqf ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the theoretical foundation of Islamic property law. It introduces a unique legal concept where ownership is transferred not to a human, but to the Divine, making it "locked" forever. Understanding this "Divine Ownership" is essential to grasp why Waqf property cannot be sold or inherited.
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Frequently asked as a theoretical question: _"Define Waqf and discuss its essential characteristics"_ or _"How does Waqf differ from Trust or Sadaqah?"_
    


***1. Definition and Meaning***

- **Literal Meaning:** The word _Waqf_ (plural _Awqaf_) literally means "detention," "stoppage," or "tying up."
    
- **Legal Definition:** In Sharia (specifically the accepted Hanafi view of the Two Disciples, Abu Yusuf and Imam Muhammad), Waqf means the **permanent dedication** by a person professing the Muslim faith of any property for any purpose recognized by the Muslim law as religious, pious, or charitable.
    
- **The "Two Disciples" Rule:** Imam Abu Hanifa originally held that Waqf was revocable (like a simple charity). However, his two disciples argued it is **absolute and irrevocable**. This view—that the Waqf is binding and cannot be cancelled—is the established law in Bangladesh and the subcontinent.
    

***2. The Core Juridical Theory (The "Shift" of Ownership)*** The concept of Waqf relies on a specific legal fiction regarding ownership:

1. **Extinguishment:** The ownership of the _Wakif_ (dedicator) is completely extinguished. They no longer own the land.
    
2. **Transfer to God:** The ownership is transferred to **Almighty God**. The property becomes "God's Acre."
    
3. **Usufruct for Mankind:** While the _Corpus_ (the physical body of the property, e.g., the land/building) belongs to God and cannot be consumed, the _Usufruct_ (the income, rent, fruit, or benefit) is detained for the benefit of mankind (the poor, the family, or the public).
    

***3. The Three Essentials of a Valid Waqf*** For the concept to be legally realized, three elements must be present:

- **A. Perpetuity (Permanence):** The dedication must be permanent. A Waqf "for 10 years" is **void**. The idea is to lock the property forever so it generates spiritual merit (_Sawab_) for the dedicator eternally. The maxim is: _"Once a Waqf, always a Waqf."_
    
- **B. Immediacy:** The Waqf must take effect immediately. A contingent Waqf (e.g., "I will make this a Waqf if my ship returns safely") is **void**.
    
    - _Exception:_ A testamentary Waqf (Waqf by Will) is valid but operates only after death and is limited to 1/3rd of the estate.
        
- **C. Irrevocability:** Once the declaration is made and possession is delivered (to the Mutawalli or to God symbolically), the _Wakif_ cannot revoke it. They cannot take the land back, sell it, or gift it to someone else.
    

***4. Purpose of Waqf*** The concept requires a "Valid Object." The purpose must be one that draws the dedicator closer to God (_Qurbat_).

- **Religious:** Mosques, prayer mats, copies of the Quran.
    
- **Charitable:** Hospitals, bridges, schools (_Madrasa_), digging wells, feeding the poor.
    
- **Pious:** Provision for one's own family (_Waqf-al-Awlad_), provided there is an ultimate reservation for the poor.
    

***5. Difference from Sadaqah and Trust***

- **Vs. Sadaqah:** In _Sadaqah_ (simple charity), both the corpus (property) and the usufruct are given away (e.g., giving a coin to a beggar). In _Waqf_, the corpus is detained (saved), and only the income is given.
    
- **Vs. Trust:** In a Western Trust, the "Trustee" is the legal owner. In _Waqf_, the "Mutawalli" (Manager) is **not** the owner; God is the owner. The Mutawalli is merely a superintendent.
    

### Important Note for Exams

**The "Mutawalli" is NOT the Owner.** Often students write "The Mutawalli owns the Waqf." This is incorrect. The Mutawalli is a manager/superintendent. If the Mutawalli dies, the land does not go to his sons; a new manager is appointed. The land belongs to no human.
