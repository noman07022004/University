## Shia Law of Inheritance ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** While less commonly applied in Bangladesh (where the majority are Sunni), understanding Shia law is crucial for comparative legal analysis. It is conceptually simpler than Sunni law because it removes the complex distinction between "Agnates" and "Cognates."
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Less frequent as a standalone mathematical problem than Sunni law. However, theoretical questions like _"Distinguish between Sunni and Shia Law of Inheritance"_ or _"Discuss the status of the Daughter in Shia Law"_ are very common.
    

### Introduction

The Shia Law of Inheritance (specifically the _Ithna Ashari_ school) differs fundamentally from the Sunni system. While Sunni law is based on tribal custom that favors male agnates (relatives through males), Shia law is based strictly on **Consanguinity (Blood Relationship)**. It treats female heirs much more favorably. In Shia law, there is no distinction between "Agnates" and "Distant Kindred." If a person is related to the deceased, they inherit, regardless of whether the link is male or female.

### 1. General Principles and Classification of Heirs

**Subtopics Covered:** _General principles and classification of heirs._

The core principle of Shia inheritance is **Class Exclusion**. Heirs are divided into three distinct classes based on proximity to the deceased.

- **The Rule of Exclusion:** **Class I excludes Class II**, and **Class II excludes Class III**.
    
    - If even _one_ heir exists in Class I (e.g., a granddaughter), everyone in Class II (e.g., a grandfather or brother) is totally excluded.
        
    - _Contrast with Sunni Law:_ In Sunni law, a Daughter (Class I equivalent) does _not_ exclude a Brother (Class II equivalent). In Shia law, she does.
        

### 2. The Three Classes of Heirs

**Subtopics Covered:** _Shite heirs of class I, II and III._

Heirs by consanguinity are divided as follows:

#### Class I: The Immediate Family

1. **Parents** (Father and Mother).
    
2. **Children** and their descendants (Son, Daughter, Grandchildren, etc.).
    
    - _Note:_ A Daughter stands on equal footing with a Son regarding the _right_ to inherit (though she gets half his share). Crucially, a **Daughter excludes all brothers, sisters, and uncles.**
        

#### Class II: The Intermediate Family

_(Inherit only if NO ONE from Class I exists)_

1. **Grandparents** (True and False alike—no distinction).
    
2. **Brothers and Sisters** and their descendants.
    

#### Class III: The Extended Family

_(Inherit only if NO ONE from Class I or Class II exists)_

1. **Paternal and Maternal Uncles and Aunts** and their descendants.
    

_(Note: The Husband and Wife are "Heirs by Affinity." They are never excluded and inherit alongside whichever class is receiving the estate.)_

### 3. Anomalies and Miscellaneous Rules

**Subtopics Covered:** _Anomalies and miscellaneous rules._

Shia law has unique rules that contradict the Sunni doctrines you previously learned.

#### A. Rejection of 'Awl (Increase)

- **The Scenario:** If the sum of the shares exceeds unity (e.g., Husband 1/2 + 2 Sisters 2/3 = 7/6).
    
- **Sunni Solution:** Increase the denominator (reduce everyone's share).
    
- **Shia Solution:** **No 'Awl.** The fraction is never increased. Instead, the **loss falls exclusively on the Daughter(s) or Sister(s)**. The Husband, Wife, and Parents get their full Quranic share, and the Daughter/Sister takes a cut.
    

#### B. Doctrine of Return (Radd)

- If shares are less than 1, the residue returns to the heirs in proportion to their shares.
    
- _Difference:_ Under classical Shia law, the **Wife** is specifically excluded from the Return (she never gets more than her 1/8 or 1/4), whereas in modern Sunni law (and some Shia views), she might participate if no blood relations exist.
    

#### C. The Childless Widow

- This is a famous peculiarity. Under traditional Shia law, a **childless widow** takes no share in her husband's **immovable property** (land). She only inherits from the value of household effects and movable property.
    
- _Note:_ A widow _with_ children inherits from both land and movables.
    

#### D. Primogeniture (Habua)

- Shia law recognizes a limited form of primogeniture. The **Eldest Son** is entitled to the father's:
    
    - Sword
        
    - Quran
        
    - Wearing apparel
        
    - Ring
        
- These are called _Habua_ and are given to him _before_ the division of the estate, provided he is of sound mind.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Daughter" Comparison:** This is the best way to score marks.
    
    - **Sunni:** Daughter gets 1/2. If Brother exists, he shares with her. If Brother doesn't exist but Uncle exists, Uncle gets the residue.
        
    - **Shia:** Daughter gets 1/2 (or residue). She **blocks** the Brother. She **blocks** the Uncle. She takes the _whole_ estate (after spouse's share) if she is alone.
        
- **Stirpes Rule (Representation):** Shia law applies the principle of representation (_Per Stirpes_) more broadly for descendants. A grandson inherits the share his parent _would have taken_ if alive.
    
- **Problem Solving:** If you see a problem with "Husband, Daughter, and Brother":
    
    - **Sunni Answer:** Husband (1/4), Daughter (1/2), Brother (Residue 1/4).
        
    - **Shia Answer:** Husband (1/4), Daughter (3/4 by Return). Brother is **Excluded** (because he is Class II, Daughter is Class I).
