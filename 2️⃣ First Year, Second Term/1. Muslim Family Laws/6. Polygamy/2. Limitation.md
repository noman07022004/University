
## 6.2 Limitation ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** It is vital to distinguish between a "Right" and a "Conditional Permission." This topic clarifies that the right to polygamy is not absolute; it is bounded by strict numerical, moral, and legal walls.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3.5/5):** Problem questions often feature a man marrying a **5th wife** or marrying his **wife's sister**. You must identify these as specific breaches of the "Limitation" of polygamy.
    

---

###  The Boundaries of the Right

While "Scope" defines the permission (0–4 wives), **Limitation** defines the specific barriers that stop a man from exercising this permission freely. In Muslim Law, the right to polygamy is not a "blank check"; it is curbed by three distinct types of limitations: **Numerical**, **Moral**, and **Relational**.

#### 1. The Numerical Limitation (The Rule of Four)

The most obvious limitation is the hard cap on the number of concurrent spouses.

- **The Rule:** A man is strictly limited to **four** wives at any one time.
    
- **Exceeding the Limit (The 5th Wife):** If a man marries a fifth woman while four valid wives are still alive and married to him, he violates the numerical limitation.
    
    - **Sunni/Hanafi View:** The marriage to the 5th wife is **Irregular (_Fasid_)**. It is not Void. The logic is that the "defect" is temporary; he can fix it by divorcing one of the first four. If he does so, the 5th marriage becomes valid.
        
    - **Shia View:** The marriage to the 5th wife is **Void (_Batil_)**. It is a legal nullity from the start.
        

#### 2. The Moral Limitation (The Condition of _Adl_)

This is the internal, religious barrier found in **Surah An-Nisa (4:3)**.

- **The Injunction:** _"But if you fear that you will not be just, then [marry only] one."_
    
- **Nature of Limitation:** This essentially acts as a **Disqualification**. If a man knows he is financially weak, physically incapable, or emotionally biased, he is _religiously_ banned from marrying a second wife, even if the court allows it.
    
    - _Legal Effect:_ Historically, this was left to the man's conscience. However, modern laws (like MFLO 1961) have transformed this "moral limitation" into a "legal inquiry" by the Arbitration Council, checking his finances before granting permission.
        

#### 3. The Relational Limitation (Unlawful Conjunction)

Even if a man has fewer than four wives (e.g., only one), he is limited in _who_ he can choose as a second wife. He cannot practice polygamy with a woman related to his existing wife.

- **The Rule:** A man cannot combine two women in marriage who, if one were a male, would be prohibited from marrying each other (e.g., two sisters, aunt and niece).
    
- **Example:** If A is married to W, he is **Limited** from marrying W's sister (S) as a second wife. He must divorce W first to marry S.
    
    - _Violation:_ If he marries the sister while the first wife is present, the second marriage is **Irregular (_Fasid_)**.
        

#### 4. The Contractual Limitation (Stipulation)

A husband’s right to polygamy can be limited by a prior contract with his first wife.

- **Tafweez:** If the husband agreed in the _Kabinnama_ (Column 18) that _"If I marry a second wife, the first wife has the power to divorce herself,"_ this acts as a massive practical limitation. He theoretically _can_ marry again, but the cost is losing the first wife.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Exam Trap (5th Wife vs. 2nd Wife without Permission):**
    
    - If A has 4 wives and marries a **5th**: The marriage is **Irregular (_Fasid_)** (Hanafi).
        
    - If A has 1 wife and marries a **2nd** (without Council permission): The marriage is **Valid (_Sahih_)** but punishable.
        
    - _Crucial Distinction:_ Violating the _Religious Limit (4)_ affects validity. Violating the _Statutory Limit (Permission)_ affects criminal liability.
        
- **Legal Maxim:** _Al-Jam' bayn al-Mahramayn_ (Unlawful Conjunction). Use this term when discussing the limitation on marrying a wife's sister.
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:** The Arbitration Council effectively enforces the "Moral Limitation." When they ask "Is this necessary?", they are checking if the moral conditions for polygamy (like wife's illness) exist.
