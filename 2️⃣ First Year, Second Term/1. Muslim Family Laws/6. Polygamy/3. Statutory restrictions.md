## 6.3 Statutory Restrictions (MFLO 1961) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the practical law of the land. In Bangladesh, you cannot advise a client on polygamy based solely on the Quran; you must consult the Ordinance. This topic explains how the State regulates a religious right.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is one of the most frequently asked questions in Bangladeshi law exams. Expect questions like: _"Discuss the provisions of Section 6 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961 regarding polygamy."_
    

---

###  Section 6 of MFLO 1961

While Classical Muslim Law (Sharia) permits polygamy up to four wives subject to the condition of justice, the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961** introduced procedural restrictions to curb the abuse of this permission.

**Section 6** of the MFLO is the governing law. It does not _abolish_ polygamy, but it "bureaucratizes" it by making it conditional upon the permission of a quasi-judicial body.

#### 1. The Requirement of Permission

- **The Rule:** No man, during the subsistence of an existing marriage, shall contract another marriage **except with the previous permission in writing of the Arbitration Council**.
    
- **The Application:** To obtain this permission, the husband must submit an application to the Chairman of the Union Parishad (or Mayor in city corporations), stating the reasons for the proposed marriage and whether the existing wife consents.
    

#### 2. The Arbitration Council

Upon receiving the application, the Chairman constitutes an **Arbitration Council**.

- **Composition:** The Council consists of the **Chairman** and **one representative** of each of the parties (husband and existing wife).
    
- **Function:** The Council reviews the application to see if the proposed marriage is "necessary and just."
    

#### 3. Grounds for Permission (Rule 14)

The Council does not have unlimited discretion. **Rule 14 of the Muslim Family Laws Rules, 1961** specifies the grounds on which permission may be granted:

1. **Sterility:** The existing wife is medically declared infertile.
    
2. **Physical Infirmity:** The wife suffers from a physical weakness unfit for conjugal relations.
    
3. **Insanity:** The existing wife is of unsound mind.
    
4. **Physical Unfitness:** For conjugal relations.
    
5. **Avoidance of Divorce:** If the marriage is necessary to avoid divorcing the first wife (e.g., she is ill but he needs a partner, and he wants to keep supporting her).
    

#### 4. Consequences of Violation (Unauthorized Polygamy)

What happens if a man marries a second wife _without_ the permission of the Arbitration Council? This is the most critical area for legal analysis.

**A. Validity of the Second Marriage**

- **Status:** The marriage is **VALID (_Sahih_)** and binding.
    
- **Legitimacy:** Children born from this marriage are legitimate and entitled to inherit.
    
- **Reasoning:** The Ordinance penalizes the _act_ of marrying without permission but does not declare the _contract_ itself void. (This is distinct from the void status in some other countries like Tunisia).
    

**B. Criminal Liability**

- **Penalty:** The husband is guilty of an offense punishable with **Simple Imprisonment** which may extend to **one year**, or with a **Fine** which may extend to **10,000 Taka**, or both.
    

**C. Civil / Financial Liability (Dower)**

- **Immediate Payment:** The entire amount of Dower (_Mahr_) of the existing wife—whether Prompt or Deferred—becomes **immediately due and payable**.
    
- **Recovery:** If not paid, it can be recovered as arrears of land revenue.
    

**D. Matrimonial Relief for the First Wife**

- **Delegated Divorce:** If the husband marries without permission, it may activate the wife's delegated power of divorce (_Tafweez-e-Talaq_) if stipulated in Column 18 of her Kabinnama.
    
- **Judicial Divorce:** Even without delegation, the **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939** (Section 2) allows a woman to seek divorce if her husband has taken an additional wife in contravention of the MFLO 1961.

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Leading Case Law:**
    
    - **_Jesmin Sultana v. Md. Elias (1997):_** The High Court Division clarified that while Section 6 discourages polygamy, it does not render the second marriage void. However, the court emphasized that judges should be strict in applying the penalties to deter the practice.
        
- **Exam Strategy:**
    
    - **Scenario:** "A has a wife B. He marries C without Council permission. B claims the marriage to C is void."
        
    - **Answer:** B is incorrect. The marriage to C is **Valid**. However, B can send A to jail, demand her full dower immediately, and sue for divorce.
        
- **Common Mistake:** Students often think "Consent of the first wife" is the only requirement. It is not. The _primary_ requirement is the **Council's Permission**. Even if the wife consents, the husband technically needs the Council's stamp (though the Council usually agrees if the wife consents).
