## 6.1 Scope ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** You must understand that the "Scope" of polygamy in Islam is not a license for lust, but a specific legal permission defined by strict numerical and moral boundaries. It sets the "playing field" for multiple marriages.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions rarely ask _only_ about scope. Usually, they ask about scope in the context of "Restrictions" or "Modern Reforms."
    

---

### Comprehensive Explanation: The Extent of Permission

The **Scope** of polygamy refers to the legal extent, boundaries, and nature of the permission granted to a Muslim male to have more than one wife. Contrary to popular belief, Islam did not _invent_ the scope of polygamy; it _restricted_ it. In the Pre-Islamic _Jahiliyya_ period, the scope was unlimited—a man could marry as many women as he wished.

The Quranic legislation defined the scope by placing a **Numerical Ceiling** and a **Moral Condition**.

#### 1. The Quranic Authority

The scope is derived primarily from **Surah An-Nisa, Verse 4:3**:

> _"And if you fear that you will not deal justly with the orphan girls, then marry those that please you of [other] women, two or three or four. But if you fear that you will not be just, then [marry only] one..."_

#### 2. The Numerical Scope (Maximum Four)

- **The Upper Limit:** The verse explicitly sets the maximum limit at **four**.
    
- **Legal Consequence:** A Muslim man can validly have up to four wives simultaneously.
    
- **Exceeding the Scope:** If a man marries a **fifth wife** while the first four are still alive and married to him:
    
    - **Sunni/Hanafi Law:** The marriage is **Irregular (_Fasid_)**. It is not Void. It can be validated if he divorces one of the previous four.
        
    - **Shia Law:** The marriage is **Void (_Batil_)**.
        

#### 3. The Moral Scope (The Condition of _Adl_)

The permission is not absolute; it is bound by the scope of **Justice (_Adl_)**.

- **Equal Treatment:** The husband must provide equal maintenance (food, shelter, clothing) and equal time (_Mabit_) to all wives.
    
- **Inner Feelings:** The scope does _not_ require equality in love or affection, as the Quran acknowledges this is humanly impossible (4:129). It requires equality in _outward treatment_.
    

#### 4. The Historical/Social Scope

The scope of this permission was revealed in the context of the **Battle of Uhud**, where many Muslim men died, leaving behind widows and orphans.

- **Purpose:** The legal scope was intended as a **Social Remedy** to absorb unprotected women into dignified family structures, rather than solely for male gratification.
    
- **Historical Concubinage:** Historically, the scope also included "what your right hands possess" (slave girls). However, since slavery is abolished by universal consensus and international law (which Muslim nations have signed), this part of the scope is legally obsolete.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Bangladeshi Context (Statutory Scope):**
    
    - The **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961** has _narrowed_ the practical scope.
        
    - While the _religious_ scope is 4, the _statutory_ scope in Bangladesh requires **Permission from the Arbitration Council** for every marriage after the first. Without this permission, the scope of "legal validity" remains, but the scope of "criminal immunity" is lost (he goes to jail).
        
- **Exam Keyword:** If asked about "Scope," always use the phrase **"Restricted Permission, not Fundamental Right."** This distinguishes the Islamic view from the unlimited pre-Islamic practice.
