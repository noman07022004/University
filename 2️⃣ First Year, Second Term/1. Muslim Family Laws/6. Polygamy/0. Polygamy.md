## Polygamy ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** It is essential to understand the distinction between the "permission" granted by traditional Sharia and the "restriction" imposed by modern state law. This topic highlights the conflict between traditional interpretation and social reform.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a favorite topic for exam problem questions. (e.g., "A husband marries a second wife without the first wife's permission. Is the marriage valid? What are the penalties?"). You _must_ know Section 6 of the MFLO 1961.
    

### Introduction

Polygamy (specifically **Polygyny**—one man having multiple wives) is permitted in Islamic Law, but it is not an unrestricted right. Historically, it was a mechanism to provide social welfare for widows and orphans in a tribal society. The Quranic verse (4:3) that permits it simultaneously imposes a strict condition of "justice" or equality between wives. In Bangladesh, this traditional permission has been significantly curtailed by statutory law, making polygamy an "exception" rather than a rule, regulated by strict procedural hurdles.

### 1. Scope and Traditional Limitations

**Subtopics Covered:** _Scope, Limitation._

Under traditional Muslim Law (prior to 1961), a Muslim male was granted the scope to contract multiple marriages, subject to specific divine limitations.

- **The Quantitative Limit:** A Muslim man may have up to **four** wives at the same time.
    
    - If he marries a fifth wife, the marriage is **Irregular (_Fasid_)** (not Void). It can be regularized only by divorcing one of the existing four wives.
        
- **The Qualitative Limit (Condition of _Adl_):** The Quran (Surah An-Nisa, 4:3) states: _"But if you fear that you will not be able to deal justly (with them), then only one..."_
    
    - This justice refers to equal treatment in maintenance, residence, and time (conjugal rights).
        
    - Emotional equality (love) is acknowledged as impossible by the Quran itself (4:129), but tangible equality is mandatory.
        
    - Traditionally, the violation of this condition was a moral sin but did not invalidate the marriage in a court of law.
        

### 2. Statutory Restrictions (MFLO 1961)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory restrictions._

In Bangladesh, the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961** introduced a major reform. **Section 6** of the MFLO places procedural restrictions on polygamy. It does not abolish it but regulates it heavily.

#### A. The Requirement of Permission

No man, during the subsistence of an existing marriage, can contract another marriage without the **previous permission in writing** of the **Arbitration Council**.

#### B. The Procedure

1. **Application:** The husband must submit an application to the Chairman of the Local Council (Union Parishad/Pourashava) stating the reasons for the proposed marriage.
    
2. **Consent:** The application must state whether the existing wife (or wives) has consented.
    
3. **Arbitration Council:** The Chairman forms a council consisting of himself, a representative of the husband, and a representative of the existing wife.
    
4. **Decision:** The Council will grant permission _only_ if it is satisfied that:
    
    - The proposed marriage is necessary and just.
        
    - Or generally, strictly necessary reasons exist (e.g., existing wife's sterility, physical infirmity, or insanity).
        

#### C. Legal Consequences of Violation

If a man marries a second wife **without** the permission of the Arbitration Council, he faces three consequences:

1. **Criminal Penalty:** He is punishable with imprisonment up to **one year** or a fine up to **10,000 Taka**, or both.
    
2. **Immediate Dower:** The entire amount of Dower (_Mahr_) (prompt and deferred) due to the existing wife becomes immediately payable.
    
3. **Ground for Divorce:** The existing wife becomes entitled to seek a dissolution of marriage (Divorce) under the _Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939_ (Section 2(iia)).

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Validity of the Second Marriage:** This is the most critical exam point.
    
    - **Question:** If a man marries a second wife _without_ permission, is the second marriage valid?
        
    - **Answer:** **YES, the marriage is VALID.** The MFLO 1961 penalizes the act (jail/fine) but does not invalidate the marriage itself. The children from this unauthorized marriage are legitimate.
        
- **The "Consent" Trap:** The law requires the permission of the **Arbitration Council**, not just the permission of the _first wife_. Even if the first wife agrees, the husband _must_ still go to the Council. Her consent is just a factor the Council considers.
    
- **Polyandry:** Remember that **Polyandry** (a woman having multiple husbands) is strictly prohibited and constitutes the crime of Bigamy (Section 494 Penal Code). Such a marriage is **Void (_Batil_)**.
    
- **Judicial Trend:** In recent cases (e.g., _Jesmin Sultana v. Md. Elias_), the Bangladeshi courts have been very strict, often equating polygamy without permission to "cruelty," granting the first wife divorce and heavy maintenance.
