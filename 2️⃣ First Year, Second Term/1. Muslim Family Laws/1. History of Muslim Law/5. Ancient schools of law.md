## 1.5 Ancient Schools of Law ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4.5/5):** This topic explains the "root cause" of the differences in Muslim Law. To understand why Hanafi law (Bangladesh) differs from Maliki law, you must understand the original split between the "School of Reason" (Iraq) and the "School of Tradition" (Medina).
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often ask to "Distinguish between the Ancient Schools of Medina and Kufa" or "Discuss the conflict between Ahl al-Hadith and Ahl al-Ra'y."

---
**Regional Schools vs. Personal Schools**

It is crucial to distinguish between the **Ancient Schools** and the **Classical Schools** (which come later in the syllabus).

- **Ancient Schools (Pre-750 AD):** These were **Regional**. A student would say, "I follow the school of Medina" or "I follow the school of Kufa."
    
- **Classical Schools (Post-750 AD):** These became **Personal**. A student would say, "I follow Imam Abu Hanifa" or "I follow Imam Malik."
    
---
The Ancient Schools were not formal colleges but rather circles of learning centered around specific cities. The two primary rivals were:


### 1. The School of Medina (Hijaz): The Home of Tradition

Medina was the city of the Prophet and the spiritual capital.

- **Characteristics:** This school was the stronghold of the **Traditionists (_Ahl al-Hadith_)**.
    
- **Methodology:**
    
    - They relied almost exclusively on the **Hadith** and the **Practice (_Amal_) of the People of Medina**.
        
    - They argued that since the Prophet lived there, the local customs of Medina were effectively a "living Sunnah" and superior to isolated logic.
        
    - They were suspicious of human reason (_Ra'y_), fearing it would corrupt the divine law.
        
- **Key Figures:** The "Seven Lawyers of Medina" (a group of scholars including Said ibn al-Musayyib) who preserved the rulings of the Caliphs.
    
- **Legacy:** This school evolved into the **Maliki School**.
    

### 2. The School of Kufa (Iraq): The Home of Reason

Kufa was a new garrison city in Iraq, far from the cradle of Islam.

- **Characteristics:** This school became the stronghold of the **Rationalists (_Ahl al-Ra'y_)**.
    
- **Context:**
    
    - **New Problems:** Kufa was a cosmopolitan melting pot of Arabs, Persians, and locals. They faced complex commercial and agricultural issues that never existed in the simple tribal society of Medina.
        
    - **Lack of Hadith:** Fewer Companions settled in Iraq, so there were fewer reliable Hadith available compared to Medina.
        
- **Methodology:**
    
    - When they couldn't find a text, they used **Reason (_Ra'y_)** and **Analogy (_Qiyas_)**.
        
    - They looked for the "Rationale" or "Intent" behind a rule rather than just the literal text.
        
    - _Example:_ If the Quran banned exchanging dates for dates unequal in weight (usury), the School of Kufa would use logic to apply this ban to _wheat_, _rice_, and _gold_ as well.
        
- **Key Figures:** Ibrahim al-Nakha'i and Hammad ibn Abi Sulayman (the teacher of Abu Hanifa).
    
- **Legacy:** This school evolved into the **Hanafi School**.
    

### 3. The Theoretical Conflict

The tension between these two ancient schools defined the development of Muslim law:

- **Medina accused Kufa** of being too liberal and prioritizing human logic over the Word of God.
    
- **Kufa accused Medina** of being too literal and unable to adapt the law to the complexities of a growing empire.
    

Eventually, the great Imams (Abu Hanifa, Shafi'i, Malik, Ahmad) synthesized these views, but the "DNA" of these ancient schools remains visible today.

---

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Bangladeshi Context:** Bangladesh follows the **Hanafi School**. Therefore, our legal system is historically rooted in the **Ancient School of Kufa (Iraq)**. This explains why Hanafi law is often more flexible and logical (using _Qiyas_) regarding commercial transactions and land rights compared to the stricter Hadith-based schools.
    
- **Exam Tip:** If asked about the difference between _Ahl al-Hadith_ and _Ahl al-Ra'y_, map them to geography:
    
    - Hadith = Medina (Tradition).
        
    - Ra'y = Kufa (Reason).
        
- **Evolution:** Remember that the "Ancient Schools" did not disappear; they _matured_ into the Classical Schools. Medina became Maliki; Kufa became Hanafi.
