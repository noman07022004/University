## 1.7 Early Systematic Reasoning of the Second Century ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the "scientific revolution" of Muslim Law. It explains how the law transformed from a collection of loose opinions into a rigorous, logical science (_Usul al-Fiqh_). Without this, Muslim law would just be a list of unconnected rules.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Extremely important for theoretical questions. Expect questions like "Contribution of Imam Shafi'i to the development of legal theory" or "The transition from Ra'y to Qiyas."

---

The **Second Century of the Hijra** (roughly 720–820 AD) is arguably the most critical period in the intellectual history of Islamic Law. It bridges the gap between the ad-hoc decision-making of the Umayyads and the crystallized schools of the classical era.

During the First Century, judges relied on **Ra'y** (loose, subjective opinion) when the texts were silent. By the Second Century, scholars realized that unrestricted opinion was dangerous—it led to inconsistent laws where "Haram" in Kufa might be "Halal" in Medina.

This era witnessed the move toward **Systematic Reasoning**—replacing "Opinion" with "Methodology." It is **The Birth of Jurisprudence (_Usul al-Fiqh_)**

---

### 1. The Architect of the System: Imam Al-Shafi'i

While Abu Hanifa and Malik were giants of this era, **Imam Al-Shafi'i** (d. 820 AD) is credited as the "Father of Muslim Jurisprudence." He saw the conflict between the Traditionists (who wanted only Hadith) and the Rationalists (who wanted Logic).

- **The Risala:** Shafi'i wrote the **Al-Risala**, the first book on the _principles_ of law (_Usul al-Fiqh_).
    
- **The Hierarchy:** He established the strict hierarchy of sources that we use today:
    
    1. **Quran:** The literal word of God.
        
    2. **Sunnah:** It explains the Quran. Shafi'i argued that a solitary Hadith (_Ahad_) overrides any human analogy.
        
    3. **Ijma (Consensus):** If the community agrees, it is binding.
        
    4. **Qiyas (Analogy):** The _only_ valid form of reasoning. He rejected arbitrary opinion.
        

### 2. The Standardization of Reasoning Tools

The major achievement of this century was defining _how_ a jurist thinks. The vague concept of "Ra'y" was broken down into specific, technical tools:

- **Qiyas (Analogical Deduction):** This became the standard tool for systematic reasoning. It is the process of applying a rule from a known case (Origin) to a new case (Branch) because they share the same effective cause (_Illah_).
    
    - _Example:_ The Quran bans _wine_ (Origin) because it _intoxicates_ (Cause). Therefore, _vodka_ (Branch) is also banned because it also _intoxicates_.
        
    - **Constraint:** Unlike earlier "Ra'y," Qiyas is strict. You cannot just say "I think this is bad." You must prove the _Illah_ links back to a text.
        
- **Istihsan (Juristic Preference) – The Safety Valve:** Championed by the **Hanafi School** (Abu Hanifa and his disciples Abu Yusuf and Shaybani).
    
    - Sometimes, strict analogy leads to a harsh or unjust result. _Istihsan_ allows the jurist to set aside the strict analogy for a "better" or "more equitable" solution based on public need.
        
    - _Example:_ By strict analogy, a contract for an object that doesn't exist yet is void. But _Istihsan_ permits "Manufacturing Contracts" (_Istisna_) because people need to order goods in advance.
        
- **Istislah (Public Interest):** Championed by the **Maliki School**. It allows laws to be deduced based on _Maslahah Mursalah_ (unrestricted public interest), even if there is no specific text, provided it doesn't violate Sharia principles.
    

### 3. The Compilation of Laws

This century also saw the move from oral tradition to written compendiums.

- **Al-Muwatta:** Written by **Imam Malik**, this was the first major book combining Hadith (Prophetic tradition) with Fiqh (local decisions of Medina). It represents the earliest attempt to systematize law based on custom and tradition.
    
- **Kitab al-Aslo:** The disciples of Abu Hanifa (specifically Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani) began writing down the Hanafi doctrines, preserving the "Systematic Reasoning" of the Iraqi school.

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Bangladeshi Context:**
    
    - The **Doctrine of Istihsan** (Equity) is crucial in Bangladesh. Although our courts apply British-style "Equity, Justice, and Good Conscience," the historical root of this flexibility for Muslims lies in the Hanafi principle of _Istihsan_. It allows the law to breathe and not suffocate the people.
        
- **Exam Key Concept:** The "Common Illah" (Effective Cause). In any problem question regarding analogy, identify the _Illah_. If you can't find the _Illah_, you can't apply Qiyas.
    
- **Key Distinction:**
    
    - **Ra'y (1st Century):** "I think this is right." (Subjective).
        
    - **Qiyas (2nd Century):** "This is right because it is chemically/legally similar to what God said is right." (Objective/Systematic).
