## 1.10 Purist Reactions ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic explains the "disruption" in Islamic legal history. It helps you understand the intellectual origins of modern movements (like Salafism) and why there is a constant tension between "Traditional Islam" (following a School/Madhhab) and "Purist Islam" (following only the Text).
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions typically appear as short notes on "Ibn Taymiyyah," "The Wahhabi Movement," or "The Rejection of Taqlid."

---

***The Revolt Against Stagnation***

After the formation of the four Sunni schools (approx. 10th Century AD), the Muslim legal world settled into a period of **Stagnation**.

- **The Closing of the Gate of Ijtihad:** Scholars declared that all major legal questions had been answered. No one was allowed to derive new laws directly from the Quran.
    
- **Taqlid (Imitation):** The duty of every Muslim was simply to follow (_Taqlid_) the rulings of established manuals (like the _Hedaya_ for Hanafis). Innovation was feared as heresy.
    

**Purist Reactions** were movements that emerged to challenge this stagnation. They argued that blind imitation of medieval scholars was idolatry and that Muslims must return to the **Pure Sources** (Quran and Sunnah) to purify the faith.

---
#### 1. The Medieval Purist: Ibn Taymiyyah (1263–1328 AD)

He is the intellectual father of all modern purist movements. Living during the Mongol invasions, he blamed the weakness of Muslims on their deviation from the original text and their obsession with rigid schools (_Madhhabs_).

- **Legal Philosophy:**
    
    - He rejected the "Closing of the Gate of Ijtihad." He argued that _Ijtihad_ (independent reasoning) is a perpetual duty.
        
    - **Rejection of Blind Taqlid:** He respected the four Imams (Abu Hanifa, Malik, etc.) but argued that if a Hadith contradicts their ruling, the Hadith _must_ be followed.
        
    - **The Triple Divorce Issue:** A famous example of his "Purist" approach: The four schools agreed that saying "Talaq" three times at once counts as three divorces. Ibn Taymiyyah went back to the Quran and early Sunnah, arguing it counts as **only one**.
        
        - _Note:_ He was imprisoned for this view in his time, but modern laws (including **Bangladesh's 1961 Ordinance**) eventually adopted his opinion 600 years later.
            

#### 2. The Modern Purist: The Wahhabi Movement (18th Century)

Founded by **Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab** in Arabia (mid-1700s). This was a socio-political movement to "cleanse" Islam of "foreign" additions.

- **Doctrine:**
    
    - **Tawheed (Monotheism):** Absolute focus on the oneness of God. They violently opposed Sufism, saint worship, and visiting tombs, considering these "Shirk" (polytheism).
        
    - **Legal Literalism:** They are often associated with the **Hanbali School** but are even stricter. They reject later juristic nuances in favor of the literal text of the Quran and Hadith.
        
    - **Impact on Law:** They established the Saudi state, where the law is applied with strict literalism (e.g., severe application of Hudud punishments like chopping off hands for theft, without the procedural loopholes often used by Hanafis to avoid such punishments).
        

#### 3. The Salafist Movement

"Salaf" refers to the "Pious Predecessors" (The Prophet and the first three generations).

- **Concept:** The argument is that the closer you are to the source (the Prophet), the purer the Islam. Everything added after that (medieval philosophy, cultural customs, complex Fiqh manuals) is pollution.
    
- **Legal Methodology:** They tend to bypass the "Schools of Law" entirely. A Salafist in Bangladesh might say, "I don't care what the Hanafi book _Hedaya_ says; show me the Hadith in _Bukhari_."
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Bangladeshi Context:**
    
    - **Ahle Hadith:** The "Purist Reaction" is represented in Bangladesh by the _Ahle Hadith_ community. They are a minority but vocal group who do not follow the Hanafi school. They pray differently (raising hands/Raf-ul-Yadain) and have different rules for divorce and inheritance.
        
    - **Legal Reform:** Paradoxically, modern _liberal_ reformers often use _Purist_ arguments. When the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961** restricted polygamy and reformed divorce, the reformers argued (like Ibn Taymiyyah) that we should not be bound by medieval Hanafi textbooks but should go back to the "true spirit" of the Quran.
        
- **Key Distinction:**
    
    - **Traditionalist:** "My grandfather did it, the Imam said it, so it is right." (Focus on Continuity/Authority).
        
    - **Purist:** "The Prophet did it, the text says it, I don't care what your Imam said." (Focus on Text/Authenticity).
