## 1.13 Nature of Islamic Law ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the philosophical conclusion of the history section. If you don't grasp the _nature_ of the law (that it is religious and all-encompassing), you won't understand why a Muslim might feel morally bound to a rule even if the State Court doesn't enforce it.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often ask to "Discuss the salient features of Islamic Law" or "Distinguish between the secular concept of law and the Islamic concept of Sharia."

---

### Characteristics of a Divine Code

Islamic Law is fundamentally different from Western or Secular law. While secular law (like the British Common Law) is "man-made, territorial, and separates church and state," Islamic Law is **Divine, Personal, and Comprehensive**.

#### 1. Divine Origin (_Wahi_)

The most defining characteristic is its source.

- **Secular Law:** Originates from the "Will of the People" (Parliament/Legislature). It can be changed by a simple vote.
    
- **Islamic Law:** Originates from the **Will of God** (Quran) and His Messenger (Sunnah).
    
    - _Consequence:_ The core principles (_Sharia_) are immutable (unchangeable). No parliament can legalize what God has explicitly forbidden (e.g., changing the shares of inheritance fixed in the Quran). However, the _interpretation_ (_Fiqh_) can change with time.
        

#### 2. The Fusion of Law, Religion, and Morality

In Western law, a distinction is made between "Sin" (religious wrong) and "Crime" (legal wrong). In Islamic Law, these are often fused. The law doesn't just ask "Is this legal?"; it asks "Is this moral?"

**The Five-Fold Classification of Acts (_Al-Ahkam Al-Khamsa_):** Unlike secular law which classifies acts merely as "Legal" or "Illegal," Islamic law evaluates every human action into five moral categories:

1. **Fard/Wajib (Obligatory):** Must do. Punishable if omitted (e.g., Daily prayers, Maintenance of wife).
    
2. **Mandub/Mustahabb (Recommended):** Good to do, but no punishment if omitted (e.g., Writing a will for <1/3rd property).
    
3. **Mubah (Permitted):** Neutral. The law is indifferent (e.g., Eating specific permissible foods).
    
4. **Makruh (Disliked):** Better to avoid, but not punishable (e.g., Divorce is the "most detested permissible thing").
    
5. **Haram (Forbidden):** Must avoid. Punishable if committed (e.g., Adultery, Usury).
    

#### 3. Personal vs. Territorial Application

- **Territorial Law:** Applies to everyone within a border (e.g., The Penal Code applies to everyone in Bangladesh).
    
- **Personal Law:** Applies to the person wherever they go, based on their faith.
    
    - _Historically:_ A Muslim traveler in China was still bound by Sharia wine prohibitions, even if Chinese law allowed it.
        
    - _Modern Context:_ In Bangladesh, this is restricted. We apply "Muslim Personal Law" only in family matters (Marriage, Divorce, Inheritance), while secular law applies in Criminal and Contract matters.
        

#### 4. The Concept of Rights (_Haqq_)

Islamic law divides rights into two distinct spheres:

- **Haqq-ul-Allah (Rights of God):** Duties owed to God (Prayers, Fasting, Hudud punishments). The state enforces these to maintain public order/morality.
    
- **Haqq-ul-Adami (Rights of Humans):** Duties owed to other people (Debts, Contract, Compensation for injury).
    
    - _Significance:_ God may forgive violations of His rights (through repentance), but He does not forgive violations of Human Rights unless the victim forgives the perpetrator.
        

#### 5. Lack of Formal State Enforcement (Historically)

Historically, Islamic law was "Jurist Law," not "State Law."

- The State did not draft the law; private scholars (Imams) did.
    
- This meant the law was often **Idealistic**—it described how a perfect Muslim _should_ behave, rather than strictly what the police would enforce.

###  Important Notes & Tips

- **Bangladeshi Context:**
    
    - In Bangladeshi courts, the "Nature of Law" has been transformed. It is no longer purely "Divine" in application; it is **Statutory**.
        
    - _Example:_ Polygamy is permitted by the Quran (Divine nature). However, the _Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961_ restricts it by requiring permission (State nature). If there is a conflict, the Bangladeshi court follows the State Law (Ordinance) over the pure Divine permission, creating a "Modern Anglo-Muslim" nature.
        
- **Exam Key Phrase:** "Sharia is a code of obligations as well as a code of law." Use this to explain why it covers hygiene and prayer alongside murder and inheritance.
    
- **Distinction:**
    
    - _Sharia:_ The path (Divine/Immutable).
        
    - _Fiqh:_ The understanding (Human/Mutable).
