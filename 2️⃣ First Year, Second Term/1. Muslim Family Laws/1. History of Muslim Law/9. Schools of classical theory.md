## 1.9 Schools of Classical Theory (_Madhhabs_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is vital. You cannot practice Muslim law without knowing which "School" applies to your client. In Bangladesh, if a client is Shia, the rules of inheritance differ drastically from a Sunni Hanafi client.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Very high frequency. Questions often ask to "Compare the Hanafi and Maliki schools," "Explain the features of the Shia schools," or "Discuss the role of the two disciples in the Hanafi school."

---

By the middle of the Abbasid era (mid-3rd century Hijri), the loose "Regional Schools" (of Kufa and Medina) crystallized into formal **Personal Schools** named after their leading Imams. This transition is known as the **Formation of the Madhhabs**.

Unlike the earlier period where students followed the "consensus of a city," they now followed the specific methodology (_Usul_) of a Master Jurist. While many schools existed (like the _Awza'i_ in Syria or _Zahiri_ in Spain), only four Sunni schools and three major Shia schools survived the test of time.

### A. The Four Sunni Schools (_Ahl al-Sunnah_)

These schools recognize each other as valid interpretations of the truth. A Muslim may follow any one of them, though in practice, populations follow the school dominant in their region.

#### 1. The Hanafi School (The School of Kufa/Reason)

- **Founder:** Imam Abu Hanifa (d. 767 AD). Known as _Al-Imam Al-A'zam_ (The Great Imam).
    
- **Theory:**
    
    - It is the most liberal and flexible school.
        
    - It relies heavily on **Qiyas** (Analogy) and **Reason**.
        
    - **Distinctive Feature:** The doctrine of **Istihsan** (Juristic Preference/Equity), which allows a judge to bypass strict analogy if it causes hardship.
        
- **The "Two Disciples" (_Sahibayn_):** Abu Hanifa laid the theory, but his students **Abu Yusuf** (Chief Justice) and **Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani** (writer) codified it.
    
    - _Note:_ In many practical matters (like judicial procedure), the view of the Two Disciples is preferred over the founder.
        
- **Relevance:** It is the dominant school in **Bangladesh**, Pakistan, India, Turkey, and Central Asia.
    

#### 2. The Maliki School (The School of Medina/Tradition)

- **Founder:** Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 795 AD).
    
- **Theory:**
    
    - It focuses on the **Practice (_Amal_) of Medina**. Malik argued that the communal practice of the people of Medina (descendants of the Companions) was a "living Sunnah" superior to isolated Hadith.
        
    - **Distinctive Feature:** **Istislah** (Public Interest) or _Maslahah Mursalah_. They allow laws based purely on public benefit if the Quran is silent.
        
- **Relevance:** Dominant in North Africa (Morocco, Algeria) and formerly Muslim Spain.
    

#### 3. The Shafi'i School (The School of Synthesis)

- **Founder:** Imam Al-Shafi'i (d. 820 AD).
    
- **Theory:**
    
    - He bridged the gap between Hanafi Rationalism and Maliki Traditionalism.
        
    - He strictly organized the sources: Quran $\rightarrow$ Sunnah $\rightarrow$ Ijma $\rightarrow$ Qiyas.
        
    - He rejected _Istihsan_ (Equity) as "legislating with human desire."
        
- **Relevance:** Dominant in Southeast Asia (Malaysia, Indonesia), East Africa, and parts of Yemen.
    

#### 4. The Hanbali School (The Strict Traditionalists)

- **Founder:** Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 855 AD).
    
- **Theory:**
    
    - The most conservative school. It emerged as a reaction against the rationalism of the Mutazila.
        
    - It relies almost exclusively on the **Quran and Hadith**.
        
    - They reject _Ijma_ (consensus) unless it is strictly the consensus of the Companions.
        
    - They prefer a "Weak Hadith" over strong "Analogy."
        
- **Relevance:** Dominant in Saudi Arabia and Qatar.
    

### B. The Shia Schools (_Shi'at Ali_)

The Shia differ from Sunnis primarily on the issue of the **Imamate** (Leadership). They believe the leader must be a designated descendant of the Prophet (via Ali and Fatima), who is divinely guided (_Ma'sum_).

#### 1. The Ithna Ashari (Twelvers)

- **Belief:** They believe in 12 Imams. The last one (Imam Mahdi) went into "occultation" (hiding) and will return.
    
- **Legal Theory:** They reject _Qiyas_ (Analogy) because human reason is fallible. Instead, they rely on the **Teachings of the Imams**.
    
- **Mut'a Marriage:** This is the only major school that permits _Nikah-ul-Mut'a_ (Temporary Marriage).
    
- **Relevance:** Majority in Iran, Iraq, and a significant minority in Pakistan/India/Bangladesh.
    

#### 2. The Ismaili (Seveners)

- **Belief:** They split from the main branch after the 6th Imam. They follow Ismail (the eldest son) rather than Musa al-Kazim.
    
- **Theory:** They focus on the _Batin_ (esoteric/inner meaning) of the Quran rather than the _Zahir_ (literal text).
    
- **Relevance:** The Aga Khan community (Mumbai, East Africa).
    

#### 3. The Zaydi (Fivers)

- **Belief:** Followers of Zayd ibn Ali.
    
- **Theory:** They are the closest to the Sunnis. They do not believe the Imam is "hidden" or "infallible"—just that he must be a descendant of Ali who fights for justice.
    
- **Relevance:** Dominant in Yemen.

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Bangladeshi Context (Crucial for Exams):**
    
    - While Bangladesh is Hanafi, **Section 5 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961** (regarding registration of marriage) applies to _all_ Muslims.
        
    - However, in inheritance, courts strictly apply the specific school of the deceased. If a Shia Muslim dies in Dhaka, the court will apply **Shia Inheritance Law** (where a daughter can inherit the whole estate), not Hanafi Law (where she gets only half, and the rest goes to agnates).
        
- **Comparisons:**
    
    - **Hanafi vs. Shafi'i on Consent:** Hanafis allow an adult woman to contract her own marriage without a guardian (_Wali_). Shafi'is (and Malikis) say a marriage is void without the _Wali's_ consent.
        
- **Visualizing the Spectrum:**
    
    - **Reason** $\leftarrow$ [Hanafi] --- [Shafi'i] --- [Maliki] --- [Hanbali] $\rightarrow$ **Tradition**.
        
- **Exam Tip:** If asked about "The survival of the fittest schools," mention that the Hanafi school survived because of **State Patronage** (Abbasids/Ottomans/Mughals), while the Maliki school survived because of **Geographical Isolation** (North Africa).
