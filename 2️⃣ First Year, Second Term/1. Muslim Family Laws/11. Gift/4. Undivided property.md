## 11.4 Undivided Property (_Mushaa_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is a unique technical concept in Muslim Law that often confuses students. It relates to the practical problem of "how to gift a share of a house without physically cutting the house in half."
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A favorite topic for problem questions. Examiners love to test the exceptions (e.g., "A gifts his share of a flat in Dhaka to his brother").
    

---

### The Doctrine of _Mushaa_

**1. Definition**

- **Literal Meaning:** The word _Mushaa_ is derived from _Shuyuu_, which means "confusion."
    
- **Legal Definition:** It refers to an **undivided share** in a joint property. It applies when a property is owned by multiple people, but their specific portions have not been separated or divided (_partitioned_) by metes and bounds.
    
    - _Example:_ A and B own a field together. A owns 50%, but there is no fence dividing "A's half" from "B's half." A's share is _Mushaa_.
        

**2. The General Rule (Hanafi Law)** The strict Hanafi rule is: **A gift of _Mushaa_ (undivided share) where the property is capable of division is Irregular (_Fasid_), but not Void (_Batil_).**

- **Reasoning:** Since "Delivery of Possession" is essential for a gift, the Hanafi jurists argued that you cannot deliver possession of a "confused" or undefined part. You must first separate your share (partition it) and _then_ gift it. If you gift it without dividing, the transaction is legally defective.
    

---

### Classification and Validity (The "Two Types" Test)

To solve any exam problem, you must ask: **"Is this property capable of being divided?"**

#### Type A: Property Incapable of Division (_Mushaa-i-Ghair-Muhtamil-ut-Qisma_)

- **Definition:** Property that, if divided, would lose its utility or value.
    
- **Examples:** A staircase, a small bathing tank, a well, a small shop, or a supporting wall.
    
- **The Rule:** A gift of an undivided share in such property is **VALID**.
    
- **Logic:** Since division is impossible or harmful, the law relaxes the rule. Possession is delivered by handing over the _right to use_ the share.
    

#### Type B: Property Capable of Division (_Mushaa-i-Muhtamil-ut-Qisma_)

- **Definition:** Property that can be divided without destroying its utility.
    
- **Examples:** A large plot of agricultural land, a large residential house, or a sum of money.
    
- **The Rule:** A gift of an undivided share is **Irregular (_Fasid_)** unless it is partitioned before delivery.
    
    - **Remedy:** The irregularity is **cured** (fixed) if the property is _subsequently_ partitioned and possession is delivered to the donee. The gift then becomes valid retrospectively.
        

---

### The Exceptions (Where Gift of Divisible Mushaa IS Valid)

Over time, the strictness of the _Mushaa_ doctrine was found to be impractical for modern life. Therefore, the courts have established vital exceptions. **A gift of undivided divisible property IS VALID in these cases:**

1. **Gift to a Co-heir:** If one heir gifts their undivided share to another co-heir.
    
    - _Example:_ A father dies leaving a son and daughter. The daughter gifts her undivided inheritance share to her brother. **Valid.**
        
2. **Gift of Share in Freehold Property in Large Commercial Towns:** Gifts of undivided shares in properties located in large cities (like Dhaka, Chittagong, Rangoon) are valid.
    
    - _Reason:_ In big cities, dividing a house or building is often practically impossible or legally restricted by municipal laws.
        
3. **Gift of Shares in a Land Company:** Gifting shares of a company is valid, even though the company's assets (land) are undivided.
    
4. **Gift to Two or More Persons (Jointly):** If a donor gifts a property to two people (e.g., "I give this house to A and B") without dividing their shares. The prevailing view in India/Bangladesh is that this is **Valid**.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Shia Law Difference:** Shia Law **does not recognize** the doctrine of _Mushaa_. Under Shia law, a gift of an undivided share is **always valid**, whether divisible or not, provided the donor withdraws control.
    
- **The "Void" Trap:** If an exam question asks, _"Is a gift of Mushaa void?"_
    
    - **Correct Answer:** **No.** It is merely **Irregular (_Fasid_)**. It can be validated by partition. Do not call it _Void (_Batil_)_.
        
- **Practical Application:** In Bangladesh today, the "Large Commercial Town" exception covers almost all urban property gifts, making the strict _Mushaa_ doctrine mostly relevant only for rural agricultural land.
