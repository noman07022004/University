## Revocation of Gift (_Hiba_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** Essential to understand the "permanence" of property transfer. It clarifies that while you can change your mind initially, once the property is handed over, it is very difficult to get it back.
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Frequently tested in problem scenarios. Example: _"A father gifts a watch to his son. Can he take it back later?"_ or _"A husband gifts land to his wife. Can he revoke it after a divorce?"_
    

---

### The Rules of Retraction

In Muslim Law, a gift is a voluntary transfer. The power to revoke (cancel) it depends entirely on whether the **Delivery of Possession** has taken place.

#### 1. Before Delivery of Possession

- **The Rule:** A gift can be revoked by the donor **at any time** before the delivery of possession is complete.
    
- **Reasoning:** Since "Delivery" is an essential pillar of a valid gift, the gift is technically incomplete until the handover happens. Therefore, the donor is simply cancelling an _intention_, not a completed transaction.
    
- **Method:** A simple declaration ("I am not giving it to you anymore") is sufficient. No court decree is needed.
    

#### 2. After Delivery of Possession

- **The Rule:** Once possession has been delivered, the gift is generally valid and complete. However, it can still be revoked, but **NOT** arbitrarily.
    
- **The Requirement:** The donor cannot revoke the gift merely by saying "I revoke it." To be valid, the revocation requires either:
    
    1. **A Decree of the Civil Court:** The donor must file a suit to cancel the gift.
        
    2. **Consent of the Donee:** The receiver must voluntarily agree to return it.
        
- **Effect:** Until the court passes a decree, the donee remains the legal owner and can sell or transfer the property.
    

---

### The Exceptions: When is a Gift IRREVOCABLE?

There are specific situations where a gift, once delivered, becomes **absolutely irrevocable**. Even a Court cannot cancel it in these cases. You can remember them with the mnemonic **"D.I.R.T."** (Death, Increase, Relationship, Transaction).

**1. Relationship Restrictions (The Strongest Bar)**

- **Gift between Spouses:** A gift from a Husband to a Wife (or vice versa) is **irrevocable** during the subsistence of the marriage.
    
    - _Bangladesh Context:_ Even if they divorce later, the gift usually remains irrevocable (unless it was a specific condition in the Kabinnama).
        
- **Gift to Prohibited Degrees (_Mahram_):** A gift to a person related by blood within the prohibited degrees of marriage is irrevocable.
    
    - _Examples:_ Gift to a **parent**, **grandparent**, **child**, **grandchild**, **brother**, **sister**, **uncle**, or **aunt**.
        
    - _Exam Trick:_ A gift to a **cousin** IS revocable (because cousins are not within prohibited degrees; you can marry them).
        

**2. Change in the Property**

- **Loss/Destruction:** If the property is lost or destroyed, there is nothing to revoke.
    
- **Change of Identity:** If the property has changed its nature (e.g., wheat is ground into flour, or cloth is sewn into a shirt).
    
- **Increase in Value:** If the property has increased in value due to the donee's efforts (e.g., the donee built a house on the gifted land).
    

**3. Death**

- **Death of the Donor:** The heirs of the donor cannot revoke the gift after his death.
    
- **Death of the Donee:** If the receiver dies, the property passes to their heirs and cannot be reclaimed.
    

**4. Transaction Change (_Iwaz_)**

- **Hiba-bil-Iwaz:** As discussed in the "Types" section, once the _Iwaz_ (return) is paid, the transaction transforms into a Sale. A Sale is **never revocable**.
    

---

### Summary Table: Revocability Status

|**Situation**|**Revocable?**|**Requirement**|
|---|---|---|
|**Before Delivery**|**YES**|Simple Declaration.|
|**After Delivery (Stranger)**|**YES**|**Court Decree** or Consent.|
|**Gift to Spouse**|**NO**|Irrevocable.|
|**Gift to Parents/Children**|**NO**|Irrevocable (Prohibited Degree).|
|**Gift to Cousin**|**YES**|Revocable (Not Prohibited Degree).|
|**Hiba-bil-Iwaz**|**NO**|Irrevocable (It is a Sale).|

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Cousin" Trap:**
    
    - _Question:_ "A gifts a car to his brother and a bike to his cousin. Can he revoke them?"
        
    - _Answer:_
        
        - **Brother:** **NO.** (Prohibited degree / Blood relation).
            
        - **Cousin:** **YES.** (Not prohibited degree; Court decree needed).
            
- **Shia Law Difference:** Under Shia law, a gift to _any_ blood relation (consanguine) is irrevocable, whereas Sunni law restricts it only to the _prohibited_ degrees.
    
- **Father to Son:** In Sunni law, a father cannot revoke a gift to his son (prohibited degree). In some other systems (like ancient Roman law), a father could, but not in Sharia.
