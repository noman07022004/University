## 11.1 Concept of Gift (_Hiba_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the cornerstone of property transfer in Muslim Law. Understanding _Hiba_ is essential because it allows a Muslim to transfer property _inter vivos_ (during lifetime) without the restrictions of inheritance law (like the 1/3rd limit).
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Extremely frequent. Questions often test the validity of oral gifts versus registered gifts in Bangladesh, or the "Three Essentials" of a valid gift.
    

---


***1. Definition and Meaning***

- **Literal Meaning:** The word _Hiba_ literally means "the donation of a thing from which the donee may derive a benefit."
    
- **Legal Definition:** In Muslim Law, _Hiba_ is defined as the **immediate and unconditional transfer of the ownership** of some property or of some right, without any consideration (_Iwaz_) or with some return (_Hiba-bil-Iwaz_).
    
- **Key Distinction:** Unlike a Will (_Wasiyyat_), which takes effect after death and is revocable, a Gift operates **immediately** and is generally **irrevocable** once completed (with exceptions). Unlike a Sale (_Bai_), there is usually no exchange of money.
    

***2. The Three Essentials of a Valid Gift***

For a gift to be valid under Muslim Law, three conditions must be strictly fulfilled. If any one is missing, the gift is void (_Batil_).

- **A. Declaration (_Ijab_):** There must be a clear and unambiguous declaration by the donor of their intention to part with the ownership of the property immediately.
    
    - _Capacity:_ The donor must be a Muslim, an adult (_major_), of sound mind, and acting voluntarily (not under coercion or undue influence).
        
- **B. Acceptance (_Qabul_):** There must be acceptance by the donee (receiver).
    
    - _Implied Acceptance:_ Acceptance can be express ("I accept") or implied (e.g., taking the keys to the house).
        
- **C. Delivery of Possession (_Qabza_):** This is the most critical element in Sunni Law. A gift is **not complete** until possession of the property is actually transferred from the donor to the donee.
    
    - _The Rule:_ A mere registration of a deed without delivery of possession does not validate the gift in strict Sharia.
        
    - _Constructive Possession:_ If actual physical delivery is impossible (e.g., the property is rented out), "constructive possession" (e.g., handing over the title deeds or notifying tenants to pay rent to the donee) is sufficient.
        

***3. Statutory Registration (The "Bangladesh" Exception)*** This is the most important point for a Bangladeshi law student.

- **Classical Law:** Under strict Hanafi law, a _Hiba_ can be **oral**. No writing is required.
    
- **Statutory Change:** The **Registration Act, 1908** (Section 17, amended in 2005) made the registration of all gifts of immovable property **mandatory**.
    
- **Current Legal Position:** An oral gift of land is **VOID** in Bangladesh courts today. To be valid, it must be:
    
    1. Written and Registered.
        
    2. Accompanied by delivery of possession (Sharia requirement).
        

***4. Who can give and receive?***

- **Donor:** Must be Major (18+), Sound Mind, and Owner of the property. A gift by a minor is void.
    
- **Donee:** Can be any person (Muslim or non-Muslim), even a minor or a person of unsound mind (their guardian accepts on their behalf). Even an **unborn child** can be a donee if born within 6 months of the gift (though this is debated, generally gift to unborn is void in Sunni law unless they exist in the womb).
    

***5. The "Mushaa" Doctrine (Gift of Undivided Share)***

- **Concept:** _Mushaa_ refers to an undivided share in a property (e.g., "I give you 50% of this unpartitioned field").
    
- **The Rule:** A gift of _Mushaa_ where the property is **divisible** is **Irregular** (_Fasid_), but not Void. It becomes valid if the property is subsequently divided and possession delivered.
    
- **Exceptions (Valid Mushaa):** A gift of an undivided share is valid if the property is:
    
    - Indivisible (e.g., a staircase).
        
    - A share in a Freehold property in a large commercial town (e.g., a flat in Dhaka).
        

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Father to Minor Child Exception:** The strict rule of "Delivery of Possession" is relaxed when a father gifts property to his **minor child**. Since the father is the guardian, his declaration is enough; he doesn't need to physically "hand over" the property to himself.
    
- **Revocation:** A gift can generally be revoked _before_ delivery of possession. _After_ delivery, it can only be revoked by a court decree.
    
    - _Irrevocable Cases:_ Gift between Husband and Wife, gift to a blood relation within prohibited degrees, or if the donee has died.
