
## 11.5 Statutory Provisions of Gift ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the most critical practical aspect of Gift law in Bangladesh today. It represents the clash between "Traditional Sharia" (which allows oral gifts) and "Modern State Law" (which mandates paperwork). If you don't understand this, you cannot advise a client on how to legally transfer land.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A guaranteed exam topic. Examiners love to ask: _"Is an oral gift valid in Bangladesh today?"_ or _"Discuss the conflict between the Transfer of Property Act and the Registration Act regarding Hiba."_
    

---

### Comprehensive Explanation: The Shift from Oral to Written

In the history of Muslim Law in the subcontinent, the method of making a gift has undergone a massive legislative change.

#### 1. The Conflict: Sharia vs. Statute

- **Traditional Sharia Rule:** Under strict Hanafi law, a gift (_Hiba_) can be made **orally**. As long as there is Declaration, Acceptance, and Delivery of Possession, the gift is valid. No writing or registration is needed.
    
- **The Statutory Intervention:** The state realized that "oral gifts" were causing endless lawsuits (false claims, forged dates, land grabbing). To fix this, the government of Bangladesh amended the laws to make registration mandatory.
    

#### 2. The Registration Act, 1908 (The Game Changer)

This is the most important statute you must cite.

- **Pre-2005 Position:** Before 2005, an oral gift of immovable property was valid in Bangladesh.
    
- **The Amendment (2004/2005):** The **Registration (Amendment) Act, 2004** (effective from July 1, 2005) inserted a new clause—**Section 17(1)(aa)**—into the Registration Act, 1908.
    
- **The Current Law:**
    
    - **Mandatory Registration:** A declaration of _Hiba_ (Gift) of immovable property **MUST be in writing and registered**.
        
    - **Effect of Non-Registration:** If a gift of land is not registered, it is **VOID** and inadmissible in evidence. You cannot prove it in court.
        
- **Result:** The concept of a "Valid Oral Gift" for land has been legally abolished in Bangladesh.
    

#### 3. The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (Section 129)

There is often confusion about this Act in exams.

- **General Rule (Sec 123):** For non-Muslims, gifts of land must be signed and attested by 2 witnesses.
    
- **The Saving Clause (Sec 129):** Section 129 explicitly states: _"Nothing in this Chapter... shall be deemed to affect any rule of Muhammadan law."_
    
    - _Meaning:_ Historically, this section "saved" oral gifts from the strict writing requirements of the Transfer of Property Act.
        
    - _Current Status:_ While Section 129 still exists, the **Registration Act amendment overrides it**. So, even though the Transfer of Property Act says "Muslim law is saved," the Registration Act says "No, you must register it anyway."
        

---

### Summary of Statutory Requirements

|**Feature**|**Classical Muslim Law**|**Current Bangladesh Law (Post-2005)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Form of Gift**|Can be **Oral**.|Must be **Written**.|
|**Registration**|Not Required.|**Mandatory** (Registration Act, S. 17).|
|**Delivery of Possession**|**Essential** (Crucial).|**Still Essential** (Registration alone is not enough; Sharia requirement remains).|
|**Validity**|Valid if 3 essentials met.|**Void** if not registered (even if possession delivered).|

### Important Exam Scenario

**Question:** _"Karim gives his land to his son orally in 2010 and hands over the keys. Is the gift valid?"_

- **Answer:** **NO.**
    
- **Reason:** Although the "Sharia essential" (Possession) is met, the "Statutory essential" (Registration) is missing. Under the Registration Act (as amended in 2004), an oral gift of immovable property is void.
