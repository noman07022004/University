## 11.3 During Death Sickness (_Marz-ul-Maut_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is a hybrid concept that blurs the line between Gift and Will. It is critical because it prevents a dying person from disinheriting their heirs by "gifting" everything away in their final moments.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Extremely frequent. Problem questions often feature a dying father gifting land to a daughter or a stranger. You must identify if it is _Marz-ul-Maut_ to apply the "1/3rd limit."
    

---


**1. The Concept**

A gift made during Marz-ul-Maut (Death Illness) is technically a Gift (Hiba) in form (because it is immediate), but it operates as a Will (Wasiyyat) in legal effect.

- **The Rule:** Because the donor is in the shadow of death, the law suspects they might be trying to cheat the inheritance rules. Therefore, such a gift is **restricted to 1/3rd** of the net estate, just like a Will.
    

**2. Essential Conditions (The Test)**

To prove that a gift was made during Marz-ul-Maut, four specific conditions must be met:

1. **Proximate Danger:** The illness must be the immediate cause of death.
    
2. **Apprehension:** There must be a genuine, subjective fear/pressure of imminent death in the mind of the sufferer.
    
3. **Inability:** The illness must render the person unable to attend to their ordinary daily duties (e.g., standing up to pray, going to the market).
    
4. **Fatal Result:** The person must actually die from that specific illness. (If they recover, it remains a valid, unrestricted Gift).
    

**3. Legal Consequences**

If the court decides the gift was made during Marz-ul-Maut:

- **To a Stranger:** Valid only up to **1/3rd** of the estate. The excess is void unless heirs consent.
    
- **To an Heir:** **Invalid** (Void) entirely, unless other heirs consent. (Because a Will to an heir is void in Sunni law).
    

**4. Comparison Table**

|**Feature**|**Normal Gift (Hiba)**|**Death-Bed Gift (Marz-ul-Maut)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Timing**|Made in good health.|Made under fear of death.|
|**Limit**|100% of property.|Restricted to **1/3rd**.|
|**To Heir**|Valid.|**Invalid** (without consent).|
|**Possession**|Delivery required.|Delivery required.|
