## Gift (_Hiba_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** _Hiba_ is the primary method of transferring property _inter vivos_ (during one's lifetime) in Muslim Law. It allows a Muslim to distribute their property freely, bypassing the strict fractional rules of inheritance.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent topic. Questions often focus on the validity of "Oral Gifts" (now changed by statute), the doctrine of _Musha_ (undivided property), and the specific rules of "Death-bed Gifts" (_Marz-ul-Maut_).
    

### Introduction

In Muslim Law, a Gift (_Hiba_) is the immediate and unconditional transfer of the ownership of some property or right from one person to another without any consideration (_Iwaz_). Unlike a Will (_Wasiyat_), which takes effect after death and is limited to one-third of the property, a Gift takes effect immediately and can cover the owner's entire property. It is an act of bounty.

### 1. Concept and Essentials

**Subtopics Covered:** _Concept._

For a Hiba to be valid under traditional Muslim Law, three essential conditions (The Three Pillars) must be met:

1. **Declaration (_Ijab_):** There must be a clear manifestation of the intention to give on the part of the donor.
    
2. **Acceptance (_Qabul_):** There must be acceptance, express or implied, by the donee (or their guardian if a minor).
    
3. **Delivery of Possession (_Qabza_):** This is the most critical element. The donor must divest themselves of possession and place the donee in possession.
    
    - _Without delivery of possession, the gift is incomplete and void._
        
    - **Exception:** If a father gifts property to his minor child, actual delivery is not required; his declaration is enough (since he is the guardian).
        

### 2. Types of Gift

**Subtopics Covered:** _Types._

While _Hiba_ is a simple gift, variations exist based on "Consideration" (return):

1. **Hiba (Pure Gift):** Transfer of corpus (the thing itself) without any return.
    
2. **Hiba-bil-Iwaz (Gift with Exchange):**
    
    - A gift is made, and the receiver gives something in return (_Iwaz_).
        
    - _Legal Nature:_ In India/Bangladesh, this is treated as a **Sale**, not a true Gift. Therefore, it requires registration and writing under the general law.
        
3. **Hiba-ba-Shart-ul-Iwaz (Gift with a Stipulation for Return):**
    
    - A gift made with a specific condition that a return will be given later.
        
4. **Sadaqah:** A gift made with the primary intention of acquiring religious merit (e.g., charity to the poor). It is irrevocable once possession is delivered.
    

### 3. The Doctrine of Musha (Undivided Property)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Undivided property._

_Musha_ refers to an undivided share in property (e.g., owning 50% of a house jointly with someone else, without specific division of rooms).

- **The Rule:** A gift of _Musha_ (undivided share) is **Irregular (_Fasid_)** if the property is **capable of division** (divisible).
    
    - _Reasoning:_ It creates confusion (_Confusion of possession_). The donor should divide the property first and then gift the specific share.
        
- **Exceptions (Valid Musha Gifts):**
    
    1. Property that is **indivisible** by nature (e.g., a staircase, a small bath).
        
    2. Gift to a co-heir.
        
    3. Gift of a share in a freehold property in a large commercial town.
        
    4. Gift of a share in a Zamindari/Land Company.
        
- **Effect:** If possession is eventually delivered, the irregularity is cured, and the gift becomes valid.
    

### 4. Gifts During Death Sickness (_Marz-ul-Maut_)

**Subtopics Covered:** _During death sickness._

This is a hybrid concept between a Gift and a Will.

- **Definition:** _Marz-ul-Maut_ is a malady which induces an apprehension of death in the sufferer, and eventually results in death.
    
- **The Rule:** If a person makes a gift while suffering from _Marz-ul-Maut_:
    
    1. It is valid only up to **one-third (1/3)** of the donor's net estate (like a Will).
        
    2. It cannot be made to an **Heir** without the consent of other heirs.
        
    3. **Possession is still required:** Even in death sickness, delivery of possession is mandatory for the gift to be valid (within the 1/3 limit).
        

### 5. Statutory Provisions (Crucial for Bangladesh)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory provisions._

This is where traditional law meets modern statutes.

- **Oral Gifts vs. Registration:**
    
    - _Traditional Law:_ An oral gift is perfectly valid if the three essentials (Declaration, Acceptance, Possession) are met. Writing is not required.
        
    - **Statutory Change (2004 Amendment):** In Bangladesh, the **Registration Act, 1908** (Section 17) was amended in 2004.
        
    - **Current Law:** All gifts of **immovable property** must be made by a **registered instrument**.
        
    - _Effect:_ An oral gift of land is **no longer valid** in Bangladesh, regardless of delivery of possession. This overrides the traditional Hanafi law on this specific procedural point.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Oral Gift" Trap:** In exam scenarios set in Bangladesh, if a problem says "A makes an oral gift of land to B in 2010," the answer is: **The gift is VOID** due to lack of registration (violating the 2004 amendment). Be careful to check the _date_ of the gift in the problem. If it was in 1990, it might be valid.
    
- **Musha is rarely Void:** Remember that a gift of _Musha_ is usually _Irregular_, not Void. It can be fixed. Courts generally dislike the doctrine of Musha and try to restrict its application.
    
- **Revocation:** A pure _Hiba_ can be revoked by the donor _before_ delivery of possession. _After_ delivery, it can still be revoked with a court decree, unless:
    
    - The donee is a spouse.
        
    - The donee is a prohibited relation (blood relative).
        
    - The donee has died.
        
    - The property is lost/destroyed.
