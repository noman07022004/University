## 11.2 Types of Gift (_Hiba_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** Essential because most "Gifts" in modern practice (especially between spouses for dower) are actually _Hiba-bil-Iwaz_. You must understand how this transforms a "Gift" into a "Sale."
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Extremely frequent. You will often face a problem question asking to distinguish between _Hiba-bil-Iwaz_ and _Hiba-ba-Shart-ul-Iwaz_, or determining if a gift is revocable.
    

---

### Classification by Consideration

Muslim Law classifies gifts based on whether there is a return (_Iwaz_) involved. This distinction changes the legal nature of the transaction from a "Gift" to a "Contract/Sale."

#### 1. Pure _Hiba_ (Simple Gift)

- **Definition:** A transfer of ownership without any consideration or return.
    
- **Characteristics:**
    
    - Must follow the 3 Essentials (Declaration, Acceptance, Delivery).
        
    - Revocable (before delivery, and sometimes after by court decree).
        
    - Motive is usually affection.
        

#### 2. _Hiba-bil-Iwaz_ (Gift with Exchange)

This is the most practical and exam-relevant type. It is arguably **not a gift** at all in the eyes of the law, but a **Sale**.

- **Definition:** A transaction made up of **two mutual gifts**.
    
    - _Step 1:_ A makes a gift to B (e.g., Husband gives land to Wife).
        
    - _Step 2:_ B makes a gift to A as a return (_Iwaz_) (e.g., Wife gives up her claim to Dower or gives a ring).
        
- **Legal Transformation:** Once the _Iwaz_ (return) is paid/completed, the transaction ceases to be a _Hiba_ and becomes a **Sale** (_Bai_).
    
- **Consequences:**
    
    1. **Irrevocability:** It becomes **absolute and irrevocable** the moment the _Iwaz_ is paid.
        
    2. **Delivery not required:** Strict physical delivery of possession (_Qabza_) is **NOT required** for validity (unlike pure Hiba).
        
    3. **Registration:** Since it is treated as a Sale, it **must be registered** in writing under the Registration Act.
        
    4. **Mushaa:** The doctrine of _Mushaa_ (invalidity of undivided share) does **not apply**.
        

#### 3. _Hiba-ba-Shart-ul-Iwaz_ (Gift with a Stipulation for Exchange)

This is a hybrid transaction that starts as a Gift and ends as a Sale.

- **Definition:** A gift made with an express **condition** (_Shart_) that a return (_Iwaz_) shall be given.
    
    - _Example:_ "I give you this car **on the condition** that you give me your watch."
        
- **Legal Nature:**
    
    - **Stage 1 (Before Return):** It is treated as a pure **Gift**. (Delivery of possession is required; it is revocable).
        
    - **Stage 2 (After Return):** Once the donee pays the return, it transforms into a **Sale**. (It becomes irrevocable).
        

#### 4. _Sadaqah_ (Charitable Gift)

- **Definition:** A gift made with the primary motive of acquiring religious merit (_Sawab_) or pleasing God.
    
- **Difference from Hiba:**
    
    - _Hiba_ is usually for affection/love. _Sadaqah_ is for piety.
        
    - _Sadaqah_ is **irrevocable** once possession is delivered (because the return is expected from God, not humans).
        
    - Like Hiba, it requires delivery of possession to be valid.
        

---

### Comparison Table (Crucial for Exams)

|**Feature**|**Pure Hiba**|**Hiba-bil-Iwaz**|**Hiba-ba-Shart-ul-Iwaz**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Consideration**|None|Yes (Iwaz is involved)|Yes (Stipulated)|
|**Delivery of Possession**|**Required**|**Not Required**|**Required**|
|**Revocability**|Revocable (generally)|**Irrevocable**|Revocable until return is paid|
|**Legal Nature**|Gift|**Sale** (_Bai_)|Gift $\rightarrow$ Sale|
|**Doctrine of Mushaa**|Applies|**Does Not Apply**|Applies (initially)|

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Dower" Scenario:** The most common exam example of _Hiba-bil-Iwaz_ is a husband gifting land to his wife in **exchange for her dower debt**.
    
    - _Analysis:_ The wife "gives" the release of her debt (Consideration), and the husband gives the land. This is a Sale. It is valid without physical delivery, but it must be registered.
        
- **Void Condition:** If a gift is made with a condition that is repugnant to ownership (e.g., "I give you this house but you cannot sell it"), the **Gift is Valid** and the **Condition is Void**. The donee takes the house absolutely.
