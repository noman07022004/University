## Sources of Information ⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** This topic is crucial for legal research methodology. It distinguishes between the _authority_ of law (God/Prophet) and the _repository_ of law (books/manuals). To find the law, you must know where to look.
>- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** Less frequently asked as a full long question compared to "Sources of Interpretation." However, short notes on specific books (like the _Hedaya_ or _Fatawa Alamgiri_) or questions about "Legal Literature" are possible.

---

While the "Sources of Interpretation" (Quran, Sunnah, etc.) are the _roots_ from which law is derived, the "Sources of Information" are the _vessels_ or literature where this law is recorded, explained, and preserved. For a modern lawyer or student, one does not usually derive law directly from the Quran (which requires the level of a _Mujtahid_); instead, one consults the authoritative literature produced by centuries of jurists. These sources serve as the library of Muslim Law, categorized by their function in preserving specific aspects of the Sharia.

---
### 1. The Core Legal Texts: Books of Fiqh

**Subtopics Covered:** _Books of fiqh._

The most direct source of information for practical legal questions is the **Fiqh literature**. These are manuals written by jurists of different schools (_Madhhabs_) that compile the rules of Sharia.

- **Nature:** These books do not just list Quranic verses; they provide the final legal rulings (_Ahkam_) derived from them. They are often organized by topic (Rituals, Marriage, Contracts, Crimes).
    
- **Hierarchy of Authority:** In the Hanafi school (dominant in South Asia), certain texts hold higher authority:
    
    - **The Hedaya:** Written by Burhanuddin al-Marghinani (d. 1197). It is the most celebrated textbook of Hanafi law. It was the first Islamic legal text translated into English (via Persian) by Charles Hamilton in 1791, becoming the basis for "Anglo-Muslim Law."
        
    - **Fatawa Alamgiri:** A massive compilation of legal verdicts commissioned by Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb. It is the definitive reference for Hanafi law in the Indian Subcontinent.
        

### 2. Interpretative Literature: Books of Tafsir

**Subtopics Covered:** _Books of tafsir._

**Tafsir** refers to the exegesis or commentary on the Quran. Since the Quran is the primary source of law, understanding the context, linguistic meaning, and legal implication of its verses is vital.

- **Legal Tafsir (_Tafsir Ayat al-Ahkam_):** While general Tafsirs cover the whole Quran, specific legal Tafsirs focus only on the legislative verses (approx. 500 verses).
    
- **Function:** They clarify the "Occasion of Revelation" (_Shan-e-Nuzul_)—why a verse was revealed. This context is essential for determining if a law is general or specific to a situation.
    
    - _Example:_ Imam Qurtubi’s _Al-Jami li-Ahkam al-Quran_ is a famous Tafsir focused on legal deductions.
        

### 3. Specialized Studies: Monographic Works

**Subtopics Covered:** _Monographic works._

Unlike general Fiqh books that cover everything from prayer to warfare, **Monographic works** (_Risalah_) are specialized treatises dedicated to a single legal topic.

- **Purpose:** They allow for a deep-dive analysis of complex subjects.
    
- **Examples:** A jurist might write a dedicated book solely on "The Law of Inheritance" (_Ilm al-Faraid_) or "The Rules of Waqf." These are valuable for lawyers dealing with specific, complex litigation where general manuals are too brief.
    

### 4. The Tools of Verification: Lexicological and Biographical Sources

**Subtopics Covered:** _Lexicological sources, Biographical sources._

To interpret the primary texts correctly, scholars rely on auxiliary sciences.

- **Lexicological Sources (Dictionaries):**
    
    - Classical Arabic is a complex language where a word can have multiple meanings depending on the context. Legal dictionaries define the technical usage of words in Sharia, which often differs from their literal dictionary meaning.
        
    - _Example:_ The word _Salat_ literally means "supplication," but legally means "the ritual prayer."
        
- **Biographical Sources (_Asma al-Rijal_):**
    
    - This is a unique science of Islam used to verify the Sunnah. Since Hadiths were transmitted orally, scholars needed to verify the chain of narrators (_Isnad_).
        
    - **Function:** Biographical books detail the lives of thousands of narrators—their memory, character, truthfulness, and dates of birth/death. If a narrator is found to be a liar or have a weak memory, the legal information they conveyed is rejected. This ensures the information reaching us is authentic.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Anglo-Muslim" Connection:** For exams in this region, always remember **The Hedaya** and **Fatawa Alamgiri**. These are not just books; they are the pillars upon which the British courts constructed the Muslim law applied in India and Bangladesh today.
    
- **Differentiation:** Be able to explain the difference between a _Book of Fiqh_ (human codification of rules) and a _Book of Tafsir_ (explanation of divine text).
    
- **Research Value:** If asked a problem question about "How to verify a Hadith?", the answer lies in the **Biographical Sources** (_Asma al-Rijal_). If asked "Where to find the specific rule on divorce?", the answer is **Books of Fiqh**.
