### 3.3 Monographic Works ⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** While general textbooks provide breadth, monographs provide depth. Understanding that legal literature includes specialized treatises helps in appreciating how complex topics (like Inheritance or Waqf) were developed in isolation before being integrated into general codes.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** It is rare to get a direct question solely on "Monographic Works." However, citing specific monographs (especially on Inheritance/Sirajiyyah) in an answer about that specific topic boosts marks significantly.
    

---

#### Introduction: The Specialists

In legal literature, **Monographic Works** (or _Risalah_) are books dedicated to a **single specific subject** of law, rather than covering the entire spectrum of Sharia (ritual, crime, family, contract).

General _Fiqh_ books (like the _Hedaya_) are like "General Medicine" textbooks. Monographic works are like "Cardiology" or "Neurology" textbooks. When a specific branch of law becomes extremely complex, scholars write separate monographs to cover every minute detail, mathematical calculation, and exceptional case that cannot fit into a general digest.

#### The Necessity of Monographs

Certain areas of Muslim Family Law are technically demanding. For instance:

- **Inheritance (_Fara'id_):** Requires complex mathematics (fractions, LCM).
    
- **Waqf (Endowments):** Requires complex property management and trust law rules.
    
- **Marriage Stipulations:** Requires detailed contract theory.
    

To address these, specialists authored monographs that became the primary reference for those specific chapters.

#### Key Monographic Works in Muslim Law

**1. Al-Sirajiyyah (The Sirajiyyah)**

- **Subject:** **Inheritance (_Fara'id_)**.
    
- **Author:** Siraj al-Din al-Sajawandi (d. 1205 AD).
    
- **Significance:** This is the absolute standard authority on Hanafi Law of Inheritance. It is a masterpiece of legal arithmetic.
    
- **Connection to Bangladesh:** In Bangladesh, the Sunni law of inheritance is practically synonymous with the rules laid down in _Al-Sirajiyyah_. It was translated by Sir William Jones (the first English book on Islamic Law translated in India) and is the basis for all inheritance calculations in our courts today.
    

**2. Al-Sharifiyyah**

- **Subject:** **Inheritance**.
    
- **Significance:** This is the most famous _Sharh_ (commentary) on _Al-Sirajiyyah_. It unlocks the difficult mathematical formulas presented in the original text.
    

**3. Hilat al-Najiz**

- **Subject:** **Dissolution of Marriage**.
    
- **Author:** Maulana Ashraf Ali Thanwi (and a committee of Ulema).
    
- **Significance:** This modern monograph (published in the 1930s) is historically critical. It analyzed the Maliki school's rules on divorce to solve the problem of Muslim women in India who had no way to divorce missing or abusive husbands under strict Hanafi law.
    
- **Legislative Impact:** This monograph directly led to the drafting and passing of the **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939**, which is a key statute in your syllabus.
    

#### Modern Monographs

In the contemporary academic context, "Monographic Works" also refers to PhD theses and specialized books by modern scholars that analyze specific legal problems.

- _Example:_ _Outlines of Muhammadan Law_ by Asaf A.A. Fyzee (while a textbook, his specific chapters are often treated as monographic studies).
    
- _Example:_ _Muslim Law of Divorce_ by K.N. Ahmed (a specialized modern work).
