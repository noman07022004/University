### 3.5 Biographical Sources ⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** Law is not just text; it is a chain of transmission. To know if a law is authentic, one must verify the character and memory of the person who narrated it. This source is the "background check" system of Islamic Law.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** Rarely asked as a direct question. However, understanding the "Hierarchy of Jurists" (which comes from these sources) is essential for answering questions on the "Schools of Classical Theory" or comparing legal opinions.
    

---

#### Introduction: The "Who's Who" of Sharia

**Biographical Sources** (known in Arabic as _Kutub al-Trajim_ or _Ilm al-Rijal_) are encyclopedias that detail the lives, characters, teachers, students, and reliability of narrators and jurists.

In modern law, we check the _current_ validity of a statute. In Islamic Law, we check the _historical_ validity of the source. If a Hadith (saying of the Prophet) is narrated by a known liar or someone with a bad memory, that legal evidence is rejected. Biographical sources provide the data needed to make that judgment.

#### The Science of Men (Ilm al-Rijal)

This discipline is unique to Islamic civilization. It involves two main processes:

1. **Jarh (Discrediting):** Identifying negative traits (e.g., "He was a pious man but had a terrible memory in his old age").
    
2. **Ta'dil (Accrediting):** Confirming reliability (e.g., "He was precise, honest, and memorized thousands of reports").
    

#### Categories of Biographical Works

**1. Tabaqat (Generations/Classes)** These books classify scholars by generation. This is crucial for establishing the **Chain of Transmission (_Isnad_)**. If Person A claims to have heard a law from Person B, but the _Tabaqat_ books show they lived in different centuries, the claim is rejected.

- **Key Work:** _Kitab al-Tabaqat al-Kabir_ by Ibn Sa'd. It details the lives of the Companions (_Sahaba_) and the Successors (_Tabi'un_).
    

**2. Biographical Dictionaries of Specific Schools** Since Bangladesh follows the **Hanafi** school, lawyers historically needed to know who the top Hanafi lawyers were to determine which opinion carries more weight.

- **Key Work:** _Al-Jawahir al-Mudiyyah_ (The Shining Jewels). This book lists the biographies specifically of Hanafi jurists.
    

#### Practical Application in Law

Why does a modern law student need this?

- **Establishing Authority:** If two books give different laws (e.g., one says divorce is valid, one says it's void), we look at the authors. Was Author A a "Mujtahid" (one who can derive law) or just a copier? Biographical sources tell us their rank.
    
- **The Hierarchy of Jurists:**
    
    1. **Mujtahid fi al-Shar'** (Absolute Jurist like Abu Hanifa).
        
    2. **Mujtahid fi al-Madhhab** (School Jurist like Abu Yusuf).
        
    3. **Ashab al-Tarjih** (Jurists competent to prefer one view over another).
        
    
    - Biographical sources tell us which category a scholar belongs to, helping judges decide which opinion to enforce.

### Important Notes & Tips

> - **Concept to Master:** **Sanad (Chain of Transmission)**. Biographical sources are the tools used to verify the _Sanad_. If the _Sanad_ is weak, the law derived from it is weak.
>     
> - **Relevance:** In the **Introduction of Islamic Law into British India** (Topic 1.12), British judges often struggled because they couldn't read these biographical sources. They sometimes treated weak opinions as strong laws because they didn't know the rank of the author they were reading.
>     
> - **Exam Tip:** If you are discussing **Imam Abu Hanifa** or his two disciples (**Abu Yusuf** and **Imam Muhammad**) in an essay, mentioning that "Biographical sources describe them as the primary shapers of the Hanafi school" adds academic flair.
