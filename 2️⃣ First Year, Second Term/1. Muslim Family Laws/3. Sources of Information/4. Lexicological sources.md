### 3.4 Lexicological Sources ⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** Legal interpretation often hinges on the precise meaning of a single word. Understanding the difference between a word's literal meaning (_Lughawi_) and its technical legal meaning (_Shar'i_) is fundamental to Islamic jurisprudence.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 2/5):** It is highly unlikely to appear as a broad essay question. However, it is useful for answering objective questions or explaining specific legal terms (like _Nikah_, _Talaq_, _Waqf_) in definitions.
    

---

#### Introduction: The Science of Legal Language

**Lexicological Sources** are, simply put, authoritative dictionaries. However, in Islamic Law, they serve a much higher purpose than a standard dictionary.

In Sharia, the Lawgiver (God) used specific Arabic words to reveal laws. Over time, the meanings of words evolve. To ensure the law remains accurate to the original divine intent, jurists rely on **classical lexicography** (_Ilm al-Lugha_) to freeze and preserve the definitions of words as they were understood during the time of the Prophet.

#### Literal vs. Technical Meaning

A primary function of these sources is to distinguish between:

1. **Literal Meaning (_Ma'na Lughawi_):** The linguistic definition used by Arabs in pre-Islamic poetry and daily speech.
    
2. **Technical Meaning (_Ma'na Shar'i_):** The specific definition assigned to that word by Islamic law.
    

- _Example:_ **Salat**.
    
    - _Literal:_ Prayer or supplication.
        
    - _Technical:_ The specific ritual worship with standing, bowing, and prostration.
        
- _Example:_ **Nikah**.
    
    - _Literal:_ Union or sexual intercourse.
        
    - _Technical:_ A contract that legalizes sexual enjoyment between a man and a woman.
        
    - _Conflict:_ If a word is used in a contract, does it imply the literal or technical meaning? Lexicological sources help judges decide.
        

#### Key Lexicological Works

For a student of Muslim law, these are the heavyweights cited in legal arguments:

**1. Lisan al-Arab (The Tongue of the Arabs)**

- **Author:** Ibn Manzur (d. 1311 AD).
    
- **Significance:** This is the most comprehensive dictionary of the Arabic language. It is essentially the "Oxford English Dictionary" of Arabic. It cites pre-Islamic poetry to prove the original meaning of words used in the Quran and Hadith.
    

**2. Al-Qamus al-Muhit (The Surrounding Ocean)**

- **Author:** Al-Firuzabadi.
    
- **Significance:** A standard, concise dictionary widely used by jurists to quickly check root meanings.
    

**3. Al-Mughrib (The Expresser)**

- **Author:** Al-Mutarrizi.
    
- **Significance:** This is a **specialized dictionary for the Hanafi school**. It explains the difficult words found specifically in Hanafi legal texts (like the _Hedaya_). It is crucial for understanding the vocabulary of the syllabus texts.
    

#### The "Polysemy" Problem in Family Law

Lexicological sources are vital when a word in the Quran has two opposite meanings (_Addad_).

- **The _Quru_ Debate:**
    
    - The Quran states a divorced woman must wait for three _Quru_ (Surah Baqarah 2:228).
        
    - **Lexicological Issue:** The word _Quru_ in Arabic can mean "Period of Menstruation" OR "Period of Purity" (between menstruations).
        
    - **Legal Consequence:** The _Hanafis_ (using lexicological evidence) argue it means menstruation. The _Shafi'is_ argue it means purity. This changes the duration of the _Iddat_ (waiting period) by several weeks.
        
    - _Conclusion:_ Without lexicological sources, we cannot determine the length of the statutory waiting period.

### Important Notes & Tips

> - **Concept to Remember:** **Haqiqi (Literal) vs. Majazi (Metaphorical)**. A major part of interpreting statutes involves deciding if a word is used metaphorically. Lexicological sources provide the boundaries for these interpretations.
>     
> - **Tip:** When defining terms in your exam (e.g., "Define _Waqf_"), always start with: _"Literally, it means detention or stoppage. Legally, it means..."_ This structure shows you understand the lexicological foundation of the law.
