## 5.3 Elements (_Arkan_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic helps you distinguish between what is _part_ of the marriage (Elements) and what is _required_ for the marriage (Conditions). This technical distinction is crucial for understanding why missing witnesses makes a marriage "Irregular" (fixable) while missing consent makes it "Void" (non-existent).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions typically ask to "Distinguish between the Pillars (_Rukn_) and Conditions (_Shart_) of marriage."
    

---

### The Pillars of the Contract

In Islamic Jurisprudence, there is a strict technical distinction between an **Element/Pillar (_Rukn_)** and a **Condition (_Shart_)**.

- **Element (_Rukn_):** Something that is an intrinsic part of the act itself. If an element is missing, the act simply does not exist. (e.g., Bowing is an _element_ of Prayer).
    
- **Condition (_Shart_):** Something that is external to the act but necessary for its validity. (e.g., Wudu/Ablution is a _condition_ for Prayer).
    

**The Conflict of Schools:**

- **Hanafi View (Bangladesh):** The Hanafis are very strict. They argue that there is only **one true Element** of marriage: The **Form** (Offer and Acceptance). Everything else (Witnesses, Capacity, Guardian) are merely Conditions.
    
- **Majority View (Shafi'i/Maliki):** They argue there are multiple elements, including the Guardian (_Wali_) and the Witnesses.
    

For the purpose of your syllabus and the Bangladeshi context, we analyze the "Elements" broadly to include the essential components that make up the contract.

#### 1. The Form (_Sigha_) – The Core Pillar

According to the Hanafis, this is the essence of the contract.

- **Offer (_Ijab_):** The declaration of willingness.
    
- **Acceptance (_Qubul_):** The agreement to the declaration.
    
- **Requirement:** As discussed in "Formation," these must be reciprocal, unconditional, and made in the same meeting. If this Element is missing (e.g., only one party speaks), there is no marriage.
    

#### 2. The Contracting Parties (_Al-Aaqidain_)

The marriage cannot exist without the subjects of the contract.

- **The Groom:** Must be a Muslim male (or Kitabi in some views, but strict interpretations vary), acting either personally or through a proxy (_Wakil_).
    
- **The Bride:** Must be a Muslim or Kitabia (Christian/Jewish) female.
    
- **The Element of Existence:** Both parties must be physically existing and identifiable at the time of the contract. You cannot marry "one of my daughters" (undefined) or an unborn child.
    

#### 3. The Subject Matter (_Mahal_)

Every civil contract must have a subject matter. In a marriage contract, the subject matter is dual:

- **Legalizing Intercourse:** The immediate legal effect is rendering sexual relations lawful (_Halal_) and the procreation of children legitimate.
    
- **Companionship:** Establishing a life of mutual enjoyment and support.
    
- _Note:_ Unlike a sale where "Goods" are transferred, here the "enjoyment" (_Manfa'a_) is the subject.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Crucial Distinction (Rukn vs. Shart):**
    
    - If an **Element** (_Rukn_) is missing (e.g., No Acceptance), the marriage is **Void (_Batil_)** ab initio (from the start). It is a legal nullity.
        
    - If a **Condition** (_Shart_) is missing (e.g., No Witnesses in Hanafi law), the marriage is **Irregular (_Fasid_)**. It exists but is defective.
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** In Bangladeshi courts, usually only the **Offer and Acceptance** are treated as the strict "Elements" that constitute the _factum_ of marriage. Issues like "Age" or "Witnesses" are treated as regulatory conditions.
    
- **Visual Aid:** Think of a table.
    
    - The **Legs** are the Elements (Offer/Acceptance). If you remove a leg, the table falls (Void).
        
    - The **Polish** is the Condition (Witnesses). If you remove the polish, the table is ugly/defective (Irregular), but it still stands.
