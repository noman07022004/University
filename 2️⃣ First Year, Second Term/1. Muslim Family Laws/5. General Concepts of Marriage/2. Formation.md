## 5.2 Formation (_Ijab-o-Qubul_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the specific legal mechanism of how the marriage happens. If the formation is defective (e.g., done in separate meetings), the marriage does not exist.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often appear as problem scenarios: _"A proposes to B via letter. B accepts in her room alone. Is the marriage valid?"_
    

---

### The Mechanics of the Contract

Since Muslim marriage is a civil contract, its formation relies entirely on the successful execution of **Offer** and **Acceptance**. Unlike a sacrament which relies on a priest's blessing, the formation of _Nikah_ relies on the mutual consent of the parties expressed in a specific legal manner.

The formation requires two pillars (_Rukn_):

1. **Declaration / Offer (_Ijab_)**
    
2. **Acceptance (_Qubul_)**
    

#### 1. The Process of Proposal and Acceptance

- **Ijab (Declaration):** This signifies the willingness to contract marriage. It is usually made by the representative of the bride, though it can come from the groom.
    
- **Qubul (Acceptance):** This signifies the consent to the proposal. It must be made by the other party.
    
- **Reciprocity:** The acceptance must exactly match the proposal.
    
    - _Example:_ If the Groom says, "I marry you for 50,000 Taka Dower," and the Bride says, "I accept for 100,000 Taka," there is no contract. This is a counter-offer, not an acceptance.
        

#### 2. The Doctrine of "Unity of Session" (_Majlis_)

This is the most critical technical rule in Hanafi Law regarding formation.

- **The Rule:** The Declaration and Acceptance must take place at the **same meeting** (_Majlis_).
    
- **Requirement:** The acceptance must be immediate and continuous with the offer.
    
- **The Problem Scenario:**
    
    - If the Groom makes a proposal in the morning, and the parties separate or change the topic of conversation, and the Bride accepts in the afternoon, the proposal has "lapsed." The marriage is **Invalid**.
        
    - If the Groom sends a **Letter** or **Messenger**: The "Meeting" is the place/time where the letter is read or the message is delivered. The Bride must accept _immediately_ upon reading/hearing it in that same session. She cannot read it, go for lunch, and then accept.
        

#### 3. Language and Tense

- **Clarity:** The words used must clearly indicate an intention to establish a permanent relationship of husband and wife (_Tamlik_).
    
- **Tense:** In classical Arabic law, the **Past Tense** (_Sigha-e-Mazi_) is preferred (e.g., "I have married you" rather than "I will marry you") because the past tense indicates **Certainty**, whereas the future tense acts merely as a promise.
    
- **Ambiguous Words:** Words like "I give myself to you" or "I sell myself to you" can effect a valid marriage if the intention is clear and witnesses are present.
    

#### 4. Absence of Religious Ceremony

It is vital to reiterate that **no specific religious rite** is required for the legal formation of the contract.

- The recitation of the _Khutbah_ (Sermon), the presence of a _Mullah/Kazi_, or the reading of verses are customary and recommended (_Mustahabb_) for spiritual blessings, but legally, the marriage is formed solely by the exchange of _Ijab_ and _Qubul_ in front of witnesses.

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Modern Context (Telephone/Zoom Marriages):**
    
    - _Question:_ Can a marriage be formed over a video call?
        
    - _Bangladeshi/Hanafi View:_ Traditionally, the witnesses must be physically present at the _same place_ as the parties.
        
    - _Solution:_ In modern practice (and under the _Majlis_ doctrine), if the witnesses are with **one party** (e.g., the bride) and the other party (groom) is on speakerphone/video, and the witnesses can clearly hear/identify the groom's voice making the proposal, it is often treated as a valid "Constructive Meeting." However, lawyers advise executing a **Written Contract** via proxy (_Wakil_) to avoid ambiguity.
        
- **Key Distinction:**
    
    - _Promise to Marry (Mangni):_ A contract to marry in the _future_. Not binding; can be broken (though gifts may be returned).
        
    - _Marriage (Nikah):_ A contract of marriage in the _present_. Binding immediately.
        
- **Exam Trap:** If a question says "A wrote a letter to B proposing marriage. B accepted the next day after thinking about it." -> **Answer:** No Marriage. The acceptance must be in the "same session" as the reading of the letter.
