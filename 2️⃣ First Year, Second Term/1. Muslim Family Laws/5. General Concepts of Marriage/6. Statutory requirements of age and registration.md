## 5.6 Statutory Requirements of Age and Registration ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the intersection of religion and state. While Sharia allows marriage at puberty and oral contracts, the State demands adulthood and written proof. A lawyer in Bangladesh deals with this "Statutory Law" far more than classical theory.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Critical for problem questions. Expect scenarios like: _"A 16-year-old girl marries without registration. Discuss the validity and criminal liability."_
    

---

### State Regulation of Sharia

In Bangladesh, the pure Sharia law regarding marriage (which allows marriage at puberty and oral contracts) has been significantly modified by "Procedural Statutes." These laws do not necessarily change the _religious validity_ of the marriage, but they impose strict _criminal penalties_ and _evidentiary requirements_.

Two primary acts govern this area:

1. **The Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017** (replacing the 1929 Act).
    
2. **The Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974**.
    

#### 1. Statutory Age Requirements

Under classical Hanafi Law, a boy or girl can marry upon attaining puberty (presumed at 15). However, the State views this as a social evil and regulates it strictly.

**A. The Legal Age Limit** According to the **Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017**:

- **Male:** Must be **21 years** or older.
    
- **Female:** Must be **18 years** or older.
    

**B. Consequences of Violation (The "Validity vs. Liability" Paradox)** This is the most critical legal nuance.

- **Criminal Liability:** If a male of 22 marries a female of 16, he (the groom), the parents, and the Kazi (Registrar) are guilty of a criminal offense. They face imprisonment (up to 2 years) and fines.
    
- **Validity of Marriage:** Crucially, the Act **does NOT declare the marriage void**.
    
    - If the parties have reached puberty (e.g., 15 years old), the marriage is **Valid (_Sahih_)** under Muslim Law despite being a crime under State Law.
        
    - _Result:_ The couple is legally married (children are legitimate), but the husband goes to jail.
        

**C. The "Special Provision" (Section 19 of 2017 Act)** The 2017 Act introduced a controversial exception. A marriage of a minor (under 18/21) is permitted if:

1. It is in the "best interest" of the minor (e.g., accidental pregnancy).
    
2. There is consent from the parents/guardians.
    
3. There is a **Court Order** permitting it.
    

#### 2. Statutory Registration Requirements

Under classical law, an oral marriage with witnesses is perfectly valid. However, proving an oral marriage in court is difficult.

**A. The Mandate** **The Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974** mandates that:

- Every marriage solemnized under Muslim Law in Bangladesh **MUST be registered**.
    
- The government appoints **Nikah Registrars** (commonly called _Kazis_) for this purpose.
    

**B. The Document: The Kabinnama** The marriage is recorded in a standard statutory form known as the **Nikahnama** (or _Kabinnama_).

- It contains crucial details: Dower amount, mode of payment, and importantly, **Stipulations** (like delegated divorce).
    

**C. Consequences of Non-Registration**

- **Criminal Liability:** Not registering a marriage is a punishable offense (up to 2 years imprisonment or fine for the Groom and Kazi).
    
- **Evidentiary Value:**
    
    - **Validity:** Non-registration does **NOT** invalidate the marriage. An unregistered oral marriage (with witnesses) is still Valid.
        
    - **Practicality:** Without a registration certificate, a wife will find it extremely difficult to file a suit for **Maintenance** or **Dower** in Family Court. Courts often view unregistered claims with suspicion.
        

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Case Law Strategy:** In **Dr. A.L.M. Abdullah v. Rokeya Khatoon**, the court held that while registration is mandatory, failure to register does not affect the _validity_ of the marriage itself. Use this to argue that an unregistered wife still has rights.
    
- **Exam Trap:** If a question asks _"Is an unregistered marriage void?"_, the answer is **NO**. It is Valid but Punishable.
    
- **Practical Tip:** Always advise clients to register. The _Kabinnama_ is the only document that secures the wife's right to **Tafweez-e-Talaq** (Delegated Divorce in Column 18). If the marriage isn't registered, she effectively loses this power because there is no written proof of the delegation.
