## 5.1 Capacity (_Ahlia_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐½

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4.5/5):** This defines _who_ can enter the ring. If you don't understand capacity, you can't determine if a marriage is valid or if a guardian's intervention was necessary. The distinction between "Physical Puberty" and "Statutory Majority" is the most critical concept here.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent topic. Questions often ask: _"When does a Muslim attain majority for the purpose of marriage?"_ or _"Discuss the conflict between Sharia and the Child Marriage Restraint Act."_
    

---

### Competence to Contract

Since Muslim marriage is a civil contract, the parties must have the legal capacity (competence) to enter into it. In Islamic legal terminology, this is known as **Ahlia**.

For a valid marriage to be contracted _personally_ (without a guardian), two essential elements must be present: **Soundness of Mind** (_Aql_) and **Puberty/Majority** (_Bulugh_).

#### 1. Soundness of Mind (_Aql_)

- **The Rule:** A person must be sane (_Aaqil_) to contract a marriage.
    
- **Lunatics/Insane Persons:** A marriage contracted by a person of unsound mind without a guardian is **Void** (or Irregular depending on the jurist).
    
    - _Exception:_ A lunatic _can_ be married validly by their legal guardian (_Wali_) if it is in their best interest (e.g., for care), but they cannot do it themselves.
        
    - _Lucid Intervals:_ If a person has periods of sanity, a marriage contracted during a "lucid interval" is valid.
        

#### 2. Majority / Puberty (_Bulugh_)

This is the area of greatest conflict between Classical Law and Modern Statutory Law.

**A. The Classical Sharia View (The 15-Year Rule)**

- Under pure Muslim Law, "Majority" for the purpose of marriage is NOT 18 years (as in the Majority Act 1875). It is governed by **Physical Puberty**.
    
- **Signs of Puberty:** It is presumed to be attained when physical signs appear (menstruation for girls, wet dreams for boys).
    
- **Presumption:** If no signs appear, the Hanafi school presumes puberty at the age of **15 years** (lunar calculation).
    
- **Consequence:** Once a boy or girl turns 15 (or hits puberty), they are free to marry anyone they choose. The consent of their father/guardian is **not required** for validity (specifically in the Hanafi school).
    

**B. The Statutory View (Bangladeshi Law)**

- The **Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017** overrides the classical view for administrative and criminal purposes.
    
- **Legal Age:**
    
    - **Male:** 21 Years.
        
    - **Female:** 18 Years.
        
- **The Conflict:** What happens if a 16-year-old girl marries?
    
    - **Criminal Liability:** The husband, the person who performed the marriage (Kazi), and the parents can be jailed or fined.
        
    - **Validity of Marriage:** Legally, the marriage itself remains **Valid** if the parties had reached puberty (15+). The statute punishes the _act_ but does not explicitly invalidate the _contract_ (unless forced).
        
    - _Note:_ Courts are increasingly reluctant to recognize these unions, but strictly speaking, under the Hanafi interpretation used in Bangladesh, the marriage is valid but punishable.
        

#### 3. Guardianship in Marriage (_Jabar_)

What if the parties lack capacity (i.e., they are minors)?

- **Marriage by Guardian:** A minor (below puberty) or a lunatic cannot contract marriage personally. However, they can be married by their **Guardian (_Wali_)**.
    
- **Power of Compulsion (_Jabar_):** The guardian has the power to impose marriage on the minor.
    
- **Who is the Guardian?** The order of priority is:
    
    1. Father.
        
    2. Paternal Grandfather.
        
    3. Brother/Other Paternal Relations.
        
- **Termination of Authority:** The moment the child attains puberty (15 years), the guardian's power of _Jabar_ ends. An adult Hanafi woman cannot be forced to marry by her father.
    

#### 4. The Option of Puberty (_Khiyar-ul-Bulugh_)

This is the safety valve for minors married off by guardians.

- **Concept:** If a minor was married by a guardian (other than the father or grandfather), they have the right to **repudiate** (cancel) the marriage upon attaining puberty.
    
- **Conditions:**
    
    1. They must exercise this right immediately upon hitting puberty (for girls) or knowing of the marriage.
        
    2. The marriage must not have been consummated.
        
- **Statutory Expansion:** Under the **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939** (applicable in Bangladesh), a girl can repudiate the marriage even if it was contracted by her **Father or Grandfather**, provided she does so before age 19 and the marriage was not consummated.
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips

- **Exam Trap (Capacity vs. Majority Act):** Do not confuse the _Majority Act 1875_ (which sets age at 18 for contracts/voting) with capacity for marriage. Section 2 of the Majority Act explicitly _excludes_ marriage and divorce. Therefore, for marriage, you go back to the Muslim Law standard (Puberty/15), subject to the penalties of the Child Marriage Restraint Act.
    
- **The "Hanafi Exception" for Women:** In the Shafi'i and Maliki schools, an adult virgin woman _cannot_ marry without her guardian's consent. In the **Hanafi school** (Bangladesh), an adult woman (15+) has full capacity to marry without any guardian. This is a crucial distinction for problem questions involving adult elopement.
    
- **Relevant Case Law:** Mention **_Nelly Zaman v. Giasuddin_** to show that courts uphold the agency and consent of the adult woman over traditional patriarchal authority.
