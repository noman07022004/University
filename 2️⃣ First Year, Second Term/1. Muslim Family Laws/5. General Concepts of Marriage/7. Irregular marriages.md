## 5.7 Irregular Marriages (_Fasid_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is a unique feature of the **Hanafi School**. Unlike Western law or Shia law (which usually see marriage as either Valid or Void), Hanafi law creates a "middle ground" to save the legitimacy of children. You cannot solve inheritance or paternity cases without understanding this.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Highly frequent. Exam questions often present a scenario (e.g., _"A marries B during her Iddat"_) and ask you to determine the status and legal consequences of the marriage.
    

---

### The "Defective" Contract

In the **Hanafi School** of interpretation (which governs Bangladesh), marriages are classified into three grades:

1. **Valid (_Sahih_)**
    
2. **Void (_Batil_)**
    
3. **Irregular (_Fasid_)**
    

An **Irregular Marriage (_Fasid_)** is a marriage that is not unlawful in itself, but is unlawful due to a temporary defect or a lack of formality. It is often described as "defective but salvageable." The defect is usually procedural and can be removed.

_Note:_ Under **Shia Law**, this category generally does not exist. For Shias, a marriage is either Valid or Void.

#### 1. Grounds for Irregularity

A marriage becomes Irregular (_Fasid_) in the following specific instances under Hanafi Law:

1. **Absence of Witnesses:** A marriage contracted without the required witnesses (Two males, or one male and two females).
    
2. **Marriage during Iddat:** A marriage with a woman who is currently observing Iddat (waiting period from a previous death or divorce).
    
3. **Difference of Religion:** A marriage between a Muslim man and a woman who is an idol worshiper or fire worshiper (not a _Kitabia_).
    
    - _Note:_ Marriage with a Christian/Jew is Valid. Marriage with a Hindu is technically Irregular (though some jurists argue Void, the courts often lean towards Fasid to protect children).
        
4. **Unlawful Conjunction (Two Sisters):** Marrying two sisters at the same time, or a woman and her aunt. The second marriage is Irregular.
    
5. **The Fifth Wife:** A man having four wives contracts a marriage with a fifth one. The fifth marriage is Irregular.
    

#### 2. Legal Effects of an Irregular Marriage

The legal consequences depend entirely on whether **Consummation** (sexual intercourse) has taken place.

**A. Before Consummation**

- **Effect:** The marriage has **Zero Effect**.
    
- It is treated legally as if it were Void.
    
- The parties can separate instantly with no formalities.
    
- The wife has no right to Dower or Maintenance.
    

**B. After Consummation (Crucial for Exams)** If the couple has had sexual relations, the law steps in to protect the consequences of that act, specifically the children.

1. **Legitimacy of Children:** The children born of such a marriage are **Legitimate**. They inherit from their father. (This is the most critical difference from a Void marriage).
    
2. **Dower (_Mahr_):** The wife is entitled to **Proper Dower** (_Mahr-e-Mithl_) or the **Specified Dower**, whichever is _less_.
    
3. **Iddat:** The wife must observe Iddat (usually 3 courses) if they separate or the husband dies.
    
4. **Inheritance:** Crucially, there are **NO mutual rights of inheritance** between the husband and wife. If the husband dies, the "Fasid" wife gets nothing (though her children do).
    
5. **Obligation to Separate:** It is the religious duty of the parties to separate or "fix" the defect immediately. The court can forcefully separate them.
    

#### 3. How to Validate (Fix) an Irregular Marriage

Unlike a Void marriage (which is dead forever), an Irregular marriage can often be "healed":

- **Case of No Witnesses:** The couple can simply contract the marriage again _with_ witnesses.
    
- **Case of 5th Wife:** The husband can divorce one of his first four wives.
    
- **Case of Iddat:** The defect is removed automatically once the Iddat period expires (though they should technically renew the contract).
    
- **Case of Religion:** The wife can convert to Islam (or Christianity/Judaism).

### 📝 Important Notes & Tips
- **Distinction Table (Void vs. Irregular):** 

|**Feature**|**Void (Bāṭil)**|**Irregular (Fāsid)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Cause**|Permanent prohibition (e.g., incest)|Temporary or formal defect|
|**Status**|Unlawful from the start|Unlawful in its attribute|
|**Children**|Illegitimate|Legitimate|
|**Dower (Mahr)**|None|Payable (if consummated)|
|**ʿIddah**|None|Required (if consummated)|
|**Inheritance**|None|None|


- **Bangladeshi Context:**
    
    - In **1961**, the _Muslim Family Laws Ordinance_ made polygamy difficult. If a man marries a second wife without permission, is it Void or Irregular?
        
    - _Answer:_ It is **Valid** (not even Irregular) under Sharia, but the husband faces criminal punishment.
        
    - However, courts in Bangladesh frequently use the concept of _Fasid_ to protect women. For example, if a Hindu woman marries a Muslim man, strictly it might be argued as Void by some, but courts treat it as _Fasid_ so her children are not branded as "bastards."
        
- **Exam Trap:** "A marries his sister." -> **Void (_Batil_)**. "A marries his wife's sister while wife is alive." -> **Irregular (_Fasid_)**.
