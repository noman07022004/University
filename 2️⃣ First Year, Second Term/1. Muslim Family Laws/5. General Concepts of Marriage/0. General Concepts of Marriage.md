## General Concepts of Marriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the "mechanics" of the course. You cannot understand family law without knowing how a marriage is validly created and what happens if the rules are broken.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Extremely high priority. Problem questions almost always involve a defect in marriage (e.g., "A marries B without witnesses"). You must be able to classify the marriage as Valid, Void, or Irregular to solve the problem.
    

### Introduction

In Muslim Law, Marriage (_Nikah_) is a civil contract entered into by two parties for the purpose of legalizing sexual intercourse and the procreation of children. Unlike other systems that require elaborate religious rituals, the formation of a Muslim marriage is surprisingly simple and procedural. However, strict rules govern the capacity of the parties and the validity of the procedure. If these rules are not followed, the marriage may fall into different legal categories (Valid, Void, or Irregular), each with drastically different consequences.

### 1. Capacity and Elements

**Subtopics Covered:** _Capacity, Elements._

For a Muslim marriage to be initiated, the parties must have the legal capacity to contract.

- **Competence of Parties:** Both the bride and groom must be:
    
    1. **Muslims:** (Or Kitabiyas for men, explained later).
        
    2. **Sound Mind:** Lunatics cannot contract marriage without a guardian.
        
    3. **Puberty (_Bulugh_):** Traditionally, the age of capacity is puberty (presumed at 15 years). However, **Statutory Law** in Bangladesh overrides this (see below).
        
- **Free Consent:** The consent must be free from coercion, fraud, or undue influence.
    

### 2. Formation and Witnesses

**Subtopics Covered:** _Formation, Conditions, Witness._

The actual process of forming the marriage relies on "Proposal and Acceptance" in the presence of witnesses.

- **The Procedure (_Formation_):**
    
    - **Ijab (Offer):** One party makes a clear proposal.
        
    - **Qabul (Acceptance):** The other party accepts it.
        
    - **Same Meeting:** The offer and acceptance must happen in the _same_ meeting (_Majlis_). If a man proposes in the morning and the woman accepts in the evening after leaving the room, there is no marriage.
        
- **The Witnesses:**
    
    - **Sunni Law:** The marriage must be witnessed by **two male Muslims** OR **one male and two female Muslims**. All must be sane and adult.
        
    - **Shia Law:** Witnesses are **not** required for the marriage contract itself (but are strictly required for divorce).
        

### 3. Statutory Requirements

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory requirements of age and registration._

While traditional Sharia allows marriage at puberty, the laws of Bangladesh impose stricter regulations to prevent child marriage and ensure record-keeping.

- **Age of Marriage:** Under the _Child Marriage Restraint Act, 2017_ (replacing the 1929 Act), the minimum age is:
    
    - **Male:** 21 years.
        
    - **Female:** 18 years.
        
    - _Note:_ A marriage contracted below this age is **not void** in Muslim law (it remains valid but is a criminal offense), though the perpetrators face penalties.
        
- **Registration:** Under the _Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974_, registration is **compulsory**.
    
    - Failure to register is a punishable offense but, crucially, **does not invalidate the marriage**. An oral marriage proved by witnesses is still valid in court, though difficult to prove.
        

### 4. Classification of Marriage (Validity)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Irregular marriages, Conditions._

This is the most critical analytical framework in Muslim law. A marriage is not simply "valid" or "invalid." It falls into one of three categories:

#### A. Valid Marriage (_Sahih_)

- **Definition:** All conditions (capacity, witnesses, no prohibitions) are fulfilled.
    
- **Legal Effects:**
    
    - Sexual intercourse becomes lawful.
        
    - The wife is entitled to Dower (_Mahr_) and Maintenance.
        
    - Mutual rights of inheritance are established.
        
    - Children are legitimate.
        

#### B. Void Marriage (_Batil_)

- **Definition:** A marriage that is unlawful from the start due to a **permanent prohibition**. It is a nullity; it never existed in the eyes of the law.
    
- **Grounds:**
    
    - Prohibited degree of relationship (Consanguinity/Affinity) e.g., marrying one's mother or sister.
        
    - Marrying a non-scriptural woman (Idolatress/Polytheist).
        
    - Polyandry (A woman marrying a second husband while the first is alive).
        
- **Legal Effects:** No legal rights or obligations created. Children are illegitimate.
    

#### C. Irregular Marriage (_Fasid_)

- **Definition:** A marriage that suffers from a **temporary or accidental defect**. It is not void, but "flawed." If the defect is removed, the marriage becomes Valid (_Sahih_).
    
- **Grounds:**
    
    - Marriage without witnesses.
        
    - Marriage with a fifth wife.
        
    - Marriage during _Iddat_ (waiting period).
        
    - Marriage between two people of different religion (Sunni male + Kitabiya female is valid, but other combinations may be irregular depending on interpretation).
        
- **Legal Effects:**
    
    - **Before Consummation:** No legal effect.
        
    - **After Consummation:**
        
        - Wife gets Dower (specified or proper, whichever is less).
            
        - Children are **legitimate** and can inherit (This is the huge difference from Void marriage).
            
        - **No** mutual inheritance between husband and wife.
            
        - Must be terminated immediately.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Batil vs. Fasid" Trap:** This is the most common exam trap.
    
    - _Question:_ "A marries his sister." -> **Void (Batil)**. (Permanent prohibition).
        
    - _Question:_ "A marries a woman without witnesses." -> **Irregular (Fasid)**. (Temporary defect—witnesses can be found later).
        
    - _Outcome:_ Children of the sister are illegitimate. Children of the unwitnessed marriage are legitimate.
        
- **Registration Paradox:** Students often think an unregistered marriage is invalid. It is not. It is _illegal_ (punishable by jail/fine) but _valid_ (creates rights/duties).
    
- **Shia Exception:** Remember that Shias do _not_ recognize the "Irregular" (_Fasid_) category. For them, a marriage is either Valid or Void.
    
- **Women as Witnesses:** In Sunni law, two women replace one man (2M or 1M+2F). A marriage with **only female witnesses** (even 4 or 10) is **invalid** (or irregular depending on jurist). There _must_ be at least one male.
