## 10.3 Custodial Care (_Hizanat_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the most practical aspect of parentage law. In real-world litigation, 90% of disputes are about _Hizanat_ (who keeps the child?), not _Wilayat_ (who manages the land?).
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Highly frequent. You will face problems like: _"The mother has died. The maternal grandmother and the father are fighting for the 4-year-old girl. Who wins?"_
    

---


### 1. Definition and Nature

- **Meaning:** _Hizanat_ literally means "the care of the infant." Legally, it refers to the **right to the physical custody** of a child for the purpose of rearing, nursing, and bringing them up.
    
- **Distinction:** It is strictly distinct from **Guardianship (_Wilayat_)**.
    
    - _Custodian (Hizana):_ Provides love, feeding, and daily care (Mother's domain).
        
    - _Guardian (Wali):_ Provides money, education decisions, and marriage contracts (Father's domain).
        
- **Nature of the Right:** Is it a right of the mother or the child?
    
    - Islamic law views it primarily as the **Right of the Child** (_Haqq al-حضana_). The law presumes that a young child needs the tender care of a woman (mother/grandmother) more than the supervision of a man. Therefore, a mother cannot simply "surrender" or sell this right if it harms the child.
        

### 2. The Mother's Right (_Haqq-i-Hizanat_) 
The mother is the most entitled person to the custody of her infant child. This right exists even if she is divorced.

- **Age Limits (Hanafi Law):**
    
    - **Son:** Until he attains the age of **7 years**.
        
    - **Daughter:** Until she attains **Puberty** (presumed at 15 years).
        
- **Right of Place:** The mother generally has the right to keep the child in her own home. The father cannot forcibly remove the child, but he can visit.
    

**3. The Order of Priority (Who comes after the Mother?)** If the mother dies, is disqualified, or loses her right, custody does **NOT** automatically go to the father. This is a common misconception. It goes to other **female relatives** first.

**The Hierarchy of Female Custodians:**

1. **Mother.**
    
2. **Maternal Grandmother** (Mother's mother, h.h.s. - how high soever).
    
3. **Paternal Grandmother** (Father's mother, h.h.s.).
    
4. **Full Sister.**
    
5. **Uterine Sister.**
    
6. **Consanguine Sister.**
    
7. **Maternal Aunts.**
    
8. **Paternal Aunts.**
    

- _Exam Tip:_ Note that the **Maternal Grandmother** beats the **Father** for custody of a young child. If a mother dies leaving a 2-year-old son, the grandmother takes him, not the father.
    

**The Hierarchy of Male Custodians:** Only when **ALL** eligible female relatives are exhausted or disqualified does the custody pass to the males (Agnates).

1. **Father.**
    
2. **Paternal Grandfather.**
    
3. **Full Brother.**
    
4. **Consanguine Brother.**
    
5. **Uncles.**
    

---

### 4. Loss of Custody (Disqualifications)

A mother (or other female custodian) loses her right to _Hizanat_ in the following specific situations:

- **A. Marriage to a Stranger (Crucial):** If the mother marries a man who is **not related** to the child within the prohibited degrees (e.g., a total stranger), she loses custody.
    
    - _Reasoning:_ The stranger step-father may not treat the child with affection, or the mother may be too occupied with the new husband.
        
    - _Exception:_ If she marries a relative (e.g., the child's **Uncle**), she **retains** custody because the uncle is a blood relative who will protect the child.
        
- **B. Removal from Jurisdiction:** If she moves the child to a distant place (so far that the father cannot visit or return in a day) without the father's consent.
    
- **C. Immorality (_Fasiqa_):** If she leads an immoral life (e.g., prostitution, open adultery) that poses a danger to the child's moral upbringing.
    
- **D. Neglect/Cruelty:** If she willfully neglects the child or is cruel.
    

---

### 5. Father's Right of Access (Visitation)

Even when the mother has legal custody (_Hizanat_), the father is never totally cut off.

- **Maintenance:** The father must pay for the child's food, clothing, and shelter while they are with the mother.
    
- **Visitation:** The father has a legal **Right of Access**. He can visit the child at reasonable times. The mother cannot prevent him from seeing his child. If she denies access, the court can compel her.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "7 Year" Cliff:** In Hanafi law, once a boy turns 7, custody transfers **automatically** to the Father.
    
    - _Statutory Override:_ Remember the _Abu Bakar Siddique_ case (discussed in 10.2). In Bangladesh, the court can refuse to hand over the boy at 7 if it is not in his **welfare**.
        
- **Shia Difference:** Under Shia law, the mother loses custody of a **son** as soon as he is **weaned (2 years)**. For a daughter, she keeps her until **7**. After that, the Father takes over.
    
- **Scenario:** _"A divorced mother marries a stranger. The father claims the child. The mother argues she is a better parent."_
    
    - _Classical Law:_ Father wins. Remarriage to a stranger is a strict bar.
        
    - _Bangladesh Law:_ Courts _might_ still favor the mother if the father is proven unfit, citing the "Welfare Principle," but the general presumption is against the remarried mother.
