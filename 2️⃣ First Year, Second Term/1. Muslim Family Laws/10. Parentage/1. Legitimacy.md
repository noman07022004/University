## 10.1 Legitimacy (_Nasab_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the bedrock of the family structure in Islam. A child's right to inherit, their identity, and their maintenance all depend entirely on whether they are "Legitimate" (_Sahih_).
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A critical exam topic. You will often be asked to resolve conflicts between **Sharia Law** (2-year gestation) and **Statutory Law** (280 days under the Evidence Act), or to explain the doctrine of **Acknowledgment** (_Iqrar_).
    

---


### 1. The Concept of _Nasab_ 
In Muslim Law, maternity (_Umm_) is defined by the biological act of birth, but paternity (_Nasab_) is a legal concept defined by a **valid marriage**.

- **The Golden Rule:** Legitimacy depends entirely on the validity of the marriage between the parents.
    
- **No Legitimation by Subsequent Marriage:** Unlike English law, Muslim law does **NOT** recognize "legitimation." You cannot make an illegitimate child legitimate by marrying the mother _after_ the child is born. The child must be conceived or born _during_ the marriage.
    

### 2. Presumption of Legitimacy (Sharia vs. Statute) 
How do we prove a child is legitimate? This is where classical religious law and modern state law clash.

**A. The Classical Sharia Rules (Hanafi)**

- **Minimum Period:** A child born at least **6 months** after the date of marriage is legitimate. (Based on the Quranic calculation of gestation).
    
- **Maximum Period:** A child born within **2 years** after the dissolution of marriage (by death/divorce) is presumed legitimate.
    
    - _Note:_ Other schools go even further (Shafi/Maliki say 4 years), based on ancient medical understanding that a pregnancy could go dormant.
        

**B. The Statutory Rule (Section 112, Evidence Act 1872)** In Bangladesh, the courts follow the secular **Evidence Act, 1872** over the classical Hanafi rule regarding the maximum period.

- **The Rule:** A child is conclusively presumed legitimate if:
    
    1. Born during the continuance of a valid marriage, OR
        
    2. Born within **280 days** after its dissolution (provided the mother remains unmarried).
        
- **Conclusive Proof:** This presumption can _only_ be rebutted by proving **"Non-Access"** (that the parents had no physical access to each other at the time of conception).
    
- **Conflict Result:** If a child is born 1 year after divorce:
    
    - _Sharia:_ Legitimate (within 2 years).
        
    - _Evidence Act:_ Illegitimate (beyond 280 days).
        
    - _Bangladesh Law:_ The **Evidence Act applies**. The child is illegitimate.
        

---

### 3. Acknowledgment of Paternity (_Iqrar_)

What happens if there is no direct proof of marriage? Muslim Law provides a special mechanism called **Acknowledgment** (_Iqrar_) to establish legitimacy.

**Definition:** It is a declaration by the father that a child is his legitimate offspring. It is not a process of "adoption" (which is invalid), but a declaration of an existing fact.

**Essential Conditions for Validity:**

1. **Unknown Paternity:** The paternity of the child must be "unknown" or doubtful. If it is _known_ that the child is illegitimate (e.g., born of Zina/Adultery), acknowledgment **cannot** validate it. You cannot acknowledge a known bastard.
    
2. **Not Impossible:** The age gap between the acknowledger and the child must be reasonable (at least 12.5 years difference) so that they _could_ be father and son physically.
    
3. **Confirmation:** The child (if an adult) must confirm or at least not deny the relationship.
    
4. **Legal Possibility:** The mother must not be the wife of another man (at the time of conception) or within prohibited degrees to the father.
    

**Effect of Acknowledgment:** Once a valid acknowledgment is made, it is **irrevocable**. The child inherits as a legitimate heir, and the mother is implied to be the wife.

---

### 4. Status of the Illegitimate Child (_Walad-uz-Zina_)

If legitimacy is not established by marriage or acknowledgment:

- **Sunni Law:** The child is illegitimate.
    
    - **Inheritance:** Cannot inherit from the **Father**. Can _only_ inherit from the **Mother** and her relations.
        
    - **Maintenance:** Father has no strict duty to maintain (though Section 488 CrPC/Family Courts may intervene on humanitarian grounds).
        
- **Shia Law:** The child is _Filius Nullius_ ("Son of No One").
    
    - **Inheritance:** Inherits from **NEITHER** father nor mother. The child is totally excluded from family property.
        

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "280 Days" Trap:** If an exam problem says: _"A child was born 300 days after the divorce. The father claims it is not his. Sharia allows up to 2 years."_
    
    - **Answer:** In Bangladesh courts, the child is **Illegitimate**. Section 112 of the Evidence Act (280 days limit) supersedes the Hanafi 2-year rule.
        
- **Zina cannot be cured:** If a man admits, "I committed adultery with this woman and this is my son," the child remains **Illegitimate**. Acknowledgment only works when the nature of the relationship was _uncertain_ (e.g., a secret marriage), not when it was definitely sinful.
    
- **DNA Testing:** Modern courts in Bangladesh are increasingly relying on DNA tests to determine paternity, which can override the presumptions of Section 112 if the evidence is scientific and conclusive, though the "Conclusive Proof" text of Section 112 remains a high hurdle legally.
