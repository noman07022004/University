## 10.2 Guardianship (_Wilayat_) and Custody (_Hizanat_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** You must distinguish between "physical care" (Custody) and "legal control" (Guardianship). Confusing these two is the most common mistake students make.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A guaranteed exam topic. Problem questions often involve a mother claiming custody after remarriage, or a father selling a minor's land. The _Abu Bakar Siddique_ case is mandatory for any Bangladeshi exam answer.
    

---

### The Two Spheres of Control

In Muslim Law, the care of a minor is split into two distinct legal concepts. Unlike Western law where "custody" often implies full control, Sharia divides the responsibilities:

#### 1. Custody (_Hizanat_) – The Right to Rearing

- **Definition:** _Hizanat_ refers to the **physical custody** and care of the child (feeding, clothing, nurturing).
    
- **Primary Right:** This right belongs primarily to the **Mother** during the tender years of the child. It is considered a right of the child to be with the mother, not just a right of the mother.
    
- **Father's Role:** While the mother holds custody, the father remains the _legal guardian_. He must pay for the child's maintenance and has the right to visit and supervise the child's upbringing, but he cannot forcibly take the child away during this period.
    

#### 2. Guardianship (_Wilayat_) – The Right of Management

- **Definition:** _Wilayat_ refers to the **legal authority** to make decisions for the child regarding finance, marriage, and property management.
    
- **Primary Right:** This belongs strictly to the **Father** (and his male ancestors). A mother is **never** a natural guardian of property in Sunni law.
    

---

### Part A: Rules of Custody (_Hizanat_)

1. Duration of Mother's Custody

The duration depends on the school of law and the sex of the child.

|**School of Law**|**Son**|**Daughter**|
|---|---|---|
|**Sunni (Hanafi)**|Until age **7**|Until **Puberty** (approx. 15)|
|**Shia (Ithna-Ashari)**|Until age **2** (weaning)|Until age **7**|

2. Disqualification of Mother (When she loses custody)

The mother may lose her right to Hizanat if:

- **Remarriage:** She marries a "stranger" (a man not related to the child within prohibited degrees).
    
    - _Exception:_ If she marries a close relative of the child (e.g., the child's uncle), she **retains** custody because the uncle is expected to be kind to his nephew/niece.
        
- **Immorality:** She leads an immoral life (_Fasiqa_) or is a prostitute.
    
- **Neglect:** She neglects the child or removes the child so far that the father cannot exercise supervision.
    
- **Apostasy:** Traditionally, changing religion was a bar, but under the _Caste Disabilities Removal Act, 1850_, this is no longer an absolute disqualification in Bangladesh.
    

3. Order of Custodians (If Mother Fails)

If the mother dies or is disqualified, custody passes to female relatives first (Maternal Grandmother $\rightarrow$ Paternal Grandmother $\rightarrow$ Sisters). Only after the females are exhausted does custody pass to the Father.

---

### Part B: Guardianship of Property (_Wilayat_)

This is where the Mother is totally excluded in strict Sharia.

1. Natural Guardians (Order of Priority)

Only the following persons are legal guardians of a minor's property in Sunni Law:

1. **Father** (The sole natural guardian).
    
2. **Executor appointed by the Father's Will**.
    
3. **Paternal Grandfather**.
    
4. **Executor appointed by the Grandfather's Will**.
    

5. The Mother's Status (De Facto Guardian)

A mother is merely a De Facto Guardian (a guardian in fact, but not in law).

- **Legal Consequence:** She has **NO power** to sell, mortgage, or alienate the minor's immovable property.
    
- **The "Void" Rule:** If a widowed mother sells her minor son's land (even to buy him food), the sale is **Void** (or voidable depending on strictness) because she had no legal authority to transfer title. She should have applied to the Court to be appointed a "Certified Guardian" first.
    

---

### Part C: Statutory Modification (The "Welfare" Principle)

This is the most critical section for your exam in Bangladesh. The strict rules of Hanafi law (e.g., "Son goes to father at 7") are often overridden by the **Guardians and Wards Act, 1890**.

**1. Section 17 of G&W Act**

- The Court, when appointing a guardian, must be guided by what appears to be for the **"Welfare of the Minor"**.
    
- This "Welfare Principle" is the **paramount consideration**, superior to the specific rules of personal law.
    

**2. The Landmark Case: _Abu Bakar Siddique v. S.M.A. Bakar_**

- **Facts:** A mother sought custody of her son who was over 7 years old (Hanafi limit exceeded). The father claimed absolute right under Muslim Law.
    
- **Judgment:** The Appellate Division of the Supreme Court of Bangladesh ruled in favor of the **Mother**.
    
- **The Ruling:** The Court held that the rules of _Hizanat_ in Muslim law are "permissive, not mandatory." If the welfare of the child demands it (e.g., the child is sick and needs mother's care), the Court can extend the mother's custody beyond the age of 7 (for boys) or puberty (for girls).
    
- **Impact:** Today, "Welfare of the Child" is the ultimate test in Bangladeshi Family Courts.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Custody vs. Guardianship:**
    
    - **Custody:** Mother (usually). Deals with _rearing_.
        
    - **Guardianship:** Father (always). Deals with _money/marriage_.
        
- **The "Step-Father" Trap:** If a mother marries a stranger, she usually loses custody. But if the father is _also_ unfit (e.g., a drug addict), the Court will **not** take the child away from the mother. The "Welfare Principle" saves her.
    
- **Illegitimate Child:** The custody of an illegitimate child belongs exclusively to the **Mother** until the child is independent. The father has no rights.
