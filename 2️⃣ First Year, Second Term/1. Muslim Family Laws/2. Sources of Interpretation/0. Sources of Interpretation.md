## Sources of Interpretation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most critical conceptual topic in Muslim Law. It defines the "Roots of Law" (_Usul al-Fiqh_). Without understanding the hierarchy of sources, you cannot understand how a legal ruling (_Hukm_) is derived.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Highly frequent in exams. Questions often ask for a "Detailed discussion on the Primary Sources" or "The validity of Qiyas as a source of law."

---
The "Sources of Interpretation" in Muslim Law are technically known as **Usul al-Fiqh** (The Roots of Jurisprudence). These sources provide the methodology for discovering the law. In Islamic legal theory, the law is not "made" by humans but "discovered" from divine texts. There is a strict hierarchy: a jurist must first look to the **Primary Sources** (Textual/Divine). If the answer is found there, the inquiry ends. If not, they move to the **Secondary Sources** (Rational/Derivative) to deduce a rule.

---

### 1. Primary Sources (_Al-Masadir Al-Asliyya_)

These are the textual sources derived directly from divine revelation. They are the absolute authority.

#### A. The Quran

The Quran is the fundamental source (_Grundnorm_) of Islamic Law. It is the word of God revealed to the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH).

- **Nature:** It is _Wahi Matlu_ (recited revelation). Its text is immutable.
    
- **Legal Content:** Out of over 6,000 verses, only a small portion (approx. 80-500 verses, known as _Ayat al-Ahkam_) deals with legal mandates. These primarily cover:
    
    - **Family Law:** Marriage, divorce, inheritance (very detailed).
        
    - **Civil Law:** Contracts, debts (general principles).
        
    - **Penal Law:** Specific punishments (_Hudud_) for crimes like theft and adultery.
        
- **Authority:** It is the first point of reference. If a text in the Quran is explicit (_Qat'i_), no other source can override it.
    

#### B. The Sunnah (Hadith)

The Sunnah refers to the practices, sayings, and approvals of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH). While the Quran provides the "constitution," the Sunnah provides the "statutes" and detailed application.

- **Nature:** It is _Wahi Ghair Matlu_ (unrecited revelation). The meaning is inspired by God, but the words are the Prophet's.
    
- **Forms of Sunnah:**
    
    1. **Sunnah Qawliya (Sayings):** Verbal instructions (e.g., "No bequest to an heir").
        
    2. **Sunnah Fi'liya (Actions):** Deeds performed by the Prophet (e.g., the method of prayer).
        
    3. **Sunnah Taqririyah (Silent Approval):** Acts done in his presence which he did not object to.
        
- **Function:** The Sunnah explains, details, and qualifies the Quran. For example, the Quran commands "Establish Prayer," but the Sunnah explains _how_ to pray.
    

---

### 2. Secondary Sources (_Al-Masadir Al-Thana'iyya_)

When a solution is not explicitly found in the Quran or Sunnah, jurists use these sources. They are based on human reasoning guided by divine principles.

#### A. Ijma (Consensus of Opinion)

_Ijma_ refers to the consensus of the Muslim jurists (_Mujtahids_) of a particular age on a specific legal issue.

- **Authority:** Based on the Hadith: _"My community will never agree upon an error."_
    
- **Types:**
    
    - **Ijma al-Ummah:** Consensus of the whole community (rare in law, mostly for beliefs).
        
    - **Ijma al-Ulama:** Consensus of the learned jurists (the standard legal source).
        
- **Relevance:** Once Ijma is established on a point, it becomes binding law. For example, the compilation of the Quran into a book form was validated by the Ijma of the Prophet's companions (_Sahaba_).
    

#### B. Qiyas (Analogical Deduction)

_Qiyas_ is the process of applying a rule from a known case (in the Quran/Sunnah) to a new case based on a shared effective cause (_Illah_). It is the primary tool of legal logic.

- **The Four Pillars of Qiyas:**
    
    1. **Asl (Original Case):** The case mentioned in the text (e.g., Wine drinking is forbidden in the Quran).
        
    2. **Far (New Case):** The new problem not mentioned in the text (e.g., Taking narcotics/modern drugs).
        
    3. **Illah (Effective Cause):** The underlying reason for the rule (e.g., Intoxication).
        
    4. **Hukm (Ruling):** Extending the ruling of the _Asl_ to the _Far_ (e.g., Narcotics are forbidden because they intoxicate like wine).
        

#### C. Other Subsidiary Sources (Brief Overview)

While not universally accepted by all schools, these are used for flexibility:

- **Istihsan (Juristic Preference):** Setting aside a strict analogy for a fairer outcome (Hanafi school).
    
- **Istislah (Public Interest):** Creating laws for the public good where texts are silent (Maliki school).
    
- **Urf (Custom):** Local customs are valid if they don't contradict the primary sources.
    

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Hierarchy is Key:** In an exam answer, you **must** present these in order. You cannot start with Qiyas before checking the Quran.
    
- **Hanafi vs. Others:** Note that the **Hanafi school** (dominant in Bangladesh) relies heavily on _Qiyas_ and _Istihsan_.
    
- **Definition of Hadith vs. Sunnah:** Technically, _Sunnah_ is the "practice/law," while _Hadith_ is the "report/story" conveying it. In general essays, they are often used interchangeably, but knowing the distinction earns extra marks.
    
- **Exam Terminology:** If a question asks about "Extra-textual sources," it refers to the Secondary sources (Ijma, Qiyas). If it asks for "Textual sources," it refers to Primary (Quran, Sunnah).
    
- **Legislative Value:** The Quran provides the _principles_, while the Sunnah provides the _details_. Ijma ensures _stability_, and Qiyas ensures _adaptability_ to new times.
