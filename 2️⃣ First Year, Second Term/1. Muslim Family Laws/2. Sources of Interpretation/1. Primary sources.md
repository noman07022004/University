
## 2.1 Primary Sources (The Revealed Law) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** These are the absolute axioms of the legal system. Just as you cannot do math without knowing numbers, you cannot do Fiqh without the Quran and Sunnah. They are the only sources considered "Infallible."
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Extremely frequent. Questions often ask to "Discuss the legislative functions of the Sunnah in relation to the Quran" or "Analyze the legal capacity of Quranic verses."

---

In the hierarchy of Muslim Law, the **Primary Sources** are those that are of **Divine Origin**. They are technically known as _Al-Adillah Al-Qat'iyyah_ (The Absolute Proofs). Unlike Secondary sources (which are products of human reason), Primary sources are believed to be the direct will of the Lawgiver (_Allah_).

---

The consensus of Sunni jurists classifies these into two specific bodies: **The Quran** (The Word of God) and **The Sunnah** (The Practice of the Prophet).

### I. The Holy Quran (The Supreme Source)

The Quran is the primary, original, and supreme authority of Islamic Law. It is defined as the speech of God (_Kalamullah_), sent down to the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) through the Angel Gabriel, transmitted to us by continuous testimony (_Tawatur_), and recited as an act of worship.

#### 1. Nature of Quranic Legislation

Legally, the Quran is the **Ground Norm** (Fundamental Law) of the Islamic system. However, it is not a "Civil Code" in the modern sense. It is primarily a book of guidance.

- **Total Verses:** Approx. 6,236 verses (Ayats).
    
- **Legal Verses (_Ayat al-Ahkam_):** Only about 200 to 500 verses deal with strict legal rules.
    
- **Legislative Style:** The Quran gives the **General Principles** rather than minute details.
    
    - _Example:_ It commands "Pay the Zakat" (Tax), but does not specify the percentage (2.5%) or the threshold (_Nisab_). That detail is left to the Sunnah.
        
    - _Exception:_ In matters of **Inheritance (_Faraid_)**, the Quran is extremely detailed, specifying exact fractions (1/2, 1/4, 1/8), leaving almost no room for human interpretation.
        

#### 2. Classification of Verses (Crucial for Interpretation)

To apply the Quran legally, jurists must distinguish between the context of the verses:

- **Meccan Verses (609–622 AD):** Revealed while the Prophet was in Mecca. These deal with theology, ethics, and the afterlife. They generally do not contain legal rules (except for moral duties).
    
- **Medinan Verses (622–632 AD):** Revealed after the migration to Medina. These are the **Legislative Verses**. Since the Prophet was now the Head of State, these verses lay down criminal laws (_Hudud_), family laws, and rules of war.
    

#### 3. Rules of Interpretation (_Tafsir_)

Since the Quran is the supreme law, how we read it matters. Jurists apply specific rules:

- **The General (_Aam_) and The Specific (_Khass_):** If a verse is general (e.g., "Mothers shall suckle their children"), it applies to all mothers. If a later specific verse restricts it, the specific prevails.
    
- **Abrogation (_Naskh_):** A later verse can repeal an earlier verse.
    
    - _Classic Example:_ The verses regarding wine. The final prohibition (Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:90) abrogated the earlier verses that merely discouraged it.
        

---

### II. The Sunnah (The Prophetic Model)

The **Sunnah** is the second primary source. It signifies the custom, practice, or usage of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH).

While the Quran is **Recited Revelation** (_Wahuy Matlu_), the Sunnah is **Non-Recited Revelation** (_Wahuy Ghair Matlu_). This means the _meaning_ was inspired by God, but the _words_ were the Prophet's own.

#### 1. The Authority of Sunnah

The legal binding force of the Sunnah is derived from the Quran itself. The Quran commands: _"What the Messenger gives you, take it; and what he forbids you, abstain from it"_ (59:7). Therefore, rejecting the Sunnah is legally equivalent to rejecting the Quran.

#### 2. The Three Modes of Sunnah

The Sunnah is not just what the Prophet "said." It comprises three distinct types of precedents:

1. **Sunnat-ul-Qawl (Verbal Sunnah/Hadith):** The sayings of the Prophet. (e.g., _"No bequest to an heir"_).
    
2. **Sunnat-ul-Fi'l (Actual Sunnah/Deeds):** The actions of the Prophet. (e.g., The way he performed prayers or Hajj).
    
3. **Sunnat-ul-Taqrir (Tacit Sunnah/Approvals):** Acts done by Companions in his presence which he did not forbid. His silence implies legality.
    
    - _Example:_ When Khalid ibn Walid ate lizard meat (_Dabb_) at the Prophet's table, the Prophet did not eat it but did not forbid it. This made it permissible (_Halal_).
        

#### 3. Legislative Functions of the Sunnah

The Sunnah serves three specific legal roles in relation to the Quran:

1. **Reiteration (_Mu'akkid_):** It merely repeats a Quranic command to emphasize importance (e.g., prohibition of false testimony).
    
2. **Explanation (_Mubayin_):** It clarifies vague Quranic concepts.
    
    - _Example:_ The Quran says "Establish Prayer." The Sunnah explains the times, units (_Raka'ts_), and movements.
        
3. **Legislation (_Mushtaqil_):** It creates new laws on matters where the Quran is completely silent.
    
    - _Example:_ The Quran forbids marriage to mothers and sisters. The **Sunnah** added the prohibition of marrying a woman and her aunt (paternal or maternal) at the same time. This rule exists _only_ in the Sunnah but is equally binding.

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Term Clarification (Hadith vs. Sunnah):**
    
    - _Sunnah_ is the **Law** (the abstract practice).
        
    - _Hadith_ is the **Vehicle** (the report/story that carries the Sunnah).
        
    - _Exam Tip:_ You can say, "The law is derived from the Sunnah, which is found in the Hadith collections."
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:**
    
    - In Bangladeshi courts, judges generally do not verify Hadith themselves. They rely on the "Six Authentic Books" (_Sihah Sitta_)—primarily **Sahih Bukhari** and **Sahih Muslim**—as authoritative records of the Primary Sources.
        
    - **The 1961 Ordinance vs. Primary Sources:** Always remember that in Bangladesh, if a Primary Source (Sunnah) allows something (like unilateral divorce) but the Statute (1961 Ordinance) restricts it, the **Statute prevails** in court. This is a critical distinction between "Pure Sharia" and "Bangladeshi Muslim Law."
        
- **Key Distinction:** The Quran is **Wording and Meaning** from God. The Sunnah is **Meaning** from God, **Wording** from the Prophet.
