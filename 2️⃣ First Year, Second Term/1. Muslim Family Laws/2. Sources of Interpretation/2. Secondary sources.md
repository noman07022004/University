
## 2.2 Secondary Sources (The Rational Law) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is where the "science" of law begins. While Primary sources provide the text, Secondary sources provide the _method_ to apply that text to the infinite number of new situations (like cryptocurrency or DNA testing) that didn't exist in the 7th century.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** High frequency. You will almost certainly be asked to "Define Ijma and its types" or "Explain the elements of Qiyas with an example."

---

According to the standard classification used in your course, **Secondary Sources** are those derived through human reasoning (_Ijtihad_) rather than direct divine revelation. They are technically known as **Rational Sources**.

---
These sources are **Dependent**; they cannot function on their own. They rely on the Primary Sources (Quran/Sunnah) for their validity. Their purpose is to discover the law for cases where the Quran and Sunnah are silent.

### I. Ijma (Consensus of Opinion)

**Ijma** is the third source of Muslim Law. It represents the collective wisdom of the legal community.

#### 1. Definition and Authority

- **Definition:** technically, it is "The consensus of the Muslim jurists (_Mujtahids_) of a particular age on a specific question of law."
    
- **Authority:** The validity of Ijma is based on the Sunnah. The Prophet (PBUH) said: _"My community will never agree upon an error."_
    
- **Binding Force:** Once Ijma is established on a point, it becomes a **Primary Proof**. It elevates a rule from being "Probable" (open to debate) to "Certain" (binding law). No later scholar can reopen the issue.
    

#### 2. Types of Ijma (Crucial for Exams)

Scholars classify Ijma into two grades based on strength:

- **Ijma of the Companions (_Ijma-as-Sahaba_):**
    
    - The agreement of the direct disciples of the Prophet.
        
    - **Status:** Absolute and infallible. It is as binding as a verse of the Quran.
        
    - _Example:_ The collection of the Quran into a single book (Mushaf) was done by the Ijma of the Sahaba.
        
- **Ijma of the Jurists (_Ijma-ul-Fuqaha_):**
    
    - The agreement of the learned scholars of a later age.
        
    - **Status:** Binding, but some schools argue it can be reopened if new evidence emerges.
        

#### 3. Modes of Constitution

- **Explicit Ijma (_Ijma Azima_):** Every jurist physically gathers and verbally agrees (very rare).
    
- **Tacit Ijma (_Ijma Rukhsat_):** One prominent jurist gives a ruling, and the others, knowing of it, remain silent and do not oppose it.
    

---

### II. Qiyas (Analogical Deduction)

**Qiyas** is the fourth source of Muslim Law. It is the primary tool of the **Hanafi School** (and thus vital for Bangladesh).

#### 1. Definition

It is the process of applying a rule from a known case (Origin) to a new case (Branch) because they share the same effective cause (_Illah_). Unlike the first three sources, Qiyas does not _create_ law; it merely _discovers_ it.

#### 2. The Four Pillars of Qiyas (_Arkan-al-Qiyas_)

For an analogy to be valid, it must have four components:

1. **Asl (The Root/Original Case):** The case mentioned in the text (Quran/Sunnah).
    
    - _Example:_ **Wine** (_Khamr_).
        
2. **Far (The Branch/New Case):** The new case not mentioned in the text.
    
    - _Example:_ **Heroin/Cocaine**.
        
3. **Illah (The Effective Cause):** The underlying reason for the original rule.
    
    - _Example:_ **Intoxication** (_Iskar_).
        
4. **Hukm (The Ruling):** The judgment transferred from the Root to the Branch.
    
    - _Example:_ **Prohibition** (_Haram_).
        

- **Logic:** Since Wine is Haram because it intoxicates, and Heroin also intoxicates, therefore Heroin is also Haram.

### III. Additional / Subsidiary Sources

_Note: While your syllabus focuses on Ijma/Qiyas as Secondary, these "Additional Sources" are essential components of the Rational category, often used when strict Qiyas fails._

#### 1. Istihsan (Juristic Preference / Equity)

- **Concept:** This is the specific doctrine of the **Hanafi School**. It means "to consider something good."
    
- **Function:** If a strict application of Qiyas leads to a harsh, unjust, or impractical result, the jurist uses _Istihsan_ to override it for a "better" solution.
    
- **Example (Guardianship):** By strict Qiyas, a mother should not be the guardian of a child's property because she might be inexperienced. However, via _Istihsan_, the law grants her custody (_Hizanat_) for the child's physical welfare.
    

#### 2. Istislah / Maslahah Mursalah (Public Interest)

- **Concept:** Developed by the **Maliki School**. It allows legislation purely for the "Public Good" (_Maslahah_) even if there is no specific text.
    
- **Example:** Traffic laws, collecting taxes for building roads, or printing currency. The Quran doesn't mention them, but they are valid because they serve the public interest.
    

#### 3. Urf (Custom)

- **Concept:** The local tradition or usage of a community.
    
- **Rule:** "Usage is authoritative." Custom is valid as long as it does not expressly contradict the Quran or Sunnah.
    
- **Bangladeshi Application:** Many marriage rituals (like gifts given during weddings) are governed by _Urf_.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Difference between Ra'y and Qiyas:**
    
    - _Ra'y_ is loose, subjective opinion ("I think this is fair").
        
    - _Qiyas_ is strict, systematic reasoning ("This is equal to that because of X cause").
        
    - The Traditionists rejected _Ra'y_ but eventually accepted _Qiyas_.
        
- **Exam Trap:** Never say Qiyas is a source of _new_ law. It is a source of _discovering_ law. The law already existed in the _Illah_ (Cause); the jurist just found it.
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:** The **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961** is often justified using these rational sources. For example, restricting polygamy (which the Quran permits) is justified by **Maslahah** (Public Interest) and the prevention of social harm, showing how modern states use secondary sources to "update" the application of primary texts.
