## Family Court Litigation ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** While the previous topics covered substantive law (what the rights are), this topic covers procedural law (how to enforce them). Understanding the _Family Courts Ordinance, 1985_ is essential for any aspiring lawyer in Bangladesh, as this is the forum where 90% of family disputes are settled.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Extremely high frequency. Exam questions often ask for a "Detailed discussion on the Jurisdiction and Procedure of Family Courts," specifically focusing on the unique "Conciliation" provisions.
    

### Introduction

Before 1985, Muslim family disputes in Bangladesh were tried in ordinary civil courts under the Code of Civil Procedure (CPC), 1908. This was a lengthy, expensive, and adversarial process often taking years. To provide a speedy, inexpensive, and specialized forum, the Government enacted the **Family Courts Ordinance (FCO), 1985**. This Ordinance overrides the general civil procedure in specific matters and introduces a unique mandatory Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mechanism embedded within the trial process.

### 1. Establishment and Jurisdiction

**Subtopics Covered:** _Establishment, Jurisdiction._

The Family Court is not a separate building but a separate "jurisdiction" exercised by existing judges.

- **Establishment (Section 4):**
    
    - Every **Assistant Judge** shall be the Judge of the Family Court for their local jurisdiction.
        
    - This means the same person sits as an "Assistant Judge" for land disputes in the morning and as a "Family Court Judge" for family disputes in the afternoon.
        
- **Jurisdiction (Section 5):**
    
    - The Family Court has **exclusive jurisdiction** to try and dispose of suits relating to **five specific subjects**. No other civil court can try these matters.
        
    - **The 5 Subjects:**
        
        1. **Dissolution of Marriage** (Divorce/Talaq/Khula).
            
        2. **Restitution of Conjugal Rights** (forcing a spouse to return).
            
        3. **Dower** (_Mahr_).
            
        4. **Maintenance** (_Nafaqah_).
            
        5. **Guardianship and Custody of Children**.
            

### 2. The Conciliation Procedure (ADR)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Conciliation procedure._

The most distinctive feature of the FCO 1985 is the mandatory attempt at compromise. The Judge acts not just as an adjudicator but as a mediator. There are **two** mandatory stages of conciliation:

#### A. Pre-Trial Conciliation (Section 10)

- **Timing:** After the written statement is filed by the defendant, but **before** the framing of issues (before the trial officially starts).
    
- **Procedure:** The Court fixes a date for a "Pre-Trial Hearing." The parties (Husband and Wife) must appear personally. The Judge meets them (often in camera/private) to try and effect a compromise.
    
- **Outcome:**
    
    - If successful: A **Compromise Decree** is drawn up immediately.
        
    - If failed: The Court proceeds to frame issues and start the trial (witness examination).
        

#### B. Post-Trial Conciliation (Section 13)

- **Timing:** After the evidence of both sides has been closed, but **before** the judgment is pronounced.
    
- **Procedure:** The Court makes one final attempt to reconcile the parties.
    
- **Outcome:**
    
    - If successful: Compromise Decree.
        
    - If failed: The Court pronounces the final Judgment.
        

### 3. Appeal

**Subtopics Covered:** _Appeal._

Since the Family Court is equivalent to an Assistant Judge's court, the hierarchy for appeals is streamlined.

- **Forum (Section 17):** An appeal against the judgment, decree, or order of a Family Court lies to the **District Judge**.
    
- **Limitation:** The appeal must be filed within **30 days** of the decree.
    
- **Bar on Appeal:** There is **no appeal** allowed against:
    
    - A decree for the dissolution of marriage (except where the ground was cruelty).
        
    - A decree for Dower not exceeding **5,000 Taka** (now practically obsolete due to inflation, but the statute remains).

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Court Fees:** A major pro-poor feature is the court fee. Unlike regular civil suits (where fees can be high ad valorem), the court fee for any Family Court suit is fixed at only **25 Taka** (Section 22). This makes justice accessible to destitute women.
    
- **The "Dual Identity" of the Judge:** In a viva or conceptual question, remember that the "Assistant Judge" applies the CPC 1908 strictly, but when sitting as a "Family Court Judge," they apply the FCO 1985 (which is faster and more flexible).
    
- **Exclusivity:** If a wife wants to sue for Dower, she _cannot_ go to the Joint District Judge even if the claim is 1 Crore Taka. She _must_ go to the Family Court (Assistant Judge) because Section 5 grants **exclusive jurisdiction** regardless of pecuniary (monetary) limits.
    
- **ADR is Mandatory:** The judge _must_ attempt conciliation. Failure to record the steps taken for Section 10 or Section 13 can sometimes be grounds for setting aside the judgment in higher courts.
