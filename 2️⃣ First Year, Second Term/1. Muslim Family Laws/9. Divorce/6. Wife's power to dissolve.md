
## 9.6 Wife's Power to Dissolve (_Talaq-e-Tafwid_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the single most powerful tool for women's rights in modern Muslim law. It equalizes the power dynamic without requiring court intervention.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A "Super Core" topic. You will almost certainly face a problem question where a wife wants to divorce her abusive husband without going to court. The answer lies in **Clause 18 of the Kabinnama**.
    

---

###  The Delegated Divorce

**1. Concept and Juridical Nature**

- **Definition:** _Talaq-e-Tafwid_ literally means "Delegated Divorce." It is a form of divorce where the husband delegates his power of _Talaq_ to the wife (or a third party), allowing her to pronounce divorce upon herself.
    
- **Agency Theory:** Legally, the wife acts as the **Agent** (_Wakil_) of the husband. When she pronounces divorce, she is not doing it under her own inherent power (which she doesn't have in Sharia), but she is exercising the **husband's power** on his behalf.
    
- **Irrevocability of Delegation:** Once the power is delegated (usually in the marriage contract), the husband **cannot revoke it**. It becomes a binding right of the wife.
    

2. The Source: Clause 18 of the Kabinnama

In Bangladesh, this right is practically secured through the standard Marriage Registration Form (Kabinnama).

- **Clause 18:** This specific column asks: _"Has the husband delegated the power of Talaq to the wife? If so, under what conditions?"_
    
- **Crucial Practice:** If the word "Yes" is written here, the wife has the power. If it is left blank or written "No," she cannot use this method and must go to court for _Faskh_ (which takes years).
    

3. Types of Delegation

The delegation can be of two types:

- **Unconditional (Absolute):** The husband says, "I authorize you to divorce yourself whenever you wish." The wife can exercise this at any time, for any reason.
    
- **Conditional:** The power is triggered only if a specific event happens.
    
    - _Common Conditions:_ "If I beat you," "If I fail to pay maintenance," "If I take a second wife without permission".
        
    - _Effect:_ If the condition is breached, the power "activates," and she can pronounce the divorce.
        

4. The Procedure (How she does it)

The procedure for Talaq-e-Tafwid is governed by Section 8 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961.

1. **Pronouncement:** The wife declares: _"I exercise the power delegated to me and divorce myself from my husband."_ (Note: She divorces _herself_; she doesn't technically divorce _him_).
    
2. **Notice:** She **MUST** send a written notice to the **Chairman** (Arbitration Council) and a copy to the **Husband**, just like a husband would do under Section 7.
    
3. **Effectiveness:** The divorce becomes effective after **90 days**, subject to reconciliation attempts.
    

---

### Comparison: _Talaq-e-Tafwid_ vs. Other Forms

This distinction is vital for exams to show you understand _why_ a wife would choose one method over another.

|**Feature**|**Talaq-e-Tafwid (Delegated)**|**Khula (Mutual/By Wife)**|**Faskh (Judicial)**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Consent Required**|**None** (Husband already consented in Kabinnama).|**Husband's Consent** is mandatory.|**Court Decree** (Judge forces it).|
|**Dower (_Mahr_)**|Wife **KEEPS** her Dower.|Wife usually **GIVES UP** Dower.|Wife **KEEPS** Dower (mostly).|
|**Speed**|Fast (90 Days).|Fast (if husband agrees).|Slow (Civil Suit takes years).|
|**Role of Court**|No Court needed.|No Court needed.|**Court is Essential**.|

### Important Exam Tips

- **The "Dower" Advantage:** In an exam, if a wife is abused and wants to divorce _and_ keep her money, recommend **Talaq-e-Tafwid**. If she uses _Khula_, she loses the money.
    
- **Husband's Right Remains:** Delegating the power to the wife does **NOT** strip the husband of his own right. He can still divorce her anytime he wants. Both hold the gun, so to speak.
    
- **Minors:** If the husband is a minor, he cannot delegate this power. The delegation is void.
