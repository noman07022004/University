## 9.3 Time Factor (_Iddat_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the "biological clock" of Muslim Law. You cannot understand the legitimacy of children, the right to maintenance, or the legality of a new marriage without understanding this waiting period.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Essential. Problem questions involving inheritance or remarriage almost always hinge on whether the _Iddat_ period had expired or not.
    

---

### The Mandatory Waiting Period

1. Concept and Definition

The "Time Factor" in divorce is legally known as Iddat (literally "counting").

- **Definition:** It is the period during which a divorced woman (or widow) is legally prohibited from marrying another man. She must wait to ensure the womb is free from pregnancy.
    
- **Object:**
    
    1. **Ascertainment of Parentage (_Nasab_):** To avoid confusion about who the father is if she remarries immediately.
        
    2. **Opportunity for Reconciliation:** In revocable divorces, it gives the husband a chance to take her back before the bond is fully severed.
        
    3. **Mourning:** In case of death, it is a period of showing respect to the deceased husband.
        

**2. When does it Start?**

- **Sharia Rule:** It starts immediately from the moment the divorce is pronounced or the death occurs.
    
- **Ignorance:** Even if the wife does not _know_ about the divorce/death, the _Iddat_ starts running from the actual date of the event. (e.g., if she hears about it 4 months later, her Iddat might already be over).
    

---

### The Duration of Time (Calculations)

The length of the time factor depends on the physical state of the woman:

|**Condition of Wife**|**Duration of Iddat**|
|---|---|
|**Menstruating Woman**|**3 Menstrual Courses** (_Tuhrs_).|
|**Non-Menstruating** (Old/Young)|**3 Lunar Months**.|
|**Pregnant**|**Until Delivery** of the child (or abortion/miscarriage).|
|**Widow** (Death of Husband)|**4 Months and 10 Days** (unless pregnant).|
|**Unconsummated Marriage**|**No Iddat**. (If divorced before sexual intercourse, she can remarry immediately).|

---

### Statutory Modification: The "90 Days" Rule (MFLO 1961)

In Bangladesh, the "Time Factor" is complicated by **Section 7 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961**.

- **The Rule:** A divorce shall not be effective until the expiration of **90 days** from the day on which notice is delivered to the Chairman.
    
- **The Conflict:** What if the Sharia Iddat (3 menstrual cycles) finishes in 80 days, but the Statute requires 90?
    
- **The Solution:**
    
    - **General Rule:** The period is **90 Days** (or until the end of pregnancy, whichever is later).
        
    - **Effect:** Even if she is not pregnant and her periods finish quickly, she is NOT divorced until the 90th day. The statute extends the time factor to a minimum of 90 days to allow for arbitration.
        

---

### Rights and Restrictions During this Time

1. **Prohibition on Remarriage:** A marriage contracted during _Iddat_ is **Void** (if purely void) or **Irregular** (_Fasid_), depending on the sect/interpretation.
    
2. **Maintenance:** The husband is bound to maintain the wife during the _Iddat_ of divorce. (But _not_ during the _Iddat_ of death).
    
3. **Inheritance:** If either spouse dies _during_ the Iddat of a revocable divorce, the other inherits. (The marriage is considered legally subsisting).
    
4. **Residence:** Traditionally, she should observe _Iddat_ in her husband's home (_Hizanat_ issue notwithstanding), but in modern practice, this varies.
    

### Important Exam Tip

**"The Pregnant Widow"**

- _Question:_ A husband dies while his wife is pregnant. Does she wait 4 months 10 days OR until delivery?
    
- _Answer:_ **Until Delivery.** Even if she gives birth 1 hour after his death, her _Iddat_ is over immediately. The "Longer period" rule applies only in some views, but generally, delivery ends the Iddat for pregnancy.
