

## 9.12 Periods of Iddat ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the biological and legal "clock" of Muslim Law. You cannot determine the legality of a new marriage, the legitimacy of a child, or the right to maintenance without mastering these calculations.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A 100% guaranteed topic. You will face calculation problems like: _"A husband dies. The wife is 2 months pregnant. How long is her Iddat?"_ or _"Discuss the conflict between Sharia Iddat and the 90-day period under MFLO."_
    

---

### The Mandatory Waiting Period

**1. Concept and Object**

- **Definition:** _Iddat_ (literally "counting") is the period during which a divorced woman or widow is legally prohibited from marrying another man after the dissolution of her marriage.
    
- **Juridical Basis:** It is considered a period of **continuing marriage** for certain purposes (like maintenance and prohibition of sister-in-law).
    
- **Objects:**
    
    1. **Certainty of Paternity (_Nasab_):** To ensure the womb is free from pregnancy so there is no confusion about who the father of a future child might be.
        
    2. **Reconciliation:** In revocable divorces, it gives the husband a "cooling-off" period to take her back.
        
    3. **Mourning:** In case of death, it is a mandatory period of mourning (_Hidad_) for the deceased husband.
        

2. Classification of Periods (The Calculation)

The duration depends on how the marriage ended (Death vs. Divorce) and the physical state of the woman.

|**Condition of Woman**|**Dissolution by Divorce**|**Dissolution by Death**|
|---|---|---|
|**Menstruating Woman**|**3 Menstrual Courses** (_Tuhrs_).|**4 Months and 10 Days**.|
|**Non-Menstruating** (Old/Young)|**3 Lunar Months**.|**4 Months and 10 Days**.|
|**Pregnant Woman**|Until **Delivery** (or abortion).|**Until Delivery** (or abortion).|
|**Marriage NOT Consummated**|**No Iddat** (Free immediately).|**4 Months and 10 Days** (Death Iddat is mandatory even if not consummated).|

---

### 3. The Statutory Conflict: MFLO 1961 (Bangladesh)

This is the most critical exam point for Bangladeshi students. Section 7 of the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961** creates a conflict with the traditional Sharia periods for **Divorce** (Talaq).

- **Sharia Rule:** Iddat ends when 3 menstrual cycles are over (could be 60 days).
    
- **Statutory Rule:** Section 7(3) says a divorce is **not effective until 90 Days** have passed from the Notice.
    
- **The "Later" Rule:**
    
    - **If Not Pregnant:** She must wait **90 days**, even if her 3 cycles finished earlier.
        
    - **If Pregnant:** She must wait for **90 days OR the end of pregnancy**, whichever is **LATER**.
        
    - _Exam Example:_ Wife is 8 months pregnant. Notice given today.
        
        - 90 days later = Baby is 1 month old.
            
        - Pregnancy ends = 1 month later.
            
        - **Result:** She waits until the 90 days are up (whichever is later). _Wait, actually Section 7(5) says "period mentioned in sub-section (3) [90 days] OR the pregnancy, whichever be later, ends."_
            
        - _Correction:_ If she delivers in 1 month, she still has to wait for the remaining 60 days of the statutory 90-day period? **YES**, because the divorce isn't effective yet. But if she is only 1 month pregnant, she waits 8 months (until delivery), which is longer than 90 days.
            

**Crucial Distinction:** The MFLO applies **ONLY to Divorce (Talaq)**. It does **NOT** apply to the _Iddat_ of Death.

- _Iddat of Death_ remains strictly **4 months 10 days** (or delivery) as per Sharia. No "Notice to Chairman" is needed for death.
    

---

### 4. Commencement of Iddat

- **General Rule:** Iddat starts from the **date of divorce or death**, not from the date the wife _receives the news_.
    
- **Scenario:** A husband dies on Jan 1st. Wife hears the news on May 1st (4 months later).
    
- **Result:** Her Iddat is almost over. She does not start a fresh count.
    

### 5. Valid Retirement (_Khilwat-us-Sahih_)

- **Concept:** If the couple retired into a private room where there was no legal or physical bar to intercourse, but they claim they didn't touch each other.
    
- **The Rule:** In Hanafi Law, Valid Retirement is equivalent to **Consummation** for the purpose of Iddat.
    
- **Effect:** If divorced after Valid Retirement (even without sex), she **MUST** observe Iddat. (This differs from the "Unconsummated" rule where no Iddat is required).

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Death during Iddat" Trick:**
    
    - _Scenario:_ Husband divorces wife (revocable). He dies _during_ her Iddat.
        
    - _Result:_ She inherits from him. Now, her Iddat changes. She must start a **fresh Iddat of Death** (4 months 10 days) from the date of his death.
        
- **The "Zina" Pregnancy:** If a woman is pregnant by illicit intercourse (_Zina_) and marries a man, does she observe Iddat?
    
    - _Strict View:_ Yes, Iddat is required to clear the womb.
        
    - _Statutory View:_ Iddat applies to the dissolution of _Marriage_. Pregnancy out of wedlock doesn't technically trigger "Iddat of divorce," but a marriage during pregnancy might be invalid/irregular.
        
- **Observation:** During Iddat, a woman is expected to observe _Hidad_ (mourning) only for **Death** (no perfume, makeup, bright clothes). For **Divorce**, strictly speaking, she is encouraged to look attractive (in revocable divorce) to encourage the husband to take her back, or just stay home (in irrevocable).
