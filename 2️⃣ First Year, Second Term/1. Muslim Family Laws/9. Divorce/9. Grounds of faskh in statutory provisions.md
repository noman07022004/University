
## 9.9 Grounds of Faskh (Statutory Provisions) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the checklist for a woman seeking judicial divorce. You must know exactly _which_ facts she needs to prove in court to win her freedom.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A guaranteed exam question. You will often be asked to "List the grounds for dissolution under the Act of 1939" or solve a problem like: _"Husband has been in jail for 5 years. Can the wife divorce him?"_
    

---

### The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939

In Bangladesh, the specific grounds on which a Muslim wife can sue for _Faskh_ are codified in **Section 2** of the **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939**.

Prior to this Act, a Hanafi woman had almost no way to divorce a negligent husband. This Act adopted the liberal Maliki view and made it the law for all Muslims.

#### The 9 Statutory Grounds (Section 2)

A woman married under Muslim law shall be entitled to obtain a decree for the dissolution of her marriage on any one or more of the following grounds:

**1. Missing Husband (Whereabouts Unknown)**

- **Ground:** That the whereabouts of the husband have not been known for a period of **4 years**.
    
- **Condition:** The decree will not take effect for another 6 months. If he returns within that time, the decree is set aside.
    

**2. Failure to Maintain (Non-Maintenance)**

- **Ground:** That the husband has neglected or failed to provide for her maintenance for a period of **2 years**.
    
- **Exam Note:** This applies even if the wife is rich. The duty to maintain is absolute. However, if the wife was _Nashizah_ (disobedient) and _refused_ to live with him without valid cause, she generally cannot use this ground (though judicial views vary).
    

**3. Imprisonment of Husband**

- **Ground:** That the husband has been sentenced to imprisonment for a period of **7 years** or upwards.
    
- **Condition:** No decree can be passed until the sentence has become final (appeals exhausted).
    

**4. Failure to Perform Marital Obligations**

- **Ground:** That the husband has failed to perform, without reasonable cause, his marital obligations for a period of **3 years**.
    
- **Scope:** This covers emotional support, sexual relations, and equal treatment (if polygamous).
    

**5. Impotence**

- **Ground:** That the husband was impotent at the time of the marriage and **continues to be so**.
    
- **Procedure:** If the husband applies, the Court may give him **one year** to prove that he has ceased to be impotent. If he fails, the divorce is granted.
    

**6. Insanity and Disease**

- **Ground:** That the husband has been insane for a period of **2 years** OR is suffering from **leprosy** or a **virulent venereal disease** (e.g., HIV/AIDS, Syphilis).
    

**7. Exercise of Option of Puberty (_Khiyar-ul-Bulugh_)**

- **Ground:** That she, having been given in marriage by her father or other guardian before she attained the age of **15 years**, repudiated the marriage before attaining the age of **18 years**.
    
- **Condition:** Provided that the marriage has **not been consummated**.
    

**8. Cruelty (Mental or Physical)**

- **Ground:** That the husband treats her with cruelty. The Act defines "Cruelty" broadly to include:
    
    - Habitually assaulting her or making her life miserable (even without physical violence).
        
    - Associating with women of evil repute or leading an infamous life.
        
    - Attempting to force her to lead an immoral life.
        
    - Disposing of her property or preventing her from exercising her legal rights over it.
        
    - Obstructing her in the observance of her religious profession or practice.
        
    - If he has more than one wife, not treating her equitably in accordance with the injunctions of the Quran.
        

**9. Any Other Ground**

- **Ground:** Any other ground which is recognized as valid for the dissolution of marriages under Muslim Law (e.g., _Li'an_ - false charge of adultery).

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Time Limit" Memory Trick:**
    
    - **2 Years:** Maintenance & Insanity.
        
    - **3 Years:** Marital Obligations.
        
    - **4 Years:** Missing Husband.
        
    - **7 Years:** Imprisonment.
        
- **Cruelty includes Property:** Cruelty is not just hitting. If a husband sells his wife's land without her permission or stops her from praying, that is legal "Cruelty" under this Act.
    
- **Option of Puberty Change:** Classical Sharia only allowed a girl to cancel a marriage if she was married by a _non-father_ guardian (like an uncle). The 1939 Act (Section 2(vii)) changed this: She can now cancel the marriage **even if her own father** contracted it, provided she does so between age 15 and 18.
