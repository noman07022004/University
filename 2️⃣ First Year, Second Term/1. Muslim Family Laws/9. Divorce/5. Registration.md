## 9.5 Registration of Divorce ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** While the _Notice_ (under Section 7) is what makes the divorce legally effective, _Registration_ is what makes it provable in court. You need to understand the distinction between "validity" and "proof."
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Frequently tested in short notes or as part of a larger problem question involving the "Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974."
    

---

### The Evidentiary Requirement

While the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO) 1961 focuses on the _procedure_ of divorce (Notice & Arbitration), the **Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974** focuses on the _record-keeping_ of that event.

#### 1. The Statutory Mandate

- **The Law:** Under **Section 6** of the Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974, the registration of all divorces effected by Muslims in Bangladesh is **mandatory**.
    
- **Who Registers?** A Nikah Registrar (commonly known as a **Kazi**) licensed by the Government.
    
- **Jurisdiction:** The divorce must be registered with the Nikah Registrar within whose jurisdiction the divorce was effected or where the parties reside.
    

#### 2. The Procedure for Registration

1. **Application:** The parties (usually the husband, or the wife if exercising _Talaq-e-Tafwid_) must apply to the Nikah Registrar.
    
2. **Documents:** They must produce the copy of the Notice sent to the Chairman and the Wife (under MFLO Section 7) to prove the process was initiated.
    
3. **Entry:** The Kazi enters the details in a specific register (Register D for Divorce by Husband, Register C for Khula/Talaq-e-Tafwid).
    
4. **Certificate:** After registration, the Kazi issues a **Certified Copy** of the divorce (Talaqnama/Divorce Certificate) to the parties.
    

#### 3. Consequences of Non-Registration (Crucial Distinction)

This is the most common point of confusion for students.

- **Does Non-Registration make the Divorce Void?**
    
    - **NO.** Unlike the "Notice to Chairman" (which is required for validity), the failure to _register_ the divorce with the Kazi does **not** legally invalidate the divorce itself. If the Notice was served and 90 days passed, the divorce is valid even if unregistered.
        
- **The Practical Consequence (Evidentiary Problem):**
    
    - Without a registration certificate, proving the divorce in court (e.g., to claim Dower, to remarry, or to inherit) is incredibly difficult.
        
    - Courts often view unregistered divorces with extreme suspicion, treating them as fake or "afterthoughts."
        

#### 4. Penalty for Non-Registration

- Whoever contravenes the provisions of the 1974 Act (i.e., fails to register) is punishable with:
    
    - **Simple Imprisonment:** Up to 2 years.
        
    - **Fine:** Up to 3,000 Taka.
        
    - **Both.**
        

---

### Important Exam Tip: "Notice vs. Registration"

You must clearly distinguish these two acts in your answers:

|**Feature**|**Notice to Chairman (MFLO 1961)**|**Registration with Kazi (1974 Act)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Purpose**|To make the divorce **Effective/Valid**.|To create **Evidence/Proof**.|
|**Consequence of Failure**|The divorce is **VOID** (Marriage continues).|The divorce is **VALID** (but hard to prove; punishable offense).|
