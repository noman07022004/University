## 9.11 Irrevocability of Divorce (_Talaq-e-Bain_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This concept defines the "Point of No Return." You must understand exactly _when_ the marriage bond is severed completely, as this dictates inheritance rights and the legality of future marriages.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Highly frequent. Problem questions often hinge on whether a divorce had become irrevocable at a specific moment (e.g., _"Husband died on the 85th day after notice. Does the wife inherit?"_).
    

---

### The Point of Severance

1. Concept and Classification

In Muslim Law, a divorce is classified into two states based on whether the marriage can be saved:

- **Revocable Divorce (_Talaq-e-Raj'i_):**
    
    - **Status:** The marriage is technically still alive. The husband has the right to take the wife back (_Raja'at_) without a new contract or dower.
        
    - **Effect:** If one spouse dies during this period, the other inherits.
        
- **Irrevocable Divorce (_Talaq-e-Bain_):**
    
    - **Status:** The marriage bond is completely severed. The parties are now strangers (_Ajnabi_) to each other.
        
    - **Effect:** They cannot reunite without a fresh marriage contract (and sometimes _Halala_). Mutual inheritance rights cease immediately.
        

---

### 2. When does it become Irrevocable? (The Conflict)

The specific moment a divorce becomes irrevocable depends on whether you apply **Classical Sharia** or **Bangladeshi Statute**.

#### A. Under Classical Sharia (Hanafi Law)

- **Talaq-al-Ahsan:** Becomes irrevocable only after the **Iddat** period expires.
    
- **Talaq-al-Hasan:** Becomes irrevocable upon the **Third Pronouncement**.
    
- **Talaq-al-Bida'at (Triple Talaq):** Becomes irrevocable **INSTANTLY** upon pronouncement. There is no waiting period for the severance of the bond (though she must still observe Iddat).
    

#### B. Under Bangladeshi Law (MFLO 1961) - **Crucial for Exam**

The **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961** has standardized the rule, overriding the "Instant Irrevocability" of Triple Talaq.

- **The 90-Day Rule:** Under Section 7(3), **ANY** divorce (even if pronounced as Triple/Irrevocable) remains **Revocable** for **90 days** from the date the Notice is delivered to the Chairman.
    
- **The Moment of Irrevocability:** The divorce becomes irrevocable _only_ when:
    
    1. **90 Days** have passed without reconciliation; OR
        
    2. If the wife is pregnant, when the **pregnancy ends** (whichever is later).
        
- **Result:** In Bangladesh, there is no such thing as an "Instantly Irrevocable" divorce. Every divorce provides a minimum 90-day window for the husband to take her back.
    

---

### 3. Legal Consequences of Irrevocability

Once the divorce becomes irrevocable (after 90 days), the following legal effects trigger immediately:

1. **Cessation of Conjugal Rights:** Sexual intercourse becomes unlawful (_Zina_). Cohabitation must cease.
    
2. **Mutual Inheritance Ceases:** If the husband dies _one day_ after the divorce becomes irrevocable, the wife gets **ZERO**. (However, if he dies on Day 89, she still inherits).
    
3. **Dower Becomes Due:** The entire unpaid Dower (Prompt + Deferred) becomes immediately payable. The husband loses any time extension he had for the "Deferred" portion.
    
4. **Right to Remarry:**
    
    - **The Parties:** Can remarry each other _without_ Halala (unless it is the 3rd time).
        
    - **Others:** The wife can marry another man immediately (as her Iddat usually runs concurrent with the 90 days).
        

---

### 4. The Exception: The "Third Time" Rule (Section 7(6))

This is the only scenario where "Irrevocability" carries a permanent penalty.

- **The Rule:** If a divorce becomes irrevocable for the **third time** between the same couple (i.e., they married, divorced, remarried, divorced, remarried, and divorced again), the MFLO allows the harsh classical rule to apply.
    
- **Consequence:** The divorce is **Permanently Irrevocable**. They **CANNOT** remarry each other unless the wife undergoes **Halala** (marries another man, consummates, and divorces).
    

---

### Summary Table: When is the Bond Severed?

|**Type of Divorce**|**Classical Sharia Moment**|**Bangladesh Law (MFLO) Moment**|
|---|---|---|
|**Ahsan (Single)**|After Iddat ends.|**90 Days** after Notice.|
|**Hasan (Triple)**|On 3rd Pronouncement.|**90 Days** after Notice.|
|**Bida'at (Triple)**|**Instantly.**|**90 Days** after Notice.|
|**Khula**|Instantly (on acceptance).|**90 Days** after Notice.|

### Important Exam Tip

**"Death during the 90 Days"**

- _Question:_ Husband gives notice of Talaq on Jan 1st. He dies on Feb 1st (Day 32). Is the wife an heir?
    
- _Answer:_ **YES.** Under MFLO, the divorce was not yet effective/irrevocable. She is legally still his widow and inherits.
