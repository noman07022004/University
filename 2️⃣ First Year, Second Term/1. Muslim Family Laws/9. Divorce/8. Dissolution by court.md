

## 9.8 Dissolution by Court (_Faskh_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This represents the intervention of the State (Judiciary) in the private sphere of marriage. It is critical to understand how the law rescues women trapped in abusive or defunct marriages where the husband refuses to pronounce Talaq.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A high-priority topic. Questions often ask to distinguish _Faskh_ from _Khula_ or to explain the historical necessity of the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939.
    

---

### The Judicial Remedy

**1. Concept and Definition**

- **Literal Meaning:** _Faskh_ literally means "cancellation," "abrogation," or "annulment."
    
- **Legal Definition:** In Muslim Law, it refers to the **dissolution of marriage by a decree of the Court** (Qadi). It is the power of the Islamic Judge to annul a marriage contract on the application of the wife, typically when the husband is at fault or there is a defect in the marriage.
    
- **Key Distinction:** Unlike _Talaq_ (which is extra-judicial and unilateral by the husband) or _Khula_ (which is extra-judicial and by agreement), _Faskh_ is **judicial** and **adversarial** (the wife fights against the husband in court).
    

**2. The Historical Necessity (Why do we have it?)**

- **The "Hanafi Problem":** Under strict classical Hanafi law, a woman had almost **no grounds** to seek divorce from a court. Even if her husband abandoned her or was cruel, she remained stuck unless he agreed to release her.
    
    - _Example:_ If a Hanafi husband went missing, the wife had to wait **90 years** (until his presumed natural death) before remarrying.
        
- **The "Maliki Solution":** The Maliki school was much more liberal, allowing women to divorce on grounds of harm (_Darar_), missing husband, etc.
    
- **The 1939 Reform:** To solve the suffering of Hanafi women in British India, scholars issued a _Fatwa_ adopting the Maliki view. This was codified into the **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939**. This Act is the statutory basis for _Faskh_ in Bangladesh today.
    

**3. Nature of the Decree**

- **Effect:** A decree of _Faskh_ operates as a **dissolution of marriage**, not technically a Talaq.
    
- **Irrevocability:** Once the decree is passed (and appeal period over), the marriage is severed permanently. The husband **cannot revoke** it (unlike a Talaq Ahsan).
    
- **Dower:** In a _Faskh_ divorce, the wife generally **retains her right to Dower** (_Mahr_), especially if the divorce is due to the husband's fault (e.g., cruelty, failure to maintain). If the divorce is due to her own defect (e.g., her own insanity), the dower might be extinguished.
    

**4. Forum in Bangladesh**

- A suit for _Faskh_ must be filed in the **Family Court** (under the Family Courts Ordinance, 1985).
    
- The burden of proof lies on the wife to prove her grounds (e.g., prove cruelty with witnesses/medical reports).
    

---

### Comparison: Faskh vs. Khula

|**Feature**|**Faskh (Court Dissolution)**|**Khula (Redemption)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Authority**|**Court Decree** (Judge decides).|**Mutual Agreement** (Husband consents).|
|**Grounds**|**Strict Grounds Required** (Cruelty, etc.).|**No Grounds Needed** (Just aversion/incompatibility).|
|**Dower**|Wife usually **Gets** Dower.|Wife usually **Gives Up** Dower.|
|**Consent**|Husband's consent **Irrelevant**.|Husband's consent is **Mandatory** (traditionally).|

### Important Exam Tip

**"The Husband's Faskh?"**

- _Question:_ Can a husband apply to the court for _Faskh_?
    
- _Answer:_ Generally, **NO**. A husband does not need the court; he has the power of _Talaq_. _Faskh_ is primarily a remedy for the **wife**. However, in rare cases (like the wife's insanity), a husband might seek a judicial declaration to avoid paying dower, but this is legally redundant given his Talaq power.
