## Divorce (_Talaq_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is arguably the most complex practical area of Muslim Law. You must navigate the intersection of ancient forms (Ahsan/Bida'i), contract law (Delegated Divorce), and modern statutes (MFLO 1961 & DMMA 1939).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Extremely frequent. Expect problem questions on the validity of a divorce (e.g., "A pronounces Triple Talaq in anger..."), the procedure of serving notice, or the grounds for judicial divorce.
    

### Introduction

In Islamic Law, marriage is a contract, and like any contract, it can be terminated. However, the Prophet (PBUH) famously declared divorce to be _"the most detestable of all permitted things"_ (_Abghad al-Mubahat_). While the husband historically held the unilateral power to divorce (_Talaq_), the law has evolved to provide wives with exit routes through delegated power, mutual consent, or court intervention. In Bangladesh, all forms of divorce are heavily regulated by the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961**, which creates a uniform procedure to prevent impulsive separations.
### 1. Classification and Husband's Right to Dissolve

**Subtopics Covered:** _Types, Husband's right to dissolve, Time factor, Irrevocability of divorce._

Divorce is broadly classified into Extra-Judicial (without court) and Judicial (via court). The husband's unilateral right is called **Talaq**.

#### A. Talaq-us-Sunnah (The Approved Forms)

These forms follow the Prophet's teachings, allowing time for reconciliation.

1. **Talaq-i-Ahsan (Most Proper):**
    
    - **Procedure:** A single pronouncement of divorce made during a _Tuhr_ (period of purity between menstruations) followed by abstinence from sexual intercourse during the _Iddat_ period.
        
    - **Status:** It is **revocable** (_Raja'i_) anytime during the Iddat. It becomes irrevocable (_Ba'in_) only after Iddat ends.
        
2. **Talaq-i-Hasan (Proper):**
    
    - **Procedure:** Three pronouncements made during three _successive_ Tuhrs, with no intercourse in between.
        
    - **Status:** It becomes irrevocable upon the third pronouncement.
        

#### B. Talaq-ul-Biddat (Triple Talaq - The Disapproved Form)

- **Procedure:** Three pronouncements made in a single breath or meeting (e.g., "I divorce you, I divorce you, I divorce you").
    
- **Status:** Historically, in Hanafi law, this was considered sinful but **legally valid** and immediately irrevocable (_Mugallazah_).
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:** Section 7 of the MFLO 1961 has effectively "defanged" this form. Even if a man pronounces Triple Talaq, the law treats it as a single, revocable divorce that only takes effect after 90 days.
    

### 2. Statutory Procedure (The MFLO 1961)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory procedure, Registration._

This is the most critical section for legal practice in Bangladesh. **Section 7 of the MFLO 1961** overrides the traditional immediate effect of divorce.

1. **Notice:** Any man who wishes to divorce his wife _must_, after pronouncing it, give **Notice in writing** to the **Chairman** (Union Parishad/Pourashava) and supply a copy to the wife.
    
    - _Penalty:_ Failure to give notice is punishable by up to 1 year imprisonment or fine.
        
2. **The 90-Day Freeze:** The divorce does **not** take effect immediately. It remains suspended for **90 days** from the date the Chairman receives the notice.
    
3. **Arbitration:** The Chairman must constitute an **Arbitration Council** to attempt reconciliation.
    
4. **Effectiveness:**
    
    - If reconciliation fails or doesn't happen, the divorce becomes effective (Irrevocable) after 90 days.
        
    - If the wife is pregnant, the divorce takes effect only after the **delivery** of the child (if that period is longer than 90 days).
        
5. **Registration:** Under the _Muslim Marriages and Divorces (Registration) Act, 1974_, registering a divorce is mandatory.
    

### 3. Wife's Power and Mutual Consent

**Subtopics Covered:** _Wife's power to dissolve, Dissolution by mutual consent._

#### A. Talaq-i-Tafwid (Delegated Divorce)

- **Concept:** The husband delegates his power of Talaq to the wife (usually in **Clause 18** of the Kabinnama).
    
- **Exercise:** The wife can divorce herself on behalf of her husband. She does not need to go to court; she simply pronounces divorce on herself and follows the Section 7 Notice procedure.
    
- **Significance:** This is the most powerful tool for a Muslim wife to secure her exit without lengthy litigation.
    

#### B. Mutual Consent

1. **Khula (Redemption):** The wife initiates the divorce and agrees to give "consideration" to the husband (usually giving up her Dower/Mahr) in exchange for her release.
    
2. **Mubara'at (Mutual Release):** Both parties desire separation. Usually, no exchange of money is involved, and mutual claims are dropped.
    

### 4. Judicial Divorce (Dissolution by Court)

**Subtopics Covered:** _Dissolution by court, Grounds of faskh in statutory provisions._

If a wife does not have the power of _Tafwid_ and the husband refuses _Khula_, she must go to the Family Court. The **Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act (DMMA), 1939** codified the grounds for _Faskh_ (Judicial Rescission).

**Key Grounds (Section 2 of DMMA 1939):**

1. **Missing Husband:** Whereabouts unknown for **4 years**.
    
2. **Failure to Maintain:** Husband has failed to provide maintenance for **2 years**.
    
3. **Imprisonment:** Husband sentenced to imprisonment for **7 years** or more.
    
4. **Impotence:** Husband was impotent at the time of marriage and continues to be so.
    
5. **Insanity/Disease:** Husband has been insane for 2 years or suffers from virulent leprosy/venereal disease.
    
6. **Cruelty:** Habitual assault, associating with women of evil repute, forcing immoral life, disposing of her property, etc.
    

### 5. Consequences of Divorce

**Subtopics Covered:** _Periods of iddat, Remarriage, Rights of divorced wife._

#### A. Iddat (Waiting Period)

- **Definition:** The period a woman must wait before remarrying to ascertain pregnancy and avoid confusion of parentage.
    
- **Durations:**
    
    - **Divorce:** 3 Menstrual Courses (if menstruating) or 3 Lunar Months (if not).
        
    - **Death of Husband:** 4 Months and 10 Days.
        
    - **Pregnancy:** Until the delivery of the child (extends the other periods).
        

#### B. Remarriage and Halala

- **Simple Divorce:** Parties can remarry each other without any intervening marriage.
    
- **The "Halala" Rule (MFLO Modification):** Traditionally, after a Triple Talaq, the couple could not remarry unless the wife married another man, consummated it, and was divorced (_Halala_).
    
    - **Section 7(6) MFLO:** Abolished the need for _Halala_ for the first two effective divorces. They can remarry simply. However, if they divorce effectively **three distinct times** (with notices and 90-day periods completed each time), the _Halala_ bar applies.
        

#### C. Rights of Divorced Wife

1. **Maintenance:** During the Iddat period only (subject to the _Hefzur Rahman_ case discussion in the previous topic).
    
2. **Dower:** Immediate payment of the entire unpaid Dower (Prompt + Deferred).
    
3. **Lodging:** Right to stay in the conjugal home during Iddat.
    
4. **Inheritance:** If the husband divorces her during "Death Illness" (_Marz-ul-Maut_) to deprive her of inheritance, she still inherits if he dies during her Iddat.

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Section 7 is Paramount:** In any problem question about divorce in Bangladesh, if the husband says "Talaq" 100 times but **does not send the written notice** to the Chairman, the divorce is **NOT valid** (Case: _Sirajul Islam v. Helana Begum_).
    
- **Tafwid Nuance:** If a wife exercises _Talaq-i-Tafwid_, technically _she_ is divorcing _herself_, but the Notice to the Chairman is sent _by her_ (signed as "Wife, acting on power delegated by Husband").
    
- **Khula vs. Faskh:**
    
    - _Khula:_ Out of court, mutual consent (wife pays).
        
    - _Faskh:_ In court, adversarial (wife proves fault).
        
- **Effect of DMMA 1939:** This Act was a revolutionary step for women's rights, allowing them to divorce even if the husband was unwilling, provided valid grounds (like cruelty or neglect) existed.
