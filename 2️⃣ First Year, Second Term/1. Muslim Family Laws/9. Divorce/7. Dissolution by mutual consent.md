

## 9.7 Dissolution by Mutual Consent (_Khula_ & _Mubara'at_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (4/5):** Essential to understand that divorce isn't always a "fight." It can be a negotiated contract to end the marriage peacefully.
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Frequently tested, especially the concept of "Consideration" (_Ewaz_) in Khula. Examiners love asking: _"Can a wife claim Khula if the husband refuses?"_ (Hint: It's tricky).
    

---

### Contractual Separation

In Muslim Law, marriage is a civil contract, and like any other contract, it can be terminated by the mutual consent of the parties. When both spouses agree that they can no longer live within the limits of God, they can agree to separate.

This mutual dissolution takes two primary forms:

#### 1. Khula (Redemption)

- **Definition:** _Khula_ literally means "to take off" (like taking off clothes). Legally, it is an agreement between the husband and wife to dissolve the marriage **at the instance of the wife**, in exchange for a consideration (_Ewaz_) paid by her to the husband.
    
- **The Essentials:**
    
    1. **Proposal:** The offer usually comes from the **Wife** (e.g., "Divorce me, and I will give up my Dower").
        
    2. **Acceptance:** The **Husband must agree**. (Unlike _Faskh_ where the court forces it, in traditional _Khula_, the husband's consent is mandatory).
        
    3. **Consideration (_Ewaz_):** The wife gives something to the husband to "buy" her freedom. Usually, she **remits (forgives) her Dower (_Mahr_)** or pays a sum of money.
        
- **Effect on Dower:** Unless explicitly agreed otherwise, the general presumption in _Khula_ is that the wife **relinquishes her right to Dower**.
    

#### 2. Mubara'at (Mutual Release)

- **Definition:** _Mubara'at_ means "mutual release" or "freeing one another." It occurs when the aversion is **mutual** (both husband and wife want to separate).
    
- **The Essentials:**
    
    1. **Proposal:** Can come from either side.
        
    2. **Acceptance:** The other side agrees.
        
    3. **No Exchange Required:** Unlike _Khula_ where the wife pays, in _Mubara'at_, usually no money changes hands because both want out. They simply mutually waive any claims against each other.
        
- **Effect on Dower:** The wife generally retains her dower unless she specifically waives it.
    

---

### Statutory Procedure (Section 8 of MFLO 1961)

In Bangladesh, you cannot just sign a paper at home and be divorced. Even mutual consent divorces must follow the statutory procedure to be legally effective.

- **The Rule:** Section 8 of the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961** applies to _Khula_ and _Mubara'at_.
    
- **The Process:**
    
    1. **Agreement:** The parties sign the Khula/Mubara'at deed.
        
    2. **Notice:** They (usually the wife) must send a **Notice to the Chairman** (Arbitration Council) and the other party.
        
    3. **90 Days:** The divorce is **suspended** for 90 days.
        
    4. **Effectiveness:** It becomes effective only after the 90 days expire.
        
- **Why Notice?** Even though they agreed, the law forces a 90-day "cooling-off" period in case they change their minds.

### Comparison Table: Khula vs. Mubara'at

|**Feature**|**Khula**|**Mubara'at**|
|---|---|---|
|**Initiator**|**Wife** (She wants out).|**Both** (Mutual aversion).|
|**Consideration**|**Yes** (Wife pays/waives Dower).|**Usually No** (Mutual waiver).|
|**Husband's Consent**|**Mandatory** (He can refuse).|**Mandatory** (Mutual agreement).|
|**Effect**|Single Irrevocable Divorce (after Iddat/90 days).|Single Irrevocable Divorce (after Iddat/90 days).|

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Can the Court Force Khula? (The "Khurshid Bib" Rule):**
    
    - _Traditional View:_ Husband must consent. If he says "No," Khula fails.
        
    - _Modern View (Pakistan/Bangladesh):_ In the landmark case _Khurshid Bibi v. Baboo Muhammad Amin_, the Supreme Court of Pakistan ruled that if the Court is satisfied the parties cannot live within the limits of God, it can **grant Khula without the husband's consent**, provided the wife returns benefits received.
        
    - _Exam Tip:_ In Bangladesh, courts generally follow this equitable principle. If the marriage has irretrievably broken down, the court may grant a decree of dissolution resembling Khula even if the husband resists.
        
- **Revocability:** Unlike a normal Talaq which is revocable by the husband alone, a _Khula_ or _Mubara'at_ cannot be revoked by one party alone because it is a **bilateral contract**. They would need to remarry to get back together.
