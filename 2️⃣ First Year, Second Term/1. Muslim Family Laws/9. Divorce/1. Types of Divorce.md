## 9.1 Types of Divorce (Dissolution of Marriage) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** You cannot understand Muslim family law without mastering the classification of divorce. It defines _who_ has the power to end the marriage and _how_ it is done.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A guaranteed exam topic. You will often be asked to "Classify dissolution of marriage" or distinguish between _Talaq-al-Ahsan_ and _Talaq-al-Bida'at_.
    

---

### Classification of Dissolution

In Muslim Law, the dissolution of marriage is not a single concept but a structured system classified by **who initiates** the separation. Unlike English law, which historically required a court decree for all divorces, Muslim law provides extra-judicial methods.

#### 1. By the Husband (_Talaq_)

The husband has the unilateral power to pronounce divorce. This power is classified into two categories based on moral approval and procedure.

**A. Talaq-al-Sunnah (Approved Forms)** These forms are in accordance with the traditions of the Prophet (PBUH) because they allow a "cooling-off" period for reconciliation.

- **Talaq-al-Ahsan (Most Approved):**
    
    - **Procedure:** A single pronouncement of divorce made during a _Tuhr_ (period of purity between menstruations) followed by abstinence from sexual intercourse during the _Iddat_ period.
        
    - **Effect:** It is **revocable** during the Iddat. If the husband resumes cohabitation, the divorce is revoked. If Iddat expires, it becomes irrevocable.
        
- **Talaq-al-Hasan (Good):**
    
    - **Procedure:** Three pronouncements made during three _successive_ Tuhrs (periods of purity).
        
    - **Effect:** The first two are revocable. Upon the third pronouncement, it becomes irrevocable.
        

**B. Talaq-al-Bida'at (Disapproved/Sinful Form)**

- **Procedure:** Three pronouncements made in a single breath or single meeting (e.g., "I divorce thee, I divorce thee, I divorce thee" or "I give you three Talaqs").
    
- **Effect (Traditional):** Under classical Hanafi law, this is sinful (_Bida'i_) but **immediately effective** and irrevocable.
    
- **Effect (Bangladesh):** The **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961** has virtually abolished the immediate effect of this form. Any Talaq (even triple) is now treated as a single, revocable divorce that only takes effect after 90 days.
    

**C. Constructive Divorce (Archaic Forms)**

- **Ila:** Where the husband swears an oath not to have sexual intercourse with his wife for 4 months or more. If he keeps the oath, the marriage dissolves.
    
- **Zihar:** Where the husband compares his wife to a prohibited relation (e.g., "You are like my mother's back to me"). This prohibits cohabitation until he performs penance (_Kaffara_).
    

---

#### 2. By the Wife (_Talaq-e-Tafwid_)

This is the **Delegated Divorce**.

- **Concept:** The husband delegates his power of _Talaq_ to the wife. This is usually recorded in **Clause 18 of the Kabinnama**.
    
- **Operation:** The wife divorces _herself_ on behalf of the husband. She says, "I divorce myself."
    
- **Conditions:** It can be unconditional or conditional (e.g., "If he beats me" or "If he marries a second wife").
    

---

#### 3. By Mutual Consent

When both parties agree to part ways.

- **Khula (Redemption):**
    
    - **Initiator:** The Wife.
        
    - **Concept:** She offers to give something (consideration) to the husband in exchange for her release. Usually, she gives up her Dower (_Mahr_).
        
    - **Consent:** The husband must agree to the offer.
        
- **Mubara'at (Mutual Release):**
    
    - **Initiator:** Both parties equally.
        
    - **Concept:** Mutual aversion. Both want to separate. Usually, no exchange of money is required, and mutual claims are waived.
        

---

#### 4. By Judicial Decree (_Faskh_)

When the husband refuses to give a divorce and there is no mutual agreement, the wife sues the husband in the **Family Court**.

- **Authority:** **The Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939**.
    
- **Grounds:** Cruelty, desertion (4 years), failure to maintain (2 years), imprisonment (7 years), impotence, insanity, etc..
    
- **Effect:** The court dissolves the marriage. This does not require the husband's consent.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **The "Triple Talaq" Trick:** If an exam question asks, _"Is Triple Talaq valid in Bangladesh?"_
    
    - **Answer:** Yes, it is valid as a _mode_ of pronouncement, BUT it is **not instantly irrevocable**. Under Section 7 of MFLO 1961, it operates as a single revocable divorce and takes effect only after 90 days and proper notice.
        
- **Khula vs. Faskh:**
    
    - **Khula:** Requires Husband's consent (he can say no).
        
    - **Faskh:** Does NOT require Husband's consent (Court forces it).
        
- **Lian (False Charge of Adultery):** If a husband falsely accuses his wife of adultery, she can sue for divorce. This is a specific ground for judicial dissolution (_Faskh_).
