## 9.2 Husband's Right to Dissolve (_Talaq_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the most distinct feature of Muslim family law—the husband's unilateral power. Understanding its scope (and its modern statutory limits) is essential to grasp the power dynamic of the marriage contract.
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Questions often focus on the _limitations_ of this right (e.g., "Is a divorce pronounced under intoxication valid?" or "Can a husband divorce without a reason?").
    

---

###  The Nature of Unilateral Power

**1. The Concept of Unilateral Right** In classical Muslim Law, the husband possesses the **absolute and unlimited right** to repudiate the marriage at his own will.

- **No Cause Needed:** He is not required to show any cause or reason (like adultery or cruelty) to justify the divorce.
    
- **No Consent Needed:** He does not need the consent of his wife.
    
- **No Court Needed:** He does not need to go to a court of law (unlike the wife, who needs a decree for _Faskh_). He can simply pronounce _Talaq_ extra-judicially.
    

**2. Capacity to Exercise the Right** To validly pronounce _Talaq_, the husband must satisfy two conditions:

- **Majority (_Bulugh_):** He must be an adult (Puberty in Sharia, but 18+ under Majority Act for legal clarity). A divorce by a minor is void.
    
- **Sanity (_Aql_):** He must be of sound mind. A divorce pronounced by an insane person or during a delirium is void.
    

**3. The Controversy of Compulsion and Intoxication** This is a frequent exam trap.

- **Compulsion (_Ikrah_):**
    
    - _Sunni (Hanafi) Law:_ A Talaq pronounced under compulsion/threat (e.g., "Divorce her or I shoot you") is **VALID**.
        
    - _Shia Law:_ It is **VOID**.
        
- **Intoxication (_Sukur_):**
    
    - _Sunni (Hanafi) Law:_ A Talaq pronounced while voluntarily intoxicated (drunk) is **VALID**.
        
    - _Shia Law:_ It is **VOID**.
        
    - _Bangladeshi Courts:_ Modern courts in Bangladesh tend to lean against the strict Hanafi rule, often arguing that if the man didn't know what he was doing, the intention (_Niyyah_) is missing, rendering it ineffective.
        

---

### Statutory Restrictions in Bangladesh (The "Check")

While Sharia gives the husband an "unlimited" right, the **Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961** places a significant procedural check on it.

- **The Restriction:** The right is no longer "instant." Under **Section 7**, a husband cannot just say "Talaq" and be done.
    
- **The Procedure:** He **MUST** give written notice to the **Chairman** and the **Wife**.
    
- **The Effect:** The divorce is **suspended** for 90 days. This forced delay restricts his arbitrary power by giving the Arbitration Council a chance to reconcile the couple.
    
    - _Note:_ If he fails to give notice, the divorce is **NO DIVORCE**. His "Right" is effectively nullified by his procedural failure.
        

---

### Shia vs. Sunni Difference (Strictness)

- **Sunni Law:** The husband can pronounce Talaq orally or in writing. No witnesses are legally required for the pronouncement itself to be valid (though recommended).
    
- **Shia Law:** The husband's right is strictly regulated.
    
    - It **MUST** be pronounced orally (unless he is mute).
        
    - It **MUST** be in the presence of **two competent witnesses**.
        
    - There must be a clear intention (_Niyyah_).
        

### Important Exam Tip

**"Delegated Right" is NOT a loss of Right.** Even if the husband delegates his power to the wife (_Talaq-e-Tafwid_), he **does NOT lose** his own right to pronounce Talaq. Both retain the power simultaneously.
