## 9.10 Remarriage ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** Essential for understanding the "aftermath" of divorce. You need to know when the parties are free to marry again to avoid creating void or irregular marriages.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** A critical practical topic. Problems often ask: _"Can the divorced couple remarry immediately?"_ or _"A husband marries his wife's sister the day after divorcing his wife. Is the marriage valid?"_
    

---

### When is Remarriage Lawful?

The rules for remarriage depend on **who** is remarrying (Husband vs. Wife) and **whom** they are marrying (a stranger or each other).

#### 1. Remarriage with a Third Party (Stranger)

**A. For the Wife**

- **The Rule:** A divorced wife cannot remarry another man until she has completed her **Iddat** period.
    
- **Reason:** To prevent confusion of parentage (_Nasab_). If she marries during Iddat, the marriage is **Void** (or Irregular in some views).
    
- **Duration:**
    
    - Divorce: 3 Menstrual courses / 3 Lunar months (or 90 days under MFLO).
        
    - Death: 4 Months 10 Days.
        
    - Pregnancy: Until delivery.
        

**B. For the Husband**

- **General Rule:** A husband can generally remarry **immediately** after pronouncing divorce. He does not have an Iddat.
    
- **Exceptions (When he MUST wait):** He cannot remarry immediately if:
    
    1. **Four Wives:** He already has 4 wives. He must wait for the divorced wife's _Iddat_ to finish before her connection is fully severed and he can marry a new 4th wife.
        
    2. **Unlawful Conjunction:** He wants to marry his divorced wife's **sister** (or aunt/niece). He must wait until the divorced wife's _Iddat_ expires because he cannot technically be "married" (even during Iddat) to two sisters at the same time.
        

---

#### 2. Remarriage Between the Divorced Couple (Halala vs. Statute)

This is the most controversial and frequently tested area. Can a husband divorce his wife and then change his mind and remarry her?

**A. The Traditional Rule (Halala)**

- **Revocable Divorce:** If it was a revocable Talaq (1 or 2 pronouncements), they can remarry without any special procedure.
    
- **Irrevocable (Triple) Talaq:** If the husband pronounced **Three Talaqs** (Triple Talaq/Bida'at), the divorce is legally "Mughallazah" (Major/Final).
    
    - **The Restriction:** He **cannot** remarry her until she undergoes **Halala**.
        
    - **Halala Process:**
        
        1. She must marry another man.
            
        2. The marriage must be valid and **consummated**.
            
        3. The new husband must die or divorce her.
            
        4. She must observe Iddat again.
            
        5. Only _then_ can she remarry her first husband.
            

**B. The Statutory Change in Bangladesh (MFLO 1961)**

- **The Reform:** **Section 7(6)** of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961 drasticallly relaxed the Halala rule.
    
- **The New Rule:** Even if a husband pronounces "Triple Talaq," the MFLO treats it as a single, revocable divorce.
    
- **Result:** The parties **CAN remarry** each other **without** the intervening marriage (Halala).
    
- **The Exception (The "Third Time" Rule):** Halala is only required if the divorce has become effective for the **third time**.
    
    - _Meaning:_ Husband divorces $\rightarrow$ Remarries her $\rightarrow$ Divorces again $\rightarrow$ Remarries her $\rightarrow$ Divorces a **3rd time**. Now, he cannot take her back without Halala.
        

---

### Summary Table: Can they Remarry?

|**Scenario**|**Classical Sharia**|**Bangladesh Law (MFLO 1961)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Wife during Iddat**|**NO** (Must wait).|**NO** (Must wait 90 days).|
|**Husband to Wife's Sister**|**NO** (Must wait for Iddat).|**NO** (Must wait 90 days).|
|**Couple (After Triple Talaq)**|**NO** (Requires Halala).|**YES** (No Halala needed).|
|**Couple (After 3rd Effective Divorce)**|**NO** (Requires Halala).|**NO** (Requires Halala).|

### Important Exam Tip

"Constructive Halala" is Void

If the parties hire a man to marry the wife for one night and divorce her the next morning just to bypass the rule (a "fixed" Halala), the Prophet (PBUH) cursed this practice (Tahleel). While strictly "legally" valid in some classical interpretations if all conditions met, it is widely condemned. However, thanks to MFLO 1961, this issue rarely arises in Bangladesh anymore for first-time divorces.
