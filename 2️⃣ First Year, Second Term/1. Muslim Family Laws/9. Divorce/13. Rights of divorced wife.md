## 9.13 Rights of Divorced Wife ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the practical outcome of the entire divorce chapter. It consolidates financial, property, and personal rights into one checklist.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Extremely high frequency. You will often see problem questions like: _"A divorced wife claims maintenance for life. Discuss in light of the Hefzur Rahman case"_ or _"What are the immediate claims a wife can make after Talaq?"_
    

---

### The Legal Checklist

Upon the dissolution of marriage (by Talaq, Khula, or Faskh), the wife becomes entitled to specific legal rights. In Bangladesh, these rights are a mix of traditional Sharia and statutory modifications.

#### 1. Financial Rights

**A. Dower (_Mahr_): The Immediate Debt**

- **The Rule:** The entire unpaid amount of Dower (both Prompt and Deferred) becomes **immediately payable** upon divorce.
    
- **Effect of Khula:** If the divorce was by _Khula_, she usually gives up her dower. However, if the agreement was silent on dower, some jurists argue she still gets it, though the general presumption is waiver.
    
- **Recovery:** She can file a suit in the **Family Court** to recover this debt.
    

**B. Maintenance (_Nafaqah_): The "Hefzur Rahman" Rule** This is the most critical exam point regarding the duration of maintenance.

- **During Iddat:** She is absolutely entitled to maintenance during the **Iddat period** (90 days or 3 months/cycles).
    
- **Post-Iddat (The Controversy):**
    
    - _High Court View (Overturned):_ In 1995, the High Court Division (_Hefzur Rahman v. Shamsun Nahar_) ruled that a husband must maintain his divorced wife **for life** (or until remarriage), citing Quranic verse 2:241 (_Mata'un Bil Ma'ruf_).
        
    - **Appellate Division View (Current Law):** In 1999, the Appellate Division **overturned** this decision.
        
    - **Binding Law:** Today, a Muslim husband in Bangladesh is **NOT** legally bound to pay maintenance beyond the Iddat period. The obligation ends when Iddat ends.
        

**C. Maintenance of Pregnancy:**

- If the wife is pregnant at the time of divorce, she is entitled to maintenance **until the delivery** of the child, even if this period exceeds the standard 90 days.
    

#### 2. Personal & Custodial Rights

**A. Right to Shelter/Residence:**

- Under Quranic injunction (Surah At-Talaq), the husband should provide her with residence during the Iddat period. She should not be expelled from the matrimonial home unless she commits indecency, though in practice, many women return to their parents.
    

**B. Custody of Children (_Hizanat_):**

- Divorce does **NOT** strip the mother of her right to custody.
    
- She retains custody of her son (until age 7) and daughter (until puberty), provided she does not marry a stranger. The father must pay for the children's maintenance while they are with her.
    

#### 3. Inheritance Rights

- **Death during 90 Days:** Since the MFLO 1961 suspends the effect of divorce for 90 days, if the husband dies _within_ this period (before the divorce becomes effective), the wife is still a legal heir and **inherits** from his estate.
    
- **Death after 90 Days:** Once the divorce becomes effective, mutual inheritance rights cease.
    

#### 4. Freedom to Remarry

- After the expiration of the **Iddat** period, she becomes completely free (_Ajnabi_) and can contract a valid marriage with any other man (or even remarry the former husband, subject to the "Third Time" restriction).
    

---

### Important Exam Tip: _Mata_ vs. _Nafaqah_

To score high marks, mention the distinction made by the Appellate Division in the _Hefzur Rahman_ case:

- **Nafaqah:** Strictly "Maintenance" (Food/Clothing/Shelter). **Mandatory** during Iddat.
    
- **Mata:** A "Consolatory Gift" or "Provision" given at the time of departure. The Court held this is a pious obligation for the righteous (_Muttaqin_), but **not a legally enforceable claim** for lifetime support in the same strict sense as Dower.
