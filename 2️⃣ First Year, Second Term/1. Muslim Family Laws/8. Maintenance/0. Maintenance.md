## Maintenance (_Nafaqah_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Maintenance is the primary daily obligation in a marriage. Understanding it requires grasping the reciprocal nature of rights: the wife gives obedience/submission (_Tamkin_), and the husband gives support. It also connects deeply to divorce laws (failure to maintain is a ground for divorce).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Very frequent in problem questions. Common scenarios include a wife living separately due to cruelty and claiming maintenance, or the controversial issue of "Post-Divorce Maintenance" (the _Shah Bano_ debate vs. Bangladeshi law).
    

### Introduction

In Muslim Law, Maintenance (_Nafaqah_) generally encompasses food, clothing, and lodging. It is the absolute right of the wife and the absolute duty of the husband. Unlike modern Western laws where maintenance might be based on who earns more, in Islamic law, the husband is the sole provider, even if the wife is wealthy. This obligation arises from the concept of _Qawwam_ (protector/maintainer) and is the counter-value for the wife's availability to the husband.

### 1. Husband's Duty and Conditions

**Subtopics Covered:** _Husband's duty._

The husband's obligation to maintain his wife is personal and non-transferable.

- **Basis:** It is based on the Quranic verse (4:34): _"Men are the protectors and maintainers of women, because Allah has given the one more (strength) than the other, and because they support them from their means."_
    
- **Quantum:** The amount is usually determined by the social status of the parties.
    
    - _Hanafi Law:_ Average of the husband's and wife's financial status.
        
    - _Shafi'i Law:_ Strictly based on the husband's means.
        
- **When is the Duty Suspended?** The husband is _not_ bound to maintain the wife if she is **Nashizah** (disobedient). This occurs if:
    
    1. She refuses to live in the conjugal domicile without valid reason.
        
    2. She refuses sexual intercourse without valid reason (e.g., unpaid Prompt Dower).
        
    3. She is imprisoned or abducted (and thus unavailable to him).
        

### 2. Statutory Rights of Maintenance

**Subtopics Covered:** _Statutory rights of maintenance._

In Bangladesh, the enforcement of maintenance has been strengthened by statutes.

#### A. The Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961 (Section 9)

This is the primary procedural remedy.

- **Arbitration Council:** If a husband fails to maintain his wife (or wives), she can apply to the Chairman of the Union Parishad/Pourashava.
    
- **Power:** The Chairman forms an Arbitration Council which has the power to:
    
    - Determine the amount of maintenance.
        
    - Issue a certificate for the recovery of the amount as a "Public Demand" (arrears of land revenue) if unpaid.
        
- **Advantage:** This is a faster, quasi-judicial remedy compared to filing a civil suit in Family Court.
    

#### B. The Family Courts Ordinance, 1985

- A wife can file a civil suit for maintenance in the Family Court.
    
- **Case Law Trend:** Bangladeshi courts (e.g., _Rustam Ali v. Jamila Khatun_) have held that a wife is entitled to **past maintenance** (arrears) for the period the husband neglected her. (Traditional Hanafi law generally did not allow claiming past maintenance unless it had been fixed by a court beforehand; modern law overrides this).
    

### 3. Maintenance During Iddat

**Subtopics Covered:** _During iddat._

- **The General Rule:** A divorced wife is entitled to maintenance **only** during the period of _Iddat_ (approx. 3 months). After _Iddat_, the husband's financial liability generally ceases.
    
- **Rationale:** After _Iddat_, she is no longer his wife and is free to remarry; thus, he is not responsible for her.
    
- **The "Post-Iddat" Controversy:**
    
    - **India:** The famous _Shah Bano (1985)_ case and subsequent Act (1986) created a complex debate about whether maintenance should continue for life if she is destitute.
        
    - **Bangladesh:** The law follows the case of _Hefzur Rahman v. Shamsun Nahar Begum (1995)_. Initially, the High Court Division ruled that a husband must maintain a divorced wife _indefinitely_ until she remarries. **However**, the **Appellate Division overturned this**, restoring the classical position: **Maintenance is due ONLY up to the Iddat period.**
        
    - _Exception:_ If the wife is pregnant at the time of divorce, maintenance continues until delivery/birth, even if it exceeds 3 months.
        

---

### Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Nashizah" Defense:** In problem questions, if a husband stops paying maintenance, check if the wife was "disobedient" (Nashizah). If she left the house because he beat her (Cruelty), she is **not** Nashizah and **is** entitled to maintenance. If she left for no reason, she gets nothing.
    
- **Past Maintenance:** Always mention that under the **Family Courts Ordinance, 1985**, courts in Bangladesh _do_ award past maintenance (arrears), which departs from the strict classical Hanafi view.
    
- **Hefzur Rahman Case:** You must cite the _Hefzur Rahman v. Shamsun Nahar Begum_ case when discussing post-divorce maintenance. It is the defining precedent in Bangladesh that settled the law (rejecting indefinite maintenance).
    
- **Section 9 MFLO:** Remember that Section 9 is an administrative remedy (Council), while Family Court is a judicial remedy. The wife can choose.
