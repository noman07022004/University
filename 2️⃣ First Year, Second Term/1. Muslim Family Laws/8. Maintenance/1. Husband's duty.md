## 8.1 Husband's Duty (_Nafaqah_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This defines the economic structure of a Muslim marriage. It establishes the husband's role as _Qawwam_ (protector/provider) and sets the condition of _Tamkin_ (submission) for the wife.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Extremely frequent. Problem questions often revolve around "When does the duty stop?" (e.g., if the wife leaves the house) or "Does a rich wife need maintenance?"
    

---

### The Absolute Obligation

**1. Concept and Definition**

- **Literal Meaning:** The word _Nafaqah_ literally means "spending" or "expenditure."
    
- **Legal Definition:** In Muslim Law, it refers to the **maintenance** or the necessities of life which a husband is legally bound to provide for his wife.
    
- **Scope:** It is generally defined to include three core elements:
    
    1. **Food:** Sufficient for her sustenance.
        
    2. **Clothing:** Suitable for the season and custom.
        
    3. **Lodging:** A separate room or house where she can live with privacy (_Purda_) and safety, free from the intrusion of the husband's relatives.
        

**2. Nature of the Duty** The husband's duty to maintain his wife is **Absolute** and **Unconditional** regarding his financial status.

- **Wife's Wealth Irrelevant:** Even if the wife is a millionaire and the husband is poor, he is still legally bound to maintain her. She is not required to spend a penny of her own money on her maintenance.
    
- **Debt:** Maintenance is considered a debt (_Dayn_) due from the husband to the wife.
    
- **Priority:** The wife's maintenance takes precedence over the maintenance of children, parents, or other relatives.
    

**3. The Condition for Entitlement (_Tamkin_)** The husband's duty is reciprocal. It arises only if the wife fulfills her marital obligations.

- **The Rule:** The wife is entitled to maintenance only if she is **faithful** and **obedient** to the reasonable commands of the husband.
    
- **Access:** She must place herself within his control and allow him free access (sexual and domestic) at all reasonable times.
    

**4. When the Duty Ceases (Disqualifications)** The husband is **NOT** bound to maintain his wife in the following situations:

- **Nushuz (Disobedience):** If the wife becomes _Nashizah_ (rebellious).
    
    - _Example:_ She leaves the matrimonial home without his permission and without valid cause.
        
    - _Example:_ She refuses sexual intercourse without a lawful excuse (e.g., unpaid Prompt Dower).
        
- **Minority:** If the wife is too young for sexual intercourse (under classical law), she is technically not entitled to maintenance, though modern statutes (Child Marriage Restraint Act) complicate this.
    
- **Imprisonment:** If the wife is imprisoned (so the husband has no access to her), the duty is suspended.
    
- **Abduction:** If she is abducted, the duty is suspended for the duration of her absence.
    

**5. Valid Reasons for Living Apart** A wife can refuse to live with her husband (and **STILL** claim maintenance) if she has a **"Valid Legal Cause"**:

- **Cruelty:** If he beats her or makes her life miserable.
    
- **Unpaid Dower:** If he fails to pay **Prompt Dower** on demand.
    
- **Concubine:** If he keeps a mistress in the house.
    
- **Second Wife:** If he takes a second wife (in contravention of MFLO provisions or without equal treatment).
    

**6. Quantum of Maintenance** How much must he pay?

- **Hanafi Law:** The amount is determined by the social status of **both** the husband and the wife. If they are of different classes, the average is taken.
    
- **Shafi Law:** It is determined solely by the husband's position.
    
- **Bangladeshi Practice:** Courts look at the husband's means and the wife's needs to fix a reasonable amount.
    

---

### Important Exam Scenario: "Past Maintenance"

- **Classical Hanafi Rule:** A wife could not claim maintenance for the past (arrears) unless it had been fixed by a court decree or agreement previously. The logic was: _Maintenance is for survival. If she survived the past without it, she doesn't need it now._
    
- **Modern Law (Bangladesh):** Courts **DO** allow claims for past maintenance.
    
    - _Scenario:_ Wife was kicked out in 2020. She sues in 2023.
        
    - _Result:_ She can recover maintenance for the 3 years (2020–2023) she was neglected, plus future maintenance.
