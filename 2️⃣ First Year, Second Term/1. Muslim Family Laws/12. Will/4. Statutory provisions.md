

## 12.4 Statutory Provisions of Will ⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (3/5):** While Sharia governs the _substance_ (who gets what), statutes govern the _procedure_ (how to validate and enforce it). Understanding this bridge between religious law and state law is key for legal practice.
    
- **Exam (3/5):** Less frequent than the 1/3rd rule, but questions often ask about "Probate" or the "Age of Majority" for a testator.
    

---

### The Legislative Framework

In Bangladesh, while the _validity_ of a Muslim Will is determined by Muslim Personal Law (Sharia), the _administration, capacity, and enforcement_ are regulated by specific state enactments.

#### 1. The Majority Act, 1875 (Age of Testator)

This is the most significant statutory modification to the traditional law of Wills.

- **Sharia Rule:** Under classical Muslim law, a person can make a valid Will upon attaining **puberty** (presumed at 15 years).
    
- **Statutory Rule:** The **Majority Act, 1875** applies to Wills. It sets the age of majority at **18 years** (or 21 years if a guardian has been appointed by the Court).
    
- **Conflict & Prevailing Law:** A Will made by a Muslim under 18 is **VOID** in Bangladesh, even if they have attained puberty. The statute overrides personal law regarding the age of capacity.
    

#### 2. The Succession Act, 1925 (Probate and Administration)

This Act governs the administration of estates in Bangladesh. While substantive Muslim law applies to the will's content, this Act provides the machinery for its enforcement.

- **Probate (Official Certification):**
    
    - **Definition:** "Probate" is a copy of the Will certified under the seal of a Court of competent jurisdiction with a grant of administration to the estate of the testator.
        
    - **Is it Mandatory?** Unlike for Christians or Hindus (in certain cases), obtaining Probate is **NOT mandatory** for a Muslim Will to be valid or to establish a right as a legatee/executor in Bangladesh courts.
        
    - **Benefit:** However, obtaining Probate is highly recommended because it conclusively proves the validity of the Will and the appointment of the executor against all opposition.
        
- **Letters of Administration:**
    
    - If there is no Will, or the Will does not name an executor, the Court grants "Letters of Administration" to manage the estate. Section 211 of the Act vests the estate in the executor/administrator.
        

#### 3. The Registration Act, 1908

- **The Rule:** The registration of a Will is **Optional**, not mandatory (Section 18).
    
- **Evidentiary Value:** A registered Will carries a strong presumption of genuineness. Since the burden of proving an oral Will is extremely heavy, lawyers always advise registering the Will to prevent future disputes among heirs.
    

#### 4. The Muslim Family Laws Ordinance (MFLO), 1961 (Indirect Effect)

While this Ordinance deals with inheritance, it impacts the _scope_ of a Will.

- **Section 4 (Orphaned Grandchildren):** By granting a mandatory inheritance share to grandchildren whose parents died earlier, the "Net Estate" available for the 1/3rd calculation is reduced.
    
- **Comparison:** In some Middle Eastern countries (like Egypt), this is legally framed as an "Obligatory Bequest" (_Wasiyyat Wajibah_). In Bangladesh, it is framed as "Inheritance," but it effectively functions as a statutory deduction that limits the testator's freedom.

### Summary of Statutory Impact

|**Feature**|**Classical Muslim Law**|**Statutory Law (Bangladesh)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Age of Testator**|Puberty (15 years)|**18 Years** (Majority Act)|
|**Proof of Will**|Oral/Written (Equal)|**Written & Registered** (Preferred by Evidence/Registration Acts)|
|**Administration**|Qadi supervision|**Probate/Letters of Admin** (Succession Act)|

### Important Exam Note

"Probate is Optional"

A common trick question is: "Can a Muslim legatee sue for his share without Probate?"

- **Answer:** **YES.** Unlike Hindu or Christian wills where rights technically cannot be established in court without probate (in specific jurisdictions), a Muslim will does not require probate to be operative. The right vests immediately upon death.
