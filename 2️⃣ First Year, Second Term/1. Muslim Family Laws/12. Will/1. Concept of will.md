

## 12.1 Concept of Will (_Wasiyyat_) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** Essential for distinguishing between the "Automatic" transfer of property (Inheritance) and the "Voluntary" transfer (Will). It represents the limited freedom a Muslim has to distribute their estate.
    
- **Exam (4/5):** Frequently tested as a theoretical question (e.g., "Define Wasiyyat and distinguish it from Hiba") or as the starting point for problem questions.
    

***1. Definition and Juridical Nature***

- **Literal Meaning:** The word _Wasiyyat_ means "a precept," "a command," or "advice."
    
- **Legal Definition:** In Muslim Law, a Will is legally defined as "a conferment of a right of property in a specific thing or in a profit or advantage, to take effect on the death of the testator."
    
- **The Core Distinction:** Unlike **Inheritance** (_Faraiz_), which is involuntary and automatic upon death, a **Will** (_Wasiyyat_) is a voluntary act of the deceased. Unlike a **Gift** (_Hiba_), which is an immediate transfer, a Will is suspended and only operates _after_ the death of the maker.
    

***2. The Object and Rationale*** Why does Islam allow Wills when the shares of heirs are already fixed in the Quran?

- **Correction Mechanism:** The Will allows a Muslim to provide for persons who are **not** legal heirs (e.g., an adopted child, a poor friend, a distant relative like an orphaned grandchild in classical law) or for charitable causes (_Sadaqah Jariyah_).
    
- **Spiritual Merit:** It is considered an opportunity for the testator to earn spiritual merit by dedicating part of their wealth to charity or needy non-heirs before they meet their Creator.
    

***3. Capacity to Make a Will (Who is Competent?)*** For a Will to be valid, the testator (maker) must meet specific criteria at the time of making it:

- **Sound Mind:** The testator must be sane. A will made by a person of unsound mind is **void**. If a person is lucid at times and insane at others, the will is valid if made during a lucid interval.
    
- **Majority:** Under strict Muslim Law, majority is puberty (15 years). However, in Bangladesh, the **Majority Act, 1875** applies to Wills. Therefore, the testator must be **18 years old** (or 21 if under a Court Guardian) to make a valid Will. A will made by a minor is void, but can be validated by ratification after attaining majority.
    
- **Free Consent:** The will must not be caused by coercion, undue influence, or fraud.
    

***4. Formalities: Oral vs. Written***

- **Strict Sharia Rule:** A Will (_Wasiyyat_) can be made **orally** or in **writing**. No specific form, signature, or attestation is technically required by religious law, provided the intention is clear. A mere gesture by a mute person (if intelligible) can also constitute a Will.
    
- **Practical/Legal Rule:** While oral wills are theoretically valid, the burden of proving them in Bangladeshi courts is incredibly heavy. In practice, Wills should be written and signed to meet the evidentiary standards of the **Succession Act, 1925**.
    

***5. Revocability***

- A fundamental characteristic of a Will is that it is **revocable**.
    
- The testator can revoke or alter the Will at _any_ time before their death.
    
- **Revocation can be:**
    
    - **Express:** "I cancel my will."
        
    - **Implied:** Doing something to the property that destroys its identity (e.g., selling the house he bequeathed, or turning wheat into flour).
        

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- **Will vs. Gift:**
    
    - **Gift (_Hiba_):** Immediate transfer. Irrevocable (mostly). No 1/3rd limit.
        
    - **Will (_Wasiyyat_):** Transfer after death. Always revocable. Strict 1/3rd limit (to be discussed in 12.2).
        
- **Suicide and Wills:**
    
    - _Sunni Law:_ A Will made by a person who _subsequently_ commits suicide is generally valid.
        
    - _Shia Law:_ A Will made _after_ taking the step towards suicide (e.g., after drinking poison) is **void**.
        
- **Probate:** In Bangladesh, obtaining **Probate** (official court certification) for a Muslim Will is **not mandatory** to establish a right as an executor or legatee (unlike for Christians/Hindus), but it is highly recommended to manage the estate legally and avoid disputes.
