

## 12.2 Restrictions on Will ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (5/5):** This is the most important concept in the Law of Wills. It defines the "limited ownership" a Muslim has over their property at death. Unlike Western law, you cannot cut your family out of your will.
    
- **Exam (5/5):** Guaranteed exam topic. You will face problems like: _"A bequeaths his entire house to a friend. Is the will valid?"_ or _"A bequeaths 1/3 to his son. Is it valid?"_
    

### The Two Limits

In Muslim Law, the testamentary power (power to make a will) is **not absolute**. It is restricted in two ways to protect the rights of the legal heirs and preserve the family structure.

#### 1. The Quantitative Limit (The "1/3rd Rule")

- **The Rule:** A Muslim cannot bequeath more than **one-third (1/3)** of their _surplus_ net estate.
    
- **Net Estate Calculation:** The 1/3rd is calculated _only_ after paying:
    
    1. Funeral expenses.
        
    2. Debts (including unpaid Dower).
        
- **Effect of Violation:** If a person bequeaths _more_ than 1/3 (e.g., "I give 50% to charity"), the will is **valid only up to 1/3**. The excess is **void**, unless the legal heirs give their consent.
    
- **Rationale:** The Prophet (PBUH) said to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas: _"A third, and a third is much."_ This ensures 2/3rds remain for the legal heirs to prevent them from becoming destitute.
    

#### 2. The Subjective Limit (The "Heir" Rule)

- **The Rule:** A bequest to a **legal heir** is **invalid**, even if it is within the 1/3rd limit.
    
- **Rationale:** The Prophet (PBUH) said: _"There is no bequest to an heir."_ The heirs already have fixed shares in the Quran. Giving an extra will to one heir (e.g., the favorite son) would disturb the divine balance and cause jealousy among siblings.
    
- **Effect:** A will saying "I give 1/3 to my Daughter" is **void**, because she is already an heir.
    

---

### The Exception: Consent of Heirs

The restrictions mentioned above are not absolute bans; they are protections for the heirs. Therefore, the heirs can choose to waive this protection.

- **Validating the Invalid:** A bequest in excess of 1/3, or a bequest to an heir, becomes **VALID** if the other heirs **consent** to it.
    
- **Timing of Consent (Crucial Distinction):**
    
    - **Sunni Law:** Consent must be given **after the death** of the testator. Consent given during the testator's lifetime is not binding and can be retracted.
        
    - **Shia Law:** Consent can be given **during the lifetime** or after the death of the testator.
        

---

### Sunni vs. Shia Differences (Exam Trap)

This is where students often lose marks. The rules for "Bequest to an Heir" differ sharply between the sects.

|**Feature**|**Sunni (Hanafi) Law**|**Shia (Ithna-Ashari) Law**|
|---|---|---|
|**Bequest to an Heir**|**Invalid** without consent of other heirs.|**Valid** up to 1/3rd **without** consent.|
|**Bequest > 1/3rd**|Invalid without consent.|Invalid without consent.|
|**Timing of Consent**|Must be **after death**.|Can be **during lifetime** or after death.|

**Example Scenario:**

- _Fact:_ A man bequeaths 1/3 of his property to his **Daughter**.
    
- _Sunni Result:_ **Void.** (Unless other heirs consent). She only gets her inheritance share.
    
- _Shia Result:_ **Valid.** She gets the 1/3 Will **PLUS** her inheritance share.
    

---

### Important Notes & Tips for Exams

- Abatement (Rateable Reduction):
    
    If a person makes multiple bequests to non-heirs that add up to more than 1/3 (e.g., 1/3 to A + 1/3 to B = 2/3 total), and heirs refuse consent:
    
    - _Sunni Law:_ The bequests are reduced **proportionately** (Rateably) to fit inside the 1/3 limit.
        
    - _Shia Law:_ The rule of **Chronological Priority** applies. The first person named gets their full share. The second gets whatever is left of the 1/3. The third gets nothing.
        
- **The "Consent" Trick:** If an exam problem says: _"The son agreed to his father's will while the father was alive, but rejected it after the father died."_
    
    - _Answer:_ Under Sunni law, the rejection is valid. The son is **not bound** by his prior consent. The will is void.
