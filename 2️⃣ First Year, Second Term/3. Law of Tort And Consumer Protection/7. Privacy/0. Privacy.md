## 7. Privacy ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 3/5):** This is a conceptually fluid area. Unlike Negligence or Trespass, which have rigid structures, "Privacy" in tort law is an evolving patchwork of equitable doctrines and human rights principles.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** It appears less frequently as a standard problem question compared to Defamation. However, it is a popular topic for theoretical essays (e.g., "Does English Law recognize a Tort of Privacy?") or as a counter-argument in Defamation problems.
    

---

### Introduction: The "Right to be Left Alone"

**Privacy** is broadly defined as the "right to be left alone." It encompasses the right of an individual to control the dissemination of information about their private life.

Historically, English Common Law (which Bangladesh largely follows) **did not recognize a specific "Tort of Privacy."**

- _Key Case:_ In _Kaye v Robertson_ (1991), a journalist invaded the hospital room of a famous actor recovering from brain surgery. The court held there was no remedy for "invasion of privacy" itself, forcing the claimant to rely on other torts like Malicious Falsehood or Trespass.
    

However, the law has evolved significantly, shifting from a total lack of protection to a robust system primarily driven by Human Rights law.

---

### 1. General Principles: From "Confidence" to "Privacy"

Since there was no direct "Privacy Tort," courts developed the **General principles** of privacy protection through the equitable doctrine of **Breach of Confidence**.

#### The Doctrine of Breach of Confidence

Originally used to protect trade secrets (commercial recipes, formulas), this doctrine was expanded to protect personal secrets.

- **The _Coco v AN Clark_ Test:** To succeed, three elements are needed:
    
    1. The information must have the "necessary quality of confidence" (it must be private, not public knowledge).
        
    2. The information must have been imparted in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence (e.g., between husband and wife, doctor and patient, or employer and employee).
        
    3. There must be an unauthorized use of that information.
        

#### The Modern Evolution: Misuse of Private Information

In modern law (post-Human Rights Act in the UK), the courts transformed "Breach of Confidence" into a new distinct tort known as **Misuse of Private Information**.

- _Key Case:_ _Campbell v MGN Ltd_ (2004). Supermodel Naomi Campbell was photographed leaving a narcotics anonymous meeting. The House of Lords held that details of her therapy were private.
    
- **The New Test:**
    
    1. **Reasonable Expectation of Privacy:** Does the claimant have a reasonable expectation of privacy in respect of the disclosed information? (Medical records, sexual life, and home life usually qualify).
        
    2. **The Balancing Exercise:** If yes, the court balances the claimant's right to privacy against the publisher's **Right to Freedom of Expression**.
        

---

### 2. Privacy as Human Right

The **General concept** of privacy is now firmly rooted in human rights statutes rather than just common law precedent.

- **International Standards:**
    
    - **UDHR Article 12:** No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with his privacy, family, home, or correspondence.
        
    - **ECHR Article 8:** Provides the "Right to respect for private and family life." This is the engine driving modern privacy law in Europe and influencing the Commonwealth.
        
- **Bangladesh Context:**
    
    - **Constitutional Right:** Article 43 of the **Constitution of Bangladesh** guarantees the right of every citizen "to be secured in his home against entry, search and seizure" and "to the privacy of his correspondence and other means of communication."
        
    - While Article 43 serves as a shield against State interference, its horizontal application (suing a private individual/newspaper for privacy breach) is still developing in Bangladeshi tort litigation.
        

---

### Important Notes & Tips

- **Defamation vs. Privacy (Crucial Distinction):**
    
    - In **Defamation**, the statement must be _false_. If the statement is true, the defendant wins (Defense of Justification).
        
    - In **Privacy**, the statement is usually _true_ (e.g., revealing a true affair or medical condition). The harm is the _revelation_ itself. Therefore, **Truth is NOT a defense** in privacy law.
        
- **Public Interest:** The only major defense to a privacy claim is "Public Interest." The media can publish private info if they can prove it serves the public good (e.g., exposing a politician's corruption is in the public interest; exposing their consensual affair might not be).
    
- **Essay Tip:** A strong essay on privacy will argue that while we don't have a "Tort of Privacy" by name, the expansion of "Breach of Confidence" has created one in practice.
