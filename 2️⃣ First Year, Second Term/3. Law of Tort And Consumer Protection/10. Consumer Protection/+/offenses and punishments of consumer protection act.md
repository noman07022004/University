Here is a full categorization of the offenses based on their penalties, from least to most severe.

### Tier 1: Standard Severity

These offenses are generally seen as economic or informational, cheating the consumer but not (usually) posing a direct, immediate threat to life.

**Penalty:** Imprisonment up to **1 (one) year**, or a fine up to **Taka 50,000** (fifty thousand), or both.

- Sec. 37 (Labeling): Failing to put required info on a product's label (like MRP, weight, or expiry date).
    
- Sec. 38 (Price List - Goods): Failing to display the price list for goods in a conspicuous place.
    
- Sec. 39 (Price List - Services): Failing to preserve or display the price list for services.
    
- Sec. 40 (Overcharging): Selling any good, medicine, or service at a price higher than what is fixed.
    
- Sec. 45 (Failure to Deliver): Not properly selling or delivering a good or service that was promised.
    
- Sec. 46 (Deceiving in Weight): Selling goods at a weight less than what was offered.
    
- Sec. 47 (Faulty Weight Instrument): Using a weight stone or instrument that shows more than the actual weight.
    
- Sec. 48 (Deceiving in Measurement): Selling goods at a measurement less than what was offered.
    
- Sec. 49 (Faulty Measuring Gauge): Committing fraud with a measuring gauge used for length.
    
- Sec. 51 (Selling Expired Goods): Selling or offering to sell any goods or medicine that is past its expiry date.
    

---

### Tier 2: Increased Severity

This is a specific category for an illegal production process.

**Penalty:** Imprisonment up to **2 (two) years**, or a fine up to **Taka 1,00,000** (one lac), or both.

- Sec. 43 (Illegal Manufacturing Process): Manufacturing or processing goods in a way that is injurious to human health _and_ prohibited by law.
    

---

### Tier 3: High Fine

This offense has the standard jail time but a much higher fine, suggesting it's seen as a major economic deception.

**Penalty:** Imprisonment up to **1 (one) year**, or a fine up to **Taka 2,00,000** (two lacs), or both.

- Sec. 44 (False Advertising): Deceiving buyers with a false or untrue advertisement to sell a good or service.
    

---

### Tier 4: Most Severe

These are the offenses the law considers the most dangerous, as they pose a direct threat to the life and health of the consumer.

**Penalty:** Imprisonment up to **3 (three) years**, or a fine up to **Taka 2,00,000** (two lacs), or both.

- Sec. 41 (Adulteration): Knowingly selling or offering to sell any adulterated goods or medicine.
    
- Sec. 42 (Mixing Harmful Ingredients): Mixing any ingredient with foodstuff that is injurious to human life or health and is prohibited.
    
- Sec. 50 (Manufacturing Fake Goods): Making or manufacturing any fake goods.
    
- Sec. 52 (Acts Detrimental to Life): A service provider doing any act that is detrimental to the life or security of a service receiver (and violates a law).
    
- Sec. 53 (Damage by Negligence): A service provider damaging the money, health, or life of a service receiver through negligence, irresponsibility, or carelessness.
    

---

### 💡 Analysis: Why the Punishments are Different

This is the key to understanding the Act's logic:

1. **Why Tier 1 is less severe:** The "1 year / 50k" penalties are for offenses that are primarily **economic** or **informational**. If you are overcharged (Sec. 40) or cheated on weight (Sec. 46), you lose _money_. If a label is missing (Sec. 37) or a price list isn't shown (Sec. 38), you are denied _information_. While serious, the law doesn't treat this as severely as a direct threat to your life.
    
    - **Interesting Case:** Selling _expired goods_ (Sec. 51) is in this lowest tier, which is surprising. However, it's grouped with these other "standard" violations.
        
2. **Why Tier 4 is the most severe:** The "3 years / 200k" penalties are reserved for actions that can cause **direct physical harm, sickness, or death**.
    
    - **Adulteration** (Sec. 41), **harmful mixing** (Sec. 42), and **fake goods** (Sec. 50) (like fake medicine) can kill someone.
        
    - **Negligent service** (Sec. 53) or **acts detrimental to life** (Sec. 52) (e.g., in private healthcare or transport) also have the potential to directly kill or injure the consumer.
        

The law draws a clear line: cheating a consumer out of money is bad (Tier 1), but endangering a consumer's life is far worse (Tier 4).
