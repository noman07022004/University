

## Society ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the primary subject matter of the entire discipline. If you do not understand what "society" is, you cannot understand sociology. It is the container in which all other concepts (law, family, crime) exist.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** While simple definitions are common in short questions, essay questions often ask about the "Evolution of Society" (Lenski's types) or the relationship between "Man and Society."
    

---

### 1. Etymology and Conceptual Meaning

The term "Society" is derived from the Latin word **"Socius,"** which means companion, ally, or associate. In its most basic sense, it refers to a group of people who share a common territory and culture.

However, in sociology, society is not just a headcount of people. It is an abstract concept referring to the **relationships** between them. As the famous sociologist **R.M. MacIver** clarified: _"Society is not a group of people; it is the system of relationships that exists between the individuals of the group."_

If a group of people are sitting in a room but ignoring each other, they are a _population_, not a society. Society emerges only when there is reciprocal awareness and interaction.

### 2. Scholarly Definitions

To write a strong academic answer, you must quote key scholars.

- **MacIver and Page (The Gold Standard):**
    
    > "Society is the web of social relationships, and it is always changing."
    > 
    > This is the most cited definition. It emphasizes that society is a "process" or a "web," not a static object.
    
- **Ralph Linton:**
    
    > _"A society is any group of people who have lived and worked together long enough to get themselves organized and to think of themselves as a social unit."_
    
- **G.D.H. Cole:**
    
    > _"Society is the complex of organized associations and institutions with a community."_
    

### 3. Essential Characteristics of Society

Society is complex, but it relies on a few fundamental pillars. MacIver and Page identified these essential elements:

#### A. Likeness (Similarity)

Society begins with resemblance. If people were entirely different (like a man and a stone), no relationship could form.

- **Biological Likeness:** We are all humans with similar needs (food, shelter).
    
- **Psychological Likeness:** We share similar emotions and capacity for reason.
    
- _Relevance:_ "Birds of a feather flock together." This creates the initial bond of kinship and community.
    

#### B. Difference (Diversity)

While likeness brings us together, **difference** makes society functional. If everyone were exactly the same (e.g., everyone is a shoemaker), society would collapse because no one would grow food or cure the sick.

- **Division of Labor:** Society relies on people having different skills, ages, and genders to perform different roles.
    
- _Law/Sociology Link:_ Modern societies are held together by what Durkheim called **Organic Solidarity**—solidarity based on difference and interdependence (I need you because you do what I cannot).
    

#### C. Interdependence

This follows from "Difference." No individual is self-sufficient.

- The lawyer needs the farmer for food; the farmer needs the lawyer for legal protection. This web of mutual dependence is the structural "glue" of society.
    

#### D. Cooperation and Conflict

Society is not a utopia; it is a mixture of harmony and tension.

- **Cooperation:** Essential for survival (e.g., building a dam, running a government).
    
- **Conflict:** Inevitable due to limited resources. Paradoxically, conflict can strengthen society by forcing it to adapt and create better laws to resolve disputes.
    

### 4. Evolution of Society (Types of Society)

Sociologists like **Gerhard Lenski** classify societies based on their primary means of subsistence (how they use technology to survive). This evolutionary view is critical for understanding how laws and norms change over time.

1. **Hunting and Gathering Societies:**
    
    - _Economy:_ Foraging, hunting.
        
    - _Social Structure:_ Nomadic, small bands (20-40 people). Very little inequality.
        
    - _Law:_ No formal law; ruled by custom and group pressure.
        
2. **Horticultural and Pastoral Societies:**
    
    - _Economy:_ Hand tools for planting (Horticulture) or domestication of animals (Pastoral).
        
    - _Social Structure:_ Semi-permanent settlements. Surplus food allows for the first signs of inequality and chieftainships.
        
3. **Agrarian Societies (Traditional Bangladesh):**
    
    - _Economy:_ Large-scale farming using plows and animal labor.
        
    - _Social Structure:_ Massive food surpluses led to cities, empires, and rigid social classes (Kings vs. Peasants).
        
    - _Law:_ Emergence of written codes and brutal punishments to protect the property of the elite.
        
4. **Industrial Societies:**
    
    - _Economy:_ Machine production and factories.
        
    - _Social Structure:_ Urbanization, breakdown of extended families, rise of individualism.
        
    - _Law:_ Focus shifts to contract law, labor rights, and regulating commerce.
        
5. **Post-Industrial Societies:**
    
    - _Economy:_ Information, services, and high technology.
        
    - _Social Structure:_ Knowledge becomes the main commodity.
        
    - _Law:_ Focus on intellectual property, cyber law, and human rights.
        

### 5. Functional Prerequisites (What does a society need to survive?)

Scholars like **Aberle** list specific things a society _must_ do to avoid extinction. This is often called the "Functional Prerequisites":

1. **Replacement of Personnel:** People die; society must reproduce (Family).
    
2. **Teaching New Recruits:** Babies must be taught the culture (Socialization/Education).
    
3. **Production/Distribution of Goods:** People need to eat (Economy).
    
4. **Preservation of Order:** Preventing chaos and violence (Government/Law).
    
5. **Providing a Sense of Purpose:** Answering "why are we here?" (Religion/Values).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Web" Metaphor:** In any exam question asking "What is Society?", you _must_ use MacIver’s phrase **"Web of social relationships"**. It is the single most important phrase for this topic.
    
- **Man vs. Society:** You may be asked about the relationship between the individual and society. Mention two theories:
    
    - _Social Contract Theory (Locke/Rousseau):_ Individuals formed society to protect their rights (basis of modern democracy/law).
        
    - _Organic Theory (Spencer):_ Society is a natural organism that precedes the individual.
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** When discussing **"Agrarian Society"**, explicitly mention that Bangladesh is historically an agrarian society currently transitioning into an **"Industrializing Society"** (due to the RMG sector). This creates a unique "transitional society" where traditional rural norms conflict with modern urban laws.
