

## Social Process ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** While "Structure" explains what society _is_ (the anatomy), "Social Process" explains what society _does_ (the physiology). It explains dynamic interaction. Without this, sociology is just a static snapshot.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Highly probable topic. Examiners frequently ask for the "Distinction between Competition and Conflict" or to explain the "Process of Assimilation."
    

---

### 1. Definition and Concept

Society is not static; it is a flow of events. Just as a verb describes action in a sentence, **Social Process** describes action in society. It refers to the **repetitive forms of behavior** that occur when people interact.

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **MacIver:** _"Social process is the manner in which the relations of the members of a group, once brought together, acquire a certain distinctive character."_
    
- **Gillin and Gillin:** _"Social processes are the ways in which individuals and groups interact and establish relationships."_
    

The Core Idea:

Whenever people meet, they interact. This interaction falls into predictable patterns. sometimes they help each other (Cooperation), sometimes they fight (Conflict), and sometimes they agree to disagree (Accommodation). These patterns are the social processes.

### 2. Classification of Social Processes

Sociologists, particularly **Park and Burgess**, classify these processes into two broad categories based on their effect on social unity:

#### A. Associative (Conjunctive) Processes

These processes bind people together and promote solidarity.

1. **Cooperation**
    
2. **Accommodation**
    
3. **Assimilation**
    

#### B. Dissociative (Disjunctive) Processes

These processes pull people apart and create distance or division.

1. **Competition**
    
2. **Conflict**
    

---

### 3. The Associative Processes (Uniting Society)

#### 1. Cooperation

The most basic social process. It involves two or more people working together to achieve a common goal.

- **Types:**
    
    - _Direct:_ Doing the same task together (e.g., two people lifting a heavy log).
        
    - _Indirect:_ Doing different tasks for a common goal (e.g., Division of labor—Lawyer pleads, Judge decides, Clerk records).
        

#### 2. Accommodation (The Legal Favorite)

This is the process of getting along in spite of differences. When conflict becomes too painful or destructive, parties agree to stop fighting and find a way to coexist. It is a "state of truce."

- **Sumner's Term:** He called it **"Antagonistic Cooperation"**.
    
- **Forms:**
    
    - _Compromise:_ Both sides give up something (e.g., A labor union accepts a lower wage raise to end a strike).
        
    - _Toleration:_ Letting the other side exist without interference.
        
    - _Arbitration/Mediation:_ Using a third party to resolve the dispute (Crucial for Law).
        

#### 3. Assimilation

The final stage of association. It is a process of **fusion** where distinct groups merge their cultural identities into one.

- **Analogy:** The "Melting Pot."
    
- **Characteristics:** It is slow, gradual, and usually unconscious.
    
- **Example:** An immigrant moving to a new country, learning the language, adopting the dress, and eventually forgetting they were ever "foreign."
    

---

### 4. The Dissociative Processes (Dividing Society)

#### 1. Competition

An impersonal struggle for limited resources. Since there isn't enough money, power, or status for everyone, people compete.

- **Key Feature:** It is focused on the **Goal**, not the rival.
    
- **Rules:** It is regulated by norms (fair play).
    
- **Example:** Students writing an exam for the top rank. They are not trying to hurt each other; they are trying to hit the target.
    

#### 2. Conflict

A conscious, personal struggle to defeat an opponent.

- **Key Feature:** It is focused on the **Rival**. The goal is to neutralize, injure, or eliminate the opponent.
    
- **Nature:** It is intermittent (happens in bursts) and often violent.
    
- **Example:** War, riots, a street fight.
    

### 5. Competition vs. Conflict (Standard Exam Question)

|**Feature**|**Competition**|**Conflict**|
|---|---|---|
|**Focus**|Impersonal (Focus on the Prize).|Personal (Focus on the Enemy).|
|**Rules**|Follows rules (Normative).|Often breaks rules (Violent).|
|**Continuity**|Continuous (Market, Education).|Intermittent (Wars end, riots stop).|
|**Goal**|To surpass the rival.|To eliminate/weaken the rival.|
|**Nature**|Unconscious/Indirect.|Conscious/Direct.|

---

### 6. The Cycle of Social Interaction (Park and Burgess)

Robert Park (Chicago School of Sociology) argued that these processes often follow a cycle in race relations or migration:

1. **Contact:** Groups meet.
    
2. **Competition:** They struggle for resources (jobs, housing).
    
3. **Conflict:** Tension rises, leading to violence/riots.
    
4. **Accommodation:** They realize fighting is costly and sign treaties/laws to coexist.
    
5. **Assimilation:** Over generations, they merge into one society.
    

### 7. Bangladeshi Context & Legal Relevance

- **ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution):** As a law student, you should know that **Accommodation** is the sociological basis of _Mediation_ and _Arbitration_. The Bangladeshi legal system is heavily promoting ADR to reduce the backlog of cases (e.g., Village Courts).
    
- **Political Process:** In Bangladesh, politics often fluctuates between _Competition_ (Elections, which have rules) and _Conflict_ (Hartals, clashes, where rules are broken). Sociology helps analyze why this shift happens (breakdown of accommodation).
    
- **Tribal Issues:** The situation in the Chittagong Hill Tracts is often analyzed through the lens of **Assimilation vs. Accommodation**. The tribal groups (Chakma, Marma) often resist _Assimilation_ (losing their culture) and prefer _Accommodation_ (peace accord with autonomy).
    

---

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Park" Cycle:** If writing an essay on ethnic relations or social change, drawing the **Competition → Conflict → Accommodation → Assimilation** cycle will get you extra marks for theoretical depth.
    
- **Conflict is Function:** Don't just say "Conflict is bad." Mention **Georg Simmel** or **Coser**, who argued that conflict can be _functional_—it binds the "In-group" together (e.g., a nation becomes very united during a war).
    
- **Legal Distinction:** In a problem question, if two parties are suing each other in court, is it Conflict or Competition?
    
    - _Answer:_ It is **Litigation**, which is a highly regulated form of **Conflict** that has been institutionalized (turned into a procedure) to prevent violence. It sits on the border of competition and conflict.
