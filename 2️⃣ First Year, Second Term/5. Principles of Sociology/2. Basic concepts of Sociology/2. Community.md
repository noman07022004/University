

## Community ⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** Understanding "Community" is essential because it represents the natural, organic bonds of human life, as opposed to the artificial bonds of an "Association" (like a corporation).
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A very frequent short question or distinction question (e.g., "Difference between Society and Community" or "Is a prison a Community?").
    

---

### 1. Definition and Meaning

The term "Community" is derived from two Latin words: _Com_ (together) and _Munis_ (to serve). Ideally, it means "serving together."

In sociology, a community is a collection of people who live in a defined geographical area and share a sense of **"We-feeling"** (belonging). It is not formed for a specific purpose; rather, it encompasses the _totality_ of life. You don't just work there; you live, celebrate, and die there.

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **MacIver and Page:** _"Whenever the members of any group, small or large, live together in such a way that they share, not this or that particular interest, but the basic conditions of a common life, we call that group a community."_
    
- **Kingsley Davis:** _"Community is the smallest territorial group that can embrace all aspects of social life."_
    

### 2. Essential Elements of Community

For a group to be strictly defined as a community in the sociological sense, it must possess two fundamental elements:

#### A. Locality (Definite Territory)

A community always occupies a specific geographic area. A group of people without a home cannot form a community (though nomadic tribes are an exception, they move _as_ a community).

- _Example:_ A village, a city, or a tribe.
    
- _Modern Note:_ While sociologists debate "Digital Communities," classical sociology requires physical proximity.
    

#### B. Community Sentiment (We-Feeling)

This is the psychological element. Members must feel a sense of identification with the group. "This is _my_ village; these are _my_ people."

- **Role-Feeling:** Each person feels they have a role to play.
    
- **Dependency:** Members feel they depend on the community for their physical and psychological needs.
    

### 3. Tönnies’ Classification: Gemeinschaft vs. Gesellschaft

To get top marks, you must mention the German sociologist **Ferdinand Tönnies** (1887). He distinguished between two types of social organization, which perfectly explains the shift from traditional to modern life.

1. **Gemeinschaft (Community):**
    
    - Based on personal, intimate, and traditional bonds.
        
    - Relationships are an end in themselves (friendship, family).
        
    - _Example:_ A rural Bangladeshi village.
        
2. **Gesellschaft (Association/Society):**
    
    - Based on impersonal, formal, and instrumental bonds.
        
    - Relationships are a means to an end (business, contracts).
        
    - _Example:_ A corporate office in Dhaka.
        

### 4. Community vs. Society

Examiners often ask for the distinction. Here is the academic breakdown:

|**Feature**|**Community**|**Society**|
|---|---|---|
|**Scope**|Narrower. A community is a _part_ of society.|Broader. Society includes many communities.|
|**Locality**|Essential. Must have a definite territory.|Not essential. Society is a web of relationships, which can be global.|
|**Nature of Sentiment**|Intense "We-feeling."|Can be impersonal (e.g., relationship with a shopkeeper).|
|**Concrete/Abstract**|Concrete (you can see a village).|Abstract (you cannot "see" society, only the people).|

### 5. The "Borderline" Cases (Exam Favorites)

Sociologists often debate whether certain groups are communities. These are standard exam "trick" questions.

- **Is a Prison or Asylum a Community?**
    
    - _Argument:_ They have a definite locality and people live there together.
        
    - _Verdict:_ **No.** They lack the spontaneous growth and "We-feeling." People are forced to be there; they don't choose it, nor do they usually feel a sense of belonging. MacIver calls them "Institutions," not communities.
        
- **Is a Caste a Community?**
    
    - _Verdict:_ **Yes and No.** It has the "We-feeling," but it lacks a specific "Locality" (members of a caste are spread across the country). Therefore, strict sociologists say it is a _social group_, not a community.
        

### 6. Bangladeshi Context: The "Samaj"

In Bangladesh, the concept of community is best represented by the traditional rural institution known as the **Samaj**.

- **What is it?** The _Samaj_ is an informal, traditional social organization in rural Bangladesh based on kinship and territory.
    
- **Function:** It performs the functions of a community—resolving disputes (Shalish), organizing religious festivals, and punishing deviance (social boycott/Huka bondha).
    
- **Legal Relevance:** For a lawyer in Bangladesh, understanding the _Samaj_ is vital because it often acts as a parallel legal system in villages, sometimes enforcing norms that contradict state laws (e.g., fatwas).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Keyword:** Always use the term **"We-feeling"**. It is the literal translation of the sociological term used by MacIver.
    
- **Distinction Tip:** If asked to differentiate between "Community" and "Association," remember: You are _born_ into a community (usually), but you _join_ an association. Community has no specific aim; Association has a specific aim.
    
- **Modern Context:** You can briefly mention that in the era of the Internet, the requirement of "Locality" is fading. We now have "Virtual Communities" (Facebook groups, Reddit), where "We-feeling" exists without physical proximity. This shows critical thinking.
