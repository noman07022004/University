
## Definition of Sociology ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** You cannot master the subject without a precise grasp of what it is (and what it is not). This defines the boundaries of your study.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** While direct "define sociology" questions are basic, they are common in short questions or quizzes. More importantly, in essay questions, you _must_ start your introduction with a strong, scholarly definition to score high marks.
    

---

### 1. Etymological Definition (The Origin)

The starting point of any academic definition is its etymology. The term "Sociology" is a hybrid term, coined in **1839** (some sources say 1838) by the French philosopher **Auguste Comte** in his series _Cours de Philosophie Positive_.

- **Latin Root:** _Socius_, meaning "companion," "associate," or "society."
    
- **Greek Root:** _Logos_, meaning "study of," "science," or "word."
    

Literally, Sociology translates to the **"Science of Society"** or the study of companionship. Comte originally wanted to use the term "Social Physics" to align it with hard sciences, but that term was already being used by a statistician named Adolphe Quetelet, so Comte pivoted to "Sociology."

### 2. Classical Scholarly Definitions

To demonstrate a deep understanding, you must move beyond the literal meaning and analyze how the "founding fathers" defined the discipline. Each definition reveals a different theoretical focus.

#### A. Auguste Comte: The Science of Social Order and Progress

Comte defined sociology as the scientific study of society. He divided it into two parts:

- **Social Statics:** The study of the forces that hold society together (stability/order).
    
- **Social Dynamics:** The study of the causes of social change (progress/evolution).
    

#### B. Émile Durkheim: The Study of Social Facts

Durkheim, who established sociology as a formal academic discipline, argued that sociology should not study individual minds (psychology) but distinct "social" things.

- **Definition:** Sociology is the "science of institutions, their genesis and their functioning."
    
- **Key Concept:** He focused on **Social Facts**—patterns of acting, thinking, and feeling that exist _outside_ the individual and exert _coercive power_ over them (e.g., laws, morals, religious beliefs).
    

#### C. Max Weber: The Study of Social Action

Weber took a different approach, focusing on the subjective meaning individuals give to their actions.

- **Definition:** Sociology is "a science which attempts the interpretive understanding of **social action** in order thereby to arrive at a causal explanation of its course and effects."
    
- **Key Concept:** An action is "social" only if the actor attaches a meaning to it and takes into account the behavior of others. (e.g., Two cyclists crashing is a physical event; two cyclists trying to avoid each other is a _social action_).
    

#### D. Karl Marx: The Study of Class Struggle (Implicit Definition)

While Marx didn't write a formal "textbook definition," his approach defines sociology as the analysis of **class conflict** and material conditions. He viewed society as a dynamic entity driven by the struggle between those who own the means of production (Bourgeoisie) and those who work (Proletariat).

### 3. Modern and Comprehensive Definitions

As the discipline evolved, definitions became more inclusive.

- **MacIver and Page:** "Sociology is 'about' social relationships, the network of relationships we call society." This definition emphasizes the _connections_ between people rather than the people themselves.
    
- **Anthony Giddens (Contemporary):** "Sociology is the study of human social life, groups, and societies. It is a dazzling and compelling enterprise, having as its subject matter our own behavior as social beings."
    
- **Scientific Definition:** Sociology is the **systematic** and **scientific** study of human social behavior, social groups, and society. It focuses on the influence of social relationships on people's attitudes and behaviors and how societies are established and change.
    

### 4. A Synthesized Definition for Legal Studies

Since your rationale is built on legal studies, here is a synthesized definition you can use in introductions:

> "Sociology is the scientific study of the structure, development, and functioning of human society. It investigates how social forces—such as institutions, culture, and stratification—shape individual actions and collective outcomes, providing the necessary context for understanding the creation and application of law."

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **The "Comte" Trap:** In exams, students often just say "Comte coined it." To get full marks, mention **1839** and his division into **Social Statics** and **Social Dynamics**.
    
- **Quote Accuracy:** You don't need to memorize long definitions word-for-word, but memorizing **Weber's "Social Action"** and **Durkheim's "Social Facts"** is crucial. These are keywords examiners look for.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** If asked to define sociology in the context of Bangladesh, you can mention that sociology here often involves the "study of the transition from agrarian tradition to modernization," as defined by scholars like **A.K. Nazmul Karim**.
    
- **Comparison:** If an exam question asks "What is Sociology?", do not just give one line. Give the etymological meaning first, then 2-3 scholarly definitions (Weber/Durkheim), and conclude with a general modern definition.
