## Social Change in Global Perspective ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Sociology was born out of the need to understand the massive changes of the Industrial Revolution. Today, understanding "Change" is essential to grasp why laws become outdated and how societies evolve from traditional to modern.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A frequent area for essay questions. Examiners love to ask about "Technological Determinism" or "The impact of Urbanization on the Bangladeshi family."
    

---

### 1. Meaning and Definition

Society is never static; it is like a flowing river. **Social Change** refers to the alteration in the social order of a society. It includes changes in social institutions, social behaviors, and social relations.

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **Kingsley Davis:** _"By social change is meant only such alterations as occur in social organization, that is, structure and functions of society."_
    
- **MacIver and Page:** _"Social change refers to a process responsive to many types of changes; to changes in the man-made conditions of life; to changes in the attitudes and beliefs of men, and to the changes that go beyond the human control to the biological and the physical nature of things."_
    

**Nature of Change:**

1. **Universal:** It happens everywhere.
    
2. **Continuous:** It never stops.
    
3. **Inevitable:** You cannot stop it.
    
4. **Temporal:** It happens through time.
    
5. **Degree:** The speed varies (Revolution = Fast; Evolution = Slow).
    

### 2. Factors of Social Change

What drives this river of change? Sociologists identify several key "engines" or factors.

#### A. Technological Factors (The Primary Engine)

Technology is often seen as the most powerful agent of social change. This view is known as **Technological Determinism** (associated with **Thorstein Veblen** and **W.F. Ogburn**).

- **Mechanism:** An invention (e.g., the Steam Engine, Internet) changes the _Material Culture_, which forces the _Non-Material Culture_ (habits, laws) to adapt.
    
- **Example:** The invention of **Contraceptives** (Technology) fundamentally changed sexual morality and the status of women (Social Structure), allowing them to control reproduction and enter the workforce.
    
- **In Bangladesh:** The **Mobile Phone** changed rural banking (bKash), market access for farmers, and communication patterns.
    

#### B. Economic Factors (Marxian View)

Karl Marx argued that the "Economy" is the base of society.

- **Mechanism:** When the mode of production changes (e.g., Agriculture to Industry), the entire social superstructure (Family, Law, Religion) changes.
    
- **Example:** The shift from feudalism to capitalism destroyed the joint family and created the nuclear family.
    

#### C. Demographic Factors

Population size and composition drive change.

- **Mechanism:** High birth rates or migration force changes in housing, law, and economy.
    
- **Example:** Bangladesh's "Demographic Dividend" (large youth population) is driving economic growth but also political instability due to unemployment.
    

### 3. Urbanization, Industrialization, and Social Change

These two processes are "twins" that usually happen together, transforming traditional societies into modern ones.

#### A. Industrialization (The Cause)

The process of shifting from hand tools to power-driven machinery.

- **Impact:**
    
    - **Work:** Work moves from the home (cottage industry) to the factory.
        
    - **Time:** Life becomes regulated by the clock, not the sun.
        
    - **Stratification:** Creates new classes (Capitalists vs. Workers).
        

#### B. Urbanization (The Effect)

The movement of population from rural to urban areas.

- **Impact on Family:** Breakdown of the Joint Family; rise of the Nuclear Family.
    
- **Impact on Social Control:** "Shalish" (village court) loses power; formal Police/Courts gain power. Anonymity in cities leads to higher crime rates.
    
- **Impact on Women:** Urbanization often emancipates women, giving them access to education and jobs (e.g., Garment workers in Dhaka).
    

### 4. Resistance to Social Change

Why do societies sometimes refuse to change?

1. **Habit and Inertia:** People prefer the familiar. "We have always done it this way."
    
2. **Vested Interests:** Those who benefit from the current system oppose change. (e.g., Landlords opposed land reform laws).
    
3. **Religious Fundamentalism:** Change is often seen as a threat to sacred traditions.
    
4. **Fear of the Unknown:** The psychological fear that the new way might be worse.
    

### 5. Theories of Social Change (The Pattern)

How does change happen?

1. **Linear Theory (Evolutionary):** Society moves in a straight line from "primitive" to "civilized" (Comte, Spencer). It assumes change is always _progress_.
    
2. **Cyclical Theory:** Society rises and falls like a human life cycle (Birth → Maturity → Death). Civilizations grow and decay (Spengler, Toynbee).
    
    - _Sorokin's Pendulum:_ Society swings between **Sensate** (Materialistic) and **Ideational** (Spiritual) cultures.
        

### 6. The Bangladeshi Context

Bangladesh is a laboratory for observing social change.

- **Digital Bangladesh:** A state-led initiative using **Technology** to drive social change (e-governance, digitalization of land records).
    
- **The "Silent Revolution":** The entry of women into the workforce (RMG sector) is the single biggest social change factor, altering marriage ages, fertility rates, and family power dynamics.
    
- **Urban Chaos:** Rapid **Urbanization** in Dhaka without adequate planning (Cultural Lag) has led to social problems like slum proliferation and traffic congestion.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Veblen's Term:** Memorize the term **"Technological Determinism."** It means technology dictates social structure. Use this when discussing the Internet or AI.
    
- **Ogburn's Lag:** Re-use the **Cultural Lag** theory here. It explains _why_ technological change causes social problems (the lag period).
    
- **Marx vs. Weber:**
    
    - _Marx:_ Economy causes change.
        
    - _Weber:_ Ideas (Religion) cause change (e.g., Protestant Ethic caused Capitalism).
        
- **Legal Link:** Law is an instrument of **Social Engineering**. It can _force_ social change (e.g., banning Child Marriage to force a change in social norms).
