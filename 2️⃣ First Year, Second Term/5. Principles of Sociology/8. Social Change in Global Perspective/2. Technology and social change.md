
## Technology and Social Change ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** We live in the "Information Age." You cannot understand modern society (or modern law, like Cyber Law) without understanding how technology reshapes human interaction.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A very popular topic. Examiners often ask questions like _"Critically examine the role of technology in social change"_ or _"Discuss the concept of the Global Village."_
    

---

### 1. Technology: The Primary Engine of Change

In sociology, **Technology** is not just about smartphones and computers. It refers to the **application of scientific knowledge** to the making of tools to solve specific problems. It includes everything from the invention of the wheel and the plow to Artificial Intelligence (AI).

**The Sociological View:** Technology is often viewed as the "prime mover" of history. It is the variable that changes _first_, forcing the rest of society (laws, families, religions) to adapt.

### 2. Theory of Technological Determinism

To write an academic answer, you must center your argument on the theory of **Technological Determinism**.

- **Origin:** Associated with **Thorstein Veblen** (who coined the term) and **W.F. Ogburn**.
    
- **The Argument:** Technology is the single most important factor in social change. It determines the structure of society.
    
    - _If you have a hand-mill, you will have a feudal society._
        
    - _If you have a steam-mill, you will have an industrial capitalist society._ (Karl Marx).
        
- **Mechanism:**
    
    1. **Invention:** A new tool is created (e.g., The Steam Engine).
        
    2. **Application:** It changes how we work (Factories replace farms).
        
    3. **Social Adaptation:** Society changes to fit the work (Urbanization, Nuclear Families, Labor Laws).
        

### 3. Major Areas of Impact

Technology transforms society through three major channels:

#### A. Industrialization (The Factory System)

The shift from animal/human power to machine power.

- **Impact on Work:** Work moved from the "Home" (Cottage Industry) to the "Factory." This separated family life from work life for the first time in history.
    
- **Impact on Women:** It initially trapped women in domestic roles but later (through light machinery and computers) allowed them to enter the workforce, changing gender roles.
    
- **Impact on Stratification:** It created two distinct classes: the **Bourgeoisie** (owners of technology) and the **Proletariat** (users of technology).
    

#### B. Urbanization (The City)

Technology mechanized agriculture (tractors, harvesters), meaning fewer people were needed to farm.

- **Result:** Mass migration from villages to cities.
    
- **Social Change:** This destroyed the "Face-to-Face" community (_Gemeinschaft_) and replaced it with the anonymous, impersonal city life (_Gesellschaft_).
    

#### C. Communication and Transport (The Global Village)

The invention of the Train, Telegraph, Internet, and Smartphone.

- **Marshall McLuhan’s Concept:** He coined the term **"Global Village."** Technology has "shrunk" the world. An event in New York is experienced instantly in Dhaka.
    
- **Impact:** It breaks down isolation. Ideas (democracy, feminism, human rights) spread instantly, challenging local traditions and dictatorships.
    

### 4. The Information Age and Cyberspace

We are currently living through the **Digital Revolution**.

- **The Network Society (Manuel Castells):** Society is no longer organized around "physical groups" but around "digital networks."
    
- **Virtual Communities:** We now have communities that exist only online (Facebook groups, Reddit). This challenges the traditional definition of "Community" (which required locality).
    
- **Surveillance:** Technology has given the State (and corporations) unprecedented power to watch citizens, raising huge legal questions about **Privacy**.
    

### 5. Negative Impacts (The Shadow Side)

Technology is not always positive.

1. **Alienation (Marx):** The worker becomes a "cog in the machine," disconnected from the product they make.
    
2. **Technological Unemployment:** Automation and AI threaten to replace human jobs, potentially creating a "useless class" (Yuval Noah Harari).
    
3. **The Digital Divide:** The gap between those who have internet access (Rich) and those who don't (Poor). This creates a new form of inequality.
    

### 6. The Bangladeshi Context

Bangladesh is a prime example of technology-driven change:

- **Agriculture (The Green Revolution):** The introduction of High-Yield Variety (HYV) seeds and irrigation pumps in the 1970s/80s transformed Bangladesh from a starving nation to a food-secure one.
    
- **Mobile Financial Services (bKash/Nagad):** A classic example of "Leapfrogging." Rural Bangladeshis who never had a bank account suddenly entered the formal economy through mobile phones. This empowered rural women and small traders.
    
- **The RMG Sector:** The import of simple sewing technology created millions of jobs for women, altering the patriarchal structure of families.
    
- **Digital Bangladesh:** The digitization of land records and government services (e-governance) is slowly breaking the traditional bureaucratic "red tape" and corruption.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Veblen is Vital:** You _must_ mention **Thorstein Veblen** when discussing Technological Determinism.
    
- **McLuhan's Quote:** "The medium is the message." Use this when discussing the Internet. The _way_ we communicate (social media) shapes us more than _what_ we say.
    
- **Legal Application:** This is huge for law students.
    
    - **Cyber Law:** The **Digital Security Act** (now Cyber Security Act) is a direct legal response to technological change.
        
    - **Evidence:** Courts now have to accept "Digital Evidence" (screenshots, recordings), changing the Law of Evidence.
        
- **Essay Structure:** If asked "Does technology cause social change?", argue **YES** (Determinism) but add a critique that **Culture** also shapes how we _use_ technology (e.g., We use nuclear tech for energy or bombs based on our values).
