

## Meaning, Characteristics, and Function of Culture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Culture is the fundamental differentiator between human society and animal herds. A deep grasp of this topic is required to answer _any_ sociological question about why people behave differently in different places.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a "bread and butter" question. You can expect a direct question like: _"Define Culture. Discuss its major characteristics and its role in molding human personality."_
    

---

### 1. Meaning and Etymology

To understand culture academically, we must first strip away its common usage. In daily life, saying someone is "cultured" implies they like classical music, art, or literature (often called **High Culture**). In Sociology and Anthropology, however, "Culture" includes **everything**. A poor farmer in a remote village has just as much "culture" as a wealthy professor in Dhaka; the content is just different.

Etymology:

The word "Culture" is derived from the Latin word "Colere," which means "to cultivate" or "to till the soil."

- _Historical Context:_ Just as agriculture (agriculture = _ager_ + _cultura_) involves improving the land to grow crops, "Culture" was originally seen as the "cultivation of the human mind."
    
- _Modern Meaning:_ It refers to the total way of life—the software of the human mind that tells us how to survive, interact, and find meaning.
    

### 2. Scholarly Definitions

You must be able to cite at least two definitions to show academic rigor.

- **E.B. Tylor (The Classical Definition, 1871):**
    
    > _"Culture is that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society."_
    
    - _Why this matters:_ This was the first scientific definition. It emphasized that culture is **acquired** (learned), not biological.
        
- **Bronislaw Malinowski (The Functional Definition):**
    
    > _"Culture is the handiwork of man and the medium through which he achieves his ends."_
    
    - _Why this matters:_ It emphasizes that culture is a **tool** humans use to survive (e.g., we build houses to survive the weather).
        
- **Robert Bierstedt:**
    
    > _"Culture is the complex whole that consists of all the ways we think and do and everything we have as members of society."_
    

### 3. Characteristics of Culture

To write a high-scoring answer, expand on these specific characteristics. Culture is:

1. **Learned, not Innate:**
    
    - We are not born with culture. A Bangladeshi baby adopted by a French family will grow up speaking French and eating cheese, not Bengali and rice. This learning process is called _Socialization_.
        
2. **Social, not Individual:**
    
    - Culture does not exist in isolation. One person cannot create a culture alone. It originates and develops through social interaction.
        
3. **Shared:**
    
    - Culture is shared by a group of people. For example, the custom of touching elders' feet is shared by millions in South Asia; it is not just one person's habit.
        
4. **Transmissive:**
    
    - Culture is passed from one generation to the next. The primary vehicle for this transmission is **Language**. Without language, culture would die with the current generation.
        
5. **Cumulative:**
    
    - Knowledge accumulates over time. We don't have to reinvent the wheel or electricity; we build upon the culture passed down by ancestors. This is often called the "Social Heritage."
        
6. **Dynamic and Adaptive:**
    
    - Culture is not static. It changes to fit new environments.
        
    - _Example:_ When Bangladeshis move to colder climates, their "clothing culture" changes from cotton Panjabis to heavy jackets.
        
7. **Idealistic:**
    
    - Culture embodies the ideals and norms of a group. It sets the standard for what is "perfect" or "correct" behavior, even if people don't always follow it perfectly.
        

### 4. Functions of Culture

Why is culture necessary? It serves crucial functions for both the **Individual** and the **Group**.

#### A. For the Individual

1. **Differentiation from Animals:**
    
    - Animals rely on instinct. Humans have very few instincts; we rely on culture to tell us how to survive. Culture makes us human.
        
2. **Molds Personality:**
    
    - Culture shapes who we are. A child raised in a strict, religious culture develops a different personality (superego) than a child raised in a liberal, secular culture.
        
3. **Provides Solutions to Life’s Problems:**
    
    - Culture provides a "ready-made" set of answers. You don't have to figure out "How do I get married?" or "How do I bury the dead?" Culture provides the ritual and the method.
        

#### B. For the Group (Society)

1. **Social Solidarity (Unity):**
    
    - Sharing the same language, gods, and festivals creates a deep sense of "We-feeling." It binds the nation together.
        
2. **Social Control:**
    
    - Culture regulates behavior. Through norms, customs, and laws, culture tells people what is right and wrong, maintaining order without the constant need for police force.
        
3. **Continuity of Society:**
    
    - Culture serves as the "memory" of society. By passing down traditions, it ensures that the society survives long after the individuals die.
        

### 5. Additional Critical Concepts (For Context)

To demonstrate a "complete understanding" as requested, you should briefly understand how we _view_ culture.

- **Ethnocentrism:** The tendency to judge other cultures by the standards of one's own culture (e.g., "My culture is the best; their food is weird"). This often leads to conflict.
    
- **Cultural Relativism:** The sociological principle that a culture should be judged by _its own_ standards, not ours. (e.g., "They eat insects not because they are 'gross,' but because it provides protein in their environment").
    
    - _Legal Relevance:_ A lawyer must possess Cultural Relativism to understand a client's background without bias.
        

### 6. Bangladeshi Context

- **The "Salaam" Example:** In Bangladesh, the exchange of "Salaam" is a cultural trait. It is _Learned_ (parents teach kids), _Shared_ (most people do it), _Transmissive_ (passed down for centuries), and serves the _Function_ of establishing social peace and hierarchy.
    
- **Adaptation:** Bangladeshi culture is currently adapting to globalization. We see the fusion of "traditional culture" (Pitha Utsab) with "global culture" (Valentine's Day).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Essay Structure:** If asked "What is Culture?", do not just write a paragraph. Use the structure:
    
    1. **Etymology** (Colere)
        
    2. **Definition** (Tylor)
        
    3. **Characteristics** (Bullet points)
        
    4. **Functions** (Individual vs Group)
        
    5. **Conclusion**
        
- **Quote W.I. Thomas:** When discussing functions, you can mention that culture **"Defines the Situation."** It tells us how to interpret a smile, a handshake, or a threat.
    
- **Nature vs. Nurture:** This topic is essentially the "Nurture" side of the famous debate. Culture is the "Nurture."
