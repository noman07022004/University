

## Culture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Culture is the software of the human mind. It distinguishes humans from animals. You cannot understand human behavior, law, or society without understanding the culture that shapes them.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A high-priority topic. "Define Culture and discuss its elements" is a standard question. The "Theory of Cultural Lag" is a specific favorite for short notes or problem questions explaining social friction.
    

---

### 1. Meaning and Definition

In everyday conversation, "culture" often means "sophistication" (e.g., liking opera or art). In sociology, however, **Culture** is a much broader concept. It refers to the **entire way of life** of a group of people.

It includes everything a society creates—both physical objects (like smartphones) and ideas (like democracy). It is the social heritage transmitted from one generation to another.

Classic Scholarly Definition (Must Memorize):

The most famous definition was given by the anthropologist E.B. Tylor in his book Primitive Culture (1871):

> _"Culture is that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society."_

**Key Concept:** Culture is not biological (it’s not in our genes); it is **acquired** and **learned**.

### 2. Characteristics of Culture

To distinguish culture from instinct, sociologists identify several key traits:

1. **Culture is Learned:** No one is born with culture. You learn your language, religion, and table manners through _socialization_.
    
2. **Culture is Shared:** It is not an individual habit (that’s an idiosyncrasy). It is shared by a group (e.g., eating rice with hands is a shared Bangladeshi habit).
    
3. **Culture is Transmissive:** It is passed down from generation to generation via language and symbols.
    
4. **Culture is Adaptive:** It changes over time to help humans survive in their environment (e.g., humans in the Arctic developed igloo culture; humans in Bengal developed river-boat culture).
    
5. **Culture is Varying:** It differs from society to society (Cultural Diversity).
    

### 3. The Elements of Culture

Sociologists divide the "complex whole" of culture into two main categories: **Material** and **Non-Material**.

#### A. Material Culture

These are the physical, tangible objects created by members of a society.

- _Examples:_ Tools, buildings, jewelry, computers, rickshaws, the Supreme Court building.
    
- _Significance:_ These objects reflect the technology and environment of the society.
    

#### B. Non-Material Culture

These are the abstract, intangible ideas and beliefs.

- **1. Symbols:** Anything that carries a specific meaning recognized by people who share the culture (e.g., The National Flag, a Red Light, a Lawyer’s Gown).
    
- **2. Language:** The system of symbols that allows people to communicate and transmit culture. It is the "storehouse of culture."
    
- **3. Values:** Abstract standards of what is good/bad (e.g., Democracy, Honesty).
    
- **4. Norms:** Specific rules of conduct (e.g., Laws, Mores, Folkways).
    
- **5. Beliefs:** Specific statements that people hold to be true (e.g., "Education leads to success," or religious beliefs).
    

### 4. Functions of Culture

Why do we need culture?

1. **Distinguishes Humans from Animals:** Animals rely on instinct; humans rely on culture to survive.
    
2. **Provides the "Blueprint" for Living:** It saves us from having to figure everything out from scratch. We know how to greet, eat, and dress because culture tells us.
    
3. **Creates Social Solidarity:** Sharing a culture (language, festivals like _Pohela Boishakh_) creates a strong sense of "We-feeling" and unity.
    
4. **Social Control:** Culture shapes our conscience. We obey laws not just because of police, but because our culture taught us it is "wrong" to steal.
    

### 5. Theory of Cultural Lag (Ogburn)

This is the most critical theoretical concept in this section. Introduced by **William F. Ogburn** in his book _Social Change_ (1922).

The Core Argument:

Culture changes, but not all parts change at the same speed.

- **Material Culture** (technology/economy) usually changes **fast**.
    
- **Non-Material Culture** (laws/religion/values) usually changes **slowly** because people are conservative and resist changing their traditions.
    

The Lag:

The gap between the fast-changing technology and the slow-changing social values is called Cultural Lag. This lag creates social problems and conflict.

**Legal/Bangladeshi Examples:**

1. **Cyber Crime:** Technology (Internet/AI) arrived quickly (Material). But our Laws and Policing methods (Non-material) took years to catch up. During this "Lag" period, cybercrime flourished.
    
2. **Women's Employment:** Industrialization brought women into the garment factories (Material change). But the patriarchal values that "women should stay home" (Non-material) changed much slower, leading to conflict and domestic violence against working women.
    

### 6. Culture vs. Civilization

In common language, these words are synonyms. In sociology (specifically the **MacIver and Page** school), they are distinct opposites.

**The Distinction:**

1. **Civilization is what we HAVE; Culture is what we ARE.**
    
    - _Civilization:_ Our tools, technology, and external achievements (Cars, Banks, Laws).
        
    - _Culture:_ Our values, art, and internal character (Poetry, Philosophy, Ethics).
        
2. **Measurement:**
    
    - Civilization can be measured (A faster car is clearly better than a slow one).
        
    - Culture cannot be measured (Is Picasso's art "better" than Michelangelo's? Is Buddhism "better" than Islam? No, they are just different).
        
3. **Transmission:**
    
    - Civilization is easily transferred (You can sell a Bangladeshi shirt to America easily).
        
    - Culture is hard to transfer (You cannot easily "export" the Bangladeshi family sentiment to America).
        
4. **Progression:**
    
    - Civilization always advances (Old computers are replaced by new ones).
        
    - Culture does not necessarily "advance" (We are not necessarily "morally better" than our ancestors, just different).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Tylor's Definition:** I cannot stress this enough: Memorize **E.B. Tylor's** definition. It is the gold standard for any exam answer on culture.
    
- **Ogburn's Lag:** If asked about "Social Problems in Bangladesh," use the **Cultural Lag** theory. Explain that problems like "Road Accidents" happen because we have modern cars (Material) but still have a traditional mindset/disregard for traffic rules (Non-material lag).
    
- **Law as Culture:** For law students, remember that Law is a part of **Non-Material Culture** (specifically, institutionalized norms). However, legal _infrastructure_ (Courts, Prisons) is Material Culture.
    
- **Bangladeshi Application:** Use the example of **Pohela Boishakh**. It is a perfect example of _Non-Material Culture_ (values, music) that binds the nation together, yet it uses _Material Culture_ (masks, clay pots, traditional dress) to express it.
