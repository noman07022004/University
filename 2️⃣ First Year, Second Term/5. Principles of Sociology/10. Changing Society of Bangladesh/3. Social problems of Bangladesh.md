## Social Problems of Bangladesh ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** As a future lawyer or leader, your career will essentially be about solving these problems. Understanding their root causes (not just symptoms) is essential for effective advocacy and policy-making.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most practical section. Examiners frequently ask for an analysis of a specific problem (e.g., _"Discuss the causes and consequences of unemployment/drug addiction in Bangladesh"_).
    

---

### 1. Definition and Nature

A "Problem" becomes a **Social Problem** when it affects a significant number of people and is perceived as a threat to the established social values, requiring collective action to solve.

**Scholarly Definition (Fuller and Myers):**

> _"A social problem is a condition which is defined by a considerable number of persons as a deviation from some social norm which they cherish."_

**Characteristics in Bangladesh:**

1. **Interconnectedness:** You cannot solve one without the others. Poverty causes Illiteracy; Illiteracy causes Unemployment; Unemployment causes Crime. It is a vicious cycle.
    
2. **Structural Origins:** Most problems in Bangladesh are not due to "bad individuals" but due to "bad structures" (e.g., corruption is built into the bureaucracy).
    

---

### 2. Theoretical Analysis: Why do we have so many problems?

Sociologists analyze the _root causes_ in Bangladesh using three main theories:

1. **Transition and Anomie (Durkheim):** Bangladesh is moving rapidly from an Agrarian society to an Industrial one. Old village norms are dying, but new urban laws are not yet fully respected. This state of normlessness (**Anomie**) leads to corruption and crime.
    
2. **Cultural Lag (Ogburn):** We have modern technology (Internet/Drugs/Weapons) but traditional institutions (Family/Police) that cannot keep up with regulating them.
    
3. **Resource Constraints:** High population density on a small landmass creates fierce competition for limited resources (land, jobs), leading to conflict.
    

---

### 3. Major Social Problems of Bangladesh

#### A. Poverty and Inequality (The Root)

While absolute poverty has decreased, **Relative Inequality** (the gap between rich and poor) has skyrocketed.

- **Nature:** It is not just a lack of money; it is **Social Exclusion**. The poor are excluded from justice, quality healthcare, and decision-making.
    
- **The Vicious Cycle:** Poor health $\rightarrow$ Low productivity $\rightarrow$ Low income $\rightarrow$ Poor education $\rightarrow$ Poor health.
    
- **Urbanization of Poverty:** As the landless move to cities, poverty is shifting from villages to urban slums (_Bastees_).
    

#### B. Population Pressure

Bangladesh is one of the most densely populated countries in the world.

- **The Issue:** Although the fertility rate has dropped (a success story), the sheer _momentum_ of the population puts immense pressure on land, housing, and the environment.
    
- **The "Youth Bulge":** We have a massive young population. If they find jobs, it is a **Demographic Dividend** (economic boom). If they do not, it becomes a **Demographic Disaster** (unrest/crime).
    

#### C. Unemployment (Specifically "Educated Unemployment")

- **The Paradox:** We have millions of unemployed graduates, yet industries have to hire foreign managers (from India/Sri Lanka) because our graduates lack technical skills.
    
- **Cause:** The education system (National University mainly) produces "Generalists" (History/Political Science degrees) while the economy needs "Specialists" (Engineers/Technicians).
    
- **Consequence:** Rise in frustration, **Drug Addiction** (_Madok_), and political violence among youth.
    

#### D. Corruption

- **Nature:** It is institutionalized. From the local land office to high-level contracts, bribery is often seen as a "standard operating procedure."
    
- **Sociological View:** It stems from **Particularism** (Topic 4). Officials feel a stronger duty to help their family/clan (nepotism) than to follow the abstract Rule of Law.
    

#### E. Violence Against Women and Children

- **Dowry:** Despite the _Dowry Prohibition Act 1980_, the practice continues because marriage is still viewed as a financial transaction in a patriarchal society.
    
- **Harassment:** Domestic violence and sexual harassment remain high due to the "Cultural Lag" where women's economic status has risen (RMG workers), but social respect for them has not kept pace.
    

#### F. Environmental Displacement (Climate Refugees)

(This is an emerging but critical problem).

- **Riverbank Erosion:** Every year, thousands of families lose their land to rivers. They migrate to Dhaka slums, becoming "Climate Refugees."
    
- **Salinity:** In the south, saltwater intrusion is destroying agriculture, forcing migration.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Interlink the Topics:** When writing an essay, show the connections. For example: _"Population pressure leads to land fragmentation, which leads to rural poverty, which leads to migration to cities, which leads to slum growth and crime."_
    
- **Use Data (Approximates):** Mentioning that Bangladesh has a population density of over **1200 per sq km** or that the youth population is over **30%** adds weight to your answer.
    
- **Legal Perspective:** Always mention the **Law vs. Reality** gap.
    
    - _Problem:_ Dowry. _Law:_ Dowry Prohibition Act. _Gap:_ Lack of enforcement due to social norms.
        
    - _Problem:_ Corruption. _Law:_ Anti-Corruption Commission Act. _Gap:_ Political interference.
        
- **Conclusion:** End on a positive note. Mention that despite these problems, Bangladesh has shown "Social Resilience" (e.g., success in immunization, disaster management, and women's employment).
