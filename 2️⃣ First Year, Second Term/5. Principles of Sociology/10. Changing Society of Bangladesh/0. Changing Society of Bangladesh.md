### 1. The Historical Roots: Social Structure of Colonial Bengal

To understand modern Bangladesh, we must look back at the structure imposed by the British Raj, specifically the **Permanent Settlement Act of 1793**. This single act by Lord Cornwallis completely engineered the social structure of Bengal for two centuries.

**The Creation of the Zamindar Class:** Before the British, land belonged to the village community or the cultivator. The British introduced the concept of **Private Property** in land.

- **The Act:** The British declared that the _Zamindars_ (who were previously just tax collectors for the Mughals) were now the **Landlords** (Owners). The peasants (_Ryots_) became mere tenants.
    
- **The Structure:** This created a rigid feudal hierarchy:
    
    1. **The British (Rulers):** At the top, extracting revenue.
        
    2. **The Zamindars (Intermediaries):** Mostly Hindu elites who owned the land but often lived in Calcutta (Absentee Landlordism). They became the "Bhadralok" (Gentlefolk).
        
    3. **The Middlemen (Jotedars/Talukdars):** Layers of rent-collectors between the Zamindar and the peasant.
        
    4. **The Peasantry (Ryots):** Mostly Muslim cultivators who worked the land but had no rights.
        

**Sociological Consequence:** This structure created a deep **Class and Communal Divide**. The struggle between the (mostly Hindu) Landlords and the (mostly Muslim) Peasants fueled the politics of Bengal, eventually influencing the Partition of 1947.

> **Key Theorist:** **A.K. Nazmul Karim**. In his classic book _Changing Society in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh_, he analyzed how this act replaced the traditional village leadership with a new feudal class loyal to the British.

### 2. Neo-Colonialism and the Emergence of Bangladesh

After the British left in 1947, Bengal (East Pakistan) did not become truly free. It entered a phase of **Internal Colonialism** by West Pakistan, which eventually led to the liberation war and the emergence of Bangladesh.

**The Concept of Neo-Colonialism:** Neo-colonialism is the practice where a powerful country controls a weaker country not through soldiers (Direct Rule), but through **Economic Exploitation** and cultural dominance.

**Phase 1: Internal Colonialism (1947-1971)** Sociologically, the relationship between West and East Pakistan was a classic "Core-Periphery" relationship (World Systems Theory).

- **Economic:** East Pakistan earned foreign exchange (Jute exports), but the money was spent developing West Pakistan (Industrialization).
    
- **Cultural:** The imposition of Urdu was an attempt at "Cultural Imperialism."
    
- **Emergence:** The Liberation War of 1971 was a **Social Revolution** where the Bengali "Middle Class" (students, teachers, lawyers) allied with the "Peasantry" to overthrow this colonial structure.
    

**Phase 2: Global Neo-Colonialism (1971-Present)** Even after 1971, Bangladesh remains part of the global capitalist structure.

- **Aid Dependency:** In the early years, dependence on foreign aid allowed donor nations (World Bank/IMF) to dictate our internal policies (Structural Adjustment Programs).
    
- **Trade Dependency:** Today, the RMG sector ties our economy to the West. While we are sovereign, our economic health depends on the buying decisions of brands in the USA and EU.
    

### 3. Social Problems of Bangladesh

A **Social Problem** is a condition that a significant number of people view as undesirable and that requires collective action to fix. Bangladesh faces several structural problems rooted in its history and rapid change.

#### A. Poverty and Inequality

Despite growth, the gap between rich and poor is widening (Class Polarization).

- **Cause:** Unequal distribution of land (historical) and unequal access to quality education (modern).
    
- **Sociological View:** It is not just about low income; it is about **Social Exclusion**. The poor are excluded from the legal and political systems.
    

#### B. Population and Demographic Pressure

- **The Problem:** High population density puts immense pressure on land and cities.
    
- **The Shift:** We are moving from "High Mortality/High Fertility" to "Low Mortality/Low Fertility." However, the **"Youth Bulge"** creates unemployment if jobs are not created fast enough.
    

#### C. Unemployment (Educated Youth)

- **Nature:** It is structurally caused by the mismatch between the education system (producing general graduates) and the market (needing technical skills).
    
- **Consequence:** This leads to **Frustration**, drug addiction ("Madok"), and brain drain.
    

#### D. Corruption

- **Sociological Analysis:** Corruption in Bangladesh is often analyzed as a result of **"Particularism."** People prioritize their family/clan obligations over their public duty. It is also a result of **Anomie** (lawlessness)—where the goal (money) is valued more than the legitimate means (hard work).
    

#### E. Violence Against Women

- **The Conflict:** As women enter the workforce (Social Change), traditional patriarchy fights back. This friction leads to domestic violence, acid attacks, and harassment. It is a symptom of a society in transition.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **A.K. Nazmul Karim is Mandatory:** For any question on the "Social Structure of Bangladesh," you _must_ cite **Prof. A.K. Nazmul Karim**. He is the father of Bangladeshi sociology. His analysis of the Zamindari system is the standard academic text.
    
- **Permanent Settlement Act (1793):** Memorize this date. It is the "Big Bang" moment for Bengal's social structure. It created the class system that defined rural politics for 150 years.
    
- **Internal Colonialism:** Use this phrase to describe the Pakistan period. It shows you understand the _structural_ nature of the exploitation, not just the political history.
    
- **Link to Law:** When discussing social problems (like Dowry or Corruption), mention that _Laws exist_ (Dowry Prohibition Act, Anti-Corruption Commission), but _Social Norms_ prevent them from being effective. This is **Cultural Lag**.
