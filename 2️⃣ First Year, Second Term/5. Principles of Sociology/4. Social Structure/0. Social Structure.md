
## Social Structure ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** If "Society" is the building, "Social Structure" is the steel framework (beams and pillars) that holds it up. You cannot understand how a legal system or economy works without understanding the underlying structure of roles, statuses, and institutions.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** Questions often focus on "Elements of Social Structure" (Status and Role) or asking you to analyze the social structure of Bangladesh.
    

---

### 1. The Anatomy of Society

Just as a biological body has a structure (skeleton, muscles, organs) that remains relatively stable even as cells die and are replaced, society has a **Social Structure**. It refers to the **organized pattern of social relationships** and social institutions that together compose society.

It is the "framework" of society that is already laid out before you were born. You don't just act randomly; you step into a structure that guides your path (e.g., the school system, the class system, the legal system).

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **Radcliffe-Brown:** Defined social structure as the _"complex network of actual existing social relations."_ He emphasized that humans are arranged in an integrated whole.
    
- **S.F. Nadel:** Argued that structure is not the people themselves, but the _pattern_ of their relationships.
    
- **Talcott Parsons:** The most famous structuralist. He defined it as the arrangement of social institutions, norms, and values that ensure system stability.
    

### 2. The Building Blocks (Elements of Social Structure)

Social structure is built from smaller units. The two most critical units are **Status** and **Role**.

#### A. Status (The Position)

A status is a socially defined position in a group or society. It is "who you are" in the structure.

- **Ascribed Status:** A position you are born into or receive involuntarily (e.g., Race, Sex, Caste, Son, Daughter).
    
- **Achieved Status:** A position you earn through effort (e.g., Lawyer, Judge, Prime Minister, Convict).
    
- _Relevance:_ Modern societies (like the West) emphasize _Achieved_ status; traditional societies (like rural Bangladesh) emphasize _Ascribed_ status.
    

#### B. Role (The Behavior)

A role is the behavior expected of someone who holds a particular status. It is "what you do."

- _Relationship:_ Status is the position; Role is the action.
    
    - _Status:_ Judge.
        
    - _Role:_ Interpreting the law, maintaining neutrality, sentencing criminals.
        
- **Role Conflict:** When the demands of two statuses clash (e.g., A Judge has to try his own son. His role as _Judge_ conflicts with his role as _Father_).
    

#### C. Other Elements

- **Groups:** Collections of people interacting (Primary/Secondary).
    
- **Institutions:** Established procedures (Family, Law, Religion).
    
- **Norms and Values:** The glue that holds the structure together.
    

### 3. Types of Social Structure (Parsons' Pattern Variables)

How do structures differ? **Talcott Parsons** provided a framework called "Pattern Variables" to distinguish between traditional (rural) and modern (urban) social structures.

1. **Universalistic vs. Particularistic:**
    
    - _Universalistic (Modern):_ Rules apply to everyone equally (e.g., The Law).
        
    - _Particularistic (Traditional):_ Rules depend on who you are (e.g., Helping a family member get a job).
        
2. **Achievement vs. Ascription:**
    
    - _Achievement:_ You are judged by what you do.
        
    - _Ascription:_ You are judged by who your father is.
        
3. **Diffuseness vs. Specificity:**
    
    - _Specific:_ Relationships are limited (e.g., Doctor-Patient).
        
    - _Diffuse:_ Relationships cover everything (e.g., Mother-Child).
        

### 4. Social Structure in Global Perspective

In a globalized world, we must look at how social structures operate on a planetary scale. This is often analyzed using **Immanuel Wallerstein’s World Systems Theory**.

- **The Global Structure:** The world is not just a collection of independent countries; it is a single structural economic unit divided into a hierarchy:
    
    1. **Core Nations (The Upper Class):** Rich, industrialized, powerful (USA, Western Europe). They own the capital and technology.
        
    2. **Periphery Nations (The Lower Class):** Poor, dependent, resource-rich but industry-poor (Many African and Asian nations). They provide cheap labor and raw materials.
        
    3. **Semi-Periphery (The Middle Class):** Developing nations (India, China, Brazil, **Bangladesh**). They are industrializing and moving up.
        
- **Global Inequality:** Just as there is class stratification _within_ a country, there is structural stratification _between_ countries. The structure of global trade often keeps the Periphery poor to benefit the Core.
    

### 5. Bangladeshi Context: A Structure in Transition

Bangladesh provides a fascinating case study of a "Dual Structure."

- **Rural Structure:** Still largely based on **Ascribed Status** (Lineage/Bongsho), **Particularism** (bias for kin), and **Informal Institutions** (Shalish).
    
- **Urban Structure:** Moving toward **Achieved Status** (Education/Jobs), **Universalism** (Rule of Law), and **Formal Institutions** (Courts/Corporations).
    
- _Conflict:_ Corruption often happens because people apply "Rural/Particularistic" norms (helping a cousin) in an "Urban/Universalistic" setting (a government office).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Status vs. Role:** A favorite short question. Remember: _You occupy a status, but you play a role._ (Analogy: Status is the title of the movie character; Role is the script).
    
- **Parsons is Key:** Mentioning **Talcott Parsons** and his "Pattern Variables" is the hallmark of a first-class answer. It shows deep theoretical knowledge.
    
- **Master Status:** This is the status that dominates all others. For most people in modern society, it is their **Occupation**. In a caste society, it is their **Caste**.
    
- **Global Link:** When discussing Global Perspective, explicitly mention **Wallerstein**. His "World Systems Theory" is the standard academic framework for this topic.
