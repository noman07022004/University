
## Deviance, Crime, and Social Control ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the study of "rule-breaking." To understand Law, you must understand _why_ people break it and _how_ society tries to stop them. It is the sociological side of Criminology.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A favorite for problem questions and essays. Expect questions on "Juvenile Delinquency in Bangladesh" or "The difference between Deviance and Crime."
    

---

### 1. The Nature of Rule-Breaking: Deviance

Society is held together by norms. When someone violates these norms, it is called **Deviance**.

**Definition:** Deviance is any behavior that violates the standards of conduct or expectations of a group or society.

- It is **relative**, not absolute. What is deviant in one culture may be normal in another.
    
    - _Example:_ Drinking alcohol is normal in the UK but deviant (and illegal) in Saudi Arabia or strictly religious communities in Bangladesh.
        

**Deviance vs. Crime (The Crucial Distinction):** Many students confuse these two.

- **Deviance:** Violation of _any_ social norm (Folkways, Mores, or Laws).
    
    - _Example:_ A man wearing a skirt in Dhaka is **Deviant** (violates folkways) but not **Criminal**.
        
- **Crime:** Violation of a _specific_ norm that has been codified into Law.
    
    - _Example:_ Robbing a bank is both **Deviant** (violates morality) and **Criminal** (violates the Penal Code).
        

> **Diagrammatic Logic:** All crimes are acts of deviance (usually), but not all acts of deviance are crimes.

### 2. Explaining Deviance: Why do people break rules?

Sociologists use three main theories to explain this.

#### A. Functionalist View (Emile Durkheim & Robert Merton)

- **Durkheim:** Deviance is actually _functional_. It clarifies moral boundaries (when a thief is punished, we all learn what "wrong" is) and promotes social unity (we unite against the criminal).
    
- **Merton’s Strain Theory:** Deviance happens when there is a gap between cultural **Goals** (e.g., "Get Rich") and the legitimate **Means** to achieve them (e.g., "Hard work/Education").
    
    - _The Innovator:_ Wants the goal (Wealth) but rejects the means (Work), so they steal (Crime).
        

#### B. Interactionist View (Labeling Theory)

- **Howard Becker:** No act is inherently "deviant." It becomes deviant only when society _labels_ it so.
    
- _Process:_ If a teacher constantly calls a student a "troublemaker," the student eventually accepts this label and acts bad. This is the **Self-Fulfilling Prophecy**.
    

#### C. Conflict View (Marxist)

- Laws are tools of the rich. Deviance is often a reaction to inequality. The poor steal to survive; the rich steal (white-collar crime) because they can get away with it.
    

### 3. Juvenile Delinquency

A specific and critical category of deviance is that committed by minors.

**Definition:** Juvenile Delinquency refers to the violation of the law committed by a person below a specific age (usually 18).

- **Sociological View:** Children are not "criminals"; they are "delinquents." They need rehabilitation, not punishment.
    

**Causes in Bangladesh:**

1. **Family Breakdown:** Divorce or lack of parental supervision.
    
2. **Poverty:** Slum children forced into theft for survival.
    
3. **Peer Pressure:** The rise of **"Kishore Gangs"** (Teen Gangs) in Dhaka. These groups provide identity and status that the family/school fails to provide.
    
4. **Cultural Lag:** Urban life exposes kids to luxury desires (via internet/media) that they cannot afford legally.
    

**Legal Context:** In Bangladesh, the **Children Act, 2013** governs this. It mandates that children be tried in separate Juvenile Courts and sent to Correction Centers (not adult prisons).

### 4. Social Control: Keeping Order

How does society prevent chaos? Through **Social Control**—the techniques and strategies for preventing deviant human behavior.

#### A. Agents of Social Control

These are divided into two types:

**1. Informal Social Control (The "Soft" Police)**

- **Agents:** Family, Peer Group, Religion, Community (Samaj).
    
- **Methods:** Gossip, ridicule, smiles, scolding, ostracism (social boycott).
    
- **Effectiveness:** Very effective in traditional/rural societies where everyone knows everyone ("What will people say?").
    

**2. Formal Social Control (The "Hard" Police)**

- **Agents:** The State (Police, Courts, Prisons, Military).
    
- **Methods:** Fines, imprisonment, execution.
    
- **Effectiveness:** Necessary in large, anonymous urban societies where informal control fails.
    

### 5. The Bangladeshi Context

- **The Shift:** Historically, Bangladesh relied on **Informal Control** (The Village Shalish). If you stole, the village elders shamed you.
    
- **The Crisis:** With urbanization, the Shalish has lost power. The **Formal Control** (Police/Courts) is overburdened and often corrupt. This gap leads to a rise in crime.
    
- **Kishore Gangs:** This is a classic example of **Differential Association Theory** (Sutherland)—kids learn crime by hanging out with other criminal kids.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Merton's Strain Theory:** If you write an essay on "Causes of Crime," you _must_ cite **Robert Merton**. His theory explains that crime is not just "bad people," but "blocked opportunities."
    
- **Delinquency Age:** Note that under the **Children Act 2013** in Bangladesh, a "Child" is anyone under **18 years** (previously it was 16 under the 1974 Act). Mentioning this shows legal awareness.
    
- **White Collar Crime:** Mention **Edwin Sutherland**. He defined crimes committed by high-status people (bribery, fraud) which are often ignored by the police compared to "street crimes" of the poor.
