## Deviance, Crime, and Juvenile Delinquency ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the core of "Criminology" within sociology. To understand why laws exist, you must understand the behavior (Deviance) that laws try to regulate.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** A high-frequency topic. Questions often ask to "Distinguish between Deviance and Crime" or "Analyze the causes of Juvenile Delinquency in Bangladesh."
    

---

### 1. Deviance: The Violation of Norms

**Definition:** In sociology, **Deviance** is any behavior that violates the standards of conduct or expectations of a group or society. It is the flip side of social control.

- It is not necessarily "bad" or "evil." It is simply **different** from the norm.
    
- _Example:_ A monk taking a vow of silence is "deviant" (most people talk), but he is not a criminal. A man wearing a bikini to a funeral is deviant, but (usually) not a criminal.
    

**The Relativity of Deviance:** Deviance is not absolute; it is **relative** to time and place.

- **Time:** Smoking cigarettes was considered "cool" in the 1950s; today, it is often viewed as deviant/harmful.
    
- **Place:** Drinking alcohol is normal in Europe but is deviant (and illegal) in Saudi Arabia or strictly religious communities in Bangladesh.
    

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **Clinard:** _"Deviance is that behavior which is not in a straight line with the standard of the group."_
    
- **Horton and Hunt:** _"Deviance is any failure to conform to the customary norms of the society."_
    

**Functions of Deviance (Émile Durkheim):** Surprisingly, Durkheim argued deviance is **functional** (useful) for society:

1. **Affirms Cultural Values:** Punishment of a deviant affirms what is "good."
    
2. **Clarifies Moral Boundaries:** When a thief is jailed, society learns exactly where the line is.
    
3. **Promotes Social Unity:** People bond together against the "common enemy" (the criminal).
    
4. **Encourages Social Change:** Yesterday's deviants (e.g., Suffragettes, Freedom Fighters) are today's heroes. Deviance challenges the status quo.

### 2. Crime: The Violation of Law

**Definition:** **Crime** is a specific category of deviance. It is the violation of a society’s formally enacted criminal law.

- While deviance violates _norms_ (folkways/mores), crime violates _laws_.
    
- It is punishable by the State (fines, prison, execution).
    

**The Relationship (The Venn Diagram):**

- **All Crime is Deviance:** (With rare exceptions). Murder is both deviant (immoral) and criminal (illegal).
    
- **Not all Deviance is Crime:** Burping loudly in a fancy restaurant is deviant (rude), but you won't be arrested for it.
    

**Types of Crime:**

1. **Blue-Collar Crime:** Street crimes committed by the poor/working class (Robbery, Assault). These are highly visible and policed.
    
2. **White-Collar Crime (Edwin Sutherland):** Crimes committed by people of high social status in the course of their occupation.
    
    - _Examples:_ Bribery, Embezzlement, Tax Evasion, Money Laundering.
        
    - _Significance:_ These often cost society _more_ money than street crime but are punished less severely.
        
3. **Organized Crime:** A business supplying illegal goods or services (Drug cartels, Mafia).
    
4. **Cyber Crime:** Crimes committed using the internet (Hacking, Identity Theft).

### 3. Juvenile Delinquency

**Definition:** Juvenile Delinquency refers to the violation of the law committed by a young person (minor) who is under a specific age.

- **Sociological View:** Children are not "criminals"; they are "delinquents." They are victims of their environment and require **correction**, not punishment.
    

**Legal Context in Bangladesh:**

- **The Children Act, 2013:** This is the governing law.
    
- **Age Limit:** It defines a child as anyone under the age of **18 years**. (Previously 16).
    
- **Justice System:**
    
    - Children cannot be tried in adult courts; they go to **Juvenile Courts**.
        
    - They cannot be sent to Prison/Jail; they are sent to **Child Development Centers** (Kishore Unnayan Kendra).
        
    - The goal is rehabilitation.
        

**Causes of Delinquency (Why do kids go bad?):**

1. **Family Disorganization:** Broken homes, domestic violence, or lack of parental supervision ("Latchkey kids").
    
2. **Poverty:** In Dhaka slums, children are often forced into theft or drug carrying (Tokai) for survival.
    
3. **Differential Association (Sutherland):** Criminal behavior is learned. If a child hangs out with a "Kishore Gang," they learn the techniques and justifications for crime.
    
4. **Cultural Lag/Anomie:** Urban youth are exposed to consumer culture (expensive phones/bikes) via social media but lack the means to buy them, leading to theft (Merton's Strain Theory).
    

**The Phenomenon of "Kishore Gangs":** In recent years, Bangladesh has seen a rise in "Teen Gangs" in urban areas (Dhaka/Chattogram).

- _Nature:_ Groups of teens engaging in turf wars, harassment, and speed-biking.
    
- _Sociological Cause:_ A crisis of masculinity and a lack of healthy recreational spaces (playgrounds), leading youth to find status in violence.

### 4. Theoretical Perspectives on Deviance

To score well, you must explain _why_ it happens using theories.

#### A. Structural Strain Theory (Robert Merton)

- **Argument:** Deviance happens when there is a gap between **Cultural Goals** (e.g., Get Rich) and **Institutional Means** (e.g., Education/Job).
    
- **The Innovation:** When a person wants the goal (Wealth) but lacks the means (No job/Education), they "Innovate" by stealing.
    

#### B. Labeling Theory (Howard Becker)

- **Argument:** Deviance is not about the _act_, but the _reaction_.
    
- **The Process:** If a teacher/police constantly calls a poor kid a "criminal," the kid eventually accepts this **Label** and starts acting like one (Self-Fulfilling Prophecy).
    
- **Application:** This is why the law protects the identity of Juvenile Delinquents—to prevent "labeling" them for life.

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Age Limit Trap:** In exams, always verify the current legal age for juvenile delinquency in Bangladesh. It is **18 years** (under the 2013 Act). Do not write 16 (that was the 1974 Act).
    
- **Sutherland's Concept:** Use the term **"White Collar Crime"** and mention **Edwin Sutherland**. This is essential for differentiating crime types.
    
- **Distinction:** If asked to distinguish Deviance and Crime, use the **"Social vs. Legal"** distinction. Deviance violates social norms; Crime violates legal codes.
    
- **Diagram:** You can draw a large circle labeled "Deviance" and a smaller circle inside it labeled "Crime." This visualizes that Crime is a _part_ of Deviance.
