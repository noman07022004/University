## Social Mobility ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Stratification is the _structure_ (the ladder), while Mobility is the _movement_ (climbing the ladder). Understanding this explains why people work hard, why revolutions happen, and how dynamic a society is.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** A favorite topic for short notes and essay questions. Examiners frequently ask for the distinction between **Inter-generational** and **Intra-generational** mobility.
    

---

### 1. Meaning and Definition

**Social Mobility** is the movement of individuals, families, or groups from one social position to another within the social stratification system. It is the "shifting" of status.

In an **Open System** (Class), mobility is common and expected. In a **Closed System** (Caste/Slavery), mobility is rare or forbidden.

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **Pitirim Sorokin (The Father of Mobility Studies):** _“Social mobility is understood as any transition of an individual or social object or value from one social position to another.”_
    
- **Barber:** _“Social mobility refers to movement, either upward or downward, between higher or lower social classes.”_
    

---

### 2. Characteristics of Social Mobility

1. **Universality:** Every open society has some degree of mobility. No modern society is completely static.
    
2. **Directional:** It can be upward (gain), downward (loss), or horizontal (neutral).
    
3. **Degree varies:** The "rate" of mobility differs. The USA has high mobility (The American Dream); feudal societies had low mobility.
    
4. **Structural vs. Individual:**
    
    - _Individual:_ You work hard and get rich.
        
    - _Structural:_ The economy booms (e.g., IT revolution), lifting _everyone_ up regardless of individual effort.
        

---

### 3. Types of Social Mobility (The Core Classification)

Sociologists categorize mobility based on direction and time.

#### A. Based on Direction

1. **Vertical Mobility:** Movement up or down the hierarchy. This changes your social status.
    
    - **Upward Mobility (Ascending):** Moving to a higher status.
        
        - _Example:_ A tea-seller’s son becoming a Prime Minister.
            
    - **Downward Mobility (Descending):** Moving to a lower status.
        
        - _Example:_ A businessman going bankrupt and becoming a clerk.
            
2. **Horizontal Mobility:** Movement from one position to another _without_ changing social status.
    
    - _Example:_ A factory worker leaving a textile factory to work in a jute factory for the same wage.
        
    - _Example:_ A District Judge transferring from Dhaka to Chittagong. The location changes, but the prestige/power remains the same.
        

#### B. Based on Generations (Time)

1. **Inter-generational Mobility (Between Generations):** Comparing the child’s status to the parent’s status.
    
    - _Upward:_ The son of a rickshaw puller becomes a lawyer.
        
    - _Downward:_ The son of a doctor becomes a taxi driver.
        
    - _Significance:_ This measures how "open" a society really is.
        
2. **Intra-generational Mobility (Within a Lifetime):** Changes in social status within an individual’s own career.
    
    - _Example:_ A person starts as a junior clerk, gets promoted to manager, and retires as a CEO. (This is a "Career Ladder").
        

---

### 4. Consequences of Social Mobility

Is mobility always good? Functionalists say yes; Conflict theorists and Psychologists warn of the costs.

#### A. Positive Consequences (Functionalist View)

1. **Efficiency:** It ensures the most talented people fill the most important jobs (Meritocracy).
    
2. **Social Justice:** It gives hope. Even the poor feel they have a "stake" in the system if they believe they can rise.
    
3. **Prevention of Revolution:** If people can climb the ladder individually, they are less likely to try to overthrow the ladder collectively.
    

#### B. Negative Consequences (Dysfunctions)

1. **Status Anxiety:** The fear of falling (Downward Mobility) creates immense stress. In a mobile society, if you fail, you blame yourself (not the system), leading to depression.
    
2. **Anomie (Durkheim):** Rapid mobility can leave people without a moral compass. The "Nouveau Riche" (newly rich) often don't know how to behave, leading to social isolation.
    
3. **Brain Drain:** Rural areas lose their smartest people to cities (Urbanization), and poor countries lose their talent to rich countries.
    
4. **Breakdown of Solidarity:** As people rise, they often abandon their old community, kin, and friends to fit into their new class.

### 5. The Bangladeshi Context

Bangladesh is undergoing a massive structural shift in mobility:

- **Education as the Elevator:** The primary engine of **Inter-generational mobility** in Bangladesh is education. A farmer sells his land to send his son to university, expecting him to become a "BCS Cadre" (Government Officer).
    
- **The RMG Effect:** The garment industry has created **Intra-generational mobility** for millions of rural women. They moved from "unpaid household labor" (low status) to "paid factory worker" (higher economic status).
    
- **Blocked Mobility:** Nepotism and Corruption often block merit-based mobility. If jobs are given based on political connections (Party) rather than merit, the system becomes "closed," leading to social unrest (e.g., Student movements for quota reform).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Sorokin is Essential:** You must cite **Pitirim Sorokin** and his book _Social and Cultural Mobility_ (1927). He is the founder of this sub-field.
    
- **Distinguish Clearly:** In exams, use a table to contrast **Inter-generational** (Father vs. Son) and **Intra-generational** (Start of career vs. End of career).
    
- **Legal Link:** Mention **"Equality of Opportunity"** (Article 19 of the Bangladesh Constitution). The law aims to ensure vertical mobility is based on merit, not lineage.
    
- **Concept - "Sanskritization":** In the South Asian context, mention **M.N. Srinivas**. He explained that lower castes try to achieve upward mobility by imitating the rituals/practices of upper castes (Horizontal becoming Vertical).
