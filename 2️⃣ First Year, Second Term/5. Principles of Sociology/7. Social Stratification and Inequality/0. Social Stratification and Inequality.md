## Social Stratification and Inequality ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the central theme of sociology. Sociology was largely born out of the desire to understand why some people are rich and others are poor. It explains the structure of power and privilege.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** Extremely high priority. You will almost certainly face a broad essay question (e.g., _"Critically discuss the Davis-Moore thesis"_ or _"Compare the Caste and Class systems"_).
    

---

### 1. The Geological Analogy: What is Stratification?

Sociologists borrowed the term "Stratification" from Geology. Just as the earth is divided into layers (strata) of rock—some high, some low—society is divided into layers of people.

**Definition:** Social Stratification is the horizontal division of society into "higher" and "lower" social units. It is the ranking of individuals based on unequal access to scarce resources like wealth, power, and prestige.

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **Raymond W. Murray:** _"Social stratification is a horizontal division of society into 'higher' and 'lower' social units."_
    
- **Ogburn and Nimkoff:** _"The process by which individuals and groups are ranked in a more or less enduring hierarchy of status is known as stratification."_
    

### 2. Characteristics of Stratification (Melvin Tumin)

To understand the nature of inequality, Melvin Tumin identified five key characteristics:

1. **It is Social:** It is not biological. A criminal is not "lower" because of his genes, but because society defines crime as "low."
    
2. **It is Ancient:** Stratification has existed in almost every society since the dawn of surplus agriculture.
    
3. **It is Universal:** No society is entirely "classless." Even in communist states, there were party leaders (high) and workers (low).
    
4. **It is Diverse:** The form changes. In India, it was Caste; in Rome, it was Slavery; in Modern USA, it is Class.
    
5. **It is Consequential:** It determines life chances—how long you live, how healthy you are, and whether you get justice in court.
    

### 3. Forms of Social Stratification

Sociologists classify stratification systems into two broad types:

- **Closed Systems:** Status is ascribed (birth-based). Mobility is impossible.
    
- **Open Systems:** Status is achieved (merit-based). Mobility is possible.
    

There are four major historical forms:

#### A. Slavery (Extreme Closed System)

The most extreme form of inequality where some individuals are owned by others as property.

- **Basis:** Economic ownership of humans.
    
- **Rights:** The slave has no rights; the master has absolute power.
    

#### B. Caste System (Closed System)

A system based on religion and purity, most famously associated with traditional India.

- **Basis:** **Ascribed Status** (Birth).
    
- **Features:** Endogamy (marrying within the group), hereditary occupation, and concepts of Purity/Pollution.
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:** While primarily Hindu, elements of caste (Ashraf vs. Atraf) exist historically in Bengali Muslim society.
    

#### C. Estate System (Feudalism)

Prevalent in Medieval Europe.

- **Basis:** Land ownership and military power.
    
- **The Three Estates:** 1. Clergy (Church), 2. Nobility (Kings/Knights), 3. Commoners (Peasants/Serfs).
    

#### D. Class System (Open System)

The dominant system in modern industrial societies.

- **Basis:** **Achieved Status** (Income, Occupation, Education).
    
- **Key Feature:** Theoretically, anyone can move up or down the ladder (Meritocracy).
    

### 4. Dimensions of Social Inequality (Max Weber)

While Karl Marx argued that inequality was purely about **Money** (Class), **Max Weber** argued it was multidimensional. He proposed the **"Three P's"** of stratification:

1. **Property (Class):** Economic position. (Wealth, income).
    
2. **Prestige (Status):** Social honor and respect.
    
    - _Example:_ A priest or a university professor may be poor (low Class) but commands high respect (high Status).
        
3. **Power (Party):** Political ability to influence others.
    
    - _Example:_ A politician may not be rich, but has the power to make laws.
        

### 5. Theories of Stratification: Why does it exist?

This is the biggest debate in sociology.

#### A. Structural Functionalism (Davis-Moore Thesis)

- **Argument:** Stratification is **necessary and beneficial**.
    
- **Logic:** Some jobs are more important (e.g., Surgeon) than others (e.g., Janitor). To motivate talented people to undergo years of difficult training to become surgeons, society must offer them higher rewards (money/prestige).
    
- _Conclusion:_ Inequality ensures the "most qualified people fill the most important roles."
    

#### B. Conflict Theory (Karl Marx)

- **Argument:** Stratification is **harmful and exploitative**.
    
- **Logic:** It is not about "talent"; it is about "power." The rich (Bourgeoisie) control the resources and exploit the poor (Proletariat) to stay rich. Inequality prevents talented poor people from succeeding.
    
- _Conclusion:_ Stratification is a tool of oppression.
    

### 6. Social Mobility

**Definition:** Social mobility is the movement of individuals or groups from one social status to another within the stratification system.

**Types of Mobility:**

1. **Vertical Mobility:** Moving up or down the ladder.
    
    - _Upward:_ Rags to riches (e.g., A clerk becoming a Manager).
        
    - _Downward:_ Riches to rags (e.g., A businessman going bankrupt).
        
2. **Horizontal Mobility:** Changing position without changing status.
    
    - _Example:_ A doctor moving from Dhaka Hospital to Khulna Hospital. The job is different, but the prestige/income is the same.
        
3. **Inter-generational Mobility:** Change in status between generations (e.g., A rickshaw puller's son becomes a Lawyer).
    
4. **Intra-generational Mobility:** Change in status within one person's lifetime (e.g., Starting as a junior lawyer and becoming a Supreme Court Justice).

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Weber vs. Marx:** In any essay, comparing **Marx (Uni-dimensional/Economic)** vs. **Weber (Multi-dimensional/Class, Status, Power)** is the standard way to get top marks.
    
- **The "Davis-Moore" Debate:** If asked "Is inequality necessary?", you must cite the **Davis-Moore Thesis**. Then, critique it using Tumin’s argument (that inequality actually _kills_ talent because poor kids don't get a chance).
    
- **Sanskritization:** When discussing the Caste system in the South Asian context, mention **M.N. Srinivas’s** concept of _Sanskritization_—where lower castes imitate higher castes to improve their status.
    
- **Bangladeshi Context:**
    
    - Discuss the **New Middle Class**: The rise of the RMG sector and IT has created a new class of wealthy people who do not come from traditional "Bongsho" (Lineage) families. This is a shift from _Ascribed_ to _Achieved_ status.
        
    - **Mobility Barrier:** Mention that while mobility is possible, the "English Medium vs. Bengali Medium" education divide acts as a structural barrier to mobility in Bangladesh.
