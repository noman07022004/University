## Definition, Characteristics, Determinants, and Forms of Social Stratification ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the core structural concept of sociology. It explains why society is not flat but hierarchical. To understand law, you must understand that different "layers" of people experience the law differently.
    
- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most standard essay question in this module. You are frequently asked to "Define stratification and discuss its various forms," specifically comparing Caste and Class.
    

---

### 1. Meaning and Definition

The term "Stratification" is borrowed from **Geology**. In geology, the earth is made up of different layers (strata) of rock, placed one on top of the other. Similarly, society is divided into layers of people.

Sociological Definition:

Social stratification is the division of a society into permanent groups or categories linked with each other by the relationship of superiority and subordination. It is an institutionalized pattern of inequality.

**Scholarly Definitions:**

- **P. Gisbert:** _"Social stratification is the division of society into permanent groups or categories linked with each other by the relationship of superiority and subordinations."_
    
- **Raymond W. Murray:** _"Social stratification is a horizontal division of society into 'higher' and 'lower' social units."_
    
- **Ogburn and Nimkoff:** _"The process by which individuals and groups are ranked in a more or less enduring hierarchy of status is known as stratification."_
    

### 2. Characteristics of Social Stratification

Sociologist **Melvin M. Tumin** identified five distinct characteristics that define stratification systems across the world. (Memorizing these 5 points is a guaranteed way to score well).

1. **It is Social:** It is not biological. A criminal is not "lower" because of his DNA, and a King is not "higher" because of his blood. Society _assigns_ these meanings. Biological traits (like skin color) only become relevant because society gives them social value.
    
2. **It is Ancient:** Stratification has existed since the beginning of human civilization. Even in ancient wandering tribes, there were chiefs (high) and commoners (low).
    
3. **It is Universal:** No society is "classless." From the US to China to Bangladesh, every society has some form of ranking (Rich/Poor, Leader/Follower).
    
4. **It is Diverse:** The _form_ changes from place to place. In ancient Rome, it was **Master/Slave**. In India, it was **Caste**. In modern London, it is **Class**.
    
5. **It is Consequential:** It matters. Your position in the hierarchy determines your **"Life Chances"**—your health, longevity, access to justice, and even happiness.
    

### 3. Determinants of Social Stratification

What decides who goes to the top and who stays at the bottom? **Talcott Parsons** identified six main determinants (factors) that societies use to rank people:

1. **Membership in a Kinship Unit:** Who are your parents? (Birth/Lineage). This is crucial in closed systems like Caste.
    
2. **Personal Qualities:** Intelligence, beauty, strength, or charisma.
    
3. **Achievements:** What have you done? (Degrees, inventions, victories).
    
4. **Possessions:** What do you own? (Land, money, houses).
    
5. **Authority:** Do you have the legal right to command others? (e.g., A CEO or Prime Minister).
    
6. **Power:** Can you influence others even without authority? (e.g., A religious leader or a mafia boss).
    

> **Weber’s Trio:** Always remember **Max Weber’s** simplified determinants: **Class** (Wealth), **Status** (Prestige), and **Party** (Political Power).

### 4. Forms of Social Stratification

Sociologists classify stratification into four major historical forms. These are generally divided into **Closed Systems** (no mobility) and **Open Systems** (mobility possible).1

#### A. Slavery (Extreme Closed System)

The most ancient and extreme form of inequality.

- **Definition:** An economic form of inequality where some people are owned as property by others.
    
- **Basis:** Economic ownership.
    
- **M6obility:** Zero. The slave has no rights, not even over their own body.
    
- **Historical Context:** Ancient Greece, Rome, and the American South.
    

#### B. Estates (Feudal System)

The system of Medieval Europe (Middle Ages).

- **Basis:** Land ownership and Military service.
    
- **The Three Estates:**
    
    1. **First Estate (Clergy):** The Priests/Church (Those who prayed).
        
    2. **Second Estate (Nobility):** The Kings/Knights/Landlords (Those who fought).
        
    3. **Third Estate (Commoners):** Peasants/Serfs (Those who worked).
        
- **Mobility:** Very limited. A peasant could rarely become a noble.
    

#### C. Caste System (Closed System)

A system based on religion and ritual purity, most prominent in the Indian Subcontinent.

- **Basis:** **Ascribed Status** (Birth). You are born into it and die in it.
    
- **Key Features:**
    
    1. **Endogamy:** You must marry within your caste.
        
    2. **Hereditary Occupation:** A barber’s son becomes a barber.
        
    3. **Hierarchy:** Brahmins (Priests) at the top; Dalits (Untouchables) at the bottom.
        
- **Bangladeshi Context:** Although Bangladesh is Muslim-majority, historical "Caste-like" features existed among Muslims:
    
    - **Ashraf:** Noble birth (claim descent from Arabs/Pathans).
        
    - **Atraf:** Common birth (local converts).
        
    - _Note:_ This system has largely faded, replaced by economic class.
        

#### D. Class System (Open System)

The dominant system in modern industrial societies, including modern Bangladesh.

- **Definition:** A group of people who occupy the same economic position in society.
    
- **Basis:** **Achieved Status** (Wealth, Income, Occupation).
    
- **Key Features:**
    
    1. **Social Mobility:** You can move up (Rags to Riches) or down.
        
    2. **Meritocracy:** Status is theoretically based on talent and effort.
        
    3. **Universal Legal Rights:** Unlike slavery, everyone is equal before the law (theoretically).

### 5. Comparison: Caste vs. Class (The Exam Favorite)

Examiners love asking you to distinguish between these two.

|**Feature**|**Caste**|**Class**|
|---|---|---|
|**Nature**|Closed System.|Open System.|
|**Basis**|Birth (Ascribed Status).|Wealth/Occupation (Achieved Status).|
|**Mobility**|Impossible (Static).|Possible (Dynamic).|
|**Marriage**|Endogamous (Must marry within group).|Exogamous (Can marry outside group).|
|**Support**|Supported by Religion/Tradition.|Supported by Economy/Law.|
|**Relation**|Purity and Pollution.|Rich and Poor.|

### 📝 Important Notes & Exam Tips

- **Tumin's 5 Points:** I repeat, memorizing **Tumin's 5 Characteristics** (Social, Ancient, Universal, Diverse, Consequential) is the easiest way to ensure full marks in the "Characteristics" section of a question.
    
- **Bangladesh Context:** When discussing "Forms," mention that Bangladesh is a **"Transitional Society."** We have moved from an _Agrarian/Feudal_ form (Zamindars) to a _Class-based_ form (Industrialists/Workers), but traces of the old status hierarchy (respect for "Bongsho" or lineage) still remain in rural areas.
    
- **Legal Perspective:** Mention that modern Constitutions (like Bangladesh's Article 27) are designed to create a **Class System** (equal opportunity) and abolish **Caste/Slavery** (hereditary inequality). Law is the tool used to shift from Closed to Open systems.
