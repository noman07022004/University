The Law of Contract is the foundation of civilized commercial life. It determines which promises the state will enforce and which it will ignore. In the context of Bangladesh, this area of law is governed by the **Contract Act, 1872**. Unlike criminal law, which imposes duties on everyone, contract law allows individuals to create their own "private laws"—rights and duties that bind only the parties who agreed to them.

Below is the detailed breakdown of the introductory concepts, organized into a logical narrative flow.

### A. The Basis and Principles of Contract Law

_(Covering: Basis of contract Law, Principles of contract)_

The fundamental **basis of contract law** is the maxim _Pacta Sunt Servanda_ (agreements must be kept). However, not every agreement is a contract. The law distinguishes between social/domestic agreements (which are not legally binding) and commercial agreements (which are).

The **principles of contract** rest on two major pillars:

1. **Freedom of Contract:** Parties are generally free to set their own terms, decide the price, and choose who they deal with, provided the terms are not illegal.
    
2. **Consensus ad Idem (Meeting of Minds):** For a contract to exist, both parties must agree to the same thing in the same sense.
    

The legal definition is provided in **Section 2(h)** of the Contract Act, 1872:

> _"An agreement enforceable by law is a contract."_

Therefore, the formula for a contract is:

$$Contract = Agreement + Enforceability$$

### B. History, Development, and Modern Character

_(Covering: History and development, Character of modern contracts)_

#### 1. History and Development

The Contract Act, 1872, applicable in Bangladesh, is a legacy of British colonial rule. It is largely based on English Common Law, specifically the diverse rules developed by English courts over centuries (originating from the "Writ of Assumpsit").

- Before 1872, different personal laws (Hindu/Muslim laws) governed contracts in the Indian subcontinent.
    
- The 1872 Act codified these principles to provide uniformity for trade and commerce.
    

#### 2. Character of Modern Contracts

While the 19th-century view emphasized absolute "freedom of contract," the character of modern contracts has shifted. The modern state often intervenes to protect weaker parties.

- **Standard Form Contracts:** Today, most contracts are "take it or leave it" (e.g., insurance policies, software terms, bank loans). Parties do not negotiate every term.
    
- **Consumer Protection:** Modern law limits the power of stronger parties to exclude liability, moving away from strict _Caveat Emptor_ (let the buyer beware) toward consumer welfare.
    

### C. Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

_(Covering: Essential elements of contract)_

According to **Section 10** of the Contract Act, 1872, all agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object.

For an agreement to be a valid contract, it must satisfy the following **essential elements**. If any one of these is missing, the contract may be void or voidable.

1. **Offer and Acceptance:** There must be a lawful offer by one party and a lawful acceptance by the other. This creates the "Agreement."
    
2. **Intention to Create Legal Relations:** The parties must intend for the agreement to have legal consequences. (e.g., inviting a friend to dinner usually lacks this intention; selling a car usually has it).
    
3. **Lawful Consideration:** Consideration is the "price" paid for the promise (quid pro quo). It must be real and lawful.
    
4. **Capacity of Parties:** The parties must be legally capable of entering a contract (i.e., of sound mind, major age, and not disqualified by law).
    
5. **Free Consent:** Consent must not be obtained through coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake.
    
6. **Lawful Object:** The purpose of the contract must not be illegal, immoral, or opposed to public policy.
    
7. **Certainty of Meaning:** The terms must be vague or ambiguous. (e.g., "I will sell you some oil" is void for uncertainty).
    
8. **Possibility of Performance:** The act must be physically and legally possible to perform.
    
9. **Not Expressly Declared Void:** The agreement must not fall under categories expressly declared void by the Act (e.g., wagering agreements).

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **The "Formula":** Always remember: **Agreement = Offer + Acceptance**. Then, **Contract = Agreement + Enforceability**.
    
- **Section 10 is Key:** Memorize the components of Section 10 of the Contract Act, 1872. It is the checklist you will use to analyze every problem question to see if a valid contract exists.
    
- **Case Law Hint:** While not explicitly listed in this sub-section of the syllabus, the case of _Balfour v Balfour_ is the standard reference for "Intention to create legal relations" (distinguishing domestic from legal agreements).
    
- **Terminology:** Distinguish between **Void** (never was a contract), **Voidable** (valid until cancelled by the aggrieved party), and **Illegal** (forbidden by law).
