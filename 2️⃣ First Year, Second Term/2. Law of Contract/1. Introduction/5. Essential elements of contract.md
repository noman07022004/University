## 📌 1.5 Essential elements of contract (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the single most important checklist in the entire course. It is the filter that the law uses to decide if an agreement is a "contract." You will use this checklist to analyze every single scenario.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the foundation of almost every exam question. Whether it's a direct essay ("What are the essential elements of a valid contract?") or a problem question ("Analyze if A and B have a valid contract"), your answer _must_ be based on this list.


This subtopic provides the answer to the most fundamental question in contract law: "What makes an agreement legally enforceable?"

As per **Section 2(h) of the Contract Act, 1872**, "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract." For an agreement to be "enforceable by law," it must meet the conditions laid out in **Section 10** of the Act, which are known as the "essential elements."

Think of it as a legal formula: **Agreement + [Essential Elements] = Contract**

An **Agreement** (Offer + Acceptance) is just the first step. To become a legally binding contract, it must be filtered through these essentials.

---

### 1️⃣ Agreement (Offer and Acceptance):

- There must be a lawful **Offer** (or "Proposal") by one party and a lawful, absolute, and unqualified **Acceptance** of that offer by the other party.
    
- This creates a "meeting of the minds" or **Consensus Ad Idem** (as discussed in 1.3).
    
- _Related Concept (from 1.3):_ The existence of this agreement is judged by the **Objective Principle**—what a reasonable person would conclude from the parties' words and actions.
    

### 2️⃣ Free Consent:

- It's not enough for the parties to just "consent." Their consent must be **free**—given voluntarily and genuinely.
    
- **Section 14** of the Act specifies that consent is _not_ free if it is caused by:
    
    - Coercion (threats)
        
    - Undue Influence (abusing a dominant position)
        
    - Fraud (intentional deception)
        
    - Misrepresentation (unintentional false statement)
        
    - Mistake (error about a key fact)
        
- If consent is not free (e.g., caused by fraud), the contract is usually **voidable** at the option of the innocent party.
    

### 3️⃣ Capacity of Parties (Competence):

- The parties entering the contract must be legally "competent" to do so.
    
- **Section 11** of the Act states a person is competent if they are:
    
    1. **Of the age of majority** (in Bangladesh, 18 years, as per the Majority Act, 1875).
        
    2. **Of sound mind** (not declared insane).
        
    3. **Not disqualified** by any other law (e.g., a foreign enemy or an insolvent).
        
- An agreement with a minor is **void ab initio** (void from the very beginning).
    

### 4️⃣ Lawful Consideration:

- This is the "bargain" principle (from 1.4). Consideration, defined in **Section 2(d)**, is the "price" for the promise—the _quid pro quo_ ("something for something").
    
- It must be "lawful" (as defined in Section 23).
    
- **Section 25** states that an agreement without consideration is **void**, subject to a few exceptions (like a written promise based on natural love and affection).
    

### 5️⃣ Lawful Object:

- The "object" or _purpose_ of the agreement must be legal.
    
- **Section 23** states that the object is unlawful if it is:
    
    - Forbidden by law.
        
    - Fraudulent.
        
    - Involves injury to another person or property.
        
    - Regarded by the court as immoral or against public policy.
        
- An agreement with an unlawful object is **void**.
    

### 6️⃣ Not Expressly Declared Void:

- The agreement must not be one that the Contract Act, 1872, (or any other law) has specifically "blacklisted" or declared to be void.
    
- _Examples:_
    
    - Agreements in restraint of marriage (Section 26)
        
    - Agreements in restraint of trade (Section 27)
        
    - Agreements in restraint of legal proceedings (Section 28)
        
    - Agreements that are uncertain (Section 29)
        
    - Wagering agreements or bets (Section 30)
        


### 7️⃣ Intention to Create Legal Relations:

- The parties must have intended their agreement to have legal consequences.
    
- **Commercial Agreements:** This intention is **presumed to exist**.
    
- **Social/Domestic Agreements:** This intention is **presumed _not_ to exist** (e.g., a promise to take a friend to dinner). This presumption can be rebutted by evidence.
    

### 8️⃣ Certainty and Possibility:

- The terms of the agreement must be clear and certain, not vague (**Section 29**). An agreement to sell "a hundred tons of oil" without specifying the _kind_ of oil is void for uncertainty.
    
- The act itself must be possible to perform (**Section 56**). An agreement to discover treasure by magic is void.
    

### 9️⃣ Legal Formalities (where required by law):

- **General Rule:** The Contract Act, 1872, does _not_ require a contract to be in writing. A valid contract can be made orally.
    
- **The Exception:** However, other _specific laws_ in Bangladesh mandate that certain types of contracts **must** be in writing and/or registered to be valid and enforceable.
    
- _Examples:_
    
    - **Sale of Immovable Property:** The **Transfer of Property Act, 1882,** requires a sale of land to be made by a **written, registered instrument**.
        
    - **Registration Act, 1908:** Requires deeds of gift, or leases over one year, to be registered.
        
    - **Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881:** Requires promissory notes and bills of exchange to be in writing.
---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Extra Information

- **The "Big Formula":** The best way to remember this topic is: **Contract = Agreement (Offer + Acceptance) + Enforceability (Free Consent + Capacity + Lawful Consideration + Lawful Object + Intention to Create Legal Relations)**.
    
- **Void vs. Voidable:** This topic is key to this distinction.
    
    - A **Void** agreement is a "non-starter." It fails one of the core elements (e.g., Capacity, Lawful Object) and is not a contract at all.
        
    - A **Voidable** contract is a valid contract _until_ the innocent party chooses to cancel it. This happens when an element is "defective" (e.g., Free Consent was stolen by fraud).
        
- **Section 10 is your guide:** **Section 10 of the Contract Act, 1872,** is the most important section for this topic. It states, *"All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void."*
