## 📌 1.2 Character of modern contracts ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:** This topic is rated 3 stars. It's moderately important because it explains the _real-world context_ in which the 19th-century principles of the Contract Act, 1872, now operate. Understanding this helps you analyze how "freedom of contract" and "free consent" have been challenged by modern business practices.

The classical contract law, as envisioned by the Contract Act, 1872, was based on the **"will theory."** This theory assumed that:

1. Parties had **equal bargaining power**.
    
2. Parties would **negotiate** the terms freely.
    
3. Parties would read and understand the contract before giving **full consent**.
    
4. The contract was often a single, "one-off" transaction.
    

The **character of modern contracts** is vastly different. Today, contracts are characterized by speed, inequality, and new technologies. The most significant types are:

### 1️⃣ Standard Form Contracts (or "Contracts of Adhesion"): 

This is arguably the most dominant characteristic of modern contracts.

- **What they are:** These are pre-drafted, "take-it-or-leave-it" contracts. One party (usually a large corporation) prepares a set of standard terms, and the other party (usually a consumer) has no power to negotiate. Their only choice is to "adhere" (stick) to the contract or not get the service/product at all.
    
- **Examples:** Insurance policies, bank account opening forms, airline tickets, mobile phone service agreements, and software "Terms of Service" agreements.
    
- **Legal Problem:** This challenges the "free consent" and "negotiation" principles of classical law. Did you really consent to Clause 54(b)(iii) of a 30-page agreement you never read?
    
- **Legal Response:** To combat potential unfairness, courts and legislatures have developed new rules. Courts may strike down "unconscionable" (grossly unfair) or "unreasonable" clauses. In Bangladesh, laws like the **Consumer Rights Protection Act, 2009**, provide an additional layer of protection for the weaker party against unfair trade practices.
    

### 2️⃣ Electronic Contracts (E-Contracts): 

The rise of the internet has created new ways to form contracts.

- **What they are:** Contracts formed through electronic means, like websites, email, or apps.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - **Click-wrap:** You must click a button or check a box that says "I Agree" before you can proceed (e.g., installing software).
        
    - **Browse-wrap:** A website's terms of use are posted (often as a link at the bottom of the page), and by simply _using_ or _browsing_ the website, you are deemed to have agreed to them.
        
- **Legal Problem:** When exactly did the "offer" and "acceptance" happen? Was the communication of acceptance valid?
    
- **Legal Response:** Laws have been passed to give electronic transactions legal validity. In Bangladesh, the **Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Act, 2006** (and later the Digital Security Act) provided legal recognition for electronic signatures and records, confirming that an e-contract is just as valid as a paper one.
    

### 3️⃣ Relational Contracts: 

This concept recognizes that many modern business deals are not isolated events.

- **What they are:** These are long-term, ongoing agreements where the relationship between the parties is as important as the written contract itself.
    
- **Examples:** Franchise agreements, long-term supply chain contracts, employment contracts, and joint ventures.
    
- **Key Feature:** These contracts are built on trust, flexibility, and cooperation. The parties often expect to resolve unforeseen problems through discussion rather than by suing each other for breach of a specific clause.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Extra Information

- **Shift in Focus:** The main "character" change is the shift away from **"Freedom of Contract"** (the idea that parties can agree to anything) towards **"Consumer Protection"** (the idea that the law must protect weaker parties from exploitation).
    
- **Inequality of Bargaining Power:** This is the core theme. Modern contract law is increasingly concerned with addressing the problems caused by one party having significantly more power, information, and resources than the other.
    
- **Role of Other Laws:** You cannot understand modern contracts by reading the Contract Act, 1872, alone. You must also consider the **Consumer Rights Protection Act**, the **ICT Act**, and other specific industry regulations.
