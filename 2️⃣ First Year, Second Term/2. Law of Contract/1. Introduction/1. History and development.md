## 📌 1.1 History and development ⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:** This topic is rated 2 stars. While it provides essential context for _why_ the law is the way it is, you are unlikely to be asked a direct, complex exam question on it. It's foundational knowledge, not a practical, problem-solving topic.


The **Contract Act, 1872**, which is the primary law of contract in Bangladesh, is not a modern or indigenous creation. Its history is directly tied to the British colonial period in the Indian Subcontinent.

### 1️⃣ Pre-British Era (Before 1772): 

Before the British established a uniform legal system, commercial and personal agreements were governed by the personal laws of the parties involved.

- **For Hindus:** The rules were derived from classic Hindu law sources (like the _Dharmashastras_), which had principles governing debts (_rin_), pledges, and deposits.
    
- **For Muslims:** The principles of Islamic law (_Shari'ah_) were applied, covering concepts like _aqd_ (contract), _ijab_ (offer), and _qabul_ (acceptance).
    

The key issue was the **lack of a single, uniform law** to govern transactions between people of different faiths or between locals and foreigners.

### 2️⃣ The British Period & Codification (1772-1872): 

When the East India Company took over, they initially applied personal laws. However, in major commercial centers (Presidency towns like Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras), English Common Law was heavily applied by British judges.

This created confusion and inconsistency. In the 19th century, a strong movement for **codification** (the process of writing laws into a systematic code) began. The British Parliament established "Law Commissions" to draft uniform codes for India.

### 3️⃣ The Enactment of the Contract Act, 1872: 

The third Law Commission, led by Sir John Romilly, drafted the Indian Contract Bill. It was heavily revised by Sir James Fitzjames Stephen, who is considered the principal architect of the final Act.

- **Basis of the Act:** The Act is almost entirely based on the principles of **English Common Law**. It codified established English principles regarding:
    
    - Offer and Acceptance
        
    - Consideration
        
    - Free Consent
        
    - Breach of Contract
        
- The Act was passed for British India and came into force on **September 1, 1872**.
    

### 4️⃣ Post-1872 Development: 

The 1872 Act was a "code," but it was not exhaustive. It was intended to cover the general principles of contracts.

- **Splitting the Act:** The original 1872 Act was much larger. Over time, specific commercial topics were considered complex enough to need their own laws. Two major parts were "carved out" of the original Contract Act:
    
    1. **The Sale of Goods Act** (Sections 76-123 of the original Act) was repealed and replaced in 1930.
        
    2. **The Partnership Act** (Sections 239-266 of the original Act) was repealed and replaced in 1932.
        
- **Adoption in Bangladesh:** After the partition in 1947, the Act was adopted by Pakistan. After the liberation in 1971, the newly formed nation of Bangladesh adopted all existing laws (including the Contract Act, 1872) through the **Laws (Continuance and Enforcement) Order, 1971**, ensuring legal continuity.
    

The "development" part of the topic refers to how courts have interpreted the Act since 1872, adapting it to new commercial realities (like e-commerce, and standard form contracts) that the original drafters could never have imagined.

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Extra Information

- **English Law is Key:** Because the Act is based on English Common Law, judicial decisions from English courts (especially from the 19th and early 20th centuries) are highly influential and often cited in the courts of Bangladesh, India, and Pakistan to understand the core principles.
    
- **Codified Law:** The 1872 Act represents a shift from unwritten, judge-made "common law" to a single, written "statute." This made the law more accessible, predictable, and uniform across the entire subcontinent.
    
- **Relevance Today:** While it's an old law, its fundamental principles are timeless, which is why it remains the central law of contract in Bangladesh over 150 years later.
