## 📌 5.3: Novation, alteration, remission, accord and satisfaction (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. These are the specific legal tools that allow parties to mutually end or change a contract. Understanding the difference between substituting a _new contract_ (Novation) and forgiving a _debt_ (Remission) is critical.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a very common topic for short notes. "Novation" and "Remission" are frequently tested as specific concepts.


This subtopic covers the specific legal mechanisms for terminating a contract by mutual agreement, as governed by **Sections 62 and 63 of the Contract Act, 1872**.

### 1️⃣ Novation (Section 62)

**Novation** means **substituting an existing contract with a new one**. When novation occurs, the parties agree to replace the original contract, which is then discharged and need not be performed.

Novation can happen in two ways:

- **Changing the Parties:** The parties remain the same, but the terms of the contract are so completely changed that it's a new agreement altogether.
    
- **Changing the Terms:** A new party is substituted for an old one. For this to be valid, **all three parties** (the original two and the new party) must consent.
    
    - **Example:** A owes B 10,000 Taka. A, B, and C agree that C will now be B's debtor, and A is released. The original contract between A and B is terminated by novation.
        

### 2️⃣ Alteration (Section 62)

**Alteration** means modifying one or more of the _material terms_ of an existing contract by **mutual consent**.

- **Effect:** The original contract is discharged, and the new, "altered" contract takes its place.
    
- **Distinction from Novation:** Alteration is a _modification_ of the original contract (e.g., changing the delivery date or interest rate), while novation is a _complete substitution_ of the contract. The line can be thin, but alteration keeps the original contract's "soul" intact, while novation replaces it entirely.
    

### 3️⃣ Remission (Section 63)

**Remission** is the act of a promisee (the person owed the promise) **forgiving or dispensing with** the performance of the promise, either wholly or in part.

- **Section 63** gives the promisee the power to:
    
    1. **Remit or dispense with performance:** Forgive the entire debt (e.g., "You don't have to pay me back at all").
        
    2. **Accept a smaller amount:** Accept a part of the debt in "full satisfaction" of the whole debt.
        
        - **Example:** A owes B 5,000 Taka. A pays 2,000 Taka, and B _accepts_ this payment as a full and final settlement. The entire 5,000 Taka debt is discharged.
            
    3. **Extend the time** for performance.
        
- **Key Point:** In Bangladesh (and India), **no new consideration is needed** for remission. This is a major departure from English law.
    

### 4️⃣ Accord and Satisfaction (English Law Concept)

This term is not used in the Contract Act but is an important English Common Law concept that is closely related to Section 63.

- **"Accord":** This is the _agreement_ by which the promisee agrees to accept something different (usually less) from what they are owed under the original contract.
    
- **"Satisfaction":** This is the _performance_ of this new agreement (e.g., the actual payment of the lesser amount).
    
- **Effect:** Once the "satisfaction" is complete, the original contract is discharged.
    

**Bangladeshi Context:** The principle of "Accord and Satisfaction" is fully covered by **Section 63** (Remission). Section 63 is actually _wider_ because it allows a promisee to remit a debt _even without_ any satisfaction (e.g., simply by "waiving" it).

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Sections 62 vs. 63:** This is a crucial distinction.
    
    - **Section 62 (Novation, Alteration, Rescission):** Requires a _new agreement_ between the parties (mutual consent) to replace or change the old one.
        
    - **Section 63 (Remission):** Is a _unilateral act_ by the **promisee** (the creditor), who can "waive" their own rights.
        
- **No Consideration for Remission:** Remember this for exams. Under Section 63, a promise to accept 2,000 Taka for a 5,000 Taka debt is **binding**, even though the debtor gave no new consideration for that 3,000 Taka discount.
