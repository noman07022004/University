## 📌 5.2: Termination by performance and mutual agreement (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the most important subtopic for understanding how contracts _should_ and _do_ end in the vast majority of cases. It covers the two most common and fundamental methods: either the contract is fulfilled, or the parties mutually agree to end it.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** High importance. While "performance" itself is covered in Topic 4, the concept of "mutual agreement" (governed by Sections 62 and 63) is a very frequent exam topic, especially novation and remission.
 

This subtopic introduces the two most common and straightforward ways a contract is discharged (terminated).

### 1️⃣ Termination by Performance

This is the primary and most natural way a contract ends. The contract is terminated because its purpose has been achieved, and all obligations have been fulfilled. This method was covered in detail in **Main Topic 4**.

It can happen in two ways:

- ***Actual Performance:*** This is the ideal scenario. Both parties (promisor and promisee) have fully and completely performed their respective promises. The contract is satisfied, and all rights and obligations are extinguished.
    
- ***Attempted Performance (Tender):*** As seen in **Section 38**, if the promisor makes a valid "offer to perform" (e.g., brings the goods to the correct place at the correct time) and the promisee _rejects_ this tender, the **promisor is discharged** from their obligation. The contract is terminated due to the promisee's refusal to accept performance.
    

### 2️⃣ Termination by Mutual Agreement

This method is based on the same principle as contract formation: _consensus ad idem_. Just as parties can mutually agree to _create_ a contract, they can also mutually agree to _end_ it.

The law for this is found in **Sections 62 and 63 of the Contract Act, 1872**. These sections allow parties to "dispense with" or "excuse" performance. The subsequent subtopics (5.3, 5.4, 5.5) are all just different forms of this mutual agreement:

- ***Novation (Subtopic 5.3):*** As per **Section 62**, this is when the parties agree to **substitute** the original contract with a _new contract_. The original contract is automatically discharged and need not be performed.
    
- ***Rescission (Subtopic 5.4):*** Also under **Section 62**, this is when all parties mutually agree to **cancel** the original contract.
    
- ***Alteration (Subtopic 5.3):*** Under **Section 62**, this is when the parties agree to **change** one or more of the _material terms_ of the original contract. This discharges the original contract and replaces it with the altered one.
    
- ***Remission/Waiver (Subtopics 5.3, 5.5):*** **Section 63** gives the promisee (the person owed the duty) the power to "waive" or "remit" the promisor's obligation. This includes the right to accept _less_ than what is owed.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **No New Consideration Needed (Section 63):** This is a critical point in Bangladeshi and Indian law. **Section 63** allows a promisee to accept less performance (e.g., accept 2,000 Taka in full payment of a 5,000 Taka debt) **without any new consideration**. This is a major departure from English law, which would require new consideration for such a promise to be binding.
    
- **The "Peaceful" End:** These methods represent the "peaceful" or "non-hostile" ways to end a contract, as opposed to "Breach" (a hostile termination) or "Frustration" (an automatic termination).
    
- **The Umbrella Topic:** Think of "Mutual Agreement" as the umbrella. Novation, Rescission, Alteration, and Remission are the different types of agreements that fall under it.
