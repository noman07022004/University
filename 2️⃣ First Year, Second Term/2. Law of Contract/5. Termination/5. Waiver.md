## 📌 5.5: Waiver (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. "Waiver" is a fundamental concept in law. It's the idea that a party can _voluntarily give up_ a right they are owed. Understanding this is key to seeing how parties can flexibly manage their contractual relationships without resorting to legal action.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐):** Less important as a standalone question. It's almost always discussed _as part of_ Section 63 ("Remission"). You are more likely to be asked about "Remission" than "Waiver" by itself.


A **waiver** is the **intentional and voluntary relinquishment (giving up) of a known right** under a contract. When a party "waives" a right, they are essentially promising not to enforce it.

**Example:** A contract states that payment must be made on the 1st of the month. The promisee (the one owed the money) tells the promisor, "It's fine, you can pay me on the 5th this month." The promisee has **waived** their right to demand payment on the 1st.

### 🔷 Waiver in the Bangladeshi Context (Section 63)

In the **Contract Act, 1872**, the concept of waiver is primarily embodied in **Section 63**, which is titled "Promisee may dispense with or remit performance of promise."

This section gives the promisee (the person owed the promise) the legal power to do any of the following, which are all forms of waiver:

1. **Dispense with performance (wholly or in part):** This is a complete waiver of the entire obligation.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. (a) to S. 63):** A promises to paint a picture for B. B (the promisee) later forbids him from doing so. B has **waived** the performance. A is no longer bound to perform the promise.
        
2. **Extend the time for performance:** This is a waiver of the right to demand performance _on the original date_.
    
3. **Remit performance (Subtopic 5.3):** This is a waiver of the right to receive the _full_ performance, by accepting less. (e.g., accepting 2,000 Taka in full payment of a 5,000 Taka debt).
    

### 🔷 Waiver vs. Remission

"Waiver" and "Remission" are often confused, but the relationship is simple:

- **Waiver:** This is the _broad principle_ of giving up any right (e.g., the right to on-time performance, the right to any performance at all).
    
- **Remission:** This is a _specific type_ of waiver, where the promisee gives up their right to the _full amount_ owed, by accepting a lesser amount.
    

In essence, "remission" is a _form_ of "waiver." Section 63 covers both the general idea (dispensing with performance) and the specific idea (remission).

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Key Elements of Waiver:** For a waiver to be valid, the person giving it up must have:
    
    1. **Knowledge** of the right they are giving up.
        
    2. **Intention** to give up that right.
        
    3. It must be **voluntary** (not caused by coercion, etc.).
        
- **No Consideration Needed:** Just like with "Remission" (5.3), a waiver under **Section 63** of the Contract Act **does not require any new consideration** to be binding.
    
- **Express or Implied:** A waiver can be **express** (stated clearly in words) or **implied** from the party's conduct. For example, if a landlord consistently accepts rent 10 days late without objection, they might be seen as having _impliedly waived_ their right to demand rent on the 1st.
