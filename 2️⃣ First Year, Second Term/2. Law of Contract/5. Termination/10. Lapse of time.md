## 📌 5.10: Lapse of time (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This topic explains how a contract can "expire." It shows that legal rights are not permanent and that failure to act in time has legal consequences. It's a key part of the "termination by operation of law" category.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐):** Less common as a major question. It's most likely to be a short note or a minor point in a problem question (e.g., identifying that a party can no longer sue).


This method of termination is not governed by the Contract Act, 1872, but by a separate and crucial law: **The Limitation Act, 1908** (in Bangladesh).

The law does not keep contractual rights alive forever. It provides a specific "period of limitation" (a deadline) within which an aggrieved party must file a lawsuit to enforce their rights.

**The Rule:** If a contract is breached (e.g., a party fails to pay a debt) and the innocent party (the creditor) does **not** take legal action within the specified period of limitation, their legal **remedy is barred by law**.

**Effect of Termination:** This is a critical legal distinction:

- The contract does **not** become "void" in the normal sense.
    
- The contract's _obligations_ still exist in theory, but they become **unenforceable** in a court of law.
    
- The innocent party loses their right to sue. This effectively **terminates the contract** because the rights can no longer be legally enforced.
    

This is directly linked to **Section 2(j)** of the Contract Act, 1872, which states: "A contract which ceases to be enforceable by law becomes void when it ceases to be enforceable".

**Example:**

- A takes a loan from B and promises to repay it on January 1, 2020.
    
- A fails to pay on that date (a breach of contract).
    
- In Bangladesh, the limitation period for filing a suit for recovery of the money is **3 years** from the date the cause of action arises.
    
- B must file a lawsuit against A before January 1, 2023.
    
- If B waits and files the suit on January 5, 2023, the court will **dismiss the suit** because it is "barred by limitation". B's remedy is gone, and the contract is effectively terminated by this "lapse of time."
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Remedy is Barred, Not the Right:** The law bars the _remedy_ (the right to sue) but not the _right_ itself (the debt is still morally owed).
    
- **Time-Barred Debt as Consideration:** This leads to a key exception in **Section 25** of the Contract Act. Normally, a promise without consideration is void. _However_, a **written promise** to pay a debt that is **"barred by the limitation law"** is a **valid contract**.
    
    - **Example:** In our case, if on January 10, 2023, A gives B a _written_ note saying, "I know my debt is time-barred, but I promise to pay you the money," this new promise is a **valid, enforceable contract**, even though B gave no new consideration for it.
