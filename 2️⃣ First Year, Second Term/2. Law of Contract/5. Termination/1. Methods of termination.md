## 📌 5.1: Methods of termination (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic is the "table of contents" or "road map" for the entire concept of ending a contract. It provides the essential framework for all the different ways a contract's legal obligations can be extinguished (discharged).
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a very common topic for a direct essay or "short note" question (e.g., "Briefly explain the various modes of discharge of a contract."). It is the necessary structure for answering any question on termination.


**"Methods of termination"** (or "discharge") refers to the different ways a contract can be brought to a legal end, releasing the parties from their obligations. A contract can be terminated (discharged) in the following primary ways:

### 1️⃣ Termination by Performance (Topic 4) 
This is the most natural and desirable method. The contract is terminated because the purpose of the contract has been fulfilled.

- **Actual Performance:** Both parties have fully performed their respective promises (e.g., the goods are delivered, and the payment is made).
    
- **Attempted Performance (Tender):** As seen in **Section 38**, if the promisor makes a valid "offer to perform" (tender) and the promisee rejects it, the _promisor_ is discharged from their obligation and can sue the promisee for breach.
    

### 2️⃣ Termination by Mutual Agreement (Sections 62 & 63) 
The parties who created the contract are free to mutually agree to end it. This is the "peaceful" way to terminate.

- **Novation (Subtopic 5.3):** Substituting the original contract with a new contract.
    
- **Rescission (Subtopic 5.4):** Mutually agreeing to cancel the contract.
    
- **Alteration (Subtopic 5.3):** Mutually agreeing to change the material terms, which discharges the original contract.
    
- **Remission/Waiver (Subtopics 5.3, 5.5):** The promisee agrees to forgive the promisor's obligation, either in whole or in part (e.g., accepting 2,000 Taka in full settlement of a 5,000 Taka debt).
    

### 3️⃣ Termination by Impossibility of Performance (Section 56) 
This occurs when performance becomes impossible _after_ the contract is made, without the fault of either party. This is known as the **Doctrine of Frustration** (Subtopic 5.8).

- **Example:** A contracts to rent a specific hall for a concert. The hall burns down before the concert date. The contract becomes **void** through "supervening impossibility" (Subtopic 5.7), and both parties are discharged.
    

### 4️⃣ Termination by Operation of Law (Subtopic 5.9) 
The law itself terminates the contract, regardless of the parties' intentions.

- **Death:** If the contract is "personal" in nature (e.g., a promise to paint a portrait), the death of the promisor terminates it.
    
- **Insolvency:** A person's declaration of insolvency or bankruptcy can discharge them from their prior contractual obligations.
    
- **Merger (Subtopic 5.6):** An inferior right under a contract "merges" into a superior right (e.g., a tenant buys the house, so the rental contract is terminated).
    

### 5️⃣ Termination by Lapse of Time (Subtopic 5.10) 
The law provides a time limit to enforce a contract (in Bangladesh, the **Limitation Act, 1908**). If a party does not file a lawsuit within that period (e.g., 3 years for a breach), the _remedy_ is barred. While the contract technically still exists, it is unenforceable, which effectively terminates the parties' rights.

### 6️⃣ Termination by Breach of Contract (Subtopic 5.11) 
This is a "hostile" termination. It occurs when one party fails or refuses to perform their obligation without a lawful excuse.

- **Actual Breach:** Failure to perform on the due date.
    
- **Anticipatory Breach (Section 39):** Stating _before_ the due date that they will not perform.
    
- A breach does _not_ automatically terminate the contract. It gives the **innocent party** the **option** to either rescind (terminate) the contract or affirm it and sue for damages.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **"Discharge" = "Termination":** These two words mean the same thing in contract law.
    
- **Framework for Topic 5:** This subtopic _is_ the framework. All the other subtopics (5.2 through 5.11) are just detailed explanations of these 6 methods.
    
- **Void vs. Voidable:** This is a crucial distinction.
    
    - Termination by **Frustration (Impossibility)** makes the contract **VOID**.
        
    - Termination by **Breach** (where time is of the essence) makes the contract **VOIDABLE** (the innocent party has a choice).
