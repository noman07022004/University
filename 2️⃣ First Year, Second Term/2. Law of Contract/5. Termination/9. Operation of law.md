## 📌 5.9: Operation of law (Overall: ⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a "catch-all" category for contract terminations that happen _automatically_ because of a legal rule, not because of the parties' actions. It groups together several distinct concepts, including some we've already seen.
>- **Exam (⭐):** Low importance. You are unlikely to be asked a broad question on "Operation of Law." You are _much_ more likely to be asked about the _specific_ components, like "Death" or "Insolvency," which are the key examples.


**Termination by operation of law** means the contract is discharged (terminated) _automatically_ by a legal rule, regardless of the parties' intentions or actions. When certain legal events occur, the law itself steps in and declares the contract over.

This category is a container for several distinct legal events:

#### 1️⃣ Death of the Promisor 
This is a key example, which we saw in **Topic 4.2 (Liability to perform)**.

- **The Rule:** The effect of death depends on the contract's nature, as per **Section 40**.
    
- **Personal Contracts:** If the contract involves **personal skill** (e.g., a promise to paint a portrait, sing, or write a book), the death of the promisor **terminates the contract**. The obligation is extinguished.
    
- **Impersonal Contracts:** If the contract is "impersonal" (e.g., a promise to pay money or deliver goods), the death of the promisor does **not** terminate it. The promisor's **legal representatives** (heirs) are bound to perform the promise using the assets of the deceased's estate.
    

#### 2️⃣ Insolvency (or Bankruptcy) 
This is a major form of termination by operation of law.

- **The Rule:** When a person is declared **insolvent** (or "bankrupt") by a court, their rights and obligations are transferred to a court-appointed official (the "Official Receiver" or "Trustee").
    
- **Effect:** The insolvency law (in Bangladesh, the **Insolvency Act, 1997**) provides for the orderly discharge of the insolvent's debts. This process effectively discharges the insolvent person from all contractual liabilities incurred before their adjudication, thus terminating those contracts. The Contract Act, 1872, also acknowledges this in **Section 201**, which states that an agency is terminated by the principal being adjudicated an insolvent.
    

#### 3️⃣ Merger 
As we discussed in **Subtopic 5.6**, this is a form of termination by operation of law. It happens when an inferior right under a contract "merges" into a superior right, extinguishing the contract that created the inferior right (e.g., a tenant buys the house, terminating the lease).

#### 4️⃣ Unauthorised Material Alteration 
This is another common law rule.

- **The Rule:** If one party to a written contract makes a **material alteration** (i.e., changes a significant term, like the price or the date) _without_ the consent of the other party, the contract becomes **void**.
    
- **Effect:** The law terminates the contract to penalize the party who tampered with the document and to protect the innocent party from being bound by terms they never agreed to.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Automatic Termination:** The key feature of this category is that the termination is _automatic_. The parties don't need to agree to it, and no one needs to "breach" it. The law simply ends the agreement.
    
- **Not a Choice:** This is different from rescission for breach, which is _voidable_ (a choice). Termination by operation of law is final and absolute.
    
- **Key Examples:** For your exams, the two most important examples to remember for this category are **Death (in personal contracts)** and **Insolvency**.
