## 📌 5.11: Breach of contract (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the "hostile" method of termination. It's the concept that all contract law is built to address. It's the "failure" state of a contract, and it is the single event that gives _rise_ to **Main Topic 6: Remedies**.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Extremely high importance. Almost _every_ problem question involves a breach of contract. You must be able to identify _if_ a breach occurred, _what type_ of breach it was, and what the _consequences_ are.


A **breach of contract** occurs when a party to a contract **fails or refuses to perform** their contractual obligations without a lawful excuse. This failure to fulfill the promise is a "breach," and it is this act that terminates the contract _at the option of the innocent party_ and gives them the right to sue for a remedy.

There are two main types of breach:

### 1️⃣ Actual Breach of Contract

This is the most common and straightforward type of breach. An actual breach occurs when a party fails to perform their obligations on the **stipulated due date** or performs them **defectively**.

- ***Failure to Perform*:** The promisor simply does not show up or does not do what they promised on the agreed date.
    
    - **Example:** A promises to deliver goods to B on November 14. If A fails to deliver the goods on that day, it is an actual breach.
        
- ***Defective Performance:*** The promisor performs, but not in accordance with the terms of the contract (e.g., delivering goods of the wrong quality or quantity).
    

### 2️⃣ Anticipatory Breach of Contract (Section 39)

This is a more complex type of breach, also known as "anticipatory repudiation." It occurs when a party declares their intention _not_ to perform the contract **before the due date of performance has arrived**.

This is governed by **Section 39 of the Contract Act, 1872**. A party can commit an anticipatory breach in two ways:

- ***Expressly:*** By words (spoken or written). The party clearly states, "I will not be performing the contract."
    
    - **Example:** A is hired in January to start a job in June. In March, A emails the company and says, "I have taken another job and will not be joining in June." This is an express anticipatory breach.
        
- ***Impliedly:*** By conduct. The party does something that makes it _impossible_ for them to perform the contract.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. (b) to S. 39):** A, a singer, agrees to sing at B's theatre every night for a month. On the sixth night, A _wilfully absents herself_. This act is an implied anticipatory breach for all future performances.
        
    - **Example:** A agrees to sell his specific car to B on December 1st. On November 20th, A sells and delivers that same car to C. A has, by his conduct, made it impossible to perform the contract, committing an anticipatory breach.
        

---

### 🔷 Consequences of Breach (The Innocent Party's Options)

A breach of contract does _not_ automatically terminate the contract. Instead, it makes the contract **voidable** at the option of the innocent party (the promisee). The innocent party is given a choice:

***Option 1: Rescind (Terminate) the Contract Immediately*** The innocent party can choose to treat the breach as the end of the contract.

- **Effect:** They can immediately stop performing their own obligations.
    
- **Remedy:** They can immediately sue the breaching party for **damages** (compensation for the loss). This is the most common remedy.
    
- **Key Section:** **Section 75** of the Act states, "A person who rightfully rescinds a contract is entitled to compensation for any damage which he has sustained through the non-fulfilment of the contract."
    

***Option 2: Affirm (Keep Alive) the Contract*** This option is most relevant in cases of _anticipatory_ breach. The innocent party can choose to _ignore_ the breach and treat the contract as still valid.

- **Effect:** They wait until the original due date of performance.
    
- **Risk:** This is a risky option. If the innocent party keeps the contract alive, the breaching party _might_ still perform. However, the contract also stays alive for the benefit of _both_ parties. If a **supervening event** (Topic 5.7) happens between the anticipatory breach and the due date (e.g., the hall burns down), the contract will be discharged by **frustration**, and the innocent party will lose their right to sue for breach.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Breach is the "Trigger":** This subtopic is the "trigger" for **Main Topic 6: Remedies**. A breach of contract is the _event_ that gives the innocent party the right to claim damages, specific performance, etc.
    
- **Section 39 is Key:** For anticipatory breach, Section 39 is the critical law. It gives the promisee the right to "put an end to the contract."
    
- **Breach of a Condition vs. Warranty:** English law (which influences Bangladeshi law) makes a further distinction (not in the Contract Act):
    
    - **Breach of a Condition:** A breach of a _fundamental_ term. This gives the right to terminate the _entire_ contract.
        
    - **Breach of a Warranty:** A breach of a _minor_ term. This does _not_ give the right to terminate; the innocent party must continue the contract and can only sue for damages.
