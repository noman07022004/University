## 5. Termination (Discharge) of Contract ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** You must understand how contractual obligations come to an end to advise clients on when they are "free" or when they are liable.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a high-priority exam topic. Questions on **Frustration (Impossibility)** and **Novation** are staples of law examinations. Problem questions often involve "Force Majeure" scenarios (e.g., a hall burns down, a law changes) requiring you to apply the Doctrine of Frustration.
    

---

**Termination** (or Discharge) of a contract means the ending of the contractual relationship between the parties. When a contract is discharged, the parties are no longer bound by their respective obligations.

While **Performance** (discussed in Topic 4) is the ideal way to end a contract, the law recognizes several other methods where a contract is terminated even without full performance.

### A. Discharge by Mutual Agreement

_(Covering: Termination by performance and mutual agreement, Novation, alteration, remission, accord and satisfaction, Rescission, Waiver, Merger)_

If the parties created the contract by agreement, they can also end it by agreement. This is governed largely by **Section 62 and 63** of the Contract Act, 1872.

1. **Novation (New Contract):**
    
    - **Definition:** The substitution of a **new contract** for an existing one. This can be between the same parties or between different parties.
        
    - **Effect:** The original contract is discharged and need not be performed.
        
    - _Condition:_ It must take place _before_ the breach of the original contract.
        
2. **Alteration:**
    
    - Changing one or more material terms of the _same_ contract.
        
    - _Difference from Novation:_ In Novation, parties may change; in Alteration, parties remain the same.
        
3. **Rescission:**
    
    - Cancellation of all or some of the terms of the contract. If parties mutually agree to cancel the contract, it is discharged.
        
4. **Remission (Accord and Satisfaction):**
    
    - **Remission (Sec 63):** Acceptance of a lesser fulfillment of the promise made (e.g., accepting 500 Taka in satisfaction of a 1000 Taka debt).
        
    - **Accord and Satisfaction:** This is the English Law term. "Accord" is the agreement to accept less, and "Satisfaction" is the actual payment/performance. Under Bangladesh law, no new consideration is needed for Remission.
        
5. **Waiver:**
    
    - The deliberate abandonment or giving up of a right which a party is entitled to under a contract. (e.g., A agrees to paint B's picture. B says "Don't bother." B has waived his right).
        
6. **Merger:**
    
    - This happens when an inferior right accruing to a party under a contract merges into a superior right accruing to the same party.
        
    - _Example:_ A holds a house on **lease** (inferior right). Later, A **buys** the house (superior right/ownership). The lease contract is terminated by merger.
        

### B. Discharge by Impossibility (The Doctrine of Frustration)

_(Covering: Supervening impossibility, Doctrine of frustration)_

Ideally, a contract is absolute. However, **Section 56** creates a major exception: **Impossibility of Performance**.

1. **Initial Impossibility (Void ab initio):**
    
    - An agreement to do an act impossible in itself is void. (e.g., "I will find treasure by magic").
        
2. **Supervening Impossibility (Doctrine of Frustration):**
    
    - A contract that was valid when made but subsequently becomes void because an event happens which makes performance **impossible** or **unlawful**.
        
    - **Grounds for Frustration:**
        
        - **Destruction of Subject Matter:** (e.g., The music hall burns down before the concert—_Taylor v Caldwell_).
            
        - **Change of Law:** (e.g., Trading with a country that suddenly becomes an enemy in war).
            
        - **Death/Incapacity:** In personal service contracts (e.g., an artist dies).
            
        - **Non-occurrence of contemplated event:** (e.g., Rooms rented solely to view a coronation procession that is cancelled—_Krell v Henry_).
            

**Crucial Distinction:** Frustration does **NOT** apply merely because performance has become:

- More expensive or difficult (Commercial Hardship).
    
- Less profitable.
    
- Delayed due to strikes (unless totally prolonged).
    

### C. Discharge by Breach of Contract

_(Covering: Breach of contract)_

Breach occurs when a party fails to perform their obligation without a lawful excuse. This does not automatically "terminate" the contract but gives the innocent party the right to terminate it and claim damages.

1. **Actual Breach:**
    
    - Failure to perform on the scheduled date or during performance.
        
2. **Anticipatory Breach:**
    
    - When a party declares their intention _not_ to perform **before** the due date.
        
    - _Remedy:_ The innocent party can either (a) Treat the contract as ended immediately and sue for damages, or (b) Wait until the due date to see if the party changes their mind.
        

### D. Other Methods

_(Covering: Operation of law, Lapse of time, Methods of termination)_

1. **By Operation of Law:**
    
    - The law automatically discharges the contract irrespective of the parties' wishes.
        
    - **Death:** Discharges contracts involving personal skill.
        
    - **Insolvency:** An insolvent's liabilities are handled by the Official Assignee; the insolvent is discharged from past debts.
        
2. **By Lapse of Time:**
    
    - The **Limitation Act** sets time limits for legal action (usually 3 years for simple contracts in Bangladesh). If the creditor does not sue within this period, the remedy is barred, and for all practical purposes, the contract is discharged (unenforceable).

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **Section 56 (Frustration) is the MVP:** Memorize the distinction: "Impossibility" must be _physical_ or _legal_. It cannot be _economic_. If the price of raw materials triples, you are still bound to perform. That is "Commercial Hardship," not Frustration.
    
- **Novation vs. Alteration:**
    
    - _Novation:_ New Contract. Old one dead. Parties _can_ change.
        
    - _Alteration:_ Old Contract modified. Parties _must_ remain same.
        
- **Anticipatory Breach Case:** _Hochster v De La Tour_. This case established that you can sue _immediately_ when someone renounces the contract, you don't have to wait for the actual date.
    
- **Effect of Frustration:** Under Section 65, if a contract becomes void (frustrated), any person who has received any advantage under it must **restore** it or make compensation (Restitution).
