## 📌 5.6: Merger (Overall: ⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a specific, technical legal doctrine that shows how a contract can be terminated automatically by the "operation of law." It's an important concept for understanding property and contract law.
>- **Exam (⭐):** Very low importance. This is a niche topic and is highly unlikely to be a standalone question. It's more of a legal detail than a major exam topic.

**Merger** is a method of terminating a contract by **operation of law**. The doctrine of merger states that a contract is discharged (terminated) when an **inferior right** held by a person under a contract "merges" into a **superior right** that the _same person_ acquires.

In simple terms, the lesser right is "absorbed" or "drowned" by the new, greater right. A person cannot hold both the superior and inferior right at the same time, so the law automatically cancels the inferior one.

### 🔷 The Classic Example

The easiest way to understand this is with a property law example that involves a contract:

- **The Contract:** A (a landlord) signs a **lease contract** with B (a tenant). B now has an "inferior right" (a leasehold) to use the property.
    
- **The New Event:** A few years later, B (the tenant) **purchases the property** from A. B now has the "superior right" (full ownership, or "fee simple").
    
- **The Merger:** The moment B acquires ownership, the lease contract is **automatically terminated by merger**.
    
- **Why?** The law says a person cannot be their own tenant. B's inferior right (to be a tenant) is absorbed into their superior right (to be the owner). The lease contract, which defined that inferior right, is therefore extinguished.
    

Another example is if a debtor (who has the liability to pay) also becomes the creditor (the person owed the money), the debt is extinguished by merger.

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Operation of Law:** This is not something the parties "agree" to; it happens automatically by law when the two rights unite in the same person.
    
- **Distinguish from "Merger Clause":** This is a critical distinction. Do not confuse this topic with a **"Merger Clause"** (also called an "integration clause") in a written contract.
    
    - **Doctrine of Merger (This Topic):** _Terminates_ an entire contract when a lesser right becomes a greater right.
        
    - **Merger Clause (Integration Clause):** A term _inside_ a contract that states the written document is the "final and complete" agreement, and it "merges" (absorbs) all _prior negotiations_ or oral promises, making them irrelevant.
