## 7.1: Contracts of indemnity (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is the first of the "special contracts." You must understand its simple, two-party structure to be able to contrast it with the more complex, three-party "Contract of Guarantee" (7.2).
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. It's often asked as a "short note" or as the first part of the classic distinction question: "Define a contract of indemnity and distinguish it from a contract of guarantee."


A **Contract of Indemnity** is a promise by one party to save the other from financial loss. The purpose is to **reimburse** the other party if they suffer a loss.

The **Contract Act, 1872,** defines it in **Section 124**:

> "A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the **conduct of the promisor himself**, or by the **conduct of any other person**, is called a 'contract of indemnity'."

**Parties to the Contract:** There are only two parties:

1. **The Indemnifier:** The person who makes the promise to compensate for the loss (the "promisor").
    
2. **The Indemnity-holder:** The person who is protected, or whose loss is to be made good (the "promisee").
    

**Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 124):** A contracts to indemnify B against the consequences of any legal proceedings which C may take against B for a certain sum of 200 Taka. This is a contract of indemnity.

- **A** is the **Indemnifier** (promisor).
    
- **B** is the **Indemnity-holder** (promisee).
    
- **C** is just a third party; he is _not_ a party to the indemnity contract.
    

---

### Rights of the Indemnity-Holder (Section 125)

The law explicitly defines the rights of the indemnity-holder (the person who is protected). When the indemnity-holder is sued, **Section 125** gives them the right to recover the following amounts from the indemnifier:

1. **All Damages:** Any damages they are compelled to pay in a lawsuit regarding the matter.
    
2. **All Costs:** Any legal costs (lawyer's fees, court fees) they are compelled to pay in bringing or defending that lawsuit, as long as they acted prudently.
    
3. **All Compromise Sums:** Any amount they paid to _compromise_ (settle) the lawsuit, as long as the compromise was reasonable and not against the indemnifier's orders.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Scope of Bangladeshi Law (Section 124):** The definition in the Contract Act, 1872, is very **narrow**. It technically only covers losses caused by **human conduct** ("conduct of the promisor... or any other person"). It does _not_ technically include losses caused by **events or accidents** (like fire, sinking of a ship, or other "Acts of God").
    
- **English Law vs. Bangladeshi Law:** English law has a much _wider_ definition, which _does_ include losses from accidents.
    
- **The Case of Insurance:** Because of the narrow definition in Section 124, a **contract of insurance** (like fire insurance or marine insurance) is _not_ technically a "contract of indemnity" under the Contract Act. Instead, in Bangladesh and India, insurance contracts are treated as a type of **"Contingent Contract" (Topic 2.9)**.
    
- **Life Insurance is NOT Indemnity:** A contract of life insurance is _never_ a contract of indemnity, because indemnity is about "making good a loss." It is impossible to calculate the financial loss of a human life.
