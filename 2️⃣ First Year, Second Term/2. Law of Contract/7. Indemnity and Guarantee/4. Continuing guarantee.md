## 7.4: Continuing guarantee (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This concept is the backbone of modern commercial credit. It's what allows for bank overdrafts, running credit accounts with suppliers, and guaranteeing someone's good conduct in a job. You must understand how it differs from a single-transaction guarantee.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a very common topic for short notes and problem questions, especially those involving the **revocation** of the guarantee (e.g., "A revokes his guarantee, but the bank lends more money. Is A liable?" or "The surety dies. Is his estate liable?").


A **"Continuing Guarantee"** is a guarantee that is not limited to a single transaction, but instead "extends to a **series of transactions**."

This is the official definition given in **Section 129 of the Contract Act, 1872**.

**Distinction from Specific Guarantee:**

- **Specific Guarantee:** This is a guarantee for a _single, specific_ debt or transaction. It is discharged and over as soon as that one transaction is complete.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. (c) to S. 129):** A guarantees payment to B for _five sacks of flour_ to be delivered to C. B delivers the five sacks, and C pays. The guarantee is now over. When B delivers _four more sacks_ and C fails to pay, A is **not liable**.
        
- **Continuing Guarantee:** This is a "rolling" or "standing" guarantee for _multiple, future_ transactions over a period of time, usually up to a certain limit.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. (b) to S. 129):** A guarantees payment to B (a tea-dealer) for any tea C buys, up to an amount of 100 Taka. B sells C 100 Taka worth of tea, and C pays. B then sells C _another_ 200 Taka worth of tea. C fails to pay. A **is liable** for 100 Taka, because the guarantee was "continuing" and covered all transactions.
        
    - **Practical Example:** This is the model used by banks for credit card guarantees or business overdraft facilities.
        

---

### Revocation of a Continuing Guarantee

Because a continuing guarantee can last for a long time, the law provides two specific ways for the surety to "turn off" their liability for the _future_.

**1. By Notice (Section 130):**

- **The Rule:** "A continuing guarantee may **at any time** be revoked by the surety, **as to future transactions**, by notice to the creditor."
    
- **Effect:** The surety is immediately freed from liability for any _new_ transactions that happen _after_ the notice is given.
    
- **Crucial Point:** The surety remains **fully liable** for all transactions that _already happened_ before the notice was given.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. (a) to S. 130):** A guarantees a bank (B) for any bills it discounts for C over 12 months. After 3 months, B has discounted bills for 2,000 Taka. A then gives notice of revocation. The bank later discounts _new_ bills. C defaults on _all_ the bills. A is **liable** for the first 2,000 Taka, but **not liable** for the new bills discounted _after_ his revocation.
    

**2. By Death of the Surety (Section 131):**

- **The Rule:** "The death of the surety operates, in the absence of any contract to the contrary, as a revocation of a continuing guarantee, so far as regards **future transactions**."
    
- **Effect:** The surety's death _automatically_ (without any notice needed) stops the guarantee from applying to any new transactions.
    
- **Liability:** The surety's estate (his property) remains **liable** for all transactions that took place _before_ his death.
    
- **Exception:** This rule does not apply if there is a "contract to the contrary" (e.g., the guarantee contract itself says that it will continue even after death, binding the surety's estate).
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Future vs. Past:** The key concept for revocation (by both notice and death) is that it only applies to **future transactions**. The liability for past transactions is already "stuck" and cannot be removed.
    
- **Reading the Contract:** The words "from time to time" or "for a series of transactions" are key indicators of a continuing guarantee.
    
- **Fidelity Guarantee:** A guarantee for an employee's good conduct (like in Illus. (a) to S. 129) is a common type of continuing guarantee.
