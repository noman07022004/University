## 7. Indemnity and Guarantee ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** These are "Special Contracts" that form the backbone of banking and insurance. Understanding the three-party structure of a Guarantee is essential for commercial law.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** "Distinguish between Indemnity and Guarantee" is a classic exam question. Problem questions often focus on the **Surety's Liability** (e.g., "Can the bank sue the guarantor directly without suing the borrower first?").
    

---

Contracts of Indemnity and Guarantee are specific types of contracts used to provide financial security. They are governed by **Chapter VIII (Sections 124–147)** of the **Contract Act, 1872**. While both involve protecting someone from loss, the mechanism and the number of parties differ significantly.

### A. Contract of Indemnity

_(Covering: Contracts of indemnity)_

**1. Definition (Section 124)**

A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself, or by the conduct of any other person.

- _Key Concept:_ It is essentially a form of insurance (excluding Life Insurance).
    
- _Example:_ A contracts to indemnify B against the consequences of any proceedings which C may take against B in respect of a certain sum of 200 Taka.
    

**2. Parties**

- **Indemnifier:** The person who promises to make good the loss (The protector).
    
- **Indemnity-holder (Indemnified):** The person whose loss is to be made good (The protected).
    

**3. Rights of Indemnity-holder (Section 125)**

If sued, the indemnity-holder is entitled to recover from the indemnifier:

- All **damages** compelled to pay in the suit.
    
- All **costs** of the suit (if they acted prudently).
    
- All **sums** paid under a compromise (if the compromise was reasonable).
    

### B. Contract of Guarantee

_(Covering: Contracts of guarantee, Continuing guarantee)_

**1. Definition (Section 126)**

A contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default.

- _Function:_ It provides a second pocket to pay if the first pocket is empty.
    

**2. The Three Parties (Tripartite Agreement)**

Unlike ordinary contracts, a guarantee involves three distinct contracts between three parties:

1. **Principal Debtor:** The person who borrows money or is primarily liable.
    
2. **Creditor:** The person to whom the guarantee is given.
    
3. **Surety (Guarantor):** The person who gives the guarantee.
    

**3. Continuing Guarantee (Section 129)**

- **Definition:** A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions, rather than a single specific debt.
    
    - _Example:_ A guarantees payment to B (a grocer) for _any_ goods C may buy over the next year up to 10,000 Taka.
        
- **Revocation (Section 130):** A continuing guarantee may be revoked by the surety at any time strictly _as to future transactions_ by giving notice to the creditor. The surety remains liable for transactions already completed before the notice.
    

### C. Distinction: Indemnity vs. Guarantee

_(Covering: Distinction)_

This is a fundamental academic comparison.

|**Feature**|**Contract of Indemnity**|**Contract of Guarantee**|
|---|---|---|
|**Number of Parties**|Two (Indemnifier, Indemnified).|Three (Creditor, Principal Debtor, Surety).|
|**Number of Contracts**|One.|Three (Principal Debt, Guarantee, Indemnity).|
|**Nature of Liability**|Primary and Independent. (Indemnifier is the _only_ one liable).|Secondary. (Surety pays _only if_ Principal Debtor defaults).|
|**Purpose**|Reimbursement of loss.|Security for a debt or performance.|
|**Request**|The indemnifier acts voluntarily or by mutual agreement, not necessarily at the request of the debtor.|The surety gives the guarantee primarily at the request of the Principal Debtor.|
|**Right to Sue**|Indemnifier cannot sue a third party in their own name (unless assigned).|After paying the debt, the Surety steps into the shoes of the Creditor and can sue the Principal Debtor.|

### D. Liability and Rights of Surety

_(Covering: Extent of liability and surety, Rights of sureties)_

**1. Extent of Liability (Section 128)**

The liability of the surety is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor, unless otherwise provided by the contract.

- _Meaning:_ If the Debtor owes 10,000 Taka plus interest, the Surety owes exactly that amount. The Creditor can sue the Surety directly without suing the Principal Debtor first (unless the contract says otherwise).
    

**2. Rights of Sureties**

The Surety is a favored debtor in the eyes of the law and has extensive rights:

- **Against the Principal Debtor:**
    
    - _Right of Subrogation (Section 140):_ Once the Surety pays the debt, they step into the shoes of the Creditor and acquire all the Creditor's rights against the Principal Debtor.
        
    - _Right to Indemnity (Section 145):_ There is an implied promise by the Principal Debtor to indemnify the Surety. The Surety can recover whatever sum they have rightfully paid.
        
- **Against the Creditor:**
    
    - _Right to Securities (Section 141):_ The Surety is entitled to the benefit of _every security_ which the Creditor has against the Principal Debtor, whether the Surety knew of the existence of such security or not. If the Creditor loses/sells the security, the Surety is discharged to the value of that security.
        
- **Against Co-Sureties:**
    
    - _Right to Contribution (Sections 146-147):_ If there are multiple sureties (Co-sureties) for the same debt, they are liable to pay equal shares (or shares proportional to their guarantee limits) if the Debtor defaults.

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **Bank Guarantees:** In modern commerce, a Bank Guarantee is the most common example. If you want to start a construction project, the employer will ask for a Bank Guarantee. If you fail to build, the bank pays.
    
- **Discharge of Surety:** A Surety is discharged (released) from liability if:
    
    - The contract is altered without their consent (Sec 133).
        
    - The Creditor releases the Principal Debtor (Sec 134).
        
    - The Creditor compounds with (gives extra time to) the Principal Debtor without Surety's assent (Sec 135).
        
- **Death of Surety:** In a Continuing Guarantee, the death of the surety operates as a revocation of the guarantee for _future_ transactions (Sec 131). The estate is liable only for past transactions.
