## 7.5: Extent of liability and surety (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the single most important operational part of a guarantee. It answers the critical question: "How much does the surety have to pay, and when?" The principle of "co-extensive" liability is the entire basis for the creditor's security.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** High importance. Problem questions about guarantees will almost always require you to apply this rule (Section 128) to determine the exact amount the surety is liable for.


This subtopic defines the _scope_ and _nature_ of the surety's (guarantor's) liability. The fundamental rule is laid down in **Section 128 of the Contract Act, 1872**.

**The Rule: Liability is "Co-extensive" (Section 128)** The section states:

> "The liability of the surety is **co-extensive** with that of the principal debtor, unless it is otherwise provided by the contract."

**"Co-extensive"** means the surety's liability is the _exact same_ in extent as the principal debtor's liability.

- **Full Amount:** The surety is liable for the _entire_ debt or promise that the principal debtor defaulted on.
    
- **Includes Interest and Charges:** If the principal debtor is liable for the main loan _plus_ interest and any late fees or charges, the surety is liable for that _entire_ amount as well.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 128):** A guarantees payment of a bill of exchange by C. C dishonors the bill. A (the surety) is liable not only for the amount of the bill but also for any **interest and charges** which have become due on it.
        

**The Exception: "Unless it is otherwise provided"** The surety can, in the original contract of guarantee, _limit_ their liability.

- **Example:** A asks a bank for a 1,000,000 Taka loan. B agrees to be the surety but states in the contract, "my liability as surety is limited to 500,000 Taka." If A defaults, the bank can only claim a maximum of 500,000 Taka from B.
    

### When does the Surety's Liability Begin?

This is a crucial point often misunderstood.

- The surety's liability is **secondary** (it only _arises_ if the principal debtor defaults).
    
- However, the _moment_ the principal debtor defaults, the surety's liability becomes **immediate** and **direct**.
    
- The creditor (the bank) **does not have to sue the principal debtor first**. They do not have to "exhaust their remedies" against the debtor. The creditor has the right to sue the surety _directly_ for the full amount as soon as the default occurs.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Co-extensive is the Key Term:** In any exam answer, you _must_ use the term "co-extensive" and cite **Section 128**.
    
- **Surety's Liability Cannot Be More:** The surety's liability is co-extensive, but it can never be _more_ than the principal debtor's liability. If the main contract is void and the principal debtor owes nothing, the surety also owes nothing.
    
- **Direct Right of Action:** Remember, the creditor can proceed against the surety _immediately_ upon default, without suing the principal debtor first. This is what makes a guarantee such a powerful security for a creditor.
