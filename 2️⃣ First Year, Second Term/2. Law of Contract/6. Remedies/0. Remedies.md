## 6. Remedies for Breach of Contract ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** The ultimate purpose of a contract is fulfillment. When that fails, understanding _Remedies_ is essential to know what the injured party can actually _get_ from the legal system.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is a favorite for problem questions. You will often be asked to calculate the **Amount of Damages** based on specific scenarios or to advise whether a client can demand **Specific Performance**.
    

---

When a contract is broken, the law provides a "remedy" to the injured party. The fundamental maxim is _Ubi jus ibi remedium_ (Where there is a right, there is a remedy).

In Bangladesh, remedies are primarily governed by the **Contract Act, 1872** (for damages) and the **Specific Relief Act, 1877** (for specific performance and injunctions).

### A. Damages (Monetary Compensation)

_(Covering: Amount of Damages, Liquidated Damages and Penalty)_

Damages are the most common remedy. The goal is **not to punish** the wrongdoer, but to **compensate** the injured party—to put them in the financial position they _would have been in_ if the contract had been performed.

**1. The Measure of Damages (Section 73)** How much money can you claim? This is determined by the famous rule in _Hadley v Baxendale_ (1854), incorporated into Section 73:

- **Ordinary Damages:** Compensation for loss that naturally arose in the usual course of things from the breach. (e.g., A fails to deliver raw material; B buys it from the market at a higher price. A pays the difference).
    
- **Special Damages:** Compensation for loss resulting from _special circumstances_ (e.g., loss of profit on a lucrative government contract). These are recoverable **only if** the parties knew about these special circumstances at the time of making the contract.
    
- **Remoteness of Damage:** You cannot claim for "remote" or indirect losses.
    

**2. Liquidated Damages vs. Penalty (Section 74)** Sometimes parties specify the amount of damages in the contract itself (e.g., "If you delay, you pay 10,000 Taka per day").

- **Liquidated Damages:** A genuine pre-estimate of the actual loss.
    
- **Penalty:** A sum intended to terrorize the other party into performing (usually essentially higher than actual loss).
    
- **The Law in Bangladesh:** Unlike English law, the Contract Act (Section 74) **does not distinguish** between the two for enforcement. The court will award **"reasonable compensation"** not exceeding the amount named in the contract.
    
    - _Example:_ Even if the contract says "Penalty: 1 Crore Taka," if the actual loss is only 1 Lakh, the court will award roughly 1 Lakh.
        

### B. Equitable Remedies (Specific Relief)

_(Covering: Specific performance, Injunction)_

Sometimes money is not enough. If the contract involves a unique item (like a piece of land, a rare painting, or an antique), monetary damages cannot compensate the loss. Here, the court uses the **Specific Relief Act, 1877**.

**1. Specific Performance**

- **Definition:** A court order compelling the defaulting party to actually perform their promise (e.g., "Sign the deed and hand over the house").
    
- **When granted:**
    
    - When there is no standard for ascertaining actual damage.
        
    - When monetary compensation is not an adequate remedy (common in land/property cases).
        
- **When NOT granted:**
    
    - Where money is an adequate relief.
        
    - Contracts involving **personal service** (e.g., you cannot force a singer to sing or an employee to work).
        
    - Contracts requiring constant court supervision (e.g., building construction).
        

**2. Injunction**

- **Definition:** A court order instructing a person to _not_ do something (Prohibitory) or to _do_ something (Mandatory).
    
- **Usage:** It is often used to enforce negative stipulations.
    
    - _Example:_ Warner Bros. contracts an actress to act _only_ for them for one year. If she tries to act for Netflix, the court can issue an **Injunction** stopping her. (Note: The court cannot force her to act for Warner Bros, but it _can_ stop her from acting for Netflix).
        

### C. Other Remedies

_(Covering: Quantum Meruit, Restitution of benefit)_

**1. Quantum Meruit**

- **Meaning:** "As much as is earned" or "in proportion to the work done."
    
- **Application:** When a person has done some work under a contract, and the other party repudiates the contract or some event makes further performance impossible, the party who did the work can claim payment for _what they have already done_.
    
    - _Example:_ An author is hired to write a book series. After the 2nd book, the publisher cancels the contract wrongfully. The author can sue on _Quantum Meruit_ for the work done on the first two books.
        

**2. Restitution of Benefit (Section 65)**

- **Principle:** If a contract becomes void (e.g., through frustration) or is discovered to be void, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to **restore it** or make compensation for it.
    
    - _Example:_ A pays B 5,000 Taka in advance for a concert. The concert hall burns down (Frustration). B must restore (refund) the 5,000 Taka to A.
        

---

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **Mitigation of Loss:** The plaintiff has a duty to **mitigate** (minimize) their loss. If A gets fired, A cannot sit at home for a year and claim a year's salary. A must try to find another job. The court will deduct what A _could_ have earned.
    
- **Hadley v Baxendale is Mandatory:** You _must_ cite this case in any question about damages. Remember the two rules: (1) Natural/Ordinary loss, (2) Special loss (only if communicated).
    
- **Nominal Damages:** If there is a breach but _no actual loss_ suffered, the court may award a small sum (e.g., 10 Taka) just to establish the right. This is called "Nominal Damages."
    
- **Specific Relief Act:** Remember that Specific Performance and Injunctions are discretionary. The court _may_ grant them, but is not _bound_ to, unlike Damages which are a right.
