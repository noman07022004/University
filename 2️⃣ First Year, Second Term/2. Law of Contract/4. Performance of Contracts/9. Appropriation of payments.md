## 📌 4.9: Appropriation of payments (Overall: ⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. It's a very specific, technical rule for a common business problem (a debtor owing multiple debts). It's a good example of how the law creates a fair, logical "default" rule when the parties are silent.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐):** Less important. This is a niche topic. It's a good candidate for a short note or a very specific part of a problem question, but it's not as high-priority as topics like "time is of the essence" or "offer/acceptance."



**"Appropriation of payments"** is the act of "applying" or "designating" a payment to a specific debt.

This subtopic deals with a very specific scenario:

1. A debtor owes **multiple, distinct debts** to the _same_ creditor.
    
2. The debtor makes a single payment that is **not large enough** to discharge (pay off) all the debts.
    

**Example:** A owes B three separate debts: 1,000 Taka (from January), 2,000 Taka (from February), and 500 Taka (from March). A pays B a single payment of 1,200 Taka. Which debt does this payment go towards?

The **Contract Act, 1872,** provides a clear, three-step hierarchy in **Sections 59-61** to resolve this.

---

### 🔷 The Three-Step Rule of Appropriation

#### Step 1: The Debtor Decides (Section 59)

The law gives the debtor (the person paying) the first right to decide.

- **The Rule:** If the debtor, _at the time of payment_, expressly states which debt the payment is for, the creditor **must** apply it as directed.
    
- **How:** This can be done by:
    
    - **Express Intimation:** The debtor says, "Here is 1,200 Taka; please apply it to the February debt."
        
    - **Implied Intimation:** The circumstances make the intention clear. For example, if A owes B 1,000 Taka and also a separate 5,250 Taka, and A sends a check for exactly 5,250 Taka, the law presumes A intended to pay the second debt.
        

#### Step 2: The Creditor Decides (Section 60)

If the debtor does not specify how to apply the payment, the right to appropriate passes to the creditor (the person receiving the money).

- **The Rule:** The creditor can, at their discretion, apply the payment to **any lawful debt** that is due and owed by the debtor.
    
- **Crucial Point:** The creditor _can_ even apply the money to a **"time-barred" debt**—a debt that is so old that the creditor can no longer sue for it under the Limitation Act, 1908.
    
- **Limitation:** The creditor cannot apply the payment to a disputed or illegal debt.
    

#### Step 3: The Law Decides (Section 61)

If neither the debtor nor the creditor makes any appropriation, the law steps in and applies the payment automatically.

- **The Rule:** The payment shall be applied to the debts in **chronological order** (in order of time). The payment goes to the **oldest debt first**.
    
- **If debts are of the same date:** If two debts are from the same date, the payment is applied **proportionately** to both.
    
- **Time-Barred Debts:** This rule _will_ apply to a time-barred debt if it happens to be the oldest one.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **The Hierarchy is Key:** The most important thing to remember is the 3-step order: **Debtor $\rightarrow$ Creditor $\rightarrow$ Law (Chronological)**. The right only passes to the creditor if the debtor fails to use it, and only to the law if both fail.
    
- **Time-Barred Debts:** The most common exam trick is about time-barred debts. Remember:
    
    - The **creditor** (Step 2) _can_ choose to apply the money to a time-barred debt.
        
    - The **law** (Step 3) _will_ apply the money to a time-barred debt if it's the oldest.
        
- **"Clayton's Case":** The rule in Section 61 (oldest debt first) is a famous legal principle known as the "Rule in Clayton's Case."
