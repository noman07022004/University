## 📌 4.3: Joint rights and liabilities (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This topic explains the legal "plumbing" for contracts that involve multiple people. The concepts of "joint and several liability" and the "right of contribution" are fundamental to any commercial or partnership agreement.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a classic topic for problem questions, especially Section 43. An exam might ask if a creditor (promisee) can sue just one of three partners for a full debt, and what rights that partner has against the other two.


This subtopic covers the rules for situations where a promise is made _by_ multiple people (joint liabilities) or _to_ multiple people (joint rights). These rules are governed by **Sections 42, 43, 44, and 45 of the Contract Act, 1872.**

We must analyze liabilities and rights separately.

### 1️⃣ Joint Liabilities (Joint Promisors)

This is about _who must perform_ when two or more people (e.g., partners A, B, and C) jointly promise to pay a debt.

- **General Rule (Section 42):** In the absence of any other agreement, joint promisors must perform the promise together. If one of them dies, their legal representative must join the surviving promisors to perform. After the last survivor dies, the legal representatives of all of them must perform.
    
- **The "Joint and Several" Rule (Section 43):** This is the **most important** rule and the one most used in practice. Section 43 gives the promisee (the creditor) three major rights:
    
    1. **Right to Sue Any One Promisor:** The promisee can "compel **any one or more** of such joint promisors to perform the **whole** of the promise."
        
        - **Practical Meaning:** This is known as **"joint and several liability."** If A, B, and C jointly promise to pay D 300,000 Taka, D does not have to sue all three. D can choose to sue _only_ A for the _entire_ 300,000 Taka. This makes it much easier for the creditor to recover their money.
            
    2. **Right of Contribution (Between Promisors):** If one promisor (like A) is forced to pay the _whole_ debt, they then have the right to "compel every other joint promisor to contribute equally... to the performance of the promise." So, A (who paid 300,000) can then sue B for 100,000 and C for 100,000.
        
    3. **Sharing of Loss:** If one promisor (say, C) is insolvent and can't pay their share, the remaining promisors (A and B) must "bear the loss arising from such default in equal shares." In our example, C's 100,000 Taka share would be split between A and B. So, B would have to pay 150,000 Taka to A (their original 100,000 + 50,000 for C's share).
        
- **Effect of Releasing One Promisor (Section 44):** If the creditor (D) decides to "release" one promisor (A) from their liability, it does **not** discharge the other promisors (B and C). B and C are still liable for the full amount. Furthermore, the released promisor (A) is still liable to B and C for contribution.
    

### 2️⃣ Joint Rights (Joint Promisees)

This is about _who can demand_ performance when a promise is made _to_ two or more people jointly (e.g., a promisor owes money to partners A, B, and C).

- **The Rule (Section 45):** "When a person has made a promise to two or more persons jointly, then... the right to claim performance rests... with them during their joint lives."
    
- **Practical Meaning:** A single promisee cannot sue for performance alone; they must _all_ join the lawsuit together.
    
- **Effect of Death:** If one of the joint promisees (say, A) dies, the right to claim performance rests with their **legal representative jointly with the surviving promisees** (B and C). After the last survivor (C) dies, the right rests with the legal representatives of _all_ of them (A, B, and C) jointly.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Liabilities vs. Rights:** The rules are opposite and crucial to remember.
    
    - **Liabilities (Promisors):** The creditor can sue _any one_ for the _whole_ amount.
        
    - **Rights (Promisees):** _All_ of them must sue _together_.
        
- **"Joint and Several" is the Key:** For joint _promisors_ (liabilities), **Section 43** effectively makes their liability "joint and several." This is a fundamental concept in business law, especially for partnerships, where each partner is individually liable for 100% of the firm's debts.
    
- **Contribution is a Separate Right:** The right of contribution is a separate right _between the promisors_. It does not affect the promisee's right to get their money from whoever is easiest to sue.
