## 📌 4.5: Contracts which need not be performed (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic is the conceptual bridge between "Performance" (Topic 4) and "Termination" (Topic 5). It explains the various legal "exit ramps" that allow parties to be excused from their obligations _without_ being in breach of contract.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This is a common exam topic, as it pulls together several key sections (like 56, 62, 63, and 67). A problem question might involve a contract that becomes impossible or one that the parties mutually agree to cancel.


While **Section 37** of the Contract Act, 1872, commands that parties _must_ perform their promises, it also provides an exception: performance is not required if it is "dispensed with or excused" under the provisions of the Act or any other law.

This subtopic lists those specific situations where a party is legally freed from their liability to perform.

A contract need not be performed in the following circumstances:

### 1️⃣ By Agreement (Novation, Rescission, Alteration - Section 62) 
The parties who made the contract can mutually agree to end it. If they do, the original contract _need not be performed_. This can happen in three ways:

- **Novation:** The parties agree to **substitute** the original contract with a new one.
    
- **Rescission:** The parties agree to **cancel** the original contract entirely.
    
- **Alteration:** The parties agree to **change** one or more of the material terms of the original contract.
    

### 2️⃣ By Remission (Promisee Dispenses with Performance - Section 63) 
The _promisee_ (the person to whom the promise is owed) can unilaterally excuse the promisor from performing. This section gives the promisee the power to:

- **Dispense with or remit** performance, either wholly or in part (e.g., "You don't have to paint my portrait after all").
    
- **Extend the time** for performance (e.g., "You can pay me next month").
    
- **Accept any other satisfaction** instead of performance (e.g., A owes B 5,000 Taka. B accepts A's 2,000 Taka payment in _full satisfaction_ of the entire debt).
    

### 3️⃣ By Supervening Impossibility (Frustration - Section 56) 
A contract _need not be performed_ if the act becomes impossible or unlawful _after_ the contract is made.

- **The Rule:** A contract to do an act that becomes **impossible** (e.g., the subject matter is destroyed) or **unlawful** (e.g., a new law bans the act) _after_ formation, due to an event the promisor couldn't prevent, **becomes void** when the act becomes impossible or unlawful.
    
- **Example:** A contracts to rent a music hall to B for a concert. The day before the concert, the hall is destroyed by fire. The contract becomes void. A _need not perform_ (provide the hall), and B _need not perform_ (pay the rent).
    

### 4️⃣ By Rescission of a Voidable Contract (Section 64) 
As we saw in Topic 2.8, a contract formed by coercion, fraud, or misrepresentation is **voidable**.

- **The Rule:** When the innocent party chooses to **rescind** (cancel) the voidable contract, the _other_ party _need not perform_ any promise therein.
    

### 5️⃣ By Promisee's Neglect (Section 67) 
If the _promisee_ refuses or neglects to provide the _promisor_ with reasonable facilities for performance, the promisor is excused.

- **The Rule:** The promisor is **excused** by such neglect or refusal for any non-performance _caused thereby_.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 67):** A contracts with B to repair B's house. B _neglects_ to point out to A the places in which his house requires repair. A is _excused_ for the non-performance of the contract _if_ it is caused by B's neglect.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & tips

- **Discharge vs. Breach:** This topic is about "Discharge of Contract" (ending the contract) by means _other than_ breach. These are all _lawful excuses_ for non-performance.
    
- **Key Sections:** For this topic, the most important sections to remember are **Section 56 (Frustration)** and **Sections 62 & 63 (Discharge by Agreement)**, as they are the most common ways performance is excused.
    
- **Section 63 is Powerful:** Remember that under Section 63, a promisee's agreement to accept less (e.g., 2,000 Taka for a 5,000 Taka debt) is a valid discharge _without_ any new consideration being required.
