## 📌 4.7: Time and place of performance (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This topic provides the "operating manual" for performance. The rules for **"when"** and **"where"** a promise must be fulfilled are critical for determining if a promisor has performed correctly or if they are in breach.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. These sections (46-50) are frequently used in problem questions to determine if a performance or an "offer to perform" was valid. You'll often be asked to analyze a situation where no time or place was specified.
    

This subtopic covers the default rules for **when (time)** and **where (place)** a promisor must fulfill their obligation. These rules are crucial because a performance done at the wrong time or in the wrong place is not a valid performance and can be a breach of contract.

The rules are laid out in **Sections 46-50 of the Contract Act, 1872**. They provide a logical flowchart based on what the contract says.

---

### 🔷 The Rules for "Time"

***1. No Time is Specified (Section 46):***

- **Rule:** If the contract doesn't set a deadline and the promisor must perform _without_ being asked by the promisee, the engagement must be performed within a **"reasonable time."**
    
- **"Reasonable time"** is not defined; it is a **"question of fact"** that depends on the specific case. For example, the reasonable time for delivering fresh fruit is very short, while for building a house, it is very long.
    

***2. A Specific Time is Specified (Section 47):***

- **Rule:** If the contract specifies a _certain day_ for performance, the promisor must perform on that day but only during the **"usual hours of business"** and at the specified place.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 47):** A promises to deliver goods to B's warehouse on January 1st. A brings the goods at 10 PM, _after_ the warehouse has closed. A has _not_ performed his promise, as it was not during usual business hours.
    

---

### 🔷 The Rules for "Place"

***3. When the Promisee Has the Duty to Request (Section 48):***

- **Rule:** This applies if the contract states that the promisor will perform on a certain day, but _only after_ the promisee "applies for" (requests) performance.
    
- **Duty:** It is the **duty of the promisee** to make that request at a **"proper place"** and within the **"usual hours of business."**
    
- **Example:** The contract says, "A will deliver 100 bags of rice on Friday _upon B's request_." It is now B's (the promisee's) job to call A and specify a proper time and place for the delivery.
    

***4. When the Promisor Has the Duty to Request (Section 49):***

- **Rule:** This is the most common default rule. It applies when the contract does _not_ fix a place for performance, and the promisor must perform _without_ being asked.
    
- **Duty:** It is the **duty of the promisor** to **"apply to the promisee to appoint a reasonable place"** for performance, and then perform it there.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 49):** A promises to deliver 1,000 maunds of jute to B on a fixed day (but no place is mentioned). A cannot just wait for B to call. A must proactively call B and ask, "Where would you like me to deliver the jute?" B must then name a reasonable place.
    

### 🔷 The Ultimate Rule: What the Promisee Wants

***5. Performance in Manner/Time Prescribed by Promisee (Section 50):***

- **Rule:** This final section overrides all the others. The promisor must perform in _any manner_ or at _any time_ that the **promisee prescribes or sanctions**.
    
- **Practical Meaning:** The promisee (the person receiving the promise) has the final say.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. (d) to S. 50):** A owes B 100 Taka. B (the promisee) asks A to send the money via a postal note. A posts the letter with the note, but the letter is lost. The debt is **discharged** because A performed _exactly_ in the manner B prescribed.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Logical Flowchart:** For any exam problem, follow this logic:
    
    1. Did the **promisee** prescribe the time/place? (Section 50) $\rightarrow$ If yes, follow that.
        
    2. If not, did the **contract** fix the time/place? (Section 47) $\rightarrow$ If yes, follow that (during business hours).
        
    3. If not, does the _promisee_ have to apply? (Section 48) $\rightarrow$ If yes, it's the promisee's duty.
        
    4. If not, does the _promisor_ have to apply? (Section 49) $\rightarrow$ If yes (this is the most common default), it's the promisor's duty to ask.
        
    5. If no time is specified at all? (Section 46) $\rightarrow$ Must be done in a "reasonable time."
        
- **"Reasonable Time" and "Usual Hours":** These are the key phrases. Performance at an unreasonable time (like 3 AM) is not valid.
    
- **Debtor Must Find the Creditor:** This is the common law summary of **Section 49**. The person who owes the duty (the promisor/debtor) is responsible for finding the person to whom it is owed (the promisee/creditor) to arrange performance.
