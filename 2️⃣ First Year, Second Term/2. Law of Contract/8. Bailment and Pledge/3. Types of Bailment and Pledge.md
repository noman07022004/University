## 8.3: Types (of Bailment) (Overall: ⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This subtopic provides the classification system for bailment. This is essential for understanding _why_ certain rules apply, particularly the different duties of a bailor in a gratuitous vs. a non-gratuitous bailment (Section 150) and the rules for termination (Sections 159 & 162).
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐):** Less important as a standalone question. It is rare to get a direct question like "List the types of bailment." This subtopic is background knowledge that you use to answer more complex questions about a bailee's liability or a bailor's duties.


A bailment, which is the temporary transfer of possession of goods, can be classified in two primary ways:

### 1. Classification Based on Reward (Consideration)

This is the most important classification under the **Contract Act, 1872**, as it directly affects the rights and duties of the parties.

**a) Gratuitous Bailment:**

- **Definition:** A bailment made **without any consideration** or reward. Neither the bailor nor the bailee receives any payment or remuneration. It is done as a favor.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Lending a book to a friend for free.
        
    - Asking a neighbor to hold your house keys or a package for you while you are away (for free).
        
- **Key Legal Rules:**
    
    - **Bailor's Duty (Section 150):** The bailor only has a duty to disclose faults that he is _aware of_.
        
    - **Termination (Section 159):** The bailor can demand the return of the goods _at any time_, even before the specified purpose is complete.
        
    - **Termination (Section 162):** A gratuitous bailment terminates _automatically_ on the death of either the bailor or the bailee.
        

**b) Non-Gratuitous Bailment (or "Bailment for Reward"):**

- **Definition:** A bailment where **consideration** passes between the parties. Either the bailor pays the bailee, or the bailee pays the bailor.
    
- **Examples:**
    
    - Giving your car to a mechanic for repair (you _pay_ for the service).
        
    - Hiring a car from a rental company (you _pay_ for the use).
        
    - Giving your clothes to a dry cleaner (you _pay_ for the service).
        
- **Key Legal Rule (Section 150):** The bailor has a _stricter_ duty. They are responsible for losses caused by _any_ faults in the goods, even faults they were _not aware of_.
    

### 2. Classification Based on Benefit (Classical)

This is the more traditional, academic way of classifying bailment, which focuses on _who_ receives the benefit of the transaction.

**a) Bailment for the Exclusive Benefit of the Bailor:**

- **What it is:** The bailee does a favor for the bailor, for free.
    
- **Example:** You ask your neighbor to look after your cat for free while you are on vacation.
    
- **(This is a _Gratuitous Bailment_)**
    

**b) Bailment for the Exclusive Benefit of the Bailee:**

- **What it is:** The bailor does a favor for the bailee, for free.
    
- **Example:** You ask your friend to lend you their motorcycle for the weekend for free.
    
- **(This is also a _Gratuitous Bailment_)**
    

**c) Bailment for the Mutual Benefit of Both:**

- **What it is:** Both parties get some benefit from the contract (this is the most common type of commercial bailment).
    
- **Example:** You take your watch to a shop for repair. You (bailor) get the benefit of a fixed watch. The watchmaker (bailee) gets the benefit of being paid for the service.
    
- **(This is a _Non-Gratuitous Bailment_)**
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Duty of Care is Uniform:** This is a crucial point for exams. In old English law, the _standard of care_ was different for each type of bailment (e.g., a gratuitous bailee only had to avoid "gross negligence").
    
- **The Bangladeshi (and Indian) law in Section 151 simplifies this.** It imposes a **uniform standard of care** for **"all cases of bailment"** (both gratuitous and non-gratuitous). The standard is always that of a **"man of ordinary prudence"** taking care of his own goods.
    
- **"Pledge" is a Type:** Remember that a Pledge (Topic 8.8) is a special type of bailment for the _mutual benefit_ of both parties (a non-gratuitous bailment).
