## 8.6: Rights and duties of finder of goods (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This topic is a perfect real-world application of **Quasi-Contract (Topic 3)** and **Bailment (Topic 8)**. It shows how the law creates a "bailment" by legal fiction to ensure fairness.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is a very common topic for short notes or a self-contained problem question (e.g., "A finds a lost wallet. What are his rights and duties?").


A **"finder of goods"** is a person who finds goods belonging to another and takes them into their custody.

The law on this topic is a fantastic blend of Quasi-Contract and Bailment principles.

### The Finder's Legal Position (Quasi-Contract)

First, the law must establish a legal relationship. This is done by **Section 71 of the Contract Act, 1872** (which is in the Quasi-Contract chapter).

- **The Rule (Section 71):** "A person who finds goods belonging to another and takes them into his custody, is subject to the **same responsibility as a bailee**."
    
- **Effect:** The finder is a **"bailee by implication"** or a "quasi-bailee." Even though there is no contract, the law _imposes_ the duties of a bailee on the finder to prevent them from simply keeping the goods and to protect the true owner.
    

---

### Duties of a Finder of Goods

Because the finder is treated as a bailee, they have all the **duties of a bailee** (as discussed in Topic 8.4):

1. **Duty to Take Reasonable Care (Section 151):** The finder must take the same care of the goods as a "man of ordinary prudence" would take of his own goods.
    
2. **Duty to Find the True Owner:** The finder must take reasonable steps to trace the original owner.
    
3. **Duty Not to Make Personal Use:** The finder must not use the goods for their own personal purposes.
    
4. **Duty Not to Mix:** The finder must not mix the found goods with their own goods.
    
5. **Duty to Return the Goods:** The finder must return the goods to the true owner when the owner is found.
    

---

### Rights of a Finder of Goods

The law also gives the finder specific rights to encourage honesty, which are detailed in **Sections 168 and 169**.

**1. Right of Lien (Section 168):**

- **The Rule:** The finder has a **particular lien** over the goods. This means they can _retain_ the goods against the true owner until they receive compensation for the "trouble and expense" incurred in preserving the goods and finding the owner.
    
- **Limitation:** The finder **cannot** _sue_ the owner to pay for these expenses. Their only right is to _keep_ the goods until they are paid.
    

**2. Right to Sue for a Reward (Section 168):**

- **The Rule:** This is different from expenses. If the owner has offered a **specific reward** for the return of the goods, the finder _can_ sue the owner for that reward. They can also _retain_ the goods until the reward is paid.
    

**3. Right to Sell the Goods (Section 169):** This is a very important right. The finder can legally sell the found goods in two specific situations:

- **(a)** If the owner **cannot be found** with "reasonable diligence"; **OR**
    
- **(b)** If the owner _is_ found but **refuses** to pay the lawful charges of the finder.
    

And, in _addition_ to (a) or (b), one of the following must _also_ be true:

1. The item is **"in danger of perishing"** or losing the greater part of its value (e.g., a box of fresh fruit).
    
2. The finder's **"lawful charges"** (for preservation and finding the owner) amount to **two-thirds (2/3) of the item's value**.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Quasi-Bailee:** Always remember that the finder's position is a **quasi-contractual bailment** created by **Section 71**.
    
- **Lien vs. Sue:** This is a classic exam distinction.
    
    - For **Expenses** (e.g., feeding a lost animal): The finder has a _Lien_ (right to keep) but **cannot sue**.
        
    - For a **Reward** (e.g., "5,000 Taka for my lost dog"): The finder has _both_ a Lien **and** the right to sue.
        
- **Right to Sell:** The right to sell (Section 169) is not absolute. The finder _must_ first try to find the owner or get them to pay. The right to sell only activates if that fails _and_ the goods are perishable or the costs are too high.
