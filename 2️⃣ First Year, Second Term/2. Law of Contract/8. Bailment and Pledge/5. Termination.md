## 8.5: Termination (of Bailment) (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This subtopic completes the "lifecycle" of a bailment, outlining the different ways it can legally end. It's essential for distinguishing the specific rules that apply to "gratuitous" versus "non-gratuitous" bailments.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. The rules for terminating a _gratuitous_ bailment (by early return or death) are common in short-note questions and as a part of problem questions.

A contract of bailment is **terminated** (or discharged) when the legal relationship between the bailor and bailee comes to an end. This can happen in several ways:

**1. On the Completion of the Purpose (Section 160)** This is the most natural way for a bailment to end.

- **The Rule:** The bailee is bound to return the goods "as soon as... the purpose for which they were bailed has been accomplished."
    
- **Example:** You give your watch to a shop for repair. When the repair is finished (the purpose is accomplished), the bailment is over, and the duty to return the watch is triggered.
    

**2. On the Expiry of the Time (Section 160)**

- **The Rule:** If the bailment was for a specific period (e.g., "renting a car for 3 days"), it terminates "as soon as the time... has expired."
    
- **Example:** You rent a car for three days. At the end of the third day, the bailment is terminated, and you must return the car.
    

**3. By the Bailee's Inconsistent Act (Section 153)** This is a termination by breach.

- **The Rule:** A contract of bailment is **"voidable at the option of the bailor"** if the bailee does _any act_ with the goods that is "inconsistent with the conditions" of the bailment.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 153):** A lets a horse to B for B's _own riding_. B drives the horse in his carriage. This act is inconsistent with the purpose ("own riding"). A has the right to immediately terminate the bailment.
    

**4. By Destruction of the Subject Matter** A bailment, like any contract, can be terminated if the subject matter is destroyed.

- **The Rule:** If the goods are destroyed _without any fault_ of the bailee (and the bailee has taken "ordinary prudent care" as per Section 151), the contract is terminated, and the bailee is excused from returning the goods (Section 152).
    
- **Example:** You are storing your friend's furniture (a gratuitous bailment). Your house is struck by lightning and burns down, destroying the furniture. You are not at fault. The bailment is terminated.
    

**5. Termination of a Gratuitous Bailment (Sections 159 & 162)** The law provides special, flexible rules for _gratuitous_ bailments (those made for free).

- **By the Bailor's Wish (Section 159):** The bailor ("lender") of a thing for free use may require its return _at any time_, even if it was lent for a specified time or purpose.
    
    - **Limitation:** If the bailee ("borrower") acted in reliance on the loan and would suffer a loss _greater_ than the benefit they received, the bailor must _indemnify_ (compensate) the bailee for that loss if they compel an early return.
        
- **By Death (Section 162):** A gratuitous bailment is terminated _automatically_ by the death of **either** the bailor **or** the bailee.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Gratuitous vs. Non-Gratuitous:** The most important distinction for termination is:
    
    - **Gratuitous (Free):** Can be terminated _at any time_ by the bailor (S. 159) and _automatically_ by the death of either party (S. 162).
        
    - **Non-Gratuitous (Paid):** Cannot be terminated at will without a breach. The death of a party does _not_ terminate it; the obligations pass to their legal representatives.
        
- **Voidable vs. Void:**
    
    - Termination by the bailee's inconsistent act (S. 153) makes the contract **voidable** (the bailor _chooses_ to end it).
        
    - Termination by death in a gratuitous bailment (S. 162) is **automatic** and makes the contract **void**.
