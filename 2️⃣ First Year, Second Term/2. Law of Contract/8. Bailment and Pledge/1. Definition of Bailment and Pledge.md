## 8.1: Definition (Bailment and Pledge) (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. These two definitions (Section 148 and Section 172) are the legal foundation for the entire topic. All the other subtopics (rights, duties, termination) are based on the legal relationship established by these definitions.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. It is common for an exam question to ask you to "Define Bailment" or "Define Pledge" as part of a larger question, especially the classic "Define bailment and pledge and distinguish between them."


This subtopic defines the two key legal concepts for this chapter, both of which are found in **Chapter IX of the Contract Act, 1872**.

### 1. Definition of Bailment

**Section 148 of the Contract Act, 1872,** defines "Bailment" and its parties:

> "A **'bailment'** is the **delivery of goods** by one person to another for some **purpose**, upon a **contract** that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be **returned** or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them."

**The Parties:**

- The person _delivering_ the goods is called the **"Bailor"**.
    
- The person _to whom_ they are delivered is called the **"Bailee"**.
    

**Examples of Bailment:**

- Giving your car to a mechanic for repair. (You are the Bailor; the mechanic is the Bailee).
    
- Lending a book to a friend. (You are the Bailor; your friend is the Bailee).
    
- Giving your clothes to a dry cleaner.
    
- Delivering goods to a transport company for shipment.
    

### 2. Definition of Pledge

**Section 172 of the Contract Act, 1872,** defines "Pledge" and its parties:

> "The **bailment of goods as security** for payment of a **debt** or performance of a **promise** is called **'pledge'**."

**The Parties:**

- The _bailor_ (the person delivering the goods as security) is called the **"Pawnor"**.
    
- The _bailee_ (the person receiving the goods as security) is called the **"Pawnee"**.
    

**Example of Pledge:**

- You take a loan from a bank and deliver your gold jewelry to the bank as security until you repay the loan. (You are the Pawnor; the bank is the Pawnee).
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Pledge is a _Special Kind_ of Bailment:** The most important thing to understand is that a **pledge is a type of bailment**. It has all the characteristics of a bailment (delivery of movable goods for a purpose).
    
- **The Key Difference:** The "purpose" of a general bailment can be anything (safekeeping, repair, use). The _only_ purpose of a **pledge** is to serve as **security** for a debt or the performance of a promise.
    
- **Core Concept:** Both concepts revolve around the transfer of **possession**, not **ownership**. In both cases, the Bailor/Pawnor remains the owner of the goods.
