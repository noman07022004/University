## 8.9: Rights of pledgor and pawnee (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic contains the _entire purpose_ of a pledge. The pawnee's "right to sell" and the pawnor's "right to redeem" are the two critical, opposing rights that define this entire transaction.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** High importance. This is a very common topic for problem questions. A typical scenario involves a debtor (pawnor) defaulting on a loan and the creditor (pawnee) wanting to sell the security. You must be able to advise both parties on their rights under Sections 176 and 177.


This subtopic outlines the specific rights given to both parties in a pledge (pawn) transaction. These rights are defined in **Sections 173 to 177 of the Contract Act, 1872**.

### 1. Rights of the Pawnee (The Creditor/Lender)

The pawnee (the person who _receives_ the goods) is given several rights to ensure their loan is secure.

**a) Right of Retainer (Section 173):**

- **The Rule:** The pawnee has the right to **retain** (keep) the pledged goods until the debt, the interest on the debt, and any "necessary expenses" for preserving the goods are paid.
    
- This is a **particular lien**, meaning the pawnee can only keep the goods for the _specific loan_ they are security for, not for other, unrelated debts (unless a new contract is made).
    

**b) Right to Extraordinary Expenses (Section 175):**

- **The Rule:** The pawnee is entitled to be reimbursed for any **"extraordinary expenses"** they incurred to preserve the goods.
    
- **Example:** If the pledged good is a live animal (e.g., a horse) and it gets sick, the pawnee can recover the veterinary bills from the pawnor.
    

**c) The Right in Case of Default (Section 176):**

- This is the pawnee's **most important right** and the main remedy of a pledge.
    
- **The Rule:** If the pawnor (debtor) **defaults** (fails to pay) at the stipulated time, the pawnee has **two options**:
    
    1. **Sue the Pawnor:** They can file a lawsuit against the pawnor for the debt _and_ **retain the goods** as collateral security; **OR**
        
    2. **Sell the Goods:** They can **sell the thing pledged** after giving the pawnor **"reasonable notice"** of the sale.
        
- **After the Sale (Section 176):**
    
    - If the sale proceeds are _less_ than the debt owed, the pawnor is **still liable to pay the balance**.
        
    - If the sale proceeds are _more_ than the debt owed, the pawnee **must pay the surplus** back to the pawnor.
        

---

### 2. Rights of the Pawnor (The Debtor/Borrower)

The pawnor (the person who _gives_ the goods) has several key rights, even after they have defaulted.

**a) The Right to Redeem (Section 177):**

- This is the pawnor's **most important right**.
    
- **The Rule:** If the pawnor defaults on payment, they _still_ have the right to **redeem** (get back) their goods at _any subsequent time_ **before the "actual sale"** of them.
    
- **Condition:** To redeem, the pawnor must pay the full debt _plus_ any expenses that have arisen from their default (e.g., late fees).
    

**b) Right to Preservation and Maintenance:**

- As the pledgor is still the _owner_, they have the right to have their goods preserved. The pawnee has a duty (as a bailee) to take reasonable care of the goods, and the pawnor can enforce this.
    

**c) Right to Receive Surplus:**

- As stated in Section 176, if the pawnee sells the goods, the pawnor has a legal right to receive any surplus money from the sale.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **S. 176 vs. S. 177:** This is the core conflict. The Pawnee has a **right to sell** (Sec 176), and the Pawnor has a **right to redeem** (Sec 177). The "actual sale" is the event that extinguishes the right to redeem.
    
- **"Reasonable Notice" is Mandatory:** The pawnee's right to sell _only_ becomes active after they have given "reasonable notice" to the pawnor. A sale _without_ proper notice is an invalid sale, and the pawnee may be liable for conversion.
    
- **Pledge vs. Lien:** A regular bailee's **lien** (Topic 8.4) only gives them the right to _keep_ the goods. A **pawnee's** rights are much stronger, as they include the right to _sell_ the goods.
