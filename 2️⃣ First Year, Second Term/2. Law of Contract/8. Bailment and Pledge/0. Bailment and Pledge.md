## 8. Bailment and Pledge ⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic governs everyday transactions like dry cleaning, parking cars, or leaving a laptop for repair. Understanding the distinction between "Possession" and "Ownership" is key here.
>- **Exam (⭐ 3/5):** Questions often focus on the **Duty of Care** (Section 151)—did the person take reasonable care of the goods?—and the specific rights of a **Finder of Goods**. "Pledge vs. Bailment" is a common theoretical question.


---

**Bailment** is the technical legal term for "changing hands" of goods without "changing ownership." It is governed by **Chapter IX (Sections 148–181)** of the **Contract Act, 1872**.

A **Pledge** (or Pawn) is simply a specific, stricter type of bailment used for securing a loan.

### A. Definition and Nature of Bailment

_(Covering: Definition, Characteristics, Types)_

**1. Definition (Section 148)** A "Bailment" is the delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them.

- **Parties:**
    
    - **Bailor:** The person delivering the goods (e.g., You giving your cloth to a tailor).
        
    - **Bailee:** The person to whom they are delivered (e.g., The tailor).
        

**2. Essential Characteristics**

- **Delivery of Possession:** Possession must move from Bailor to Bailee. Merely leaving goods in a room where you keep the key is _not_ bailment (e.g., a bank locker).
    
- **Specific Purpose:** It is temporary (e.g., for repair, safe custody, transport).
    
- **Return of Goods:** The _same_ goods (or altered form, like cloth into a suit) must be returned. If the goods are sold or consumed, it is not bailment.
    

**3. Types of Bailment**

- **Gratuitous Bailment:** No consideration involved (e.g., lending a book to a friend).
    
- **Non-Gratuitous (Reward) Bailment:** Consideration is paid (e.g., hiring a car, giving a watch for repair).
    

### B. Rights and Duties of Bailor and Bailee

_(Covering: Rights and duties of a bailee and a bailor)_

**1. Duties of the Bailor (Section 150)**

- **Duty to Disclose Faults:**
    
    - _Gratuitous:_ Must disclose _known_ faults. If not, liable for damages.
        
    - _For Reward:_ Responsible for _all_ faults, whether known or unknown. (Strict Liability).
        
- **Duty to Indemnify:** Must repay the Bailee for necessary expenses incurred for the purpose of the bailment.
    

**2. Duties of the Bailee (The "Care" Standard)**

- **Duty of Care (Section 151):** The Bailee is bound to take as much care of the goods as a **man of ordinary prudence** would take of his own goods of similar value. If this standard is met, the Bailee is _not liable_ for loss (Section 152).
    
- **Duty not to make Unauthorized Use (Section 154):** If used unauthorizedly, the Bailee is liable for _any_ damage, even if accidental.
    
- **Duty not to Mix Goods (Section 155-157):** Should not mix Bailor's goods with their own without consent.
    
- **Duty to Return (Section 160):** Must return goods on time. If delayed, the Bailee becomes an "insurer" and is liable for any loss thereafter.
    

**3. Rights of the Bailee (Lien)**

- **Particular Lien (Section 170):** The Bailee can retain the specific goods on which they have exercised skill or labor until paid (e.g., a jeweler repairing a ring).
    
- **General Lien (Section 171):** Bankers, factors, wharfingers, and attorneys can retain _any_ goods to settle a general balance of account.
    

### C. The Finder of Goods

_(Covering: Rights and duties of finder of goods)_

A finder of lost goods is treated exactly like a **Bailee** (Section 71).

**Rights of a Finder (Sections 168–169):**

1. **Right of Lien:** Can keep the goods until compensated for expenses/trouble found in finding the owner (but cannot sue for this money).
    
2. **Right to Sue for Reward:** If a specific reward was offered, the finder can sue for it.
    
3. **Right to Sell:** The finder can sell the goods **if**:
    
    - The owner cannot be found despite reasonable diligence; OR
        
    - The owner refuses to pay lawful charges; AND
        
    - The goods are perishable; OR
        
    - The lawful charges amount to **two-thirds** of the value of the goods.
        

### D. Termination and Suits

_(Covering: Termination, Suits against wrongdoer)_

**1. Termination of Bailment**

- **Efflux of Time:** When the fixed period expires.
    
- **Accomplishment of Purpose:** When the repair/transport is done.
    
- **Inconsistent Act (Section 153):** If the Bailee does something inconsistent with the conditions (e.g., selling the car they rented), the Bailor can terminate immediately.
    
- **Death:** A gratuitous bailment terminates on the death of either party.
    

**2. Suits against Wrongdoers (Section 180)** If a third party injures the goods or deprives the Bailee of use:

- Either the **Bailor** or the **Bailee** may sue the third party.
    
- Whatever compensation is obtained is divided between them according to their respective interests.
    

### E. Pledge (Bailment by way of Pawn)

_(Covering: Bailment of way of pledge or pawn, Rights of pledgor and pawnee)_

**1. Definition (Section 172)** The bailment of goods as **security for payment of a debt** or performance of a promise is called "Pledge".

- **Pawnor (Pledgor):** The person who delivers the goods (Borrower).
    
- **Pawnee (Pledgee):** The person who receives the goods (Lender/Bank).
    

**2. Distinction from Ordinary Bailment** The Pawnee has a stronger right than an ordinary Bailee: they have the **Right to Sell** the goods on default, whereas an ordinary Bailee generally only has a **Right of Lien** (Right to retain).

**3. Rights of Pawnee (Lender)**

- **Right of Retainer (Section 173):** Can keep goods not just for the debt, but for interest and expenses.
    
- **Right to Extraordinary Expenses:** Can sue to recover costs for preserving the goods (e.g., feeding a pledged horse).
    
- **Right on Default (Section 176):** If the Pawnor fails to pay:
    
    - The Pawnee can sue the Pawnor and retain the goods as collateral.
        
    - OR, the Pawnee can **sell the goods** after giving **reasonable notice** to the Pawnor.
        

**4. Rights of Pawnor (Borrower)**

- **Right to Redeem (Section 177):** Can get the goods back any time before the actual sale by paying the debt + interest + expenses.
    
- **Right to Surplus:** If the Pawnee sells the goods for more than the debt, the surplus money must be paid back to the Pawnor.
    

---

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **Section 151 (Standard of Care):** This is the "Golden Rule" of bailment. The Bailee is _not_ an insurer. If the Bailee took reasonable care (like a prudent man), and the goods are stolen or burned, the Bailee is **not liable**.
    
- **Bank Lockers:** Technically, hiring a bank locker is often argued _not_ to be bailment because the bank does not have exclusive possession (they don't have your key). It is a relationship of Landlord and Tenant. However, if you hand over jewelry to the bank manager for "Safe Custody," that _is_ bailment.
    
- **Finder's Sale:** Memorize the "Two-Thirds Rule" (Section 169). A finder can sell if expenses = 2/3 of value.
    
- **Pledge Notice:** The "Reasonable Notice" of sale (Section 176) is mandatory. A sale without notice is void and constitutes conversion.
