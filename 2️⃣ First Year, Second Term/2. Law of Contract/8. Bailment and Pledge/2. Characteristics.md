## 8.2: Characteristics (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. The "characteristics" are the legal _checklist_ or _essential elements_ of a bailment. The "Definition" (8.1) is the rule, and the "Characteristics" (8.2) are the _components_ of that rule. You must know these to identify a bailment in a problem scenario.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is frequently combined with 8.1. An exam question will often be, "Define bailment and explain its essential characteristics."
 

The characteristics (or essential elements) of a bailment are the set of conditions that must _all_ be met for the transaction to be legally considered a bailment under **Section 148 of the Contract Act, 1872**. These are derived directly from the definition.

**1. Delivery of Possession** This is the most important characteristic.

- **Possession, Not Ownership:** Bailment is _only_ about the temporary transfer of **possession** and control. The bailor remains the full owner.
    
- **Custody vs. Possession:** This is a key distinction. Mere "custody" without legal possession is not a bailment. For example, a servant holding their master's umbrella is in _custody_, but the master still has legal possession. A guest using a host's plate is not a bailee.
    
- **Types of Delivery (Section 149):** The delivery can be:
    
    - **Actual Delivery:** Physically handing over the goods (e.g., giving your car to a mechanic).
        
    - **Constructive Delivery:** A symbolic act that transfers control (e.g., handing over the _keys_ to a car or the _key_ to a warehouse where the goods are stored).
        

**2. A Contract (Express or Implied)** The delivery of goods must be based on a contract between the bailor and the bailee.

- **Express Contract:** The terms are clearly stated, either in writing or orally (e.g., a formal car rental agreement).
    
- **Implied Contract:** The contract is inferred from the actions of the parties (e.g., handing your coat to a cloakroom attendant).
    
- **Exception (Quasi-Contract):** The law _implies_ a bailment in one key situation: a **Finder of Lost Goods (Section 71)**. The finder becomes a bailee by operation of law, even without a contract with the owner.
    

**3. Delivery for a Specific Purpose** The goods must be delivered for a specific, agreed-upon purpose.

- **Examples:** Safekeeping (in a locker), repair (to a mechanic), transport (to a courier), or for the bailee's own use (lending a book).
    

**4. Return of the _Specific_ Goods** This is a fundamental characteristic. The bailee is obligated to return the _identical goods_ that were delivered, although their form may be altered.

- **Example:** If A bails cloth to B (a tailor), B must return a suit made from that _specific_ cloth.
    
- **This is _not_ a bailment:** If A gives B a bag of rice and B is only required to return _a_ bag of rice (not the _same_ bag), it is not a bailment.
    
- **The "Money in a Bank" Rule:** Depositing money in a bank account is **not a bailment**. The bank does not return the _exact same_ currency notes. It returns an _equivalent amount_. The relationship is one of **debtor and creditor**.
    

**5. Movable Goods** Bailment only applies to **movable goods** (chattels). It does not apply to:

- **Immovable Property (Land):** A lease of land is not a bailment.
    
- **Money (Currency):** As explained above, money is not "bailed," it is "loaned."
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Possession is the Key:** The entire concept hinges on the transfer of _possession_. If the owner retains control, it is not a bailment.
    
    - **Parking Lot Example:**
        
        - **Bailment:** You give your car keys to a _valet_ parker. You have transferred possession and control.
            
        - **Not Bailment (License):** You park your car in a self-park garage, keep your keys, and lock the car. You have merely rented a _space_ (a "license"); you have not transferred possession to the garage owner.
            
- **Locker Example:** Renting a bank locker is _not_ a bailment, because the bank does not have full possession of the contents (it requires both your key and the bank's key to open).
