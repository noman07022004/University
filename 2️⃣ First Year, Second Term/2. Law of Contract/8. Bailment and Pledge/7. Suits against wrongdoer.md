
## 8.7: Suits against wrongdoer (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This topic is a key part of the "rights" of the bailor and bailee. It answers the practical question: "If a third party damages my bailed property, who gets to sue them?"
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐):** Less common. This is a technical, procedural point that is good for a short note or as a minor part of a larger problem question, but it's not as foundational as the duties of the bailee.


This subtopic deals with the rights of the bailor and bailee against a **"wrongdoer"** (a third party) who interferes with the bailed goods.

What happens if A (bailor) gives his car to B (bailee) for repair, and C (a wrongdoer) negligently crashes into the car while it's parked at B's garage? Who can sue C?

The law on this is clearly laid out in **Sections 180 and 181 of the Contract Act, 1872**.

### 1. The Right to Sue (Section 180)

**The Rule:** "If a third person wrongfully deprives the bailee of the use or possession of the goods bailed, or does them any injury, the **bailee is entitled to use such remedies as the owner** might have used... and **either the bailor or the bailee may bring a suit** against a third person for such deprivation or injury."

This is a very powerful and important right. It means:

- The **bailee** (who is only the possessor) has the right to sue the wrongdoer for the _full value of the goods_ or the _full extent of the damage_, just as if they were the owner.
    
- The **bailor** (the owner) _also_ has the right to sue.
    
- The wrongdoer (C) cannot use the defense, "You're just the bailee, you don't own the car, so you can't sue me." Section 180 explicitly prevents this defense.
    

This rule is for the convenience of justice, allowing the party in possession (the bailee) to immediately take action to protect the goods.

### 2. The Apportionment of Compensation (Section 181)

This section answers the next logical question: If the bailee sues and wins the money, what do they do with it?

**The Rule:** "Whatever is obtained by way of relief or compensation in any such suit shall, as between the bailor and the bailee, be **dealt with according to their respective interests**."

- **Meaning:** The person who sues (whether it's the bailor or bailee) must share the money with the other party based on their financial stake in the goods.
    
- **Example:**
    
    - B (bailee) has a 10,000 Taka repair charge (a "lien") on A's (bailor's) car.
        
    - C (wrongdoer) destroys the car, which is valued at 500,000 Taka.
        
    - B (the bailee) sues C and recovers the full 500,000 Taka.
        
    - Under Section 181, B must "apportion" this money. B is entitled to keep 10,000 Taka to satisfy his "respective interest" (the repair bill), and he must hand over the remaining 490,000 Taka to A (the owner).
        

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **A Single Lawsuit:** The purpose of this rule is to allow a single lawsuit to resolve the entire matter. The wrongdoer is not forced to face two separate lawsuits (one from the bailee for loss of possession, and one from the bailor for loss of ownership).
    
- **Bailee Sues on Behalf of Bailor:** In effect, when the bailee sues for the full value, they are acting as the representative for the bailor for the portion of the value that exceeds their own personal interest (like repair charges or lien).
