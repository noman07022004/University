## 8.8: Bailment of way of pledge or pawn (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic defines "Pledge," which is a specific _type_ of bailment. Understanding that a pledge _is_ a bailment, but one with a special purpose (security), is the key to this entire section.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This section (Section 172) is the foundational definition you must use to answer any question about pledge, pawn, or the rights of a pawnee.



This subtopic introduces the legal definition of a **Pledge**, also commonly known as a **Pawn**. A pledge is _not_ a separate concept from bailment; it is a **special kind of bailment**.

This means that **all the essential characteristics of a bailment (Topic 8.2) must also be present in a pledge**.

The definition is provided in **Section 172 of the Contract Act, 1872**:

> "The **bailment of goods as security** for payment of a **debt** or performance of a **promise** is called 'pledge'."

### The Two Essential Components of a Pledge

Based on this definition, a pledge has two unique characteristics that separate it from a general bailment:

1. **It is a Bailment of Goods:** All the rules of bailment apply. There must be a **delivery of possession** (actual or constructive) of _movable goods_ from one person to another.
    
2. **The Purpose is Security:** The _only_ purpose of a pledge is to provide the goods as **collateral or security** to the person receiving them. This security is for:
    
    - The payment of a debt; or
        
    - The performance of a promise.
        

### Parties to a Pledge (Section 172)

The law gives the parties to a pledge special names to distinguish them from a regular bailment:

- **The "Pawnor" (or "Pledgor"):** This is the _bailor_—the person who delivers the goods as security (i.e., the debtor or borrower).
    
- **The "Pawnee" (or "Pledgee"):** This is the _bailee_—the person who receives the goods as security (i.e., the creditor or lender).
    

**Example:** You (Pawnor) go to a pawn shop (Pawnee) and give them your laptop (the "pledged goods") in exchange for a 15,000 Taka loan. This is a "bailment by way of pledge." The pawn shop holds the laptop as _security_ until you repay the debt.

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Pledge vs. Bailment:**
    
    - **Purpose:** A bailment can be for _any_ purpose (repair, safekeeping, etc.). A pledge is _only_ for security.
        
    - **Consideration:** A bailment can be gratuitous (for free). A pledge is _always_ non-gratuitous (it is security for a debt/promise).
        
    - **Right to Sell:** This is the most important difference. A normal bailee only has a _lien_ (right to keep the goods). A **pawnee** has a special **right to sell** the pledged goods if the pawnor defaults (as we will see in the next subtopic).
