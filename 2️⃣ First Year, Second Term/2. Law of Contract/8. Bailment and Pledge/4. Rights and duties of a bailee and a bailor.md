## 8.4: Rights and duties of a bailee and a bailor (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the "engine" of the entire topic. The definitions are the foundation, but this subtopic provides the _legal consequences_. The rights and duties create the framework for liability and are the entire reason parties go to court over bailments.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is one of the most important subtopics in the entire chapter and a very high-frequency exam question. You will almost certainly be asked to "Explain the duties of a bailee" or solve a problem where you must identify if a bailee's (or bailor's) duty was breached.


The law of bailment creates a set of mutual obligations for both the bailor (the owner) and the bailee (the possessor).

---

### Duties of the Bailee (The Receiver)

The bailee has the primary responsibility, as they are in possession of the other person's goods.

**1. Duty of Reasonable Care (Section 151):**

- This is the **most important duty**.
    
- **The Rule:** "In all cases of bailment," the bailee must take as much care of the goods as a **"man of ordinary prudence"** would take of his _own_ goods of the same type and value.
    
- If the bailee _has_ taken this level of care, they are **not responsible** for the loss, destruction, or damage of the goods (**Section 152**).
    
- **Example:** If a mechanic (bailee) locks your car (bailed goods) in a secure garage, and a thief breaks through the wall and steals it, the mechanic is probably _not_ liable. But if they left the keys in the ignition and it was stolen, they _are_ liable for breaching their duty of care.
    

**2. Duty Not to Make Unauthorized Use (Section 154):**

- **The Rule:** The bailee must use the goods _only_ for the specific purpose for which they were bailed.
    
- **Consequence:** If the bailee makes an unauthorized use, they are liable for _any_ damage that occurs, even if they were not negligent.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. (a) to S. 154):** A lends a horse to B for B's _own_ riding. B allows his family member, C, to ride the horse. C rides with care, but the horse accidentally falls and is injured. B is **liable** to A for the injury.
    

**3. Duty Not to Mix Goods (Sections 155-157):**

- **The Rule:** The bailee must keep the bailor's goods separate from their own.
    
- **Consequence:**
    
    - If mixed _with consent_, the parties share an interest in the mixture.
        
    - If mixed _without consent_ (but can be separated), the bailee must pay the cost of separation.
        
    - If mixed _without consent_ (and _cannot_ be separated), the bailee must **compensate the bailor for the entire loss** of the goods.
        

**4. Duty to Return the Goods (Section 160):**

- **The Rule:** The bailee must return the goods (without the bailor having to ask) as soon as the purpose is accomplished or the time has expired.
    
- **Consequence (Section 161):** If the bailee fails to return the goods at the proper time, they become **responsible for any loss, destruction, or damage** that happens after that point, even if it's not their fault.
    

**5. Duty to Return any "Increase" (Section 163):**

- **The Rule:** The bailee must deliver any natural increase or profit that has "accrued" from the goods.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 163):** A leaves a cow in B's custody. The cow has a calf. B is bound to deliver both the calf and the cow to A.
    

---

### Rights of the Bailee

**1. Right to Necessary Expenses (Section 158):**

- **The Rule:** The bailor must repay the bailee for any "necessary expenses" (both ordinary and extraordinary) for a _gratuitous bailment_. In a _non-gratuitous_ bailment, the bailor is only responsible for "extraordinary" expenses.
    
- **Example:** A lends his car to B for a trip (gratuitous). B has to pay for petrol (ordinary expense). The car's engine breaks down (extraordinary expense). A must reimburse B for the engine repair, but not the petrol.
    

**2. Right of Lien (Sections 170-171):**

- This is the bailee's most powerful right: the right to **retain** the bailor's goods until their payment is made.
    
- **a) Particular Lien (Section 170):** This is the default right for all bailees.
    
    - **The Rule:** The bailee can retain _only the specific goods_ for which they have rendered services involving "the exercise of labour or skill."
        
    - **Example:** A tailor (bailee) is given cloth to stitch into a suit. The tailor can _keep the suit_ until the stitching charges are paid. The tailor _cannot_ keep the bailor's other clothes.
        
- **b) General Lien (Section 171):** This is a much broader, special right.
    
    - **The Rule:** Allows certain types of bailees to retain _all_ the goods of the bailor for _any_ outstanding general balance of account.
        
    - **Who has it?** This right is only available to **Bankers, Factors, Wharfingers, Attorneys of a High Court, and Policy-brokers.**
        

---

### Duties of the Bailor (The Owner)

**1. Duty to Disclose Faults (Section 150):**

- This is the bailor's primary duty and a major exam topic. The duty changes based on the type of bailment.
    
- **Gratuitous Bailment (for free):** The bailor must disclose all faults in the goods that they are **aware of** and which pose extraordinary risks.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. (a) to S. 150):** A _lends_ his horse to B, _knowing_ it is vicious. A does not tell B. The horse runs away and injures B. A is responsible for the damage.
        
- **Non-Gratuitous Bailment (for hire/payment):** The bailor's duty is _stricter_. They are responsible for damages from _all_ faults, **"whether he was or was not aware"** of them.
    
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. (b) to S. 150):** A _hires_ a carriage from B. The carriage is unsafe (e.g., a wheel is about to break), but B _does not know_. A is injured. B is **still responsible** for A's injury.
        

**2. Duty to Repay Expenses (Section 158):**

- (See "Right to Necessary Expenses" under Bailee's rights).
    

**3. Duty to Receive Back the Goods (Section 164):**

- **The Rule:** The bailor must accept the goods back when the bailee returns them at the proper time. If the bailor refuses, they are liable for any expenses the bailee incurs for safekeeping.
    

---

### Rights of the Bailor

**1. Right to Terminate the Bailment (Section 153):**

- **The Rule:** The bailor can terminate the contract _at any time_ if the bailee does any act with the goods that is "inconsistent with the conditions" of the bailment (e.g., makes an unauthorized use).
    

**2. Right to Demand Return (Gratuitous) (Section 159):**

- **The Rule:** In a gratuitous bailment, the bailor can demand the return of the goods at _any time_, even if they were lent for a specific purpose.
    

**3. Right to Increase or Profit (Section 163):**

- (See "Duty to Return any Increase" under Bailee's duties). The bailor has the _right_ to receive any such profits.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **S. 151 (Care) and S. 150 (Faults):** These are the two most important sections in this subtopic.
    
- **Bailee's Duty:** Section 151 - **Uniform standard of care** for _all_ bailments (gratuitous or not).
    
- **Bailor's Duty:** Section 150 - **Different standards of disclosure** for gratuitous (known faults) vs. non-gratuitous (all faults).
    
- **Lien (S. 170/171):** This is the bailee's primary weapon. Know the difference between a **particular lien** (for all bailees) and a **general lien** (for bankers, etc., only).
