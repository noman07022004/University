## 📌 3.1: Definition (Quasi-Contracts) (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic defines the entire _concept_ of Topic 3. You must understand this definition to see how it's fundamentally different from a regular contract (which is based on agreement) and to understand the principle of "unjust enrichment."
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. It's a very common question for a "short note" or as the required introduction to a longer essay question (e.g., "Define quasi-contract and explain the different types recognized by law.").

A **quasi-contract** is not a contract. It is a legal obligation created or "implied" by a court to prevent one person from being **unjustly enriched** at the expense of another.

It is a "legal fiction" because it lacks the most essential element of a true contract: **agreement** (offer and acceptance). The parties never intended to make a contract, but the law steps in and imposes a "contract-like" obligation on one party to pay the other.

- **True Contract:** Arises from the **consent** of the parties.
    
- **Quasi-Contract:** Arises from the **operation of law**, based on the principles of justice, equity, and good conscience.
    

The fundamental basis of quasi-contract is the principle of **unjust enrichment**. This principle holds that:

1. One person (the Defendant) has been **enriched** (received a benefit).
    
2. This enrichment was **at the expense** of another person (the Plaintiff).
    
3. It would be **unjust** or **inequitable** for the Defendant to keep that benefit without paying for it.
    

### 🔷 Bangladeshi Context: 

The **Contract Act, 1872,** does not use the term "quasi-contract." Instead, **Chapter V (Sections 68-72)** is titled "Of Certain Relations Resembling those Created by Contract." This title perfectly captures the concept: they are not _actual_ contracts, but _relations resembling_ them. The specific types of quasi-contracts (like supplying necessaries or paying by mistake) are the subtopics that follow.

**Example:** You accidentally receive a pizza you didn't order from a delivery person. You know it's not yours, but you eat it anyway.

- There is **no contract**; you never made an offer or acceptance.
    
- But, you (Defendant) were **enriched** (you got a free pizza).
    
- This was at the **expense** of the pizza parlor (Plaintiff).
    
- It would be **unjust** for you to keep that benefit without paying. Therefore, the law imposes a quasi-contractual obligation on you to pay the price of the pizza. This is a classic example of **Section 70 (Non-gratuitous act)**.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Key Phrase:** The most important phrase to remember and use is **"unjust enrichment."** This is the entire foundation of the topic.
    
- **Remedy is Restitution:** The remedy for breach of a _contract_ is **damages** (to compensate for loss). The remedy for a _quasi-contract_ is **restitution** or **reimbursement** (to return the unfair benefit).
    
- **Not a Contract:** In an exam, never say a quasi-contract _is_ a type of contract. Always define it in _opposition_ to a true contract. Emphasize that it is a legal fiction that _lacks_ consent.
