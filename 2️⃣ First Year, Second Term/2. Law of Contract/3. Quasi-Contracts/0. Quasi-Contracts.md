## 3. Quasi-Contracts ⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 4/5):** This topic is critical for understanding the "gap-filling" nature of law. It explains how obligations arise even without the mutual consent that forms the basis of standard contract law. Understanding the principle of _Unjust Enrichment_ is fundamental to civil law.

### Introduction: The Nature of Quasi-Contract

In the strict legal sense, a "contract" requires an agreement, a meeting of minds (_consensus ad idem_), and free consent. However, there are circumstances where the law imposes an obligation upon a person even though they haven't entered into any agreement. These are known as **Quasi-Contracts**.

In the **Contract Act, 1872** (applicable in Bangladesh), these are technically termed **"Certain Relations Resembling Those Created by Contract"** (Chapter V, Sections 68–72). The term "Quasi-Contract" is an English law concept derived from Roman law.

The foundational principle of Quasi-Contract is the theory of **Unjust Enrichment**. This doctrine, famously articulated by Lord Mansfield in _Moses v Macferlan (1760)_, states that _nemo debet locupletari ex aliena jactura_—no one should be enriched at the expense of another. The law implies a promise to pay to prevent injustice.

To provide a complete academic narrative, we will categorize the syllabus subtopics into the **Definitions & Theory** and the **Statutory Applications** (Sections 68, 69, and 70).

---

### 3.1 Definition & Theoretical Basis

**Syllabus Covered:** Definition

A **Quasi-Contract** is a legal fiction. It is not a contract at all because the essential element of a contract—willing agreement—is missing. Instead, it is an obligation imposed by law to ensure justice.

**Distinction from Contract:**

- **Contract:** Rights and obligations arise from the _agreement_ of parties.
    
- **Quasi-Contract:** Rights and obligations are imposed by _law_ (operation of law).
    

**Key Elements:**

1. Absence of an agreement (offer and acceptance).
    
2. Based on equity and good conscience.
    
3. Right is available against a specific person (Right _in personam_), not against the whole world.
    

---

### 3.2 Supply of Necessaries to Incapable Persons (Section 68)

**Syllabus Covered:** Incapable persons

This corresponds to **Section 68** of the Contract Act, 1872. It addresses the protection of those who provide essential support to individuals legally incapable of entering a contract (e.g., minors or persons of unsound mind).

**The Rule:** If a person supplies "necessaries" suited to the condition in life of a person who is incapable of entering into a contract (or to anyone whom such incapable person is legally bound to support), the supplier is entitled to be reimbursed from the **property** of such incapable person.

**Academic Analysis:**

- **"Necessaries":** This is not limited to bare food and shelter. It includes things necessary to maintain the person in their particular station in life (e.g., education for a minor, funeral expenses for a parent).
    
- **Nature of Liability:** The liability is **not personal**. You cannot sue the minor personally to pay from their future income or jail them. The reimbursement comes strictly from their **estate (property)**. If they have no property, the supplier cannot recover anything.
    

---

### 3.3 Reimbursement of Person Paying Money Due by Another (Section 69)

**Syllabus Covered:** Reimbursement

This corresponds to **Section 69** of the Contract Act. It covers situations where one person pays money that another person was actually bound by law to pay.

**The Rule:** A person who is interested in the payment of money which another is bound by law to pay, and who therefore pays it, is entitled to be reimbursed by the other.

**Essential Conditions:**

1. **Interested Person:** The payer must have a bona fide interest in making the payment to protect their own interest (e.g., a tenant paying the landlord's property tax to avoid the property being seized). They cannot be a "volunteer" or a stranger just paying out of generosity.
    
2. **Legal Liability:** The other person must be bound by law to pay that money.
    
3. **Actual Payment:** The payment must be made to a third party, not to the person liable.
    

---

### 3.4 Obligation to Pay for Non-Gratuitous Acts (Section 70)

**Syllabus Covered:** Non-gratuitous act

This corresponds to **Section 70**, arguably the most frequently cited quasi-contractual provision. It protects people who do work for others without intending to do it for free.

**The Rule:** Where a person lawfully does anything for another person, or delivers anything to him, **not intending to do so gratuitously** (for free), and such other person enjoys the benefit thereof, the latter is bound to make compensation to the former.

**Three Core Conditions (The "B.K. Mondal" Test):**

1. **Lawful Act:** The act must be lawful.
    
2. **Non-Gratuitous Intent:** The person doing the act must not intend to do it for free.
    
3. **Enjoyment of Benefit:** The person for whom the act is done must actually enjoy the benefit.
    

**Example:** A tradesman leaves goods at B’s house by mistake. B treats the goods as his own (consumes them). B is bound to pay A for them. If B had not consumed them and asked A to collect them, B would not be liable.

### Knowledge Gap Fill: Completing the Picture

_While the syllabus highlights the above three specifically, the Contract Act includes two other vital Quasi-Contracts:_

1. **Finder of Goods (Section 71):** A person who finds goods belonging to another and takes them into custody is subject to the same responsibility as a bailee. _(Note: Your syllabus covers this extensively in Section B, Topic 8 "Bailment and Pledge", so we will detail it there)._
    
2. **Money Paid by Mistake or Coercion (Section 72):** A person to whom money has been paid, or anything delivered, by mistake or under coercion, must repay or return it. (e.g., if you overpay a bill by mistake, the company _must_ refund you; this is a quasi-contractual obligation).

### Important Notes & Exam Tips 📝

- **Terminology:** In exams, if you write "Constructive Contract" or "Implied Contract" alongside Quasi-Contract, it shows wider reading.
    
- **The "Minor" Trap:** A common exam trick is: "A lends money to a minor to buy a movie ticket." This is NOT a necessary. However, "A lends money to a minor to pay for a necessary medical surgery." This IS recoverable under Section 68.
    
- **Gratuitous vs. Non-Gratuitous:** If you invite a friend to dinner, that is a social/gratuitous act. You cannot bill them later under Section 70. Section 70 applies where the context implies a commercial or professional expectation of payment.
    
- **Damages:** The remedy for Quasi-Contract is usually **Restitution** (restoring the benefit) or **Quantum Meruit** (payment for as much as is earned), rather than damages for breach, because there was no contract to breach.
