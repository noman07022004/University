## 📌 3.4: Non-gratuitous act (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This is the broadest and most powerful quasi-contractual principle. It's a "catch-all" rule that covers any situation where one person benefits from another's work (not just money or goods) and it would be unfair to let them keep that benefit for free.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This section (Section 70) is a common subject for problem questions involving services rendered or goods delivered by mistake, requiring you to analyze if the act was "gratuitous" and if the benefit was "enjoyed."
    

This subtopic covers the legal obligation to pay for the benefit received from a **"non-gratuitous act."** A non-gratuitous act is one done with the expectation of being paid; it is the opposite of a gratuitous act, which is done for free (like a gift or an act of charity).

The governing law is **Section 70 of the Contract Act, 1872**. This section creates a quasi-contractual obligation based on the "unjust enrichment" principle.

**The Rule (Section 70):** The section states that if a person:

1. Lawfully does anything for another person, or delivers anything to them;
    
2. Does **not** intend to do so **gratuitously** (for free); and
    
3. The other person **enjoys the benefit** of that act or delivery... ...then the person who _received_ the benefit is bound to pay compensation to the person who _provided_ it.
    

---

### 🔷 The Three Conditions for Section 70

To successfully claim compensation under Section 70, the Plaintiff (the one who did the act) must prove three things:

1. ***The Act was Lawful:*** The act done or thing delivered must be legal.
    
2. ***The Act was Non-Gratuitous:*** The Plaintiff must have had no intention of doing it for free. They must have expected payment or compensation.
    
3. ***The Defendant Enjoyed the Benefit:*** This is the most critical part. The Defendant (the one who received the benefit) must have had a _choice_ to either **accept** or **reject** the benefit. If they had no choice, they cannot be forced to pay. But if they had a choice and _chose_ to accept or use the benefit, the law presumes they agreed to pay for it.
    

---

### 🔷 Classic Example

This rule is perfectly illustrated by the example of a mistaken delivery:

- A, a tradesman, leaves goods at B's house by **mistake**.
    
- B sees the goods are not his, but he _chooses_ to treat them as his own and **uses them**.
    
- **Analysis:**
    
    1. A's act of delivering goods was **lawful**.
        
    2. A (a tradesman) did **not intend to do it gratuitously**; he expected to be paid (by the correct customer).
        
    3. B **enjoyed the benefit**. Crucially, B had a _choice_: he could have rejected the goods or called A to pick them up. By _choosing_ to use them, he accepted the benefit.
        
- **Conclusion:** Under Section 70, B is bound to pay compensation to A.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **The "Choice" is Key:** The most important factor in this subtopic is **choice**. If a benefit is "forced" on someone, they are not liable. For example, if A (without being asked) cleans B's car while B is away, B is not liable to pay, as B had _no choice_ to reject the service.
    
- **Distinction from Section 69:**
    
    - **Section 69 (3.3):** Involves a _payment of money_ that another was bound to pay.
        
    - **Section 70 (3.4):** Is much wider. It involves payment for _acts_ or _services_ or the _delivery of goods_.
        
- **Basis of "Quantum Meruit":** This section is the statutory basis in Bangladesh for the common law principle of _quantum meruit_ ("as much as he has earned"), where a person can claim reasonable payment for services rendered, even without a valid contract.
