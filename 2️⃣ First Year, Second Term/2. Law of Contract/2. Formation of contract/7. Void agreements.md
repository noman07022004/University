## 📌 2.7: Void agreements (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Absolutely critical. This topic is the "master list" of agreements that the law flatly refuses to enforce, even if they have all the other elements like offer, acceptance, and (in some cases) consideration. It defines the concrete boundaries of enforceable contracts.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Extremely high importance. Questions (both essay and problem-based) will frequently test your ability to identify if an agreement is void under one of these specific sections (e.g., restraint of trade, uncertainty, wager).

A **void agreement** is a "nullity" in the eyes of the law. It is an agreement that is not enforceable by law from the very beginning.

The **Contract Act, 1872,** defines it in **Section 2(g)**:

> "An agreement not enforceable by law is said to be void."

This means a void agreement creates **no legal rights or obligations** for any party. It is considered _void ab initio_ (void from the very beginning), as if it never existed. No party can sue the other for "breach of contract" because, in the eyes of the law, there was no contract to breach.

It is crucial to distinguish this from other concepts:

- **Void vs. Voidable Agreement:** A **void** agreement is a dead end; it's a "stillborn" contract. A **voidable** agreement (which we will cover in 2.8) is a valid contract that can be "killed" or rescinded at the _option_ of one of the parties (e.g., if their consent was obtained by fraud).
    
- **Void vs. Illegal Agreement:** This is a subtle but important distinction. An **illegal** agreement (one with an unlawful object/consideration under Section 23, as we saw in 2.5/2.6) is _always_ void. But "void" is a wider term. An agreement can be **void _without_ being illegal** (e.g., an agreement with a minor). All illegal agreements are void, but not all void agreements are illegal.

### 🔷 Agreements Expressly Declared to be Void

While many agreements are void for failing an "essential element" (like capacity or lawful object), the **Contract Act, 1872,** provides a specific "blacklist" of other agreements that are **expressly declared to be void**. This list, found in **Sections 25-30**, is the core of this subtopic.

#### 1️⃣ Agreements Without Consideration (Section 25) 
As we saw in Subtopic 2.4, this is the "bargain" rule. A one-sided, gratuitous promise is void. (This is subject to the three exceptions listed in Section 25, such as a written promise from "natural love and affection").

#### 2️⃣ Agreements in Restraint of Marriage (Section 26) 
Any agreement that _restrains_ (prevents) any person, _other than a minor_, from marrying is completely void.

- **Example:** A promises to pay B 100,000 Taka _if_ B never marries. This agreement is void.
    

#### 3️⃣ Agreements in Restraint of Trade (Section 27) 
This is a very important category for business law. Any agreement that restricts a person from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is, to that extent, **void**.

- **Example:** Two competing shops in a town agree that one will close down permanently in exchange for a payment. This agreement is void because it restrains trade.
    
- **The Main Exception (Sale of Goodwill):** The law _does_ allow a "reasonable" restriction. When a person sells their business (including its "goodwill" or reputation), they can agree with the buyer _not_ to start a _similar_ business, within _specified local limits_, for a _reasonable time_. This is a valid and necessary exception to protect the buyer.
    

#### 4️⃣ Agreements in Restraint of Legal Proceedings (Section 28) 
An agreement is void if it:

- a) Absolutely restricts a party from enforcing their legal rights in a court of law.
    
- b) Sets a shorter time limit to sue than what is provided by the **Limitation Act, 1908**.
    
- **Exception:** An "arbitration clause" is _not_ void. An agreement to "refer any future disputes to an arbitrator" instead of a court is perfectly valid.
    

#### 5️⃣ Agreements Void for Uncertainty (Section 29) 
An agreement is void if its terms are not certain, or are not _capable of being made certain_. The law cannot enforce a promise it cannot understand.

- **Example:** A agrees to sell B "a hundred tons of oil." There is nothing to show what _kind_ of oil was intended. The agreement is void for uncertainty.
    
- **Example:** A agrees to sell B "my house for 1 crore Taka or 1.1 crore Taka." This is void as the price is not certain.
    

#### 6️⃣ Wagering Agreements (Section 30) 
Agreements by way of "wager" (a bet) are **void**. A wager is an agreement to pay money based on the happening or non-happening of an _uncertain future event_, where the parties have no other interest in the event _except_ for the bet itself.

- **Example:** A and B bet 1,000 Taka on which team will win a cricket match. This is a wager and is void. Neither can sue the other for the money. (This links directly to Subtopic 2.10).

### 🔷 Outside Agreements Expressly Declared to be Void

#### 1️⃣ Agreement with a Party Lacking Capacity (Section 11) 
As we covered in the "Essential Elements" (1.5), parties must be competent to contract. **Section 11** states that a person who is _not_ of the age of majority (a minor) or is _not_ of sound mind is incompetent.

- **The Rule:** An agreement entered into by a **minor** (under 18 in Bangladesh) is **void ab initio**. It is a complete nullity.
    
- _Landmark Case:_ **_Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose_** (a Privy Council case that established this rule for the subcontinent).


#### 2️⃣ Agreement under a Bilateral Mistake of Fact (Section 20) 
This is a critical rule. An agreement is **void** if:

- a) **Both** parties are under a mistake;
    
- b) The mistake is about a **matter of fact** (not law); and
    
- c) The fact is **essential** to the agreement.
    
- **Example:** A agrees to buy a specific horse from B. Unknown to both parties, the horse was already dead at the time of the agreement. Since the _subject matter_ of the contract (the horse) did not exist, the agreement is void.
    

#### 3️⃣ Agreement with an Unlawful Consideration or Object (Section 23) 
As we covered in subtopics 2.5 and 2.6, if _either_ the "price" (consideration) or the "purpose" (object) of an agreement is illegal, immoral, or against public policy, the **entire agreement is void**.

- **Example:** A promises to pay B 100,000 Taka (legal) in exchange for B committing a crime (unlawful consideration). The agreement is void.
    
- **Example:** A rents a car from B (legal) for the _purpose_ of smuggling (unlawful object). The agreement is void.

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Void = No Contract:** The most important takeaway is that "void" means "no legal rights." No damages can be claimed for non-performance.
    
- **The Core List:** For exams, **Sections 25-30** are the heart of this topic. You should be able to identify an agreement that falls into one of these categories.
    
- **Consequence of Void Agreements (Restitution):** Even though no one can _enforce_ a void agreement, **Section 65** provides a remedy. If a party has received any _advantage_ (like an advance payment) under an agreement that is later _discovered_ to be void, they must **restore** that advantage (return the money) to the person they received it from. This is not damages; it is "restitution" to prevent unjust enrichment.
