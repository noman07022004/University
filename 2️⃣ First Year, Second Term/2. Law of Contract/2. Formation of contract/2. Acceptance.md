## 📌 2.2: Acceptance (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Acceptance is the "second half" of an agreement. It is the final, decisive act that turns a one-sided offer into a binding, two-sided promise (a contract). Understanding its strict rules is non-negotiable.  
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is a guaranteed exam topic, almost always paired with "Offer" (2.1). Problem questions will require you to analyze if a response was a valid acceptance, a counter-offer, or an invalid acceptance (e.g., not communicated).


**Acceptance** is the final and unqualified expression of assent to the terms of an offer. It is the "key" that fits the "lock" of the offer.

**Section 2(b) of the Contract Act, 1872**, defines it:

> "When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal, when accepted, becomes a promise."

Once an offer is accepted, the offeror is bound. They can no longer revoke or withdraw their offer.

---

### 🔷 Key Rules of a Valid Acceptance

For an acceptance to be valid, it must follow several strict rules, which are primarily found in **Section 7 and Section 8** of the Contract Act.

#### 1️⃣ Acceptance Must be Absolute and Unqualified (The "Mirror Image" Rule) 
This is the most important rule. The acceptance must be an exact "mirror image" of the offer, without any changes, conditions, or variations.

- **If the offeree changes the terms:** If the offeree says, "I accept, but I will only pay 9,000 Taka instead of 10,000," this is **not an acceptance**. It is a **Counter-Offer**.
    
- **Effect of a Counter-Offer:** A counter-offer _destroys_ the original offer. The original offer is now "dead" and can no longer be accepted.
    
    - _Landmark Case:_ **_Hyde v. Wrench_**. (A offered to sell his farm for £1,000. B offered £950. A refused. B then tried to accept the original £1,000. The court held there was no contract, as B's counter-offer of £950 had _terminated_ A's original offer).
        

#### 2️⃣ Acceptance Must be Communicated to the Offeror 
An acceptance has no legal effect until it is communicated _to_ the offeror (or their authorized agent). You cannot accept an offer in your own mind.

- _Landmark Case:_ **_Brogden v. Metropolitan Railway_**.
    
- **Silence is Not Acceptance:** The offeror _cannot_ impose a condition that the offeree's silence will be treated as acceptance. (e.g., "If I don't hear from you by Friday, I will assume you have bought the car").
    
    - _Landmark Case:_ **_Felthouse v. Bindley_**.
        
- **Exception: Acceptance by Conduct:** In some cases, acceptance is communicated by performing the act specified in the offer.
    
    - _Landmark Case:_ **_Carlill v. Carbolic Smoke Ball Co._** The company (offeror) did not require Mrs. Carlill to _write_ to them and say "I accept." The offer was accepted by the _conduct_ of her using the smoke ball as directed.
        

#### 3️⃣ Acceptance Must be in the Prescribed or Reasonable Mode

- **Prescribed Mode:** If the offeror specifies _how_ to accept (e.g., "Please reply by email"), the acceptance must be given in that mode (**Section 7(2)**).
    
- **Reasonable Mode:** If no mode is prescribed, acceptance must be given in a "usual and reasonable" manner (e.g., if an offer is made by post, a reply by post is reasonable).
    

#### 4️⃣ The Postal Rule (A Key Exception to Communication) 
This is a famous exception to the rule that acceptance must be _received_ by the offeror.

- **The Rule:** When an acceptance is sent by **post** (mail), the acceptance is legally **complete as soon as the letter is posted** (i.e., put in the mailbox).
    
- **Governed by Section 4:** "The communication of an acceptance is complete, as against the proposer, when it is put in a course of transmission to him, so as to be out of the power of the acceptor."
    
- **Effect:** A binding contract is formed the moment the letter is posted, _even if the letter is delayed or lost in the mail_ and never reaches the offeror.
    
    - _Landmark Case:_ **_Adams v. Lindsell_**.
        
- **Modern Context:** This rule generally _does not_ apply to instantaneous forms of communication like email, telephone, or telex, where the acceptance must be _received_ to be effective.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **"Absolute, Communicated, and in the Right Mode":** This is your 3-point checklist for any acceptance.
    
- **Counter-Offer vs. Acceptance:** This is the most common exam problem. Be sure you can tell the difference. A mere "request for information" (e.g., "Is credit available?") is _not_ a counter-offer and does not destroy the original offer.
    
- **The Postal Rule is a Trap:** Remember, the postal rule applies _only_ to acceptance (not to offers or revocations) and _only_ when post is a reasonable method.
    
- **Silence:** The "silence is not acceptance" rule is a fundamental protection for offerees.
