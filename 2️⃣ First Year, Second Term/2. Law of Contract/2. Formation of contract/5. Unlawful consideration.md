## 📌 2.5: Unlawful consideration (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic, along with 2.6 (Lawful Object), defines the limits of "Freedom of Contract." It establishes that even if you have a perfect agreement with consideration, the law will refuse to enforce it if the "price" (the consideration) is illegal, immoral, or against public policy.
>- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This is frequently tested, often in conjunction with "Void Agreements" (2.7). Exam questions will present a scenario and ask you to determine if the contract is valid or void due to an unlawful consideration.


This subtopic is part of the larger essential element that both the **consideration** (the "price") and the **object** (the "purpose") of an agreement must be lawful. If _either_ is unlawful, the entire agreement is **void** and legally unenforceable.

The governing law for this is **Section 23 of the Contract Act, 1872**. This section provides a single "blacklist" of what makes consideration _or_ object unlawful.

According to **Section 23**, the consideration (or object) of an agreement is unlawful if:

### 1️⃣ It is forbidden by law:

- This refers to acts that are expressly prohibited by a specific statute or law.
    
- **Example:** A promises to pay B 100,000 Taka in consideration for B obtaining a government license for A through _bribery_. Since bribery is a criminal act forbidden by the **Prevention of Corruption Act, 1947**, the consideration (the bribe) is unlawful, and the agreement is void.
    

### 2️⃣ It is of such a nature that, if permitted, it would _defeat the provisions of any law_:

- This is more subtle. The act itself might not be _expressly_ forbidden, but it would undermine the _purpose_ or "spirit" of another law.
    
- **Example:** A tenant is legally forbidden by a rent control law from sub-letting his apartment. He agrees to sub-let it to B in exchange for 5,000 Taka. This agreement, if permitted, would _defeat the provisions_ of the rent control law. Therefore, the consideration is unlawful, and the agreement is void.
    

### 3️⃣ It is fraudulent:

- The consideration involves committing a fraud on a third party.
    
- **Example:** A agrees to pay B 50,000 Taka. In consideration, B (who is an agent for C) agrees to sell C's property to A at an undervalued price, without C's knowledge. This agreement is a fraud on C. The consideration is fraudulent, and the agreement is void.
    

### 4️⃣ It involves or implies _injury to the person or property of another_:

- The consideration requires harming someone else or their property.
    
- **Example:** A promises to pay B 10,000 Taka in consideration for B publishing a defamatory article about C. Since this involves injury to the _person_ (reputation) of C, the consideration is unlawful, and the agreement is void. An agreement to commit an assault or trespass would also fall here.
    

### 5️⃣ The Court regards it as _immoral_:

- This category is based on public morality, which can change over time. It typically includes acts considered sexually immoral or in violation of social norms.
    
- **Example:** A promises to pay B a sum of money in consideration for B committing adultery or engaging in prostitution with A. The court will regard this consideration as immoral, and the agreement is void.
    

### 6️⃣ The Court regards it as _opposed to public policy_:

- This is the broadest and most flexible category. "Public policy" refers to the welfare of the public or the good of the community. An agreement is against public policy if it is harmful to the public interest.
    
- **Common Examples:**
    
    - **Agreements to Stifle Prosecution:** A promises to pay B 100,000 Taka in consideration for B dropping a _criminal_ (not civil) case against A. This is void because it interferes with the administration of justice.
        
    - **Trafficking in Public Offices:** An agreement to pay money to a public official to secure a job or a title.
        
    - **Agreements in Restraint of Parental Rights:** An agreement where a parent gives up custody of their child for money.
        

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Section 23 is the Key:** For both this subtopic (2.5) and the next one (2.6), **Section 23** is your "master list." You should be familiar with its six categories.
    
- **Effect is VOID:** The consequence of unlawful consideration is not "voidable"; it is **void ab initio** (void from the very beginning). The court will not help either party.
    
- **Unlawful Consideration vs. Unlawful Object:** These two concepts are almost always studied together because Section 23 applies to both.
    
    - **Unlawful Consideration:** The "price" is illegal. (e.g., A pays B 5,000 Taka [lawful] in exchange for B committing a crime [unlawful consideration]).
        
    - **Unlawful Object:** The "purpose" is illegal. (e.g., A rents a house [lawful] from B for the _purpose_ of running an illegal gambling operation [unlawful object]).
        
- **"In Pari Delicto":** This is a related legal maxim: _"in pari delicto potior est conditio defendentis"_ (where both parties are equally at fault, the position of the defendant is stronger). This means if the illegal agreement has been performed (e.g., the bribe was paid), the court will generally not help the payer get their money back.
