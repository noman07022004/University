## 2. Formation of Contract ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** This is the "engine room" of contract law. You simply cannot proceed without mastering how an offer turns into a binding promise and what makes that promise valid.
>- **Exam (⭐ 5/5):** This is the most frequently tested section. Expect problem questions asking, "Is there a valid contract?" involving revocation of offers, lack of consideration, or coerced consent.
    

---

The formation of a contract is a process, not a singular event. It begins with a proposal and ends with a binding legal obligation. For an agreement to mature into a contract under the **Contract Act, 1872**, it must pass through specific stages: Agreement (Offer + Acceptance), Consideration, and Enforceability (Free Consent & Legality).

Below is the detailed academic breakdown of these stages.

### A. The Agreement: Offer and Acceptance

_(Covering: Offer, Acceptance, Termination of offer)_

The genesis of every contract is the **Agreement**. This requires a "meeting of minds" (_consensus ad idem_) achieved through a proposal and its subsequent acceptance.

#### 1. Offer (Proposal)

Defined in Section 2(a): When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything, with a view to obtaining the assent of that other to such act or abstinence, he is said to make a proposal.

- **Types:**
    
    - _General Offer:_ Made to the world at large (anyone can accept).
        
    - _Specific Offer:_ Made to a specific person.
        
- **Offer vs. Invitation to Treat:** An offer must be distinguished from a mere "invitation to treat" (e.g., goods displayed in a shop window). An invitation to treat is an invitation for others to _make_ an offer.
    

#### 2. Acceptance

Defined in Section 2(b): When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. Once accepted, a proposal becomes a promise.

- **Rules of Acceptance:**
    
    - It must be absolute and unqualified (Mirror Image Rule).
        
    - It must be communicated to the offeror.
        
    - It must be in the prescribed or reasonable mode.
        

#### 3. Termination of Offer (Revocation)

An offer does not last forever. According to Section 6, an offer is terminated/revoked by:

- **Notice of revocation:** The offeror cancels it before acceptance.
    
- **Lapse of time:** If not accepted within the prescribed or reasonable time.
    
- **Failure of condition precedent:** If the acceptor fails to fulfill a condition required to accept.
    
- **Death or insanity:** If the offeror dies/goes insane and the acceptor knows this before acceptance.
    

### B. The Price: Consideration

_(Covering: Consideration, Unlawful consideration)_

#### 1. Consideration

Defined in Section 2(d), Consideration is the "quid pro quo" (something for something). A promise without consideration is generally void (Nudum Pactum).

- **Definition:** When, at the desire of the promisor, the promisee has done or abstained from doing something (Past), does or abstains from doing something (Present), or promises to do or abstain from doing something (Future).
    
- **Key Rule:** Consideration need not be adequate (a house can be sold for 1 Taka if agreed), but it must be real and have value in the eyes of the law.
    

#### 2. Unlawful Consideration

Consideration must be lawful. Under Section 23, consideration is unlawful if it:

- Is forbidden by law.
    
- Defeats the provisions of any law.
    
- Is fraudulent.
    
- Involves injury to the person or property of another.
    
- Is regarded as immoral or opposed to public policy.
    

### C. The Validity Check: Capacity, Consent, and Legality

_(Covering: Free consent, Void agreements, Legality of object)_

Even if there is Offer, Acceptance, and Consideration, the contract may fail if the "quality" of the agreement is defective.

#### 1. Free Consent

Consent is "free" when it is not caused by specific defects (Section 14). If consent is not free, the contract is usually voidable (the victim can cancel it).

- **Coercion (Sec 15):** Committing or threatening to commit an act forbidden by the Penal Code, or detaining property.
    
- **Undue Influence (Sec 16):** One party dominates the will of the other (e.g., Doctor/Patient) to obtain an unfair advantage.
    
- **Fraud (Sec 17):** Intentional deception to induce a contract.
    
- **Misrepresentation (Sec 18):** Innocent false statement causing the other to enter the contract.
    
- **Mistake (Sec 20-22):** Where both parties are under a mistake of fact essential to the agreement (this makes the contract **Void**, not Voidable).
    

#### 2. Void Agreements

Certain agreements are expressly declared Void (invalid from the start) by the Contract Act, regardless of consent:

- Restraint of Marriage (Sec 26).
    
- Restraint of Trade (Sec 27).
    
- Restraint of Legal Proceedings (Sec 28).
    
- Uncertain Agreements (Sec 29).
    

#### 3. Legality of Object

Just like consideration, the object (purpose) of the agreement must be lawful. If the object is illegal (e.g., an agreement to rent a house to store smuggled goods), the agreement is void.

### D. Special Types: Contingent Contracts

_(Covering: Contingent contracts, Distinction between contingent contract and wagering agreement)_

Not all contracts are absolute; some depend on "Ifs."

#### 1. Contingent Contracts (Section 31)

A contingent contract is a contract to do or not to do something, if some event, collateral to such contract, does or does not happen.

- _Example:_ A contracts to pay B 10,000 Taka if B's house is burnt. This is a valid contingent contract (Insurance is the classic example).
    

#### 2. Distinction: Contingent Contract vs. Wagering Agreement

A Wagering Agreement (Betting) is void under Section 30, whereas a Contingent Contract is valid.

|**Feature**|**Contingent Contract**|**Wagering Agreement**|
|---|---|---|
|**validity**|Valid and Enforceable.|Void (cannot be enforced).|
|**Interest**|Parties have a real interest in the event (e.g., protecting property).|Parties have no interest other than winning/losing money.|
|**Nature**|It is an investment or protection.|It is a gamble/game of chance.|
|**Reciprocity**|Not necessarily reciprocal promises.|Essentially consists of reciprocal promises to pay money.|

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **The "Postbox Rule":** In English law (and applicable in Bangladesh), acceptance by post is complete against the proposer _as soon as the letter is posted_, even if it gets lost. This is a favorite exam trick.
    
- **Silence is not Acceptance:** You cannot write "If I don't hear from you, I assume you bought the car." This is invalid (_Felthouse v Bindley_).
    
- **Consideration Exception:** **Section 25** allows contracts without consideration in specific cases: (1) Natural love and affection (written & registered), (2) Compensation for past voluntary service, (3) Time-barred debt.
    
- **Void vs. Illegal:** All illegal agreements are void, but not all void agreements are illegal. (e.g., A wager is void but not a crime; hiring a hitman is illegal).
