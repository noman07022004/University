## 9.4: Types of agents (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** Very important. This subtopic provides the vocabulary for the law of agency. You cannot analyze an agency problem without first classifying the agent. The _type_ of agent (e.g., General vs. Special) is what determines the _scope_ of their authority and the _liability_ of the principal.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐):** Less common as a standalone question. It is rare to get an essay question like "List all the types of agents." Instead, this is essential background knowledge you _must_ use to solve problem questions about an agent's authority or liability.


Agents are classified into several types based on two main criteria:

1. The **extent of their authority**.
    
2. The **nature of the work** they perform (e.g., "Mercantile Agents").
    

### 1. Classification by Extent of Authority

This is the most important legal classification, as it defines the agent's power.

- **a) General Agent:**
    
    - **Definition:** A General Agent is employed to act on behalf of the principal in _all_ matters concerning a **specific business, trade, or job**.
        
    - **Authority:** They have "general authority," which includes all the implied authority necessary to carry on that business (as per Section 188), such as buying materials or hiring staff.
        
    - **Example:** The **manager of a store**. The owner (principal) has authorized them to do everything needed to run that store (e.g., place new orders, hire cashiers, handle customer complaints).
        
- **b) Special Agent:**
    
    - **Definition:** A Special Agent is employed to do _only a specific act_ or to perform a _single, particular transaction_.
        
    - **Authority:** Their authority is limited to that _one act_. Third parties cannot assume they have broad authority.
        
    - **Example:** The principal (A) hires an agent (B) _only_ to **"sell this one specific house."** The moment the house is sold, B's authority as a special agent is over.
        
- **c) Universal Agent:**
    
    - **Definition:** A Universal Agent is one whose authority is **unlimited**. They are authorized to do _all_ acts that the principal can lawfully do and delegate.
        
    - **Creation:** This type of agency is very rare in business. It is almost always created by a very broad **Power of Attorney** (Subtopic 9.2).
        
    - **Example:** A principal who is moving abroad indefinitely gives an agent a universal power of attorney to "manage all my property, finances, and legal affairs" in Bangladesh.
        

### 2. Classification by Nature of Work (Mercantile Agents)

These are specific types of commercial agents, defined by their function in trade.

- **a) Broker:**
    
    - **Definition:** A broker is a "middleman" whose job is to **negotiate and make contracts** between two other parties (the principal and a third party).
        
    - **Key Characteristic:** A broker **does not have possession** of the goods. They are purely a negotiator.
        
    - **Example:** A **stockbroker** (who buys/sells shares on your behalf) or a **real estate broker** (who finds a buyer for your house).
        
- **b) Factor:**
    
    - **Definition:** A factor is a mercantile agent who _is_ given **possession of the principal's goods** for the purpose of selling them.
        
    - **Key Characteristic:** Because they have possession, a factor can sell the goods in their own name, receive payment, and give a valid discharge.
        
- **c) Commission Agent:**
    
    - **Definition:** A commission agent is a person employed to buy or sell goods for a principal, in return for a payment called a "commission." This is a broad category that can include factors and brokers.
        
- **d) Del Credere Agent:**
    
    - **Definition:** This is a special type of commission agent who, for an _extra_ commission (called a "del credere commission"), **guarantees the solvency** of the third party.
        
    - **Effect:** They are a combination of an agent and a surety (guarantor). If the third party (the buyer) fails to pay the principal, the _del credere agent_ must pay.
        
- **e) Auctioneer:**
    
    - **Definition:** An agent authorized to sell goods by public auction.
        
    - **Key Characteristic:** An auctioneer is a _special agent_. At first, they are the agent for the _seller_. The moment the hammer falls, they _also_ become the agent for the _buyer_ for the limited purpose of signing the memorandum of sale.
        

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Authority is the Difference:** The most important distinction to make in any problem is between a **general agent** (who has broad implied authority for a business) and a **special agent** (who only has authority for one specific task).
    
- **Possession is the Difference:** The key distinction between a **Broker** and a **Factor** is possession. A broker _never_ has the goods; a factor _always_ has the goods.
