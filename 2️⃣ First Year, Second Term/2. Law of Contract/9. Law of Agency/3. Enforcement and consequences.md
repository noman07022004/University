## 9.3: Enforcement and consequences (of Agency) (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the "so what?" of agency law. It explains the legal results of an agent's actions, which is the entire point of the relationship. It's the framework that links the principal's authority, the agent's actions, and the third party's rights.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** High importance. This topic is the core of most agency-based problem questions. You'll be asked to analyze if a principal is bound by an agent's act, or if the agent is personally liable.


This subtopic deals with the legal consequences that arise from an agent's actions, particularly how those actions bind the principal to third parties.

### 1. The Core Consequence: Principal is Bound (Section 226)

This is the most important rule of agency.

- **The Rule:** "Contracts entered into through an agent, and obligations arising from acts done by an agent, may be **enforced in the same manner**, and will have the **same legal consequences**, as if the contracts had been entered into and the acts done by the **principal in person**."
    
- **Meaning:** When an agent acts within their authority, they are legally invisible. The contract they make is a direct, binding contract between their principal and the third party.
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. (b) to S. 226):** A is B's agent with authority to receive money. A receives a sum of money from C on B's behalf. C's payment to A has the same legal effect as paying B directly. C's debt to B is discharged.
    

### 2. Consequence of Exceeding Authority

This rule is _conditional_ on the agent acting within their authority. If the agent does _more_ than they were authorized to do, the consequences are split:

- **When the acts are _separable_ (Section 227):**
    
    - **The Rule:** If the authorized part of an act can be separated from the unauthorized part, the principal is **bound by the authorized part only**.
        
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 227):** P authorizes A to insure a ship for 4,000 Taka. A gets one policy for the ship for 4,000 Taka, and a _second_ policy for the cargo for 4,000 Taka. The acts are separable. P is **bound** to pay the premium for the _ship's_ policy but **not** for the _cargo's_ policy.
        
- **When the acts are _inseparable_ (Section 228):**
    
    - **The Rule:** If the authorized and unauthorized parts _cannot_ be separated, the principal **is not bound to recognize the transaction** and can repudiate (reject) the _entire_ deal.
        
    - **Example (from Act, Illus. to S. 228):** P authorizes A to buy 500 sheep. B buys 500 sheep _and_ 200 lambs for a _single, combined price_. P can **repudiate the whole transaction** because the authorized purchase (500 sheep) cannot be separated from the unauthorized one (200 lambs).
        

### 3. Consequence of Notice to Agent (Section 229)

- **The Rule:** "Any notice given to or information obtained by the agent... in the course of the business... shall... have the **same legal consequences as if it had been given to... the principal**."
    
- **Meaning:** The principal is legally "deemed to know" whatever their agent knows. A principal cannot claim ignorance if their agent was properly notified. This is the "doctrine of imputed knowledge."
    
- **Example (from Act, Illus. (a) to S. 229):** A is B's agent to buy goods from C. During the negotiation, A learns the goods actually belong to D. B, the principal, is _ignorant_ of this fact. B cannot later claim he didn't know; the law says B _did_ know, because his agent knew.
    

### 4. Consequence for the Agent's Liability (Section 230)

- **The Rule:** The agent is a mere "conduit." Therefore, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, an agent **cannot personally enforce** contracts, nor are they **personally bound** by them. The fight is between the principal and the third party.
    
- **Exceptions (Presumption of Agent's Liability):** Section 230 presumes the agent _is_ personally liable in three cases:
    
    1. Where the agent acts for a **merchant resident abroad** (a foreign principal).
        
    2. Where the agent **does not disclose the name** of their principal (undisclosed principal).
        
    3. Where the principal, though disclosed, **cannot be sued** (e.g., they are a minor or a foreign sovereign).
        

### 5. Consequence of Agent's Fraud (Section 238)

- **The Rule:** "Misrepresentations made, or frauds committed, by agents acting in the course of their business for their principals, have the **same effect** on agreements... as if such... frauds had been... committed by the **principals**."
    
- **Meaning:** The principal is responsible for their agent's fraud, as long as the agent was acting within the scope of their business. The third party who was defrauded can **rescind the contract** with the principal.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Authority is the Key:** The central theme of this topic is that the principal's liability is _conditional_ on the agent's authority (whether actual or apparent).
    
- **Agent's Personal Liability (Section 230):** This is a key area for exams. The general rule is "no liability," but you _must_ know the three major exceptions where the agent is presumed liable.
    
- **Principal's Liability for Agent's Wrong:** Remember the rule for fraud (Section 238). The principal is liable for the agent's wrongful acts _in the course of business_, even if the principal did not authorize the specific wrongful act.
