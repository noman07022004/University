## 9.11: Principal's rights and duties, Responsibility of agents (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐):** This is the "internal law" of the agency relationship. It explains the rules, obligations, and fiduciary duties that govern the principal and the agent. This is the core of the agency contract itself and the source of all internal disputes.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐⭐):** High importance. Questions on the agent's fiduciary duty (not to make a secret profit, not to have a conflict of interest) and the principal's duty to indemnify the agent are very common in problem scenarios.
 

This subtopic covers the entire internal relationship between the principal and the agent, which is defined by a set of mutual rights and duties. The most important concept underpinning this entire relationship is that **agency is a fiduciary relationship**. This means it is a relationship of the **highest trust and confidence**.

The responsibilities and rights of both parties are laid out in **Sections 211-225 of the Contract Act, 1872**.

### 1. Responsibility of Agents (The Agent's Duties)

The agent's "responsibilities" are their duties to the principal. Because this is a fiduciary relationship, these duties are very strict.

- **Duty to Follow Instructions (Section 211):** The agent's _primary_ duty is to conduct the business according to the principal's directions. If they fail to do so, they are liable for any loss the principal suffers.
    
- **Duty of Reasonable Care and Skill (Section 212):** An agent must act with the same level of care and skill that is "generally possessed by persons engaged in similar business."
    
    - **Example:** An insurance agent is expected to know the insurance market. If they get a bad policy due to their own incompetence, they are liable to their principal.
        
- **Duty to Render Proper Accounts (Section 213):** The agent has an absolute duty to keep clear and accurate accounts of all transactions and money received, and to show them to the principal on demand.
    
- **Duty to Communicate (Section 214):** The agent must use all reasonable diligence to communicate with the principal in cases of difficulty and to seek their instructions.
    
- **Duty Not to Deal on Their Own Account (Section 215):** This is a key fiduciary duty. An agent cannot act for themselves in the business of the agency without the principal's consent.
    
    - **Example:** P hires A to sell his house. A cannot _secretly buy the house for himself_ and then resell it for a profit. If A wants to buy it, he must first get P's _full and informed consent_.
        
- **Duty to Pay Sums Received (Section 218):** The agent must pay the principal all sums received on their behalf (after deducting their own fees and expenses).
    

### 2. The Fiduciary "No-Profit" Rule (Section 216)

This is the most important expression of the agent's responsibility.

- **The Rule:** If an agent deals on their own account _without_ the principal's consent, the principal is entitled to **"claim from the agent any benefit** which may have resulted to him from the transaction."
    
- **Meaning:** An agent **cannot make a secret profit** from their position. Any secret profit, bribe, or extra benefit they receive belongs _legally_ to the principal.
    
- **Example:** P hires A to buy a specific painting. A finds the painting for 50,000 Taka but tells P it costs 60,000 Taka. A buys it and "sells" it to P, pocketing the 10,000 Taka difference. This is a secret profit. If P discovers this, P can recover the 10,000 Taka from A.

### 3. Principal's Duties (The Agent's Rights)

The principal's duties are, for the most part, the "rights" of the agent.

- **Duty to Indemnify for Lawful Acts (Section 222):**
    
    - **The Rule:** The principal is bound to **indemnify** (reimburse) the agent for all lawful acts done by the agent in the exercise of their authority.
        
    - **Example:** P asks A to sign a contract to buy goods. A signs it. P is now bound to provide A with the money to pay for those goods.
        
- **Duty to Indemnify for Acts Done in Good Faith (Section 223):**
    
    - **The Rule:** The principal must also indemnify the agent for the consequences of an act done _in good faith_, even if that act _ends up_ causing injury to a third party.
        
    - **Example:** P asks A to sell some goods. A sells them, believing in good faith they are P's. It turns out the goods were stolen. The true owner sues A. P must indemnify A for the legal costs and damages.
        
- **Duty to Pay Remuneration (Section 219):**
    
    - **The Rule:** The principal must pay the agent the agreed-upon commission or remuneration _when the agent has completed their work_.
        
    - **Exception (Section 220):** An agent who is guilty of **"misconduct"** in the business of the agency (e.g., they made a secret profit or were negligent) **loses their right** to any remuneration.
        

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **Fiduciary Duty is Everything:** The "Responsibilities of agents" can be summarized in one word: **fiduciary**. The agent must _always_ put the principal's interests above their own. This is the most tested part of the internal relationship.
    
- **Agent's Rights (Mnemonic):** The agent's three main rights are the "3 R's":
    
    1. **R**emuneration (Right to be paid).
        
    2. **R**etainer (Right to keep the principal's money to pay their own dues).
        
    3. **R**eimbursement (Right to be indemnified for expenses).
        
- **Agent's Misconduct (S. 220):** This is a powerful tool for the principal. An agent who breaches their fiduciary duty (e.g., takes a bribe) not only has to return the bribe (S. 216) but also _loses their right to be paid any commission_ for that transaction (S. 220).
