
## 9. Law of Agency ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

>**Rating Justification:**
>- **Understanding (⭐ 5/5):** Agency is the concept that allows corporations and businesses to function. Since a company cannot speak for itself, it acts through agents. Understanding how one person becomes liable for another's actions is fundamental to commercial law.
>- **Exam (⭐ 4/5):** This is a heavy-weight topic. Problem questions frequently test **"Agent's Authority"** (did they exceed it?) and **"Ratification."** The distinction between **Sub-Agents** and **Co-Agents** is a common technical question.
    

---

The Law of Agency is based on the Latin maxim _Qui facit per alium facit per se_—"He who does an act through another is deemed in law to do it himself." It creates a legal fiction where the hand of the Agent is considered the hand of the Principal.

This area is governed by **Chapter X (Sections 182–238)** of the **Contract Act, 1872**.

### A. Definition, Nature, and Test of Agency

_(Covering: Definition and nature, Test of agency)_

**1. Definition (Section 182)**

- **Agent:** A person employed to do any act for another, or to represent another in dealings with third persons.
    
- **Principal:** The person for whom such act is done, or who is so represented.
    

**2. Nature of the Relationship**

It is a fiduciary relationship (based on trust). The essence of agency is the power of the agent to bind the principal to legal relations with third parties.

- _Capacity:_ The Principal must be competent (major/sound mind) to appoint an agent. However, the _Agent_ need not be competent (a minor can be an agent, but they won't be personally liable).
    

**3. Test of Agency**

The true test is not what the parties call themselves (e.g., "distributor" or "representative"), but whether the person has the legal authority to create a binding contract on behalf of the other. If A cannot bind B, A is not B's agent.

### B. Creation and Types of Agents

_(Covering: Types of agents, Power of attorney, Agent's authority)_

**1. Types of Agents**

- **General Agent:** Authorized to do all acts connected with a particular trade/business (e.g., a manager).
    
- **Special Agent:** Authorized to do a _single_ specific act (e.g., sell a specific house).
    
- **Mercantile Agents:**
    
    - _Broker:_ Negotiates contracts but does not have possession of goods.
        
    - _Factor:_ Has possession of goods and can sell in their own name.
        
    - _Del Credere Agent:_ Guarantees the solvency of the buyer (for an extra commission).
        

**2. Power of Attorney**

A formal legal document (deed) that expressly confers authority on an agent.

- **General Power of Attorney:** Gives broad powers to act in all matters.
    
- **Special Power of Attorney:** Restricts power to specific acts.
    
- _Note:_ Strictly construed by courts. If it says "sell," it does not imply "mortgage."
    

**3. Agent's Authority (Section 186-189)**

- **Express Authority:** Given by words spoken or written.
    
- **Implied Authority:** Inferred from circumstances (e.g., a manager of a shop has implied authority to order stock).
    
- **Authority in Emergency (Section 189):** An agent has authority to do all acts to protect the principal from loss (like a prudent man would), even without permission (e.g., selling perishable fruit when the train breaks down).
    

### C. Delegation: Sub-Agents and Co-Agents

_(Covering: Sub and Co-agents)_

The general rule is _Delegatus non potest delegare_—a delegate cannot further delegate. An agent cannot transfer their duties to someone else. However, there are exceptions.

**1. Sub-Agent (Section 191)**

- A person employed by, and acting under the control of, the _original agent_.
    
- **Chain of Liability:** Principal $\to$ Agent $\to$ Sub-Agent.
    
    - The Sub-Agent is responsible to the Agent.
        
    - The Agent is responsible to the Principal for the Sub-Agent's acts.
        
    - **Privity:** Generally, the Principal cannot sue the Sub-Agent directly (no privity of contract).
        

**2. Co-Agent (Substituted Agent - Section 194)**

- A person named by the Agent (on express or implied authority) to act _for the Principal_.
    
- _Example:_ You ask your lawyer to hire an auctioneer to sell your house. The auctioneer is a _Co-Agent_, not a Sub-Agent.
    
- **Effect:** Direct relationship between Principal and Co-Agent. The original Agent is not liable for the Co-Agent's negligence (if they selected the Co-Agent with care).
    

### D. Rights, Duties, and Liabilities (Internal Relationship)

_(Covering: Principal's rights and duties, Responsibility of agents)_

**1. Duties of Agent (Principal's Rights)**

- **Duty to follow instructions (Sec 211):** Must act according to directions. If loss occurs due to deviation, Agent pays.
    
- **Duty of Skill (Sec 212):** Must use reasonable skill/diligence.
    
- **Duty to Render Accounts (Sec 213):** Must show accounts on demand.
    
- **Duty to Communicate (Sec 214):** Must contact Principal in difficulty.
    
- **Duty not to deal on own account (Sec 215):** Cannot buy the Principal's property for themselves without disclosure.
    
- **Duty not to make secret profit:** Any secret profit must be returned to the Principal.
    

**2. Rights of Agent (Principal's Duties)**

- **Right to Remuneration:** When the act is completed.
    
- **Right of Lien (Sec 221):** Can keep goods/papers until paid.
    
- **Right to Indemnity (Sec 222):** Principal must indemnify Agent for lawful acts.
    

### E. Relationship with Third Parties

_(Covering: Enforcement and consequences, Liability, Misrepresentation and fraud by agent)_

**1. Liability of Principal**

The Principal is liable for all acts of the Agent committed within the scope of authority.

- **Misrepresentation & Fraud (Sec 238):** Misrepresentations made or frauds committed by agents _in the course of their business_ have the same effect as if committed by the Principal. (Vicarious Liability).
    

**2. Personal Liability of Agent (Section 230)**

Generally, an Agent cannot personally enforce nor be bound by contracts on behalf of a Principal.

Exceptions (Agent IS liable):

- Where the contract expressly provides for it.
    
- Where the agent acts for a **foreign principal** (merchant residing abroad).
    
- Where the agent acts for an **undisclosed principal** (and declines to disclose).
    
- Where the principal cannot be sued (e.g., a minor or nonexistent company).
    

### F. Termination of Agency

_(Covering: Termination)_

Agency is terminated (Section 201) by:

1. **Revocation:** Principal revokes authority (must give notice).
    
2. **Renunciation:** Agent quits (must give notice).
    
3. **Completion of Business:** The job is done.
    
4. **Death or Insanity:** Of either Principal or Agent.
    
5. **Insolvency:** Of the Principal.
    

---

### Important Notes for Exam & Memorization

- **Ratification (Section 196):** This is a huge exam concept. If an Agent acts _without_ authority (or exceeds it), the Principal can later "Ratify" (approve) it. The effect is retrospective—it is treated as if the Agent had authority from the start.
    
    - _Condition:_ The Principal must exist at the time of the act (a company cannot ratify contracts made before it was incorporated).
        
- **Undisclosed Principal:** If an Agent makes a contract in their own name without revealing they have a Principal, the Third Party, upon discovering the truth, can sue _either_ the Agent _or_ the Principal (but not both).
    
- **Irrevocable Agency:** An agency cannot be revoked if the Agent has an **"Interest in the subject matter"** (Section 202).
    
    - _Example:_ You authorize A to sell your land and pay himself the debt you owe him from the proceeds. You cannot fire A until the debt is paid.
