## 9.2: Power of attorney (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This is not just a theoretical concept but a _formal legal instrument_. It is the most common and powerful example of an **express agency** (where authority is given in writing).
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. It is a very good topic for a "short note" or as part of a question on "how agency is created." Understanding the distinction between a "General" and "Special" Power of Attorney is key.
 

A **Power of Attorney (POA)** is a formal, written, legal document that one person (the "Principal") signs to give another person (the "Agent" or "Attorney-in-fact") the authority to act on their behalf.

It is the primary instrument used to create an **express agency**.

In **Bangladesh**, this is a formal legal area governed by its own specific law, **The Power of Attorney Act, 2015**. This Act (and the rules under it) provides the specific formats and procedures for creating a valid POA, especially one that needs to be registered or used from abroad.

### Relationship to Contract of Agency

- **Contract of Agency (Topic 9.1):** This is the _legal relationship_ where one person can bind another. It can be created orally, in writing, or just by conduct.
    
- **Power of Attorney (Topic 9.2):** This is the _formal document_ that proves an express agency exists and clearly spells out the exact scope of the agent's authority.
    

### Key Types of Power of Attorney

The main distinction, which is critical to understand, is based on the _scope_ of the authority given:

**1. General Power of Attorney (GPA)**

- **What it is:** This is a very broad instrument. It gives the agent the authority to perform _all_ acts that the principal could lawfully do.
    
- **Example:** A principal moving abroad might give an agent a GPA to "manage all my financial affairs, sign contracts, operate my bank accounts, and deal with my properties" while they are gone.
    

**2. Special Power of Attorney (SPA)**

- **What it is:** This is a very limited instrument. It gives the agent authority to perform _only one specific act_ or transaction.
    
- **Example:** A principal in Dhaka wants to sell a specific apartment in Khulna but cannot travel. They give an agent a _Special Power of Attorney_ with the _only_ authority being "to sign the deed of sale for my apartment at [specific address] and receive the payment."
    
- **Termination:** The SPA automatically terminates once that specific act is completed.
    

**3. Irrevocable Power of Attorney**

- **What it is:** This is a POA that the principal _cannot_ revoke (cancel).
    
- **When it's used:** Under **The Power of Attorney Act, 2015**, this is used when the POA is given as part of a commercial transaction where the agent has their own interest, such as in a contract for the sale of land, a mortgage, or a development agreement.
    

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **"Attorney-in-Fact":** The agent in a POA is often called an "Attorney-in-Fact." This does **not** mean they are a lawyer (an "Attorney-at-Law"). It is just the legal term for an agent appointed under a POA.
    
- **Bangladeshi Law is Specific:** While the Contract Act (1872) provides the general law, the **Power of Attorney Act, 2015** provides the specific, modern rules. For any matter involving a POA in Bangladesh (especially for land), that Act is the primary authority.
    
- **Formalities:** A POA is a formal document. It must be in writing, signed, witnessed, and (especially for property) registered with the government to be valid.
