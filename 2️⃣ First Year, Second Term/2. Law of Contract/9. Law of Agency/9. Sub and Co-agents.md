## 9.9: Sub and Co-agents (Overall: ⭐⭐⭐)

**Rating Justification:**

- **Understanding (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. This topic is a technical but crucial part of agency law. It explains the legal difference between an agent's _assistant_ (a sub-agent) and an agent's _colleague_ (a co-agent), which has significant consequences for liability.
    
- **Exam (⭐⭐⭐):** Moderately important. The distinction between a sub-agent and a substituted agent (co-agent) is a classic short-note question and can be a tricky part of a problem question.
- 

This subtopic deals with the rules of delegation. The general legal maxim is **_"Delegatus non potest delegare,"_** which means **"A delegate cannot further delegate."**

This principle is codified in **Section 190 of the Contract Act, 1872**. It states that an agent, who was chosen for their personal skill or trust, cannot lawfully employ _another_ person to do that work.

However, Section 190 provides two exceptions: an agent _can_ delegate their work if:

1. The **"ordinary custom of trade"** allows it (e.g., a bank hiring a foreign bank to collect a payment abroad).
    
2. The **"nature of the agency"** requires it (e.g., the work is purely administrative, like copying documents).
    

When an agent delegates, they create either a "Sub-Agent" or a "Co-Agent."

---

### 1. Sub-Agent

A sub-agent is an agent's agent.

- **Definition (Section 191):** "A 'sub-agent' is a person employed by, and acting under the control of, the **original agent** in the business of the agency."
    
- **Relationship:** The sub-agent is an employee of the _agent_, not the principal. There is **no "privity of contract"** (direct legal relationship) between the principal and the sub-agent.
    

The consequences of this depend entirely on whether the appointment was proper or improper.

- **If _Properly_ Appointed (Section 192):**
    
    - **Liability:** The **Principal is bound** by the sub-agent's acts (just as if the original agent had done them).
        
    - **The Agent** is responsible to the Principal for the sub-agent's actions.
        
    - The **Sub-Agent** is responsible _only to the agent_ (their boss), **except** for cases of fraud or willful wrong, where they are also responsible to the principal.
        
- **If _Improperly_ Appointed (Section 193):** (i.e., the agent delegated without any authority to do so)
    
    - **Liability:** The **Principal is _not_ bound** by the sub-agent's acts.
        
    - The **Agent is personally liable** to _both_ the principal and the third party for the sub-agent's actions.
        
    - The **Sub-Agent** is responsible only to the agent.
        

### 2. Co-Agents (or "Substituted Agents")

This is a completely different relationship that is often confused with a sub-agency. A co-agent is _not_ the agent's agent; they are the _principal's_ agent.

- **Definition (Section 194):** This is a person who is **"named" by the agent**, based on an express or implied authority from the principal, to act _for the principal_ in a specific part of the business.
    
- **Relationship:** The substituted agent is a **direct agent of the principal**. There _is_ privity of contract between the principal and the substituted agent.
    
- **Liability:** The _original agent_ has **no responsibility** for the acts or negligence of the substituted agent, _if_ the agent exercised reasonable prudence in selecting that person (as per **Section 195**).
    
- **Classic Example (from Act, Illus. (a) to S. 194):**
    
    - A (Principal) directs B (his solicitor) to sell his estate by auction.
        
    - B (Agent) names C, an auctioneer, to conduct the sale.
        
    - **Result:** C is **not a sub-agent**. C is A's **substituted agent** for the purpose of the sale. B's job was just to select the auctioneer; B is not responsible if C is negligent.
        

---

### 🔔 Important Notes & Tips

- **The Key Distinction (Test for Exam):**
    
    - **Sub-Agent (S. 191):** Works _under_ the agent. Has no contract with the principal.
        
    - **Substituted Agent (S. 194):** Works _for_ the principal. Has a direct contract with the principal, created _through_ the agent.
        
- **"Co-Agent" vs. "Substituted Agent":** The syllabus uses "Co-agent." While "Substituted Agent" is the term in Section 194, "Co-agent" can also mean one of several agents appointed by the principal. In the context of "Sub-agent," it is almost always used to mean a "Substituted Agent" (one agent appointing another _for the principal_).
